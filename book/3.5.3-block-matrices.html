3.5.3 Block Matrices

Proposition 3.5.32

Let 𝐴=ℤ[𝑋11,…,𝑋𝑛𝑛] be a polynomial ring in 𝑛2 indeterminates. Consider the matrix (𝑋𝑖𝑗)∈Mat𝑛×𝑛(𝐴) and let 𝐷𝑛 denote the determinant of this matrix.

Then for any commutative ring 𝐵 and matrix 𝑀∈Mat𝑛×𝑛(𝐵) we have

det(𝐵)=𝐷𝑛(𝐵11,…,𝐵1𝑛,𝐵21,…,𝐵𝑛𝑛)

where the right hand side corresponds the obvious evaluation homomorphism 𝐴→𝐵.

Proposition 3.5.33 (Block Matrix Determinant)

Suppose 𝑀𝑖𝑗 (1≤𝑖≤𝑛,1≤𝑗≤𝑛) are 𝑚×𝑚 matrices with coefficients in a ring 𝐴 and which commute pairwise. Form the matrix 𝑀 with coefficients in 𝐴 as follows

𝑀=(𝑀11…𝑀1𝑛⋮⋮𝑀𝑛1…𝑀𝑛𝑛)

Then

det(𝑀)=det(𝐷𝑛(𝑀11,…,𝑀1𝑛,𝑀21,…,𝑀𝑛𝑛))

Proof. We observe without proof that there is a “block” correspondence

Mat𝑛×𝑛(Mat𝑚×𝑚(𝐴))↔Mat(𝑛𝑚)×(𝑛𝑚)(𝐴)

which commutes with matrix multiplication.

Let 𝐵 denote the commutative subring of Mat𝑚×𝑚(𝐴) generated by the matrices 𝑀𝑖𝑗. Then we may regard 𝑀 as an element of Mat𝑛×𝑛(𝐵), and denote this by 𝑀̃ to disambiguate. As 𝐵 is commutative we my consider the adjugate matrix 𝑀̃ad. Further consider the matrix 𝑀′ given in block form by

𝑀′=((𝑀̃ad)110…0(𝑀̃ad)21𝐼𝑚…0⋮⋮⋱⋮(𝑀̃ad)𝑛10…𝐼𝑚)

Recall by (3.5.23) and (3.5.32) that

𝑀̃11ad=𝐷𝑛−1(𝑀22,…,𝑀2𝑛,𝑀31,…,𝑀𝑛𝑛)

and

𝑀̃𝑀̃ad=(det(𝑀̃)…0⋮⋱⋮0…det(𝑀̃))

as elements of Mat𝑛×𝑛(𝐵). Therefore computing the matrix product block-wise we find

𝑀𝑀′=(det(𝑀̃)𝑀12…𝑀1𝑛0𝑀22…𝑀2𝑛⋮⋮⋮0𝑀𝑛2…𝑀𝑛𝑛)

Then we may show using the Leibniz Expansion that

det(𝑀)⋅det(𝑀′)=det(𝑀𝑀′)=det(det(𝑀̃))⋅det(𝑀[𝑛+1,𝑚𝑛,𝑛+1,𝑚𝑛])

By induction on 𝑛 we may then assume det(𝑀[𝑛+1,𝑚𝑛,𝑛+1,𝑚𝑛])=det(𝐷𝑛−1(𝑀22,…,𝑀2𝑛,…,𝑀𝑛𝑛))=det((𝑀̃ad)11). Similarly we may argue by the Leibniz expansion that det(𝑀′)=det((𝑀̃ad)11). When this is not a zero-divisor in 𝐴 then we conclude that

det(𝑀)=det(det(𝑀̃))=det(𝐷𝑛(𝑀11,…,𝑀1𝑛,…,𝑀𝑛𝑛))

as required.

In order to handle the case where det((𝑀̃ad)11) is a zero divisor we may replace the ring 𝐴 with the polynomial ring 𝐴[𝑍] and consider the matrix 𝑁 for which

𝑁̃=𝑀̃+(𝑍⋅𝐼𝑚)⋅𝐼𝑛.

Then the submatrices of 𝑁 still commute pairwise. Further det((𝑁̃ad)11) is a polynomial of non-zero degree in 𝑍 whose leading coefficient is 1, and therefore is not a zero-divisor. We conclude from the previous case that

det(𝑁)=det(𝐷𝑛(𝑁11,…,𝑁1𝑛,…,𝑁𝑛𝑛))

Finally we may apply the ring homomorphism 𝐴[𝑍]→𝐴 (evaluation at 0) to deduce the general case. ∎
