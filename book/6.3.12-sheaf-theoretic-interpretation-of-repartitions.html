6.3.12 Sheaf-Theoretic Interpretation of Repartitions

Unlike the rest of this chapter, this section depends on sheaf theory developed later in this book (4.2.2), and so is not logically self-contained at this point in the text; nothing later in this chapter depends on it.

Let 𝑋≔ℙ𝐾, the set of places of 𝐾/𝑘, equipped with the cofinite topology (opens are ∅ and complements of finite subsets); as ℙ𝐾 is infinite, any two nonempty opens intersect, so 𝑋 is irreducible. Let 𝒦︀𝑋 be the constant sheaf with value 𝐾 (4.2.50). For 𝐷∈Div(𝐾) define the subsheaf

𝒪︀(𝐷)(𝑈)≔{𝑥∈𝐾|𝑣𝑃(𝑥)≥−𝑣𝑃(𝐷)∀𝑃∈𝑈}⊆𝒦︀𝑋(𝑈)

generalising the structure sheaf (𝒪︀(0)=𝒪︀𝑋), with inclusion 𝜄𝐷:𝒪︀(𝐷)↪︎𝒦︀𝑋.

Proposition 6.3.61 (Stalk of 𝒪︀(𝐷))

For 𝑃∈𝑋, the canonical map 𝒪︀(𝐷)𝑃→𝒦︀𝑋,𝑃=𝐾 is injective, identifying 𝒪︀(𝐷)𝑃 with the fractional ideal

{𝑦∈𝐾|𝑣𝑃(𝑦)≥−𝑣𝑃(𝐷)}≕𝜋𝑃−𝑣𝑃(𝐷)𝒪︀𝑃

of 𝐾.

Proof. Drafted by Claude Sonnet 5, reviewed by David Rufino.

Stalk of 𝒦︀𝑋. Since 𝑋 is irreducible, 𝒦︀𝑋(𝑈)=𝐾 for every nonempty open 𝑈⊂𝑋, with every restriction map the identity (4.2.50). We can therefore represent the stalk as 𝒦︀𝑋,𝑃=𝐾 with the identity maps. Under this identification, the canonical map 𝒪︀(𝐷)𝑃→𝒦︀𝑋,𝑃=𝐾 is given by (𝑈,𝑥)↦𝑥.

Injectivity. Two representatives (𝑈,𝑥),(𝑈′,𝑥′) of elements of 𝒪︀(𝐷)𝑃 become identified in the stalk exactly when they agree on some common refinement; as restriction maps are the identity, this means 𝑥=𝑥′ already, as elements of 𝐾. So (𝑈,𝑥)↦𝑥 is injective, and we identify 𝒪︀(𝐷)𝑃 with its image in 𝐾.

𝒪︀(𝐷)𝑃⊆{𝑦:𝑣𝑃(𝑦)≥−𝑣𝑃(𝐷)}. Every element of 𝒪︀(𝐷)𝑃 is represented by a pair (𝑈,𝑥), 𝑃∈𝑈, 𝑥∈𝒪︀(𝐷)(𝑈), whose image in 𝒪︀(𝐷)𝑃 is 𝑥 itself (viewed in 𝐾); taking the defining condition of 𝒪︀(𝐷)(𝑈) at the point 𝑃∈𝑈 gives 𝑣𝑃(𝑥)≥−𝑣𝑃(𝐷).

{𝑦:𝑣𝑃(𝑦)≥−𝑣𝑃(𝐷)}⊆𝒪︀(𝐷)𝑃. Let 𝑦∈𝐾 with 𝑣𝑃(𝑦)≥−𝑣𝑃(𝐷). By (6.2.116), 𝑦 has finitely many poles, so together with finiteness of Supp(𝐷), the set 𝑇≔{𝑄∈𝑋|𝑣𝑄(𝑦)<−𝑣𝑄(𝐷)} is finite. As 𝑃∉𝑇, 𝑈≔𝑋∖𝑇 is an open neighbourhood of 𝑃, and 𝑦∈𝒪︀(𝐷)(𝑈) by construction of 𝑇; so (𝑈,𝑦) represents an element of 𝒪︀(𝐷)𝑃 mapping to 𝑦.

Fractional ideal. By the previous two paragraphs 𝒪︀(𝐷)𝑃={𝑦∈𝐾|𝑣𝑃(𝑦)≥−𝑣𝑃(𝐷)}. Writing 𝜋𝑃 for a uniformiser of 𝒪︀𝑃, (3.31.2) represents every 𝑦∈𝐾⋆ uniquely as 𝑦=𝑢𝜋𝑃𝑣𝑃(𝑦) for 𝑢∈𝒪︀𝑃⋆, so 𝑣𝑃(𝑦)≥−𝑣𝑃(𝐷)⟺𝑦∈𝜋𝑃−𝑣𝑃(𝐷)𝒪︀𝑃; thus 𝒪︀(𝐷)𝑃=𝜋𝑃−𝑣𝑃(𝐷)𝒪︀𝑃. This is the image of 𝒪︀𝑃 under multiplication by the fixed element 𝜋𝑃−𝑣𝑃(𝐷)∈𝐾, hence an 𝒪︀𝑃-submodule of 𝐾 and so a fractional ideal of 𝒪︀𝑃 (3.30.1) (indeed 𝒪︀𝑃 is a discrete valuation ring by (6.2.114), so this is the fractional ideal 𝔪𝑃−𝑣𝑃(𝐷) of (3.31.7)); in particular it is a 𝑘-vector subspace of 𝐾, since 𝑘⊂𝒪︀𝑃 (6.2.113). ∎

Proposition 6.3.62 (Stalk of the Cokernel)
Since 𝜄𝐷 is a monomorphism (6.3.61), write 𝒦︀𝑋/𝒪︀(𝐷)≔coker(𝜄𝐷) (4.2.36). There is a unique morphism 𝜃𝑃 making
commute, and 𝜃𝑃 is (moreover) an isomorphism; in particular (𝒦︀𝑋/𝒪︀(𝐷))𝑃≅𝐾/𝒪︀(𝐷)𝑃=𝐾/𝜋𝑃−𝑣𝑃(𝐷)𝒪︀𝑃.

Proof. Drafted by Claude Sonnet 5, reviewed by David Rufino.

This is (4.2.37), applied to 𝜄𝐷:𝒪︀(𝐷)→𝒦︀𝑋 (using 𝒦︀𝑋,𝑃=𝐾), together with (6.3.61) identifying 𝒪︀(𝐷)𝑃 with 𝜋𝑃−𝑣𝑃(𝐷)𝒪︀𝑃. ∎

Used freely below.

Proposition 6.3.63 (Repartitions compute the Cokernel Sheaf)

Writing 𝑝𝑋:𝐾=Γ(𝑋,𝒦︀𝑋)→Γ(𝑋,𝒦︀𝑋/𝒪︀(𝐷)) for the global sections of the cokernel map (4.2.35), there is an exact sequence of 𝑘-vector spaces

0⟶𝐿(𝐷)⟶𝐾→𝑝𝑋Γ(𝑋,𝒦︀𝑋/𝒪︀(𝐷))→Φ𝒜︀𝐾/(𝒜︀𝐾(𝐷)+𝐾)⟶0

where Φ sends 𝜏∈Γ(𝑋,𝒦︀𝑋/𝒪︀(𝐷)) – a function 𝜏:𝑋→∐𝑃∈𝑋𝐾/𝒪︀(𝐷)𝑃 (4.2.35) – to the class of any repartition (𝛼𝑃)𝑃∈𝒜︀𝐾 with 𝛼𝑃≡𝜏(𝑃)(mod𝒪︀(𝐷)𝑃) for every 𝑃; that is, sending a representative 𝑥∈𝐾 to its corresponding residue vector. In particular 𝑖(𝐷) is (via (6.3.63)) the dimension of the cokernel of 𝑝𝑋 – via sheaf cohomology, the group 𝐻1(𝑋,𝒪︀(𝐷)), a cohomological invariant of 𝑋, not merely of the algebra of repartitions.

Sketch. (𝜄𝐷)𝑈 is the inclusion 𝒪︀(𝐷)(𝑈)↪︎𝐾, injective.

By (4.2.35) a global section of 𝒦︀𝑋/𝒪︀(𝐷) is represented by a cover of 𝑋 together with representatives in 𝐾 over each open, and since the cofinite topology is quasi-compact (4.1.93) this cover may be taken finite, say 𝑋=𝑈1∪⋯∪𝑈𝑛 together with representatives 𝑥𝑖∈𝐾, well-defined modulo 𝒪︀(𝐷)(𝑈𝑖), that agree at every point of overlap: for every place 𝑃∈𝑈𝑖∩𝑈𝑗, the images of 𝑥𝑖,𝑥𝑗 in the stalk (𝒦︀𝑋/𝒪︀(𝐷))𝑃=𝐾/𝜋𝑃−𝑣𝑃(𝐷)𝒪︀𝑃 (6.3.62) coincide, that is

𝑣𝑃(𝑥𝑖−𝑥𝑗)≥−𝑣𝑃(𝐷)

Given such data, set 𝛼𝑃≔𝑥𝑖 (a literal element of 𝐾, well-defined only up to the choice of 𝑖) for any 𝑖 with 𝑃∈𝑈𝑖. The class of 𝛼𝑃 modulo 𝜋𝑃−𝑣𝑃(𝐷)𝒪︀𝑃 is independent of the choice of 𝑖: if also 𝑃∈𝑈𝑗, then 𝑃∈𝑈𝑖∩𝑈𝑗, so the overlap condition above applies directly at this 𝑃 and gives 𝑣𝑃(𝑥𝑖−𝑥𝑗)≥−𝑣𝑃(𝐷), that is 𝑥𝑖≡𝑥𝑗(mod𝜋𝑃−𝑣𝑃(𝐷)𝒪︀𝑃). Each 𝑥𝑖 has finitely many poles (6.2.116), so {𝑃|𝛼𝑃∉𝒪︀𝑃}⊆⋃𝑖=1𝑛{𝑃∈𝑈𝑖|𝑣𝑃(𝑥𝑖)<0}, a finite union of finite sets, is finite; so 𝛼𝑃∈𝒪︀𝑃 for all but finitely many 𝑃, giving (𝛼𝑃)𝑃∈𝒜︀𝐾.

Independence of the choice of representing data. Let (𝑈𝑖,𝑥𝑖)𝑖=1𝑛 and (𝑈𝑖′,𝑥𝑖′)𝑖=1𝑚 be two choices representing the same 𝜏, with resulting repartitions (𝛼𝑃)𝑃,(𝛼𝑃′)𝑃∈𝒜︀𝐾 as above. For each 𝑃, choosing 𝑖,𝑖′ with 𝑃∈𝑈𝑖∩𝑈𝑖′, both 𝑥𝑖 and 𝑥𝑖′ represent 𝜏(𝑃)∈𝐾/𝒪︀(𝐷)𝑃 (4.2.35), that is 𝑥𝑖≡𝑥𝑖′(mod𝒪︀(𝐷)𝑃), so 𝛼𝑃−𝛼𝑃′=𝑥𝑖−𝑥𝑖′∈𝒪︀(𝐷)𝑃. As this holds for every 𝑃, (𝛼𝑃−𝛼𝑃′)𝑃∈𝒜︀𝐾(𝐷); so (𝛼𝑃)𝑃 and (𝛼𝑃′)𝑃 have the same class in 𝒜︀𝐾/(𝒜︀𝐾(𝐷)+𝐾). This assignment therefore descends to a well-defined map

Φ:Γ(𝑋,𝒦︀𝑋/𝒪︀(𝐷))⟶𝒜︀𝐾/(𝒜︀𝐾(𝐷)+𝐾)

Exactness at 𝐾. For 𝑐∈𝐾, 𝑝𝑋(𝑐) is represented by the trivial cover {𝑋} with 𝑥1≔𝑐, so 𝑝𝑋(𝑐)=0 if and only if 𝑐∈𝒪︀(𝐷)(𝑋)=𝐿(𝐷); that is, ker(𝑝𝑋)=𝐿(𝐷), matching exactness at 𝐾 (the map 𝐿(𝐷)↪︎𝐾 being the inclusion).

Exactness at Γ(𝑋,𝒦︀𝑋/𝒪︀(𝐷)). (Im(𝑝𝑋)⊆ker(Φ)) For 𝑐∈𝐾, 𝑝𝑋(𝑐) has 𝛼𝑃=𝑐 for every 𝑃, that is, the constant repartition 𝛿(𝑐) (diagonal embedding 𝛿:𝐾↪︎𝒜︀𝐾), whose class in 𝒜︀𝐾/(𝒜︀𝐾(𝐷)+𝐾) is 0, since 𝛿(𝑐)∈𝐾; so Φ(𝑝𝑋(𝑐))=0.

(ker(Φ)⊆Im(𝑝𝑋)) Suppose Φ(𝜏)=0, that is (𝛼𝑃)𝑃∈𝒜︀𝐾(𝐷)+𝐾, say (𝛼𝑃)𝑃=𝛽+𝛿(𝑐) with 𝛽∈𝒜︀𝐾(𝐷), 𝑐∈𝐾. Then 𝑥𝑖−𝑐∈𝒪︀(𝐷)(𝑈𝑖) for every 𝑖 (as 𝑥𝑖−𝑐=𝛼𝑃−𝑐=𝛽𝑃∈𝒪︀(𝐷)𝑃 for every 𝑃∈𝑈𝑖), so 𝜏 and 𝑝𝑋(𝑐) are represented by the same local data, that is 𝜏=𝑝𝑋(𝑐)∈Im(𝑝𝑋).

Exactness at 𝒜︀𝐾/(𝒜︀𝐾(𝐷)+𝐾). Given (𝛼𝑄)𝑄∈𝒜︀𝐾, let

𝑆≔{𝑄|𝛼𝑄∉𝒪︀𝑄}∪Supp(𝐷)={𝑃1,…,𝑃𝑛}

finite, being a union of two finite sets; note 𝑣𝑄(𝐷)=0 for every 𝑄∉𝑆, so 𝒪︀(𝐷)𝑄=𝒪︀𝑄 there. Set 𝑥0≔0 and, for 𝑖=1,…,𝑛, 𝑥𝑖≔𝛼𝑃𝑖 and 𝑍𝑖≔{𝑄≠𝑃𝑖|𝛼𝑃𝑖∉𝒪︀𝑄}, the poles of 𝛼𝑃𝑖 other than possibly at 𝑃𝑖 itself, finite by (6.2.116). Since the cofinite topology has no “small” neighbourhoods – any nonempty open already excludes only finitely many points – each 𝑈𝑖 (𝑖≥1) must be taken to also avoid 𝑥𝑖’s own poles, not merely to contain 𝑃𝑖: set

𝑈0≔𝑋∖𝑆,𝑈𝑖≔𝑋∖((𝑆∪𝑍𝑖)∖{𝑃𝑖})(𝑖=1,…,𝑛)

These cover 𝑋: 𝑈0∪⋯∪𝑈𝑛⊇(𝑋∖𝑆)∪{𝑃1,…,𝑃𝑛}=𝑋, as 𝑃𝑖∈𝑈𝑖 for each 𝑖.

Each 𝑈𝑖 meets 𝑆 in at most the single point 𝑃𝑖: 𝑈0∩𝑆=∅, and 𝑈𝑖∩𝑆={𝑃𝑖} for 𝑖≥1, since 𝑈𝑖 excludes every other point of 𝑆. Consequently, for 𝑖≠𝑗, no point of 𝑆 lies in both 𝑈𝑖 and 𝑈𝑗 (it would have to be the marked point of each, and the marked points – or absence thereof, for 𝑖=0 – differ), so 𝑈𝑖∩𝑈𝑗⊆𝑋∖𝑆; in particular 𝑄∈𝑈𝑖∩𝑈𝑗 gives 𝑣𝑄(𝐷)=0 and 𝑄≠𝑃𝑖,𝑃𝑗.

They agree at overlaps: for 𝑖≠𝑗 in {0,…,𝑛} and 𝑄∈𝑈𝑖∩𝑈𝑗, 𝑣𝑄(𝑥𝑖)≥0 (trivially if 𝑖=0, as 𝑥0=0; else since 𝑄∈𝑈𝑖, 𝑄≠𝑃𝑖 gives 𝑄∉𝑍𝑖) and likewise 𝑣𝑄(𝑥𝑗)≥0; so by the ultrametric inequality 𝑣𝑄(𝑥𝑖−𝑥𝑗)≥min(𝑣𝑄(𝑥𝑖),𝑣𝑄(𝑥𝑗))≥0=−𝑣𝑄(𝐷).

This data therefore glues to a class 𝜏∈Γ(𝑋,𝒦︀𝑋/𝒪︀(𝐷)) with 𝜏(𝑄)=𝛼𝑄(mod𝒪︀(𝐷)𝑄) for every 𝑄 (on 𝑈0 this matches 𝑥0=0, since 𝑄∉𝑆 gives 𝛼𝑄∈𝒪︀𝑄=𝒪︀(𝐷)𝑄; at 𝑄=𝑃𝑖 this matches 𝑥𝑖=𝛼𝑃𝑖 directly), so Φ(𝜏)=(𝛼𝑄)𝑄 exactly; so Φ is surjective, matching exactness at the final term. ∎
