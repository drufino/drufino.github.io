3.18.8 Separability (Algebraic Case)

We follow Lang and not only characterize separability but define a “separability degree” which equals the extension degree if and only if it’s separable. The proofs are somewhat technical, especially in light of the fact most base fields will be perfect.

Definition 3.18.74 (Separable Element)

We say 𝛼∈𝐾/𝑘 is separable over 𝑘 if it is algebraic and 𝑚𝛼,𝑘(𝑋) is a separable polynomial.

We say 𝐾/𝑘 is separable algebraic (or just separable if 𝐾/𝑘 is assumed to be algebraic) if every 𝛼∈𝐾 is separable.

Lemma 3.18.75

𝛼∈𝐾/𝑘 is separable if and only if it is a root of a separable polynomial in 𝑘[𝑋].

In particular 𝛼 separable over 𝑘 implies it is separable over any subfield 𝑖𝑘𝐾(𝑘)⊂𝐸⊂𝐾.

Proof. One direction is obvious. Conversely suppose 𝑓(𝛼)=0 with 𝑓 separable. Then 𝑚𝛼,𝑘|𝑓 so the result follows from (3.16.40). ∎
Proposition 3.18.76 (Separability Degree)

Let 𝐾/𝑘 be an algebraic extension and 𝐿/𝐾 an extension such that 𝐿/𝑘 is normal (e.g. 𝐿=𝑘― or 𝐿 is a normal closure). Then define the separability degree

[𝐾:𝑘]𝑠≔#︎Mor𝑘(𝐾,𝐿)

This is independent of the choice of 𝐿/𝐾.

Proof. Given such an 𝐿, let 𝐿′/𝐾 be the intersection of all subfields of 𝐿/𝐾 normal over 𝑘. This is a normal closure of 𝐾/𝑘. Let 𝜎∈Mor𝑘(𝐾,𝐿) and 𝛼∈𝐾. Then 𝜎(𝛼) is a root of 𝑚𝛼,𝑘(𝑋) along with 𝑖𝐾𝐿(𝛼)∈𝐿′. As 𝐿′ is normal we have 𝜎(𝛼)∈𝐿′. Therefore without loss of generality we may replace 𝐿 with 𝐿′. As the normal closure is unique up to isomorphism the degree is well-defined. ∎

First we prove a key lemma regarding simple extension

Lemma 3.18.77 (Separability Degree of Simple Extension)

If 𝑘(𝛼)/𝑘 is a simple extension and 𝐿/𝑘 normal overfield then

[𝑘(𝛼):𝑘]𝑠=#︎{roots of 𝑚𝛼 in 𝐿}≤deg(𝑚𝛼)=[𝑘(𝛼):𝑘]

Furthermore equality holds iff 𝛼 is separable over 𝑘.

Proof. The first equality follows from (3.18.49), the final equality from (3.18.42). The inequality follows from (3.16.35). The final statement follows from (3.18.11). ∎

The main results of this section are the following

Proposition 3.18.78 (Separability Degree)

Let 𝐿/𝐾/𝐹/𝑘 be a tower of finite extensions with 𝐿/𝑘 normal. Then the restriction map

Mor𝑘(𝑖𝐹𝐾,𝐿):Mor𝑘(𝐾,𝐿)→Mor𝑘(𝐹,𝐿)

is surjective with finite fibers of order [𝐾:𝐹]𝑠. In particular we have

[𝐾:𝑘]𝑠=[𝐾:𝐹]𝑠[𝐹:𝑘]𝑠

Further [𝐾:𝑘]𝑠≤[𝐾:𝑘] with equality if and only if 𝐾/𝑘 is separable.

Proof. For a tower 𝐿/𝐾/𝐹/𝑘 with 𝐿/𝑘 normal, consider the restriction map

𝜓≔Mor𝑘(𝑖𝐹𝐾,𝐿):Mor𝑘(𝐾,𝐿)→Mor𝑘(𝐹,𝐿)

It is surjective by (3.18.70). Consider 𝜎∈Mor𝑘(𝐹,𝐿) and the fibre 𝜓−1(𝜎)=Mor𝐹(𝐾,(𝐿/𝐹,𝜎)). This has order equal to #︎𝜓−1(𝜎)=[𝐾:𝐹]𝑠 for all 𝜎, because by (3.18.76) this does not depend on the embedding 𝑖𝐹𝐿. As Mor𝑘(𝐾,𝐿) is equal to the disjoint union of all the fibres, then the multiplicativity result follows.

It is possible to decompose 𝐾/𝑘 as a tower of simple extensions

𝐾=𝐾𝑛/…/𝐾0=𝑘

with 𝐾𝑖=𝐾𝑖−1(𝛼𝑖). By (3.18.77) we have

[𝐾𝑖:𝐾𝑖−1]𝑠≤[𝐾𝑖:𝐾𝑖−1]

with equality iff 𝛼𝑖 separable over 𝐾𝑖−1. By multiplicativity the inequality follows.

If 𝐾/𝑘 is separable then by (3.18.75) 𝛼𝑖 is separable over 𝐾𝑖−1 and we have [𝐾𝑖:𝐾𝑖−1]𝑠=[𝐾𝑖:𝐾𝑖−1] and [𝐾:𝑘]𝑠=[𝐾:𝑘] by multiplicativity. Conversely if [𝐾:𝑘]𝑠=[𝐾:𝑘] then [𝐾𝑖:𝐾𝑖−1]𝑠=[𝐾𝑖:𝐾𝑖−1] and 𝛼𝑖 is separable over 𝐾𝑖−1. Since the choice of 𝛼1 was arbitrary we see that 𝐾/𝑘 is separable. ∎

Proposition 3.18.79 (Towers of Separable Extensions)
Consider a tower of algebraic extensions 𝐾/𝐸/𝑘. Then 𝐾/𝐸 and 𝐸/𝑘 is separable iff 𝐾/𝑘 is.

Proof. 𝐾/𝑘 separable ⟹ 𝐾/𝐸 and 𝐸/𝑘 separable follows from (3.18.75).

Conversely the finite case follows from (3.18.78) by multiplicativity. For the general case, consider 𝛼∈𝐾. Then (3.18.46) shows the existence of a finite subextension 𝐸0/𝑘 of 𝐸 such that 𝑚𝛼,𝐸=𝑚𝛼,𝐸0. Therefore 𝛼 is separable over 𝐸0. By (3.18.77) we see that [𝐸0(𝛼):𝐸0]𝑠=[𝐸0(𝛼):𝐸0]. As 𝐸/𝑘 is separable a-fortiori 𝐸0/𝑘 is separable so by (3.18.78) [𝐸0:𝑘]𝑠=[𝐸0:𝑘]. By multiplicativity [𝐸0(𝛼):𝑘]𝑠=[𝐸0(𝛼):𝑘] and the same result again shows that 𝐸0(𝛼)/𝑘 is separable. In particular 𝛼 is separable over 𝑘 as required. ∎

Proposition 3.18.80
An algebraic extension 𝐾=𝑘(𝛼1,…,𝛼𝑛)/𝑘 is separable iff 𝛼𝑖 are.

Proof. Let 𝐾=𝑘(𝛼1,…,𝛼𝑛). Then we may construct a tower of finite (simple) extensions

𝐾=𝐾𝑛/…/𝐾0=𝑘

with 𝐾𝑖=𝑘(𝛼1,…,𝛼𝑖) and 𝐾𝑖=𝐾𝑖−1(𝛼𝑖). By (3.18.75) 𝛼𝑖 is separable over 𝐾𝑖−1. Therefore [𝐾𝑖:𝐾𝑖−1]𝑠=[𝐾𝑖:𝐾𝑖−1] by (3.18.77) and [𝐾:𝑘]𝑠=[𝐾:𝑘] by multiplicativity. (3.18.78) shows that 𝐾/𝑘 is separable. ∎

Proposition 3.18.81
Let 𝐾/𝑘 be a splitting field for finitely many separable polynomials 𝑓𝑖. Then 𝐾/𝑘 is finite and Galois.
Proof. By definition 𝐾 is generated by the (finitely) many roots 𝛼𝑖𝑗 and so is finite by (3.18.44) and normal by (3.18.68). By (3.18.75) each 𝛼𝑖𝑗 is separable and so by (3.18.80) 𝐾/𝑘 is separable. ∎
Proposition 3.18.82
Let 𝐾/𝑘 be a finite separable extension and 𝐿/𝑘 a normal closure. Then 𝐿/𝑘 is separable.
Proof. By (3.18.73) 𝐿/𝑘 is the splitting field of finitely many minimal polynomials 𝑚𝛼𝑖,𝑘(𝑋) for 𝛼𝑖∈𝐾. By (3.18.80) the 𝛼𝑖 are separable and therefore so are 𝑚𝛼𝑖,𝑘(𝑋). By (3.18.81) we see that 𝐿/𝑘 is separable. ∎
Proposition 3.18.83 (Equivalent Definition of Separability)

Let 𝐾/𝑘 be an algebraic extension. TFAE

  1. 𝐾/𝑘 is separable
  2. 𝐸/𝑘 is separable for every finite subextension
  3. [𝐸:𝑘]𝑠=[𝐸:𝑘] for every finite subextension

Proof. 𝑎)⟹𝑏) is trivial and 𝑏)⟺𝑐) follows from (3.18.78). We need only show 𝑏)⟹𝑎).

Consider 𝛼∈𝐾. Then by (3.18.46) there exists a finite subextension 𝐸/𝑘 such that 𝛼 is algebraic over 𝐸. Therefore 𝐸(𝛼)/𝑘 is finite, and by assumption 𝐸(𝛼)/𝑘 separable as required. ∎
