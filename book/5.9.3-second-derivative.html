5.9.3 Second Derivative

Definition 5.9.18

For a map 𝛼:𝑋×𝑋→𝑌 we say that 𝛼(𝑣,𝑤) is sublinear if for all 𝜀>0 there exists 𝛿>0 such that

‖(𝑣,𝑤)‖<𝛿⟹‖𝛼(𝑣,𝑤)‖<𝜀‖𝑣‖‖𝑤‖

where the norm on 𝑋×𝑋 is the product norm.

As before we have the following uniqueness property

Lemma 5.9.19
Let 𝛼:𝑋×𝑋→𝑌 be a bilinear map which is also sublinear. Then 𝛼=0.
Proposition 5.9.20 (Second derivative is symmetric)

Let 𝑓:𝑈→𝑌 be a 𝐶2 map. Then for all 𝑥∈𝑈 we have 𝐷2𝑓(𝑥) is the unique bilinear map 𝑋×𝑋→𝑌 such that

𝑓(𝑥+𝑣+𝑤)−𝑓(𝑥+𝑤)−𝑓(𝑥+𝑣)+𝑓(𝑥)−𝐷2𝑓(𝑥)(𝑣,𝑤)is sublinear

Furthermore it is symmetric.

Proof. Uniqueness follows from (5.9.19) and from this it immediately follows that 𝐷2𝑓(𝑥) is symmetric. Therefore we only need to show the required property. Let 𝑣,𝑤∈𝑋 be such that 𝑥+𝑡𝑣+𝑠𝑤∈𝑈 for all 0≤𝑠,𝑡≤1. Then define

𝑔(𝑥)≔𝑓(𝑥+𝑣)−𝑓(𝑥)

By (…) applied to 𝑔 and 𝐷𝑓 we find

𝑔(𝑥+𝑤)−𝑔(𝑥)=∫01[𝐷𝑓(𝑥+𝑣+𝑡𝑤)(𝑤)−𝐷𝑓(𝑥+𝑡𝑤)(𝑤)]d𝑡=∫01∫01𝐷2𝑓(𝑥+𝑠𝑣+𝑡𝑤)(𝑣)d𝑠(𝑤)d𝑡=𝐷2𝑓(𝑥)(𝑣,𝑤)+∫01∫01[𝐷2𝑓(𝑥+𝑠𝑣+𝑡𝑤)(𝑣)−𝐷2𝑓(𝑥)(𝑣)]d𝑠(𝑤)d𝑡

Let 𝜓(𝑣,𝑤) denote the integral then

‖𝜓(𝑣,𝑤)‖≤∫01∫01‖𝐷2𝑓(𝑥+𝑠𝑣+𝑡𝑤)(𝑣)−𝐷2𝑓(𝑥)(𝑣)‖d𝑠‖𝑤‖d𝑡≤∫01∫01‖𝐷2𝑓(𝑥+𝑠𝑣+𝑡𝑤)−𝐷2𝑓(𝑥)‖d𝑠d𝑡⋅‖𝑣‖‖𝑤‖≤sup0≤𝑠,𝑡≤1‖𝐷2𝑓(𝑥+𝑠𝑣+𝑡𝑤)−𝐷2𝑓(𝑥)‖‖𝑣‖‖𝑤‖

where the estimate is finite by continuity of 𝐷2𝑓 and the Boundedness Theorem (5.7.8). Note that ‖𝑠𝑣+𝑡𝑤‖≤‖𝑣‖+‖𝑤‖≤2‖(𝑣,𝑤)‖. Therefore we may use the continuity of 𝐷2𝑓 to show that 𝜓 is sublinear as required. ∎

Proposition 5.9.21 (Symmetry of partial derivatives)

Let 𝑋=𝑋1×⋯×𝑋𝑛, 𝜑:𝑈→𝑌 be a 𝐶2 map and 𝑥∈𝑈. Then

𝐷2𝜑(𝑥)((0,⋯,ℎ𝑖,⋯,0),(0,⋯,ℎ𝑗,⋯,0))=𝐷𝑖𝐷𝑗𝜑(𝑥)(ℎ𝑖,ℎ𝑗)

In particular

𝐷𝑖𝐷𝑗𝜑(𝑥)(ℎ𝑗,ℎ𝑖)=𝐷𝑗𝐷𝑖𝜑(𝑥)(ℎ𝑖,ℎ𝑗)
Proof. The first equation follows from (5.9.14) and the second from (5.9.20). ∎
Proposition 5.9.22 (Hessian Matrix)

Let 𝑋 be a finite-dimensional Banach spaces with basis {𝑒1,⋯,𝑒𝑛}, 𝑈⊂𝑋 an open subset and 𝜑:𝑈→𝐾 a 𝐶2 map. Then the second order partial derivatives 𝜕2𝜑𝜕𝑒𝑖𝜕𝑒𝑗≔𝜕𝜕𝑒𝑗𝜕𝜑𝜕𝑒𝑖:𝑈→𝐾 exist for all 1≤𝑖,𝑗≤𝑛.

Denote by

𝐻𝜑≔(𝜕2𝜑𝜕𝑒𝑖𝜕𝑒𝑗)1≤𝑖,𝑗≤𝑛

the Hessian Matrix which is the matrix of the bilinear form 𝐷2𝜑 with respect to the given basis, and in particular symmetric.
