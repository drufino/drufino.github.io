3.3.2 Sums in a Ring

Proposition 3.3.17 (Distributivity for Sums in a Ring)

Let 𝐴 be a ring, 𝑟∈𝐴 and (𝑎𝑖)𝑖∈𝐼 a family in 𝐴 with all but finitely many terms zero. Then (𝑟𝑎𝑖)𝑖∈𝐼 and (𝑎𝑖𝑟)𝑖∈𝐼 again have all but finitely many terms zero, and

𝑟∑𝑖∈𝐼𝑎𝑖=∑𝑖∈𝐼𝑟𝑎𝑖(∑𝑖∈𝐼𝑎𝑖)𝑟=∑𝑖∈𝐼𝑎𝑖𝑟

If (𝑏𝑗)𝑗∈𝐽 is another such family then (𝑎𝑖𝑏𝑗)(𝑖,𝑗)∈𝐼×𝐽 has all but finitely many terms zero, and

(∑𝑖∈𝐼𝑎𝑖)(∑𝑗∈𝐽𝑏𝑗)=∑(𝑖,𝑗)∈𝐼×𝐽𝑎𝑖𝑏𝑗=∑𝑖∈𝐼∑𝑗∈𝐽𝑎𝑖𝑏𝑗

and likewise with the order of summation exchanged.

Proof. Drafted by Claude Sonnet, reviewed by David Rufino.

By the distributive property 𝑥↦𝑟𝑥 and 𝑥↦𝑥𝑟 are homomorphisms of the additive group of 𝐴, so the first claims are (3.2.51) for these homomorphisms. If 𝑎𝑖𝑏𝑗≠0 then 𝑎𝑖≠0 and 𝑏𝑗≠0, so 𝑎𝑖𝑏𝑗 vanishes outside a product of two finite sets. Write 𝑏≔∑𝑗∈𝐽𝑏𝑗. Then by the first part applied to 𝑏 and then to each 𝑎𝑖

(∑𝑖∈𝐼𝑎𝑖)𝑏=∑𝑖∈𝐼𝑎𝑖𝑏=∑𝑖∈𝐼𝑎𝑖∑𝑗∈𝐽𝑏𝑗=∑𝑖∈𝐼∑𝑗∈𝐽𝑎𝑖𝑏𝑗=∑(𝑖,𝑗)∈𝐼×𝐽𝑎𝑖𝑏𝑗

where the last equality is (3.2.53). The other order of summation is identical. ∎
