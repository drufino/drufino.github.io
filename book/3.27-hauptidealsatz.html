3.27 Hauptidealsatz

The main result of this section is the following result due to Krull

Proposition 3.27.1 (Generalized Hauptidealsatz for Noetherian Rings)

Suppose 𝐴 is a Noetherian ring then the following properties hold

  1. Every prime ideal of height 𝑛 is minimal over some ideal 𝔞≔(𝑥1,…,𝑥𝑛). Furthermore 𝔞 may be chosen such that ht(𝔞)=𝑛 and every minimal prime of 𝔞 is of height 𝑛

  2. We have the following characterization of height

    ht(𝔭)=min{𝑛|𝔭minimal over(𝑥1,…,𝑥𝑛)}

In particular if 𝔭 is minimal over (𝑥1,…,𝑥𝑛) then ht(𝔭)≤𝑛.

In full generality the proof is quite subtle and in most cases of interest a simpler proof is possible. Therefore we introduce the following notions.

Definition 3.27.2 (Hauptidealsatz Ring)

We say a ring 𝐴 is a generalized hauptidealsatz ring if for all 𝑥1,…,𝑥𝑛∈𝐴 and 𝔭 prime ideals minimal over (𝑥1,…,𝑥𝑛) we have ht(𝔭)≤𝑛

We say a ring 𝐴 is simply a hauptidealsatz ring if this holds for 𝑛=1.

Lemma 3.27.3
Let 𝐴 be a hauptidealsatz ring and 𝔭 a prime ideal. Then 𝐴𝔭 is hauptidealsatz.
Proof. Any prime ideal of 𝐴𝔭 has the form 𝔮𝐴𝔭 by the correspondence of ideals under localization (3.6.19). If 𝔮𝐴𝔭 is minimal over (𝑓/𝑠)=(𝑓/1), then clearly (𝑓)⊆𝔮. Furthermore (𝑓)⊆𝔮′⟹(𝑓/1)⊆𝔮′𝐴𝔭. So 𝔮 is also minimal over (𝑓) and therefore has height at most 1 by assumption. By the same result 𝔮𝐴𝔭 has height at most 1. ∎
Proposition 3.27.4 (Hauptidealsatz ⇒ Generalized Hauptidealsatz (Geometric))
Suppose that 𝐴 is catenary, and hauptidealsatz for every quotient by a prime ideal. Then 𝐴 is generalized hauptidealsatz, and so is every localization at a prime ideal.

Proof. We consider the case first that 𝐴 is integral (which means it is hauptidealsatz by assumption because (0) is prime) and assume wlog that 𝑛>1. Let 𝔭 be a minimal prime of (𝑥1,…,𝑥𝑛) and 𝔮 a minimal prime of (𝑥1,…,𝑥𝑛−1). By considering the localization 𝐴𝔭 we may choose 𝔮⊆𝔭. By induction ht(𝔮)≤𝑛−1. Then 𝔭/𝔮 is a minimal prime over (𝑥𝑛+𝔮) and therefore by assumption has height at most 1. By the codimension formula (3.22.9) we see ht(𝔭)≤𝑛 as required.

For the general case, suppose 𝔭 is minimal over (𝑥1,…,𝑥𝑛) and let 𝔯⊆𝔭 be a minimal prime. Then the ring 𝐴/𝔯 is integral and we see that ht(𝔭/𝔯)≤𝑛 by the first part. Taking the supremum over all minimal primes 𝔯 shows that ht(𝔭)≤𝑛.

For the last statement we need only show that every localization at a prime ideal satisfies the hypotheses of the theorem. By correspondence of ideals under localisation (3.6.19) we see that every such ring is catenary. By (3.6.25) 𝐴𝔭/𝔮𝐴𝔭≅(𝐴/𝔮)𝔭/𝔮 for any prime ideal 𝔮⊆𝔭 so every quotient by a prime ideal is hauptidealsatz by the previous Lemma. ∎

The catenary assumption may be relaxed by making a more complicated argument.

Proposition 3.27.5 (Hauptidealsatz ⇒ Generalized Hauptidealsatz (Algebraic))
Suppose 𝐴 is a ring such that every quotient by a prime ideal satisfies the hauptidealsatz. Then 𝐴 is generalized hauptidealsatz and so is every localization at a prime ideal 𝔭.

Proof. We prove this by induction on 𝑛 for a fixed ring 𝐴. Let 𝔭 be a minimal prime over (𝑥1,…,𝑥𝑛). Suppose ht(𝔭)>𝑛 then we may choose a saturated chain 𝔮⊊𝔭 such that ht(𝔮)≥𝑛. By minimality of 𝔭 we have 𝔮∩{𝑥1,…,𝑥𝑛}={𝑥1,…,𝑥𝑟} with 𝑟<𝑛 after a suitable reordering (possibly with 𝑟=0). Furthermore we may choose 𝔮 such that 𝑟 is maximal. Then by the induction hypothesis 𝔮 is not minimal over (𝑥1,…,𝑥𝑟) and there is a chain

(𝑥1,…,𝑥𝑟)⊆𝔯⊊𝔮⊊𝔭

We claim that 𝔭 is minimal over (𝔯,𝑥𝑟+1) for suppose

(𝔯,𝑥𝑟+1)⊆𝔭′⊆𝔭

then by construction of 𝔮 (𝑟 was maximal) we see that 𝔭′=𝔭. Taking quotients by 𝔯 the hauptidealsatz property shows that ht(𝔭/𝔯)≤1. On the other hand we have a chain

(0)⊊𝔮/𝔯⊊𝔭/𝔯

which is a contradiction.

The final statement follows a similar argument as before. ∎

Remark 3.27.6
This argument is from [Kap74] (Sec. 3.2 Ex. 6).

Before proceeding to the main result we need a some technical results related to height of ideals.

Lemma 3.27.7

Let 𝔮⊆𝔭 be prime ideals then

ht(𝔮)≤ht(𝔭)

with equality iff 𝔭=𝔮.

Proof. The inequality is clear since any maximal chain below 𝔮 may be extended to a maximal chain below 𝔭. Similarly 𝔮⊊𝔭 implies that the inequality is strict. ∎
Lemma 3.27.8

Suppose 𝔞⊆𝔟. Then

ht(𝔞)≤ht(𝔟)

If these are equal and 𝔟 is prime, then it must be a minimal prime of 𝔞.

Proof. We consider first the case 𝔟=𝔭 is prime. By (3.3.43) there is a minimal prime 𝔭′ such that 𝔞⊆𝔭′⊆𝔭. By definition ht(𝔞)≤ht(𝔭′). Furthermore by the previous Lemma ht(𝔭′)≤ht(𝔭), which yields the required result.

Suppose ht(𝔭)=ht(𝔞) then by definition ht(𝔞)≤ht(𝔭′)≤ht(𝔭) and therefore we conclude ht(𝔭)=ht(𝔭′). By the previous lemma we conclude that 𝔭=𝔭′ is a minimal prime.

For the general case every minimal prime of 𝔟 also contains 𝔞 so the inequality follows by taking the minimum over all minimal primes. ∎

Lemma 3.27.9

Let 𝔞⊆𝔟 be ideals such that there exists 𝑥∈𝔟 not contained in any minimal prime of 𝔞. Then

ht(𝔞)<ht(𝔟)
Proof. By the previous Lemma we have ht(𝔞)≤ht(𝔟). Suppose they are equal then there is a minimal prime 𝔭 of 𝔟 of the same height, which by (3.27.8) is a minimal prime of 𝔞. This must contain 𝑥 which then contradicts the assumption. ∎
Proposition 3.27.10 (Prime Avoidance Theorem)
Let 𝔭1,…,𝔭𝑛 be ideals and 𝔞 an ideal such that 𝔞⊈𝔭𝑖 for all 𝑖=1…𝑛. Then there exists 𝑥∈𝔞∖(𝔭1∪…∪𝔭𝑛).

Proof. We proceed by induction on the number of prime ideals 𝑛, the case 𝑛=1 being trivial. Consider then the case of 𝑛+1 prime ideals and suppose on the contrary that 𝔞⊆𝔭1∪…∪𝔭𝑛+1. By the induction hypothesis there exists 𝑥𝑖∈𝔞∖⋃𝑗≠𝑖𝔭𝑗, and therefore by our assumption 𝑥𝑖∈(𝔞∩𝔭𝑖)∖⋃𝑗≠𝑖𝔭𝑗. Define

𝑦≔𝑥1…𝑥𝑛

Then 𝑦∉𝔭𝑛+1 by primality and clearly 𝑦∈𝔞∩𝔭1∩…∩𝔭𝑛. Define 𝑧≔𝑦+𝑥𝑛+1 then we see

  1. 𝑧∈𝔞
  2. 𝑧∉𝔭𝑛+1
  3. 𝑧∉𝔭𝑖 for 𝑖=1…𝑛

which contradicts our original assumption. ∎

Lemma 3.27.11

Let 𝐴 be a generalized hauptidealsatz ring and 𝔞=(𝑥1,…,𝑥𝑛). Then the following are equivalent

  1. ht(𝔞)=𝑛
  2. every minimal prime of 𝔞 has height 𝑛

Proof. a) ⟹ b) By definition this means every minimal prime has height at least 𝑛, and the reverse inequality follows from generalized hauptidealsatz assumption.

b) ⟹ a) Recall from (3.22.1) we may take the infimum over only minimal prime ideals. ∎

Proposition 3.27.12 (Alternative characterization of height)

Suppose 𝐴 is a Noetherian ring satisfying generalized hauptidealsatz, then the following properties hold

  1. Every prime ideal of height 𝑛 is minimal over some ideal 𝔞≔(𝑥1,…,𝑥𝑛). Furthermore 𝔞 may be chosen such that ht(𝔞)=𝑛 and every minimal prime is of height 𝑛

  2. We have the following characterization of height

    ht(𝔭)=min{𝑛|𝔭minimal over(𝑥1,…,𝑥𝑛)}

In particular every prime ideal has finite height.

Proof. We first demonstrate a). Suppose ht(𝔭)=𝑛 then there is a saturated chain of prime ideals

𝔭0⊊…⊊𝔭𝑛=𝔭

It’s clear from the above chain that ht(𝔭𝑖)≥𝑖. Furthermore by (3.27.7) ht(𝔭𝑖)<ht(𝔭𝑖+1) whence we see ht(𝔭𝑖)=𝑖.

We show by induction that there are elements 𝑥1,…,𝑥𝑛∈𝐴 for which 𝔭𝑖 is a minimal prime over 𝔞𝑖≔(𝑥1,…,𝑥𝑖) for all 𝑖=0…𝑛 and ht(𝔞𝑖)=𝑖.

The case 𝑖=0 is clear, so suppose 𝑖>0. In general by (3.22.6) there are finitely many minimal primes over 𝔞𝑖−1 say 𝔮1,…,𝔮𝑟 which all have height 𝑖−1 by (3.27.11). In particular 𝔭𝑖⊈𝔮𝑘.

By prime avoidance (3.27.10) we may choose 𝑥𝑖∈𝔭𝑖∖(𝔮1∪…∪𝔮𝑟). Clearly

𝔞𝑖−1⊆𝔞𝑖⊆𝔭𝑖

Then by (3.27.8) and (3.27.9) we have 𝑖−1=ht(𝔞𝑖−1)<ht(𝔞𝑖)≤𝑖 whence ht(𝔞𝑖)=𝑖. By (3.27.8) again we see 𝔭𝑖 is a minimal prime of 𝔞𝑖. This completes the inductive step.

To show b) let 𝑚 denote the right hand side. Then by the generalized hauptidealsatz hypothesis ht(𝔭)≤𝑚. On the other hand by part a) we have 𝑚≤ht(𝔭) whence they are equal.

Finally by the Noetherian hypothesis 𝔭 is finitely generated and clearly minimal over itself. ∎
