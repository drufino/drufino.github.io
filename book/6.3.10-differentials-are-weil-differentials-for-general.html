6.3.10 Differentials are Weil Differentials, for General 𝑘

Fix a transcendental 𝑡∈𝐾. By (6.3.20), every 𝐷∈Der𝑘(𝐾,𝐾) is 𝐷(𝑡)⋅𝜕𝑡, so a differential 𝑧d𝑡 (𝑧∈𝐾) already carries all the information of an element of the (not yet formally constructed) module Ω𝐾/𝑘, and Res𝑃(𝑧d𝑡) is available at every place (6.3.53). We show this recovers Ω𝐾 exactly, generalising (6.3.19) beyond 𝑘=ℂ: the complex-analytic ingredients there (contour integration, Stokes’ theorem, Mittag-Leffler) are replaced by the algebraic Residue Theorem, the trace formalism of (6.3.53), and the following fact, taken as given in this subsection.

Proposition 6.3.58 (Ω𝐾 is a 𝐾-Vector Space)
Let 𝐾/𝑘 be an algebraic function field. For 𝑥∈𝐾 and 𝜔∈Ω𝐾(𝐷) (𝐷∈Div(𝐾)), define 𝑥𝜔∈Hom𝑘(𝒜︀𝐾,𝑘) by (𝑥𝜔)(𝛼)≔𝜔(𝑥𝛼) (using that 𝒜︀𝐾 is a 𝐾-algebra). Then 𝑥𝜔∈Ω𝐾(𝐷−(𝑥))⊂Ω𝐾, and this action makes Ω𝐾 a 𝐾-vector space.

Proof. Drafted by Claude Sonnet 5, reviewed by David Rufino.

𝑥𝜔∈Ω𝐾(𝐷−(𝑥)). For 𝛼∈𝒜︀𝐾(𝐷−(𝑥)), 𝑣𝑃(𝛼𝑃)≥−𝑣𝑃(𝐷−(𝑥))=𝑣𝑃(𝑥)−𝑣𝑃(𝐷) for every 𝑃, so 𝑣𝑃(𝑥𝛼𝑃)≥−𝑣𝑃(𝐷), that is 𝑥𝛼∈𝒜︀𝐾(𝐷); and 𝑥𝐾=𝐾. As 𝜔∈Ω𝐾(𝐷) vanishes on 𝒜︀𝐾(𝐷)+𝐾, it follows that (𝑥𝜔)(𝛼)=𝜔(𝑥𝛼)=0 for every 𝛼∈𝒜︀𝐾(𝐷−(𝑥))+𝐾, that is 𝑥𝜔∈Ω𝐾(𝐷−(𝑥)).

Module axioms. For 𝑥,𝑦∈𝐾, 𝜆∈𝑘, 𝛼,𝛽∈𝒜︀𝐾: (𝑥𝜔)(𝛼+𝛽)=𝜔(𝑥𝛼+𝑥𝛽)=(𝑥𝜔)(𝛼)+(𝑥𝜔)(𝛽) and (𝑥𝜔)(𝜆𝛼)=𝜔(𝜆𝑥𝛼)=𝜆(𝑥𝜔)(𝛼), so 𝑥𝜔∈Hom𝑘(𝒜︀𝐾,𝑘) is again 𝑘-linear; (𝑥(𝑦𝜔))(𝛼)=(𝑦𝜔)(𝑥𝛼)=𝜔(𝑦𝑥𝛼)=((𝑥𝑦)𝜔)(𝛼) and 1𝜔=𝜔; ((𝑥+𝑦)𝜔)(𝛼)=𝜔((𝑥+𝑦)𝛼)=𝜔(𝑥𝛼)+𝜔(𝑦𝛼)=(𝑥𝜔)(𝛼)+(𝑦𝜔)(𝛼); and (𝜔↦𝑥𝜔) is 𝑘-linear by the same computation applied to 𝜔1+𝜔2. These are exactly the axioms making Ω𝐾 a 𝐾-module, that is a 𝐾-vector space (being already a 𝑘-vector space). ∎

Proposition 6.3.59 (Ω𝐾 is 1-Dimensional over 𝐾)
Let 𝐾/𝑘 be an algebraic function field. Then dim𝐾Ω𝐾=1 (for the 𝐾-vector space structure of (6.3.58)).
Proposition 6.3.60 (Differentials are Weil Differentials)

Let 𝐾/𝑘 be an algebraic function field and 𝑡∈𝐾 transcendental. For 𝑧∈𝐾, define 𝜑𝑧d𝑡∈Hom𝑘(𝒜︀𝐾,𝑘) by

𝜑𝑧d𝑡(𝛼)≔∑𝑃Res𝑃(𝛼𝑃𝑧d𝑡),𝛼=(𝛼𝑃)𝑃∈𝒜︀𝐾

(a finite sum: 𝛼𝑃∈𝒪︀𝑃 for all but finitely many 𝑃, and 𝑧d𝑡 is regular at all but finitely many 𝑃, so Res𝑃(𝛼𝑃𝑧d𝑡)=0 for almost all 𝑃 (6.3.55)). Then 𝜑𝑧d𝑡∈Ω𝐾, and

𝐾⟶Ω𝐾,𝑧⟼𝜑𝑧d𝑡

is a canonical 𝐾-linear isomorphism (for the 𝐾-vector space structures of (6.3.58) and 𝐾 itself).

Proof. Drafted by Claude Sonnet 5, reviewed by David Rufino.

𝜑𝑧d𝑡∈Ω𝐾. Let 𝐷 be the divisor with 𝑣𝑃(𝐷)≔𝑣𝑃(𝑧𝜕𝜋(𝑡)) for a uniformiser 𝜋 at 𝑃 (independent of the choice of 𝜋: for another uniformiser 𝜋′, 𝜕𝜋′(𝑡)=𝜕𝜋′(𝜋)𝜕𝜋(𝑡) (6.3.22), with 𝜕𝜋′(𝜋) a unit at 𝑃, so 𝑣𝑃(𝑧𝜕𝜋′(𝑡))=𝑣𝑃(𝑧𝜕𝜋(𝑡)); and this vanishes for all but finitely many 𝑃, as 𝑧 has finitely many poles and 𝑡 finitely many poles). For 𝛼∈𝒜︀𝐾(𝐷), 𝑣𝑃(𝛼𝑃)≥−𝑣𝑃(𝐷)=−𝑣𝑃(𝑧𝜕𝜋(𝑡)) for every 𝑃, so 𝑣𝑃(𝛼𝑃𝑧𝜕𝜋(𝑡))≥0, that is 𝛼𝑃𝑧𝜕𝜋(𝑡)∈𝒪︀𝑃, giving Res𝑃(𝛼𝑃𝑧d𝑡)=0 (6.3.55) so 𝜑𝑧d𝑡(𝛼)=0. For 𝑓∈𝐾 embedded diagonally, 𝜑𝑧d𝑡(𝑓,𝑓,…)=∑𝑃Res𝑃(𝑓𝑧d𝑡)=0 by (6.3.57) (the Residue Theorem), applied to 𝑓𝑧∈𝐾. So 𝜑𝑧d𝑡 vanishes on 𝒜︀𝐾(𝐷)+𝐾, that is 𝜑𝑧d𝑡∈Ω𝐾(𝐷)⊂Ω𝐾.

𝐾-linearity. 𝑘-linearity in 𝑧 is immediate from (6.3.54).a), applied place by place and summed. For 𝑥∈𝐾, using 𝑥𝛼𝑃⋅𝑧=𝛼𝑃⋅(𝑥𝑧) in the field 𝐾,

(𝑥𝜑𝑧d𝑡)(𝛼)=𝜑𝑧d𝑡(𝑥𝛼)=∑𝑃Res𝑃((𝑥𝛼𝑃)𝑧d𝑡)=∑𝑃Res𝑃(𝛼𝑃(𝑥𝑧)d𝑡)=𝜑(𝑥𝑧)d𝑡(𝛼),

so 𝑥𝜑𝑧d𝑡=𝜑(𝑥𝑧)d𝑡: the map is 𝐾-linear (6.3.58).

Injectivity. Suppose 𝜑𝑧d𝑡=0 and 𝑧≠0; we derive a contradiction. Fix a place 𝑃 and a uniformiser 𝜋 at 𝑃, and let 𝑑,𝑃1′,…,𝑃𝑑′,𝜄1,…,𝜄𝑑 be as in (6.3.49). As 𝜕𝜋 is a nonzero derivation and 𝑡 is transcendental, 𝜕𝜋(𝑡)≠0 (6.3.20), so 𝑤≔𝑧𝜕𝜋(𝑡)≠0 in 𝐾. Since 𝑤∈𝐾 is fixed pointwise by Aut(𝑘―/𝑘) (embedded diagonally in 𝐾𝑘―) while 𝜎∗ permutes {𝑃1′,…,𝑃𝑑′} preserving valuations (6.3.47), 𝑚≔𝑣𝑃𝑗′(𝑤⊗1) is independent of 𝑗. Let 𝑐≔𝜋−𝑚−1∈𝐾⋆; then 𝑣𝑃𝑗′(𝑐𝑤⊗1)=−1 for every 𝑗, so

𝑢𝑗≔Res𝑃𝑗′((𝑐𝑧⊗1)d(𝑡⊗1))

is the (nonzero) leading Laurent coefficient of 𝑐𝑤⊗1 at 𝑃𝑗′. By (6.3.52) (applied to 𝑡,𝑐𝑧 in place of 𝑡,𝑧), 𝑢𝑗=𝜄𝑗(𝛾) for a single 𝛾∈𝑘(𝔪𝑃); as 𝑢1≠0 and 𝜄1 is injective, 𝛾≠0. As 𝑘 is perfect, 𝑘(𝔪𝑃)/𝑘 is separable (3.19.103), so by (3.19.131) there is 𝑒―∈𝑘(𝔪𝑃) with Tr𝑘(𝔪𝑃)/𝑘(𝑒―𝛾)≠0; lift it to a unit 𝑒∈𝒪︀𝑃⋆ (any lift of a nonzero residue class is a unit, 𝒪︀𝑃 being local). As 𝑒⊗1 is a unit of 𝒪︀𝑃𝑗′ with residue class 𝜄𝑗(𝑒―), and 𝑐𝑤⊗1 has valuation exactly −1 with leading coefficient 𝑢𝑗=𝜄𝑗(𝛾), the product (𝑒⊗1)(𝑐𝑤⊗1) again has valuation exactly −1 (no lower-order term of 𝑒⊗1 to interact with, as 𝑐𝑤⊗1 has none below 𝑋−1), with leading coefficient 𝜄𝑗(𝑒―)𝜄𝑗(𝛾)=𝜄𝑗(𝑒―𝛾): that is Res𝑃𝑗′((𝑒𝑐𝑧⊗1)d(𝑡⊗1))=𝜄𝑗(𝑒―𝛾) for every 𝑗, so

Res𝑃(𝑒𝑐𝑧d𝑡)=∑𝑗=1𝑑𝜄𝑗(𝑒―𝛾)=Tr𝑘(𝔪𝑃)/𝑘(𝑒―𝛾)≠0.

Testing 𝜑𝑧d𝑡 against the repartition supported at 𝑃 with value 𝑒𝑐∈𝐾 then gives 𝜑𝑧d𝑡(…)=Res𝑃(𝑒𝑐𝑧d𝑡)≠0, contradicting 𝜑𝑧d𝑡=0.

Surjectivity. The map 𝐾→Ω𝐾 is 𝐾-linear and injective, and 𝐾 is a 1-dimensional 𝐾-vector space over itself; so its image is a 1-dimensional 𝐾-subspace of Ω𝐾, which by (6.3.59) is itself 1-dimensional over 𝐾. So the image is all of Ω𝐾. ∎
