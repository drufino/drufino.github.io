4.2.2 Sheafification

Definition 4.2.13 (Sheafification)

Let 𝑋 be a topological space, ℬ︀ a basis of open sets for 𝑋 closed under finite intersections and ℱ︀ a ℬ︀-presheaf (with values in a category 𝒞︀). Then we say that a pair (𝜂ℱ︀,ℱ︀+) is a sheafification of ℱ︀ if

  1. ℱ︀+ is a sheaf (with values in a category 𝒞︀)
  2. 𝜂ℱ︀:ℱ︀→ℱ︀+|ℬ︀ is a morphism of ℬ︀-presheaves

  3. for every sheaf 𝒢︀ there is a bijection

    (−)♯:Mor(ℱ︀+,𝒢︀)⟶∼Mor(ℱ︀,𝒢︀|ℬ︀)𝜙→𝜙|ℬ︀∘𝜂ℱ︀

    which is natural in 𝒢︀, with the inverse map denoted by (−)♭.

In other words (ℱ︀+,(−)♯) represents the functor 𝒢︀→Mor(ℱ︀,𝒢︀|ℬ︀).

In this case we denote by 𝜀𝒢︀:(𝒢︀|ℬ︀)+→𝒢︀ the unique morphism which maps to 1𝒢︀|ℬ︀ under the bijection.

Proposition 4.2.14 (Sheafification is Functorial)

Using the notation of (4.2.13) suppose there exists a sheafification (𝜂ℱ︀,ℱ︀+) for all pre-sheaves ℱ︀. Then this can be extended to a functor

(−)+:𝐏𝐒𝐡(𝑋;ℬ︀)→𝐒𝐡(𝑋)

uniquely determined by either the condition (−)+ is left-adjoint to (−)|ℬ︀ or that the following diagram commutes

for all morphisms 𝜙:ℱ︀1→ℱ︀2. More precisely we have the relationship

𝜙+=(𝜂ℱ︀2∘𝜙)♭

In particular (𝜓∘𝜙)+=𝜓+∘𝜙+ for all morphisms 𝜓:ℱ︀2→ℱ︀3 and 𝜂ℱ︀ is the unit of the adjunction and 𝜀𝒢︀ the counit.

Furthermore (−)|ℬ︀:𝐒𝐡(𝑋)→𝐏𝐒𝐡(𝑋;ℬ︀) is fully faithful, and 𝜀 is a natural isomorphism.

Proof. See (2.6.74).

By (4.2.12), 𝐒𝐡(𝑋)→𝐒𝐡(ℬ︀;𝒞︀) is fully faithful; composing with the (trivially fully faithful) inclusion 𝐒𝐡(ℬ︀;𝒞︀)↪︎𝐏𝐒𝐡(𝑋;ℬ︀) shows (−)|ℬ︀:𝐒𝐡(𝑋)→𝐏𝐒𝐡(𝑋;ℬ︀) is fully faithful, so by (2.6.72), 𝜀 is a natural isomorphism. ∎

Proposition 4.2.16 (Sheafification)

Let 𝒞︀ be an algebraic category (2.6.184) then the sheafification (4.2.13) exists.

More precisely define

ℱ︀+(𝑈)≔{𝑠:𝑈→∐𝑥∈𝑈ℱ︀𝑥|𝑈=⋃𝑖∈𝐼𝑈𝑖,𝑈𝑖∈ℬ︀,𝜎𝑖∈ℱ︀(𝑈𝑖),𝑠(𝑥)=(𝜎𝑖)𝑥∀𝑥∈𝑈𝑖}

Then this is evidently a sheaf and the adjunction 𝜙→𝜙♯ is the unique presheaf morphism such that

𝜙𝑈♯(𝜎)𝑥=𝜙((𝜎𝑥)𝑥∈𝑈)𝑥∀𝑈∈ℬ︀,𝜎∈ℱ︀(𝑈),𝑥∈𝑈

and the inverse adjunction 𝜓♭←𝜓 is the unique sheaf morphism such that

𝜓♭(𝑠)(𝑥)=𝜓𝑥(𝑠(𝑥))∀𝑥∈𝑈

(using (4.2.9) to identify (𝒢︀|ℬ︀)𝑥 and 𝒢︀𝑥). The unit of the adjunction is given by

(𝜂ℱ︀)𝑈(𝜎)=(𝜎𝑥)𝑥∈𝑈

and for a morphism of ℬ︀-sheaves 𝜙:ℱ︀→𝒢︀ the morphism 𝜙+ defined in (4.2.14) is simply

𝜙+(𝑠)(𝑥)=𝜙𝑥(𝑠(𝑥)).

Furthermore 𝜂ℱ︀ is an isomorphism if and only if ℱ︀ is a ℬ︀-sheaf.

Proof. We first demonstrate this when 𝒞︀=𝐒𝐞𝐭. The sheaf conditions are easy to verify. There is an obvious unit morphism

𝜂ℱ︀(𝑈):ℱ︀(𝑈)→ℱ︀+(𝑈)𝜎→𝑥→𝜎𝑥

which is a natural transformation by (4.2.7). According to (2.6.74) it is enough to ensure the following map is a bijection and natural in 𝒢︀

Mor(ℱ︀+,𝒢︀)→Mor(ℱ︀,𝒢︀|ℬ︀)𝜙→𝜙|ℬ︀∘𝜂ℱ︀

Naturality in 𝒢︀ follows formally. Suppose 𝜙,𝜓:ℱ︀+→𝒢︀ are morphisms such that 𝜙|ℬ︀∘𝜂ℱ︀=𝜓|ℬ︀∘𝜂ℱ︀. For a given 𝑠∈ℱ︀+(𝑈) we wish to show that 𝜙(𝑠)=𝜓(𝑠). Suppose that 𝑠 is determined by sections 𝜎𝑖∈ℱ︀(𝑈𝑖), that is to say 𝑠|𝑈𝑖=𝜂ℱ︀(𝜎𝑖). Therefore by hypothesis 𝜙(𝑠)|𝑈𝑖=𝜙(𝑠|𝑈𝑖)=𝜓(𝑠|𝑈𝑖)=𝜓(𝑠)|𝑈𝑖. By the concrete sheaf conditions (4.2.4) on 𝒢︀ we conclude that 𝜙(𝑠)=𝜓(𝑠), and so the given map is injective.

Conversely given 𝜙:ℱ︀→𝒢︀|ℬ︀, we wish to construct a morphism of sheaves 𝜙♭:ℱ︀+(𝑈)→𝒢︀(𝑈) such that 𝜙♭((𝜎𝑥)𝑥∈𝑈)=𝜙(𝜎) for all 𝜎∈ℱ︀(𝑈) and 𝑈∈ℬ︀. We claim that there exists a family of maps {𝜙𝑈♭:ℱ︀+→𝒢︀} such that 𝜙𝑈♭(𝑠)𝑥=𝜙𝑥(𝑠(𝑥)) for all 𝑠∈ℱ︀+(𝑈). Such a family is unique by (4.2.8).

To show existence consider a section 𝑠∈ℱ︀+(𝑈), then by definition 𝑠(𝑥)=((𝜎𝑖)𝑥) for some 𝜎𝑖∈ℱ︀(𝑈𝑖), 𝑈𝑖∈ℬ︀ and all 𝑥∈𝑈𝑖. Define 𝜏𝑖≔𝜙(𝜎𝑖) then by (4.2.7) (𝜏𝑖)𝑥=𝜙𝑥((𝜎𝑖)𝑥)=𝜙𝑥(𝑠(𝑥)). This shows that (𝜏𝑖)𝑥=(𝜏𝑗)𝑥 for all 𝑥∈𝑈𝑖∩𝑈𝑗 so by (4.2.8) 𝜏𝑖|𝑈𝑖∩𝑈𝑗=𝜏𝑗|𝑈𝑖∩𝑈𝑗. By (4.2.4) there exists 𝜏∈𝒢︀(𝑈) such that 𝜏|𝑈𝑖=𝜏𝑖. This yields a map 𝜙𝑈♭ satisfying 𝜙𝑈♭(𝑠)𝑥=𝜏𝑥=(𝜏𝑖)𝑥=𝜙(𝜎𝑖)𝑥=𝜙𝑥((𝜎𝑖)𝑥)=𝜙𝑥(𝑠(𝑥)) as required.

It remains to show that 𝜙♭ is a morphism of sheaves, that is the following diagram is commutative for all open sets 𝑉⊂𝑈

The large and bottom squares commute by construction. Therefore for 𝑠∈ℱ︀+(𝑈) we have (𝜙𝑈♭(𝑠)|𝑉)𝑥=𝜙𝑥(𝑠|𝑉(𝑥))=𝜙𝑉♭(𝑠|𝑉)𝑥. As this holds for all 𝑥∈𝑉 we find from (4.2.8) that 𝜙𝑈♭(𝑠)|𝑉=𝜙𝑉♭(𝑠|𝑉) as required.

As (−)♯ is injective it is sufficient by (2.6.36) to show that it has a right inverse, specifically (𝜓♭)♯=𝜓. However 𝜓(𝜎)𝑥=𝜓♭((𝜎𝑥′)𝑥′∈𝑈)(𝑥)=(𝜓♭)♯(𝜎)𝑥 by construction so we are done by (4.2.8).

Evidently when 𝜂ℱ︀ is an isomorphism then ℱ︀ is a ℬ︀-sheaf. We claim that the converse holds, namely suppose ℱ︀ is a ℬ︀-sheaf, then we require to show (𝜂ℱ︀)𝑈 is injective and surjective. It is injective by (4.2.8). Similarly it is surjective for suppose 𝑠∈ℱ︀+(𝑈) for 𝑈∈ℬ︀ and 𝑠(𝑥)=(𝜎𝑖)𝑥 for all 𝑥∈𝑈𝑖 and 𝜎𝑖∈ℱ︀(𝑈𝑖). Then by the same result we have (𝜎𝑖)|𝑈𝑖∩𝑈𝑗=(𝜎𝑗)|𝑈𝑖∩𝑈𝑗, whence by (4.2.4) these lift to a section 𝜎∈ℱ︀(𝑈) such that 𝜎|𝑈𝑖=𝜎𝑖, and in particular 𝜎𝑥=(𝜎𝑖)𝑥=𝑠(𝑥) for all 𝑥∈𝑈𝑖. Therefore we conclude the map (𝜂ℱ︀)𝑈 is surjective and therefore an isomorphism in 𝐒𝐞𝐭, which means 𝜂ℱ︀ is an isomorphism of sheaves.

We now turn to the case of a general algebraic category 𝒞︀, determined by (Ω,𝐸) (2.6.184) write 𝑈:𝒞︀→𝐒𝐞𝐭 for the forgetful functor.

The remainder of this proof is drafted by Claude Sonnet 5, reviewed by David Rufino.

By (2.6.190), for 𝑈∈ℬ︀ the product ∏𝑥∈𝑈ℱ︀𝑥 exists in 𝒞︀, with each 𝜔∈Ω acting coordinatewise.

ℱ︀+(𝑈) is a subalgebra of ∏𝑥∈𝑈ℱ︀𝑥. Given 𝜔∈Ω and 𝑠(1),…,𝑠(𝑛𝜔)∈ℱ︀+(𝑈), and 𝑥∈𝑈: choose for each 𝑟=1,…,𝑛𝜔 a representative 𝜎(𝑟)∈ℱ︀(𝑉𝑟), 𝑉𝑟∈ℬ︀, 𝑥∈𝑉𝑟, with 𝑠(𝑟)(𝑦)=(𝜎(𝑟))𝑦 for all 𝑦∈𝑉𝑟. As 𝑛𝜔 is finite and ℬ︀ is closed under finite intersections, 𝑁≔⋂𝑟𝑉𝑟∈ℬ︀, and, by functoriality of stalks (4.2.7), 𝜏≔𝜔ℱ︀(𝑁)(𝜎(1)|𝑁,…,𝜎(𝑛𝜔)|𝑁)∈ℱ︀(𝑁) has 𝜏𝑦=𝜔ℱ︀𝑦(𝑠(1)(𝑦),…,𝑠(𝑛𝜔)(𝑦)) for every 𝑦∈𝑁. So the germ-function 𝑦↦𝜔ℱ︀𝑦(𝑠(1)(𝑦),…,𝑠(𝑛𝜔)(𝑦)) — which is exactly 𝜔 applied coordinatewise to 𝑠(1),…,𝑠(𝑛𝜔) in ∏𝑥∈𝑈ℱ︀𝑥 — is locally represented by 𝜏 near every point, hence lies in ℱ︀+(𝑈). So ℱ︀+(𝑈) is closed under every 𝜔∈Ω, and by (2.6.193) is a subalgebra, in particular an object of 𝒞︀, with 𝜔ℱ︀+(𝑈) given pointwise as above.

The maps constructed above are morphisms of 𝒞︀. If ℎ:𝐴→𝐵 is a homomorphism and 𝑆⊆𝑈(𝐴), 𝑇⊆𝑈(𝐵) are subalgebras with ℎ(𝑆)⊆𝑇, then ℎ|𝑆:𝑆→𝑇 is a homomorphism, as 𝜔𝑆,𝜔𝑇 are literally restrictions of 𝜔𝐴,𝜔𝐵. We apply this three times.

For the restriction maps: the coordinate projection ∏𝑥∈𝑈ℱ︀𝑥→∏𝑥∈𝑉ℱ︀𝑥 (𝑉⊆𝑈 in ℬ︀) is a homomorphism, carries ℱ︀+(𝑈) into ℱ︀+(𝑉) by definition of restriction of germ-functions, and restricts to 𝜌𝑈𝑉.

For the unit: each stalk map 𝜌𝑈𝑥:ℱ︀(𝑈)→ℱ︀𝑥 is a morphism of 𝒞︀ (4.2.5), so (𝜌𝑈𝑥)𝑥∈𝑈:ℱ︀(𝑈)→∏𝑥∈𝑈ℱ︀𝑥 is a homomorphism (a map into a product is a homomorphism iff each component is); its image lies in ℱ︀+(𝑈) (represented by 𝜎 itself, via the cover {𝑈}), and it restricts to (𝜂ℱ︀)𝑈.

For 𝜙♭: given a morphism 𝜙:ℱ︀→𝒢︀|ℬ︀ of 𝒞︀-presheaves with 𝒢︀ a ℬ︀-sheaf, each 𝜙𝑥:ℱ︀𝑥→𝒢︀𝑥 is a morphism of 𝒞︀ (4.2.7), so ℎ≔∏𝑥∈𝑈𝜙𝑥:∏𝑥∈𝑈ℱ︀𝑥→∏𝑥∈𝑈𝒢︀𝑥 is a homomorphism. As above, (𝜌𝑈𝑥)𝑥∈𝑈:𝒢︀(𝑈)→∏𝑥∈𝑈𝒢︀𝑥 is an injective (by (4.2.8), as 𝒢︀ is a sheaf) homomorphism, so identifies 𝒢︀(𝑈) with a subalgebra 𝑇 of ∏𝑥∈𝑈𝒢︀𝑥 — its image, which is a subalgebra since the image of a homomorphism is always closed under the operations of the target. By the construction of 𝜙𝑈♭ above, ℎ(ℱ︀+(𝑈))⊆𝑇: indeed for 𝑠∈ℱ︀+(𝑈) we exhibited 𝜏∈𝒢︀(𝑈) with 𝜏𝑥=𝜙𝑥(𝑠(𝑥)) for all 𝑥∈𝑈. So ℎ restricts to a homomorphism ℱ︀+(𝑈)→𝑇≅𝒢︀(𝑈), which is 𝜙𝑈♭.

Given this, every map constructed in the case 𝒞︀=𝐒𝐞𝐭 above is now a morphism of 𝒞︀, and the remaining verifications — injectivity of 𝜙↦𝜙|ℬ︀∘𝜂ℱ︀, existence and commutativity of 𝜙♭, and the final isomorphism criterion — proceed entirely by manipulating elements of underlying sets via (4.2.8) and (4.2.4), both already stated for general algebraic categories, so the argument given there applies verbatim; in the final step, bijectivity of (𝜂ℱ︀)𝑈 on underlying sets upgrades to an isomorphism of 𝒞︀ since 𝑈 reflects isomorphisms (2.6.189). ∎

Corollary 4.2.17

Let 𝒞︀ be an algebraic category (2.6.184) then there is an equivalence of categories

𝐒𝐡(𝑋;ℬ︀)⇌(−)|ℬ︀(−)+𝐒𝐡(𝑋)
Proof. By the last statement of (4.2.16), and (2.6.73) the essential image of (−)|ℬ︀ is the full subcategory of ℬ︀-sheaves, and the unit and counit natural transformations induce the equivalence of categories. ∎
Lemma 4.2.18 (Sheafification Preserves Stalks)
Let 𝒞︀ be algebraic (2.6.184), ℱ︀ a presheaf on 𝑋 and 𝑥∈𝑋. Then 𝜂ℱ︀,𝑥:ℱ︀𝑥→(ℱ︀+)𝑥 (4.2.7) is an isomorphism.

Proof. Drafted by Claude Sonnet 5, reviewed by David Rufino.

Surjectivity. Let 𝜉∈(ℱ︀+)𝑥; by (4.2.6), 𝜉=𝑠𝑥 for some 𝑠∈ℱ︀+(𝑈), 𝑈∋𝑥. By (4.2.16), 𝑠(𝑦)=(𝜎𝑖)𝑦 for all 𝑦∈𝑈𝑖, for some cover 𝑈=⋃𝑖𝑈𝑖 and 𝜎𝑖∈ℱ︀(𝑈𝑖); choosing 𝑖 with 𝑥∈𝑈𝑖, this says 𝑠|𝑈𝑖=𝜂𝑈𝑖(𝜎𝑖), so

𝜉=𝑠𝑥=(𝑠|𝑈𝑖)𝑥=(𝜂𝑈𝑖(𝜎𝑖))𝑥=𝜂𝑥((𝜎𝑖)𝑥)

so 𝜉∈Im(𝜂𝑥).

Injectivity. Suppose 𝜂𝑥(𝑎)=𝜂𝑥(𝑏) for 𝑎,𝑏∈ℱ︀𝑥. By (4.2.6), 𝑎=𝜎𝑥, 𝑏=𝜏𝑥 for some 𝜎∈ℱ︀(𝑈), 𝜏∈ℱ︀(𝑉), 𝑈,𝑉∋𝑥; then 𝜂𝑥(𝑎)=(𝜂𝑈(𝜎))𝑥 and 𝜂𝑥(𝑏)=(𝜂𝑉(𝜏))𝑥, so by (4.2.8).a) there is 𝑊⊆𝑈∩𝑉, 𝑥∈𝑊, with 𝜂𝑈(𝜎)|𝑊=𝜂𝑉(𝜏)|𝑊 in ℱ︀+(𝑊), that is 𝜂𝑊(𝜎|𝑊)=𝜂𝑊(𝜏|𝑊). As elements of ℱ︀+(𝑊) are functions 𝑊→∐𝑦∈𝑊ℱ︀𝑦 (4.2.16), this equality holds in particular at 𝑦=𝑥:

(𝜎|𝑊)𝑥=𝜂𝑊(𝜎|𝑊)(𝑥)=𝜂𝑊(𝜏|𝑊)(𝑥)=(𝜏|𝑊)𝑥

that is 𝜎𝑥=𝜏𝑥, so 𝑎=𝑏. ∎
