3.19.17 Transcendental Field Extensions

Definition 3.19.132 (Algebraic Independence)

Let 𝐾/𝑘 be a field extension and 𝑆⊂𝐾. We say 𝑆 is algebraically independent over 𝑘 if for every finite subset of distinct elements 𝑥1,…,𝑥𝑛∈𝑆 we have

𝑓(𝑥1,…,𝑥𝑛)=0⟹𝑓=0

for all 𝑓∈𝑘[𝑋1,…,𝑋𝑛].

For a subset 𝑆⊂𝐾 define the closure operator

𝑐(𝑆)≔𝑘(𝑆)―∩𝐾≔{𝑥∈𝐾|𝑥algebraic over𝑘(𝑆)}

We say Γ is algebraically spanning if 𝑐(Γ)=𝐾, equivalently if 𝐾/𝑘(Γ) is algebraic.

We say that ℬ︀ is a transcendence base if it is both algebraically independent and spanning (i.e. 𝐾/𝑘(ℬ︀) is algebraic).

Essentially we show that (𝐾,𝑐) satisfies the properties of a matroid, in analogy with vector spaces, so that we can use the results of the Matroids section to show that transcendence bases exist and they satisfy certain properties.

Proposition 3.19.133

Let 𝐾/𝑘 be a field extension and 𝑆,𝑇 subsets of 𝐾. Then the following are equivalent

  1. 𝑆∪𝑇 is algebraically independent and 𝑆∩𝑇=∅
  2. 𝑆 is algebraically independent over 𝑘 and 𝑇 is algebraically independent over 𝑘(𝑆)
  3. 𝑇 is algebraically independent over 𝑘 and 𝑆 is algebraically independent over 𝑘(𝑇)

Proof. By symmetry it’s enough to show that 𝑎)⟺𝑏).

Suppose 𝑎) holds, then a-fortiori 𝑆 is algebraically independent over 𝑘. Suppose 𝑇 is algebraically dependent over 𝑘(𝑆), then there exists 𝑡1,…,𝑡𝑛∈𝑇 such that 𝐹(𝑡1,…,𝑡𝑛)=0 for some 0≠𝐹∈𝑘(𝑆)[𝑋1,…,𝑋𝑛]. By clearing denominators we may assume that 𝐹∈𝑘[𝑆][𝑋1,…,𝑋𝑛]. As there are only finitely many coefficients we may also take 𝑆={𝑠1,…,𝑠𝑚} to be finite. Explicitly

𝐹(𝑋1,…,𝑋𝑛)=∑𝛼∈ℕ𝑛𝜆𝛼𝑋1𝛼1…𝑋𝑛𝛼𝑛

and

𝜆𝛼=𝐺𝛼(𝑠1,…,𝑠𝑚)

for some 𝐺𝛼∈𝑘[𝑌1,…,𝑌𝑚]. We may then define 𝐹̂∈𝑘[𝑋1,…,𝑋𝑛,𝑌1,…,𝑌𝑚] by

𝐹̂(𝑋1,…,𝑋𝑛,𝑌1,…,𝑌𝑚)≔∑𝛼∈ℕ𝑛𝐺𝛼(𝑌1,…,𝑌𝑚)𝑋1𝛼1…𝑋𝑛𝛼𝑛

Then 𝐹̂(𝑡1,…,𝑡𝑛,𝑠1,…,𝑠𝑚)=0, and by assumption this is an algebraic dependence (as 𝑆∩𝑇=∅). Therefore by assumption 𝐹̂=0 and in particular 𝐺𝛼(𝑠1,…,𝑠𝑚)=0 for all 𝛼. This implies 𝐹=0, a contradiction.

𝑏)⟹𝑎) Evidently 𝑆∩𝑇=∅ otherwise there is a trivial algebraic dependence of 𝑇 on 𝑘(𝑆). Suppose there is an algebraic dependence of 𝑆∪𝑇 on 𝑘. We may assume wlog that 𝑆={𝑠1,…,𝑠𝑚} and 𝑇={𝑡1,…,𝑡𝑛} are finite. Then the evaluation homomorphism

𝑘[𝑋1,…,𝑋𝑛,𝑌1,…,𝑌𝑚]→𝐾

may be written as a composite

𝑘[𝑋1,…,𝑋𝑛,𝑌1,…,𝑌𝑚]→𝑘(𝑆)[𝑋1,…,𝑋𝑛]→𝐾

which are both injective by hypothesis. ∎

Corollary 3.19.134 (Exchange Property)
Let 𝐾/𝑘 be a field extension, Γ⊆𝐾. Suppose 𝑥 is algebraic over 𝑘(Γ∪{𝑦}) and transcendental over 𝑘(Γ), then 𝑦 is algebraic over 𝑘(Γ∪{𝑥}).

Proof. By considering the extension 𝐾/𝑘(Γ) we may reduce to the case Γ=∅.

It’s enough to show that 𝑥 transcendental over 𝑘 and 𝑦 transcendental over 𝑘(𝑥) implies 𝑥 transcendental over 𝑘(𝑦). This follows directly from (3.19.133) by considering 𝑆={𝑥} and 𝑇={𝑦}. ∎

Corollary 3.19.135 (Extension Property)
Let 𝐾/𝑘 be a field extension, 𝑆⊆𝐾 algebraically independent and 𝑥∈𝐾 transcendental over 𝑘(𝑆). Then 𝑆∪{𝑥} is algebraically independent.
Proof. Follows immediately from (3.19.133). ∎
Lemma 3.19.136 (Embeddings Preserve Transcendence Degree)
Let 𝐾/𝑘, 𝐿/𝑘 be field extensions of finite transcendence degree and 𝜙:𝐾→𝐿 a 𝑘-algebra homomorphism. Then trdeg(𝐾/𝑘)≤trdeg(𝐿/𝑘).
Proof. As 𝜙 is a ring homomorphism from a field, ker(𝜙) is a proper ideal of 𝐾, hence 0, so 𝜙 is injective. Let 𝑆⊂𝐾 be a transcendence base of 𝐾/𝑘, so |𝑆|=trdeg(𝐾/𝑘) (3.19.141); as 𝜙 is injective, |𝜙(𝑆)|=|𝑆|. Given distinct 𝑠1,…,𝑠𝑛∈𝑆 and 𝑓∈𝑘[𝑋1,…,𝑋𝑛] with 𝑓(𝜙(𝑠1),…,𝜙(𝑠𝑛))=0: as 𝜙 fixes 𝑘, 𝜙(𝑓(𝑠1,…,𝑠𝑛))=𝑓(𝜙(𝑠1),…,𝜙(𝑠𝑛))=0, so 𝑓(𝑠1,…,𝑠𝑛)=0 by injectivity of 𝜙, whence 𝑓=0 by algebraic independence of 𝑆 over 𝑘; so 𝜙(𝑆) is algebraically independent over 𝑘. By (3.19.140) applied to 𝐿/𝑘, trdeg(𝐾/𝑘)=|𝑆|=|𝜙(𝑆)|≤trdeg(𝐿/𝑘). ∎
Corollary 3.19.137 (Equivalent form of independence)

Let 𝐾/𝑘 be a field extension and 𝑆⊂𝐾. Then the following are equivalent

  1. 𝑆 is algebraically independent
  2. 𝑥 is transcendental over 𝑘(𝑆∖{𝑥}) for all 𝑥∈𝑆

In other words the algebraically independent subsets are precisely the matroid independent subsets.

Proof. 𝑎)⟹𝑏) Follows from (3.19.133).

𝑏)⟹𝑎) Let ℱ︀ be the family of algebraically independent subsets. We’ve shown that all such sets are also matroid independent, and by definition the family is of finite character. Furthermore by (3.19.135) it satisfies the extension property. Therefore by (2.3.6) it is precisely the family of matroid independent sets. ∎

Proposition 3.19.138 (Transcendence Base Exists)
Let 𝐾/𝑘 be a field extension. Suppose 𝑆 is an algebraic independent subset of 𝐾 and Γ⊃𝑆 is such that 𝐾/𝑘(Γ) is algebraic. Then there exists a transcendence base ℬ︀ such that 𝑆⊆ℬ︀⊆Γ.
Proof. This is (2.3.7). ∎
Proposition 3.19.139 (Transcendence Base)

Let 𝐾/𝑘 be a field extension and 𝑆⊂𝐾 a subset. Then the following are equivalent

  1. 𝑆 is a transcendence base
  2. 𝑆 is a maximal algebraically independent set
  3. 𝑆 is minimal under the condition 𝐾/𝑘(𝑆) is algebraic

When 𝐾/𝑘 admits a finite algebraic spanning set then all bases are finite of the same size (transcendence degree). Write this as trdeg(𝐾/𝑘) or trdeg𝑘(𝐾).

Proof. Follows from (2.3.8) and (2.3.12). ∎
Proposition 3.19.140

Let 𝐾/𝑘 be a field extension of finite transcendence degree and 𝑆⊂𝐾 a subset. Then

  • 𝑆 algebraically independent ⟹|𝑆|≤trdeg(𝐾/𝑘)
  • 𝐾/𝑘(𝑆) is algebraic ⟹|𝑆|≥trdeg(𝐾/𝑘)
Proof. Follows directly from (2.3.13). ∎
Proposition 3.19.141

Let 𝐾/𝑘 be a field extension of finite transcendence degree and 𝑆⊂𝐾 a subset. Then the following are equivalent

  • 𝑆 is a transcendence base
  • 𝑆 is algebraically independent and |𝑆|≥trdeg(𝐾/𝑘)
  • 𝐾/𝑘(𝑆) is algebraic and |𝑆|≤trdeg(𝐾/𝑘)

In this case |𝑆|=trdeg(𝐾/𝑘).

Proof. Follows directly from (2.3.14). ∎

In case 𝐾/𝑘 is finitely generated then we guarantee that 𝐾/𝑘(𝑆) is finite.

Proposition 3.19.142

Let 𝐾/𝑘 be a finitely generated field extension. Then

  • trdeg(𝐾/𝑘)<∞
  • Suppose 𝐾/𝑘(Γ) is algebraic then it is in fact finite.

Proof. 𝑎) If 𝐾/𝑘 is finitely generated then the set of generators is algebraically spanning and so trdeg(𝐾/𝑘)<∞ by (3.19.139)

𝑏) A-fortiori 𝐾/𝑘(Γ) is finitely generated and by assumption algebraic so by (…) it is finite ∎
