5.7.7 Mean-Value Theorem

Proposition 5.7.11 (Mean-Value Theorem)

Let 𝑓:[𝑎,𝑏]→ℝ be a continuous function which is differentiable on (𝑎,𝑏). Then there exists 𝑐∈(𝑎,𝑏) such that

𝑓′(𝑐)=𝑓(𝑏)−𝑓(𝑎)𝑏−𝑎

Proof 1.. Define the function

𝑔(𝑥)≔𝑓(𝑥)−𝑓(𝑏)−𝑓(𝑎)𝑏−𝑎(𝑥−𝑎)

Then 𝑔 satisfies the same conditions with 𝑔(𝑎)=𝑔(𝑏)=𝑓(𝑎) and 𝑔′(𝑥)=𝑓′(𝑥)−𝑓(𝑏)−𝑓(𝑎)𝑏−𝑎. Evidently it is sufficient to find 𝑐∈(𝑎,𝑏) such that 𝑔′(𝑐)=0.

By (5.7.8) 𝑔 is bounded and attains its maximum and minimum, say at 𝑐,𝑑 respectively. If both 𝑐,𝑑∈{𝑎,𝑏} then 𝑔 must be the constant function whence 𝑔′ is exactly zero. Otherwise we may assume for example that 𝑐∈(𝑎,𝑏). By (5.5.2)

lim𝑛→∞𝑔(𝑐±1𝑛)−𝑔(𝑐)±1𝑛=𝑔′(𝑐)

As 𝑐 is an extremum we may deduce from (5.1.21) that both 𝑔′(𝑐)≤0 and 𝑔′(𝑐)≥0, whence 𝑔′(𝑐)=0. ∎

Corollary 5.7.12
Let 𝑓:[𝑎,𝑏]→ℝ a continuous function which is differentiable on (𝑎,𝑏). If 𝑓′(𝑥) is positive (resp. strictly positive, negative, strictly negative) then 𝑓(𝑥) is increasing (resp. strictly increasing, decreasing, strictly decreasing).
Proposition 5.7.13
Let 𝑓:(𝑎,𝑏)→ℝ be a strictly increasing (resp. decreasing) continuous function. Then 𝑓 is a homeomorphism onto its image.

Proof. Evidently 𝑓 is injective, therefore it is only required to show that the map

𝑓−1:𝑓(𝑈)→𝑈

is continuous. Without loss of generality we may assume that 𝑓 is strictly increasing. Suppose 𝑦=𝑓(𝑥) then we require to show for every 𝜀>0 there exists 𝛿>0 such that

|𝑦′−𝑦|<𝛿⟹|𝑓−1(𝑦′)−𝑓−1(𝑦)|<𝜀

Define 𝜀′≤𝜀 such that (𝑥−𝜀′,𝑥+𝜀′)⊂(𝑎,𝑏). Define 𝛿≔min(𝑓(𝑥+𝜀′)−𝑓(𝑥),𝑓(𝑥)−𝑓(𝑥−𝜀′)). Then

|𝑦′−𝑦|<𝛿⟹𝑓(𝑥−𝜀′)<𝑦′<𝑓(𝑥+𝜀′)⟹𝑥−𝜀′<𝑓−1(𝑦′)<𝑥+𝜀′⟹|𝑓−1(𝑦′)−𝑓−1(𝑦)|<𝜀′≤𝜀

as 𝑓−1 is also monotonic. ∎
