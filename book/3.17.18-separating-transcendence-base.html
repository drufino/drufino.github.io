3.17.18 Separating Transcendence Base

In applications the existence of a separating transcendence base is important, and is guaranteed when 𝑘 is perfect, or a weaker condition.

Definition 3.17.146 (Separating Transcendence Base)

A field extension 𝐾/𝑘 is separably generated if there exists a transcendence base 𝑆 such that 𝐾/𝑘(𝑆) is separable (and algebraic). We call such an 𝑆 a separating transcendence base.

Note when char(𝑘)=0 then every transcendence base is separating.

Definition 3.17.147 (Maclane's Criterion)

We say that an algebra 𝐴/𝑘 of characteristic exponent 𝑝 satisfies Maclane’s Criterion if it is reduced and for all 𝑎1,…,𝑎𝑛∈𝐴 the following property holds

{𝑎1,…,𝑎𝑛}k-linearly independent⟹{𝑎1𝑝,…,𝑎𝑛𝑝}k-linearly independent

When 𝑝=1 this is trivially always satisfied and when 𝑝>1 such an algebra is automatically reduced by the following result

Lemma 3.17.148

Let 𝐴 be a 𝑘-algebra and 𝑚>1 an integer. Then the following are equivalent

  1. 𝐴 is reduced
  2. 𝑥𝑚=0⟹𝑥=0

In particular if 𝐴 has characteristic exponent 𝑝>1 then it is reduced iff the Frobenius homomorphism is injective.

Proof. 𝑎)⟹𝑏) is trivial. Conversely suppose 𝑥𝑛=0. Then there exists 𝑎>0 such that 𝑛≤𝑎𝑚. In particular 𝑥𝑎𝑚=0 and therefore by induction 𝑥=0 as required. ∎

This is a generalization of the case of a perfect base field by the following result.

Proposition 3.17.149 (Perfect ⟹ Maclane)

Let 𝐴/𝑘 be a reduced algebra with 𝑘 perfect. Then it satisfies Maclane’s Criterion.

In particular this holds for every field extension 𝐾/𝑘 of a perfect base field.

Proof. By assumption 𝐴 is reduced so we may consider only the case 𝑝>1.

Suppose 0=∑𝑖𝜆𝑖𝑎𝑖𝑝 then by hypothesis 𝜆𝑖=𝜇𝑖𝑝. By (3.17.6) 0=(∑𝑖𝜇𝑖𝑎𝑖)𝑝. As 𝐴 is reduced we find 0=∑𝑖𝜇𝑖𝑎𝑖 as required. ∎

Lemma 3.17.150

Let 𝑛≥0 an integer and 𝐾=𝑘(𝑥1,…,𝑥𝑛+1) an extension of 𝑘 such that

  • char(𝑘)≠0
  • {𝑥1,…,𝑥𝑛} is a transcendence base
  • 𝐾/𝑘 satisfies Maclane’s Criterion

Then for some 𝑥𝑖 the set {𝑥1,…,𝑥𝑖̂,…,𝑥𝑛+1} is a separating transcendence base for 𝐾/𝑘.

Note when 𝑛=0 this means precisely that a simple algebraic extension satisfying Maclane’s Criterion is separable.

Proof. By assumption 𝑥𝑛+1 is algebraic over 𝑘(𝑥1,…,𝑥𝑛), and therefore there exists a non-zero 𝐹∈𝑘[𝑋1,…,𝑋𝑛+1] such that 𝐹(𝑥1,…,𝑥𝑛+1)=0. Let 𝐹 be such a polynomial of minimal total degree (total degree = the maximum degree of a monomial with non-zero coefficient appearing in 𝐹). We claim it is irreducible. For suppose not, then one of the irreducible factors must vanish at (𝑥1,…,𝑥𝑛+1), and this factor would have smaller total degree.

We wish to show that some monomial 𝑋𝛼 of 𝐹 (3.10.1) has an exponent 𝛼𝑖 that is not a multiple of 𝑝≔char(𝑘), for some 𝑖. Suppose not, so that every exponent of every monomial of 𝐹 is a multiple of 𝑝; then the elements

{𝑥1𝛼1…𝑥𝑛+1𝛼𝑛+1|𝜆𝛼≠0}

are 𝑘-linearly dependent where 𝜆𝛼 is the coefficient of the monomial 𝑋𝛼 in 𝐹 and 𝛼∈ℕ𝑛+1. Then by Maclane’s Criterion the set

{𝑥1𝛼1/𝑝…𝑥𝑛+1𝛼𝑛+1/𝑝|𝜆𝛼≠0}

is linearly dependent, that is ∑𝛼𝜇𝛼𝑥𝛼/𝑝=0 for some 𝜇𝛼∈𝑘 not all zero, the sum being over the 𝛼 with 𝜆𝛼≠0. Then ∑𝛼𝜇𝛼𝑋𝛼/𝑝 is a non-zero polynomial (distinct 𝛼 give distinct monomials) vanishing at 𝑥, of total degree at most (deg𝐹)/𝑝<deg𝐹. This contradicts the minimality of 𝐹.

Choose such a monomial 𝑋𝛼 and index 𝑖, and put 𝑚0≔𝛼𝑖, so that 𝑝∤𝑚0 and in particular 𝑚0≥1. Grouping the monomials of 𝐹 according to the exponent of 𝑋𝑖 we write 𝐹=∑𝑚𝐺𝑚𝑋𝑖𝑚 with 𝐺𝑚∈𝑘[𝑋𝑗|𝑗≠𝑖]. Then 𝐺𝑚0≠0, as it contains the monomial 𝑋𝛼 with 𝑋𝑖𝑚0 removed. Define

𝐹𝑖(𝑇)≔𝐹(𝑥1,…,𝑥𝑖−1,𝑇,𝑥𝑖+1,…,𝑥𝑛+1)∈𝑘[𝑆𝑖][𝑇]

where 𝑆𝑖≔{𝑥1,…,𝑥𝑖̂,…,𝑥𝑛+1}, so that 𝐹𝑖(𝑇)=∑𝑚𝐺𝑚(𝑆𝑖)𝑇𝑚. The coefficient of 𝑇𝑚0 in 𝐹𝑖(𝑇) is 𝐺𝑚0(𝑆𝑖), where 𝐺𝑚0 is a non-zero polynomial in the other variables of total degree <deg𝐹, so is non-zero by minimality of 𝐹. Hence 𝐹𝑖(𝑇) is non-zero and clearly 𝐹𝑖(𝑥𝑖)=0 so that 𝑥𝑖 is algebraic over 𝑘(𝑆𝑖), and therefore by (3.17.44) 𝐾 is also algebraic over 𝑘(𝑆𝑖). By (3.17.144) 𝑆𝑖 is a transcendence base for 𝐾 (as 𝐾/𝑘(𝑆𝑖) is algebraic and |𝑆𝑖|≤𝑛=trdeg(𝐾/𝑘), since 𝑥1,…,𝑥𝑛 is a transcendence base) and in particular algebraically independent. Therefore 𝑘[𝑆𝑖][𝑇] may be identified with 𝑘[𝑋1,…,𝑋𝑛+1] (the evaluation 𝑋𝑗↦𝑥𝑗, 𝑗≠𝑖, is an isomorphism 𝑘[𝑋𝑗|𝑗≠𝑖]→∼𝑘[𝑆𝑖] by algebraic independence, which extends to an isomorphism 𝑘[𝑋1,…,𝑋𝑛+1]→∼𝑘[𝑆𝑖][𝑇] with 𝑋𝑖↦𝑇, sending 𝐹=∑𝑚𝐺𝑚𝑋𝑖𝑚 to 𝐹𝑖=∑𝑚𝐺𝑚(𝑆𝑖)𝑇𝑚) and we may conclude that 𝐹𝑖(𝑇) is irreducible. Further 𝑘[𝑆𝑖]≅𝑘[𝑋𝑗|𝑗≠𝑖] is a UFD (3.15.46) and 𝐹𝑖 has positive degree in 𝑇, so by (3.15.44) 𝐹𝑖(𝑇) is irreducible as a polynomial in 𝑘(𝑆𝑖)[𝑇]. By construction (its coefficient of 𝑇𝑚0 is non-zero and 𝑝∤𝑚0) 𝐹𝑖(𝑇)∉𝑘(𝑆𝑖)[𝑇𝑝] so by (3.17.94) 𝐹𝑖 is separable. Therefore 𝑥𝑖 is separable over 𝑘(𝑆𝑖), and 𝐾/𝑘(𝑆𝑖) is separable by (3.17.82) as required. ∎

Proposition 3.17.151

Let 𝐾/𝑘 be a finitely-generated field extension which satisfies Maclane’s Criterion (e.g. 𝑘 is perfect). Then 𝐾/𝑘 is separably generated.

More precisely every generating set contains a separating transcendence base.

Proof. When char(𝑘)=0 the generating set contains a transcendence base by (3.17.142). Every subfield of 𝐾 also has characteristic 0, so then we are done by (3.17.106). So we may only consider the positive characteristic case, 𝑝>1.

Suppose 𝐾=𝑘(𝑥1,…,𝑥𝑛). By (3.17.141) there is a (possibly empty) subset which is a transcendence base, say {𝑥1,…,𝑥𝑑} after renumbering. By (3.17.44) [𝐾:𝐾′]<∞ where 𝐾′=𝑘(𝑥1,…,𝑥𝑑), and we may choose 𝐾′ such that the degree of inseparability [𝐾:𝐾′]𝑖 is minimal. If 𝐾/𝐾′ is separable then we are done. Otherwise we may assume by renumbering that 𝐾′(𝑥𝑑+1)/𝐾′ is not separable (3.17.82) and therefore [𝐾′(𝑥𝑑+1):𝐾′]𝑖>1 (3.17.96). Then by (3.17.150) there is an index 𝑖≤𝑑+1 such that [𝐾′(𝑥𝑑+1):𝐾″]𝑖=1 where 𝐾″≔𝑘(𝑥1,…,𝑥̂𝑖,…,𝑥𝑑+1).
