3.3.5 Chinese Remainder Theorem

Proposition 3.3.61 (Chinese Remainder Theorem)

Let 𝐴 be a commutative ring with ideals 𝔞1,…,𝔞𝑛 which are pairwise coprime (i.e. 𝔞𝑖+𝔞𝑗=𝐴 for all 1≤𝑖<𝑗≤𝑛). Then there is a canonical isomorphism

𝐴/⋂𝑖=1𝑛𝔞𝑖⟶𝐴/𝔞1×…×𝐴/𝔞𝑛

Further we have the following identity

⋂𝑖=1𝑛𝔞𝑖=∏𝑖=1𝑛𝔞𝑖

Proof. Consider the canonical ring homomorphism

𝜑:𝐴⟶𝐴/𝔞1×…×𝐴/𝔞𝑛.

Trivially it has kernel ⋂𝑖=1𝑛𝔞𝑖. Therefore by (3.3.55) it is sufficient to show it is surjective. First we show that 𝑒1≔(1,0,…,0) is in the image of 𝜑. For we have 𝑥𝑖+𝑦𝑖=1 for 𝑥𝑖∈𝔞1, 𝑦𝑖∈𝔞𝑖 and 1<𝑖≤𝑛. Take 𝑓1=𝑦2…𝑦𝑛∈⋂𝑖=2𝑛𝔞𝑖; since 𝑦𝑖=1−𝑥𝑖, 𝑓1=∏𝑖=2𝑛(1−𝑥𝑖)∈1+𝔞1 (as each 𝑥𝑖∈𝔞1), so combined with 𝑓1∈⋂𝑖=2𝑛𝔞𝑖, 𝜑(𝑓1)=𝑒1 as required.

Similarly there exists 𝑓𝑖 such that 𝜑(𝑓𝑖)=(0,…,1,…,0)≕𝑒𝑖. Finally for 𝑥1―∈𝐴/𝔞1,…,𝑥𝑛―∈𝐴/𝔞𝑛 then

𝜑(𝑓1𝑥1+…+𝑓𝑛𝑥𝑛)=(𝑥1―,…,𝑥𝑛―)

For the final statement we may assume by induction that

⋂𝑖=1𝑛−1𝔞𝑖=∏𝑖=1𝑛−1𝔞𝑖

We have equations

𝑥𝑖+𝑦𝑖=1𝑥𝑖∈𝔞𝑖,𝑦𝑖∈𝔞𝑛

Then

𝑥1…𝑥𝑛−1=∏𝑖=1𝑛−1(1−𝑦𝑖)∈1+𝔞𝑛

so we may conclude

⋂𝑖=1𝑛−1𝔞𝑖+𝔞𝑛=𝐴.

This then reduces to the case 𝑛=2. Suppose 𝑥∈𝔞∩𝔟, by assumption 1=𝑦+𝑧 for 𝑦∈𝔞 and 𝑧∈𝔟. Then 𝑥=𝑥(𝑦+𝑧)∈𝔞𝔟, so 𝔞∩𝔟⊂𝔞𝔟. The reverse inclusion is obvious. ∎

Proposition 3.3.62 (Chinese Remainder Theorem II)

Let 𝐴 be a commutative ring with 𝔞1,…,𝔞𝑛 co-prime ideals. For any positive integers 𝑘1,…,𝑘𝑛 there is a canonical isomorphism

𝐴/⋂𝑖=1𝑛𝔞𝑖𝑘𝑖⟶𝐴/𝔞1𝑘1×…×𝐴/𝔞𝑛𝑘𝑛

and

⋂𝑖=1𝑛𝔞𝑖𝑘𝑖=∏𝑖=1𝑛𝔞𝑖𝑘𝑖
Proof. By (3.3.46) the ideals 𝔞1𝑘1,…,𝔞𝑛𝑘𝑛 are pairwise co-prime. Therefore we may apply (3.3.61). ∎
