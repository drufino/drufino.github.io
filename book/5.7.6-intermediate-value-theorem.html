5.7.6 Intermediate Value Theorem

Proposition 5.7.9 (Intervals are connected)

Let 𝑋⊂ℝ. Then the following are equivalent

  1. 𝑋 is connected
  2. For every 𝑥<𝑦<𝑧 such that 𝑥,𝑧∈𝑋, we have 𝑦∈𝑋

In particular (𝑎,𝑏],(𝑎,𝑏),[𝑎,𝑏) are connected.

Proof. 𝑏)⟹𝑎) Suppose 𝑋 is disconnected, then 𝑋=𝑈⊔𝑉 with 𝑈,𝑉 non-empty open and closed subsets of 𝑋. Let 𝑥∈𝑈 and 𝑧∈𝑉, and assume wlog that 𝑥<𝑧. Define

𝛼≔sup(𝑈∩(𝑥,𝑧))

By (5.1.21) 𝛼∈[𝑥,𝑧]⊂𝑋. By (5.1.18) there exists 𝛼𝑛∈𝑈∩(𝑥,𝑧) such that 𝛼𝑛↑𝛼. Then by (4.1.20) we have 𝛼∈clℝ(𝑈)∩𝑋=(4.1.21)cl𝑋(𝑈)=(4.1.19)𝑈. If 𝛼=𝑧 then we reach a contradiction as 𝑈 and 𝑉 were assumed to be disjoint. Similarly if 𝛼<𝑧, as 𝑈 is open in 𝑋, we may choose 0<𝜀<𝑧−𝛼 such that (𝛼−𝜀,𝛼+𝜀)∩𝑋⊂𝑈. By assumption 𝛼+𝜀∈𝑋 whence 𝛼+𝜀∈𝑈, which contradicts maximality of 𝛼.

𝑎)⟹𝑏) Suppose we have 𝑥<𝑦<𝑧 such that 𝑥,𝑧∈𝑋 but 𝑦∉𝑋. Then define the open subsets 𝑈=(−∞,𝑦)∩𝑋 and 𝑉=(𝑦,∞)∩𝑋. Then

𝑈≔(−∞,𝑦)∩𝑋=(−∞,𝑦]∩𝑋

is both open and closed. ∎

Proposition 5.7.10 (Intermediate Value Theorem)
Let 𝑎<𝑏 be real numbers and 𝑓:[𝑎,𝑏]→ℝ continuous such that 𝑓(𝑎)<𝑓(𝑏). Then for every 𝑓(𝑎)<𝑐<𝑓(𝑏) there exists 𝑥∈(𝑎,𝑏) such that 𝑓(𝑥)=𝑐.

Proof 1.. Consider

𝑋≔{𝑥′∈[𝑎,𝑏]|𝑓(𝑥′)<𝑐}

By definition 𝑋 is bounded, and 𝑎∈𝑋, so it is non-empty, so we may define 𝑥≔sup𝑋. We claim that 𝑓(𝑥)=𝑐. By (5.1.18) there is a sequence 𝑥𝑛∈𝑋 such that 𝑥𝑛→𝑥. By (5.4.13) 𝑓(𝑥𝑛)→𝑓(𝑥) and by (5.1.21) 𝑥≤𝑏 and 𝑓(𝑥)≤𝑐. Further by assumption 𝑥≠𝑏.

To show 𝑓(𝑥)≥𝑐 consider the sequence 𝑥𝑛≔𝑥+min(1𝑛,𝑏−𝑥)∈[𝑎,𝑏]. Evidently 𝑥𝑛→𝑥 so by continuity 𝑓(𝑥𝑛)→𝑓(𝑥). By construction 𝑓(𝑥𝑛)≥𝑐 whence 𝑓(𝑥)≥𝑐.

Therefore we conclude 𝑓(𝑥)=𝑐 as required. ∎
