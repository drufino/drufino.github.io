4.1.4 Quasi-Homeomorphism

Proposition 4.1.31 (Quasi-Homeomorphism)

Let 𝑓:𝑋→𝑌 be a map. The following are equivalent

  1. The map 𝑊→𝑓−1(𝑊) is a bijection between open sets
  2. The map 𝑍→𝑓−1(𝑍) is a bijection between closed sets

In this case we say 𝑓 is a quasi-homeomorphism. Such a map induces a bijection between irreducible sets, respectively irreducible components.

Proof. This follows by taking complements and observing 𝑋∖𝑓−1(𝐴)=𝑓−1(𝑌∖𝐴). ∎
Proposition 4.1.32 (Induced Quotient)

Let 𝜋:𝑋→𝑌 be a surjective map where 𝑋 is a topological space. The family

𝒯︀𝑌≔{𝑈⊂𝑌|𝜋−1(𝑈)∈𝒯︀𝑋}

is a well-defined topology on 𝑌. Suppose that 𝜋−1(𝜋(𝑉))⊂𝑉 for all 𝑉 open (resp. closed) subsets of 𝑋. Then 𝜋 is both open, closed and a quasi-homeomorphism.

Proof. As arbitrary intersections and unions commute with inverse image we see that 𝒯︀𝑌 is a well-defined topology.

As 𝜋 is surjective we have 𝜋(𝜋−1(𝑈))=𝑈 for all open subsets 𝑈⊂𝑌. Generically 𝑉⊂𝜋−1(𝜋(𝑉)) and by assumption the reverse inclusion holds. Therefore we see 𝜋 induces a quasi-homeomorphism. This also shows 𝜋 is open and therefore closed. ∎
