3.31 Discrete Valuation Rings

A Discrete Valuation Ring (DVR) corresponds to a regular local ring of dimension 1 and therefore regular (or in the case of perfect base field, non-singular) points on a curve. There are a number of equivalent conditions which are summarised here.

Lemma 3.31.1
Let (𝐴,𝔪) be a local domain such that 𝔪 is finitely generated and dim𝐴=1. Then for every non-zero ideal 𝔞 there exists some 𝑛 such that 𝔪𝑛⊆𝔞.
Proof. As dim𝐴=1 every minimal prime over 𝔞 is equal to 𝔪, whence 𝔞=𝔪 (i.e. 𝔞 is 𝔪-primary). As 𝔪 is finitely generated there is some 𝑛 such that 𝔪𝑛⊆𝔞 (3.3.47). ∎
Proposition 3.31.2 (DVR Criteria)

Let 𝐴 be an integral domain which is not a field. Then the following are equivalent.

  1. 𝐴 is a valuation ring whose valuation group is order-isomorphic to ℤ
  2. 𝐴 is a local principal ideal domain
  3. 𝐴 is a Noetherian local ring with maximal ideal 𝔪=(𝜋) and dim𝐴=1
  4. 𝐴 is a local ring such that every ideal is of the form (𝜋𝑘) for some 𝑘≥0 (and these are distinct)
  5. There exists a non-unit 𝜋∈𝐴 such that every element 𝑎∈𝐴 may be represented as 𝑢𝜋𝑘 for some 𝑢∈𝐴⋆ and non-negative integer 𝑘.
  6. 𝐴 is a Noetherian valuation ring
  7. 𝐴 is a Noetherian integrally closed local domain with dim𝐴=1
  8. 𝐴 is a Noetherian regular local ring with dim𝐴=1

In this case we say 𝐴 is a discrete valuation ring (or DVR) and let 𝐾=Frac(𝐴). For every generator 𝜋 of 𝔪, the ideals (𝜋𝑛) are distinct and

  • for every 𝑥∈𝐾⋆ there is a unique integer 𝑘 and unit 𝑢 such that 𝑥=𝑢𝜋𝑘, and
  • there is a well-defined valuation 𝑣:𝐾⋆→ℤ determined uniquely by the relation 𝑥=𝑢𝜋𝑣(𝑥)

Proof. a) ⟹ b) Consider a valuation 𝑣:𝐾→ℤ with 𝑣−1(ℤ≥0)=𝐴. For any ideal 𝔞 choose 𝑓∈𝔞 such that 𝑣(𝑓) is minimal. For 𝑔∈𝔞 we have 𝑣(𝑔)≥𝑣(𝑓)⟹𝑣(𝑔𝑓−1)≥0⟹𝑔𝑓−1∈𝐴⟹𝑔∈(𝑓). Therefore 𝔞=(𝑓) is principal. Further by (3.25.2) 𝐴 is a local ring.

b) ⟹ c). By (3.22.4) dim𝐴=1 and by assumption 𝔪=(𝜋). Evidently 𝐴 is Noetherian.

c) ⟹ d). By (3.29.1) the sequence (𝜋𝑘) is strictly decreasing. Assume wlog that 𝔞 is proper and non-zero, then as dim𝐴=1 every prime ideal is maximal and we find 𝔞=(𝜋) by (3.3.45).

Suppose that 𝔞⊆(𝜋𝑘) for all 𝑘. Then 𝜋∈𝔞⟹(𝜋𝑘)=𝔞 for all sufficiently large 𝑘 which contradicts the fact the sequence is strictly decreasing. Therefore we may assume 𝔞⊆(𝜋𝑘) for some 𝑘 and there exists 𝑎∈𝔞∖(𝜋𝑘+1). Therefore 𝑎=𝑢𝜋𝑘 and by construction 𝑢∉(𝜋)⟹𝑢∈𝐴⋆⟹𝜋𝑘∈𝔞. Therefore 𝔞=(𝜋𝑘) as required.

d) ⟹ e). By assumption (𝑎)=(𝜋𝑘) for some integer 𝑘, whence by (3.17.3) 𝑎=𝑢𝜋𝑘 for some 𝑢∈𝐴⋆. This representation is unique because the ideals (𝜋𝑘) are all distinct and 𝐴 is cancellable.

e) ⟹ a). We may show easily that the integer 𝑘 is uniquely defined, denote this by 𝑣(𝑎) where 𝑎=𝑢𝜋𝑣(𝑎) for 𝑢∈𝐴⋆. We need to show that 𝑣 is a valuation. By uniqueness we deduce 𝑣(𝑎𝑏)=𝑣(𝑎)+𝑣(𝑏). Suppose that 𝑎,𝑏,𝑎+𝑏∈𝐴∖{0}. Then (𝜋𝑣(𝑎+𝑏))=(𝑎+𝑏)⊆(𝜋min(𝑣(𝑎),𝑣(𝑏))). As the sequence (𝜋𝑘) is strictly decreasing we find 𝑣(𝑎+𝑏)≥min(𝑣(𝑎),𝑣(𝑏)) as required. We may extend to 𝐾⋆ by 𝑣(𝑎𝑏−1)≔𝑣(𝑎)−𝑣(𝑏). Then 𝑣(𝑎𝑏−1)≥0⟺𝑣(𝑎)≥𝑣(𝑏)⟺(𝑎)⊆(𝑏)⟺𝑏∣𝑎⟺𝑎𝑏−1∈𝐴. Further 𝑣(𝑎)=0⟺𝑎∉(𝜋)⟺𝑎∈𝐴⋆. Therefore 𝐴 is the valuation ring associated to 𝑣.

d) ⟹ f) ⟹ b). This follows from (3.25.20).

f) ⟹ g). A valuation ring is integrally closed (3.25.2) and we’ve already shown 𝐴 is local with dimension 1.

g) ⟹ c). Let 0≠𝑎∈𝔪. As dim𝐴=1 from (3.31.1) we know 𝔪𝑛⊆(𝑎) for some 𝑛≥1. Choose 𝑛 minimal and 𝑏∈𝔪𝑛−1∖(𝑎).

Let 𝑥=𝑎−1𝑏∈𝐾=Frac(𝐴). Evidently 𝑥∉𝐴, for otherwise 𝑏∈(𝑎); further 𝑥𝔪=𝑎−1𝑏𝔪⊆𝑎−1𝔪𝑛⊆𝑎−1(𝑎)=𝐴 is an ideal of 𝐴.

We claim that 𝑥𝔪=𝐴. Suppose not then 𝑥𝔪⊆𝔪 by (3.3.36). Then 𝔪 is a faithful finite 𝐴[𝑥]-module and 𝑥 is integral over 𝐴 (3.24.3). By assumption 𝐴 is integrally closed so 𝑥∈𝐴 which is a contradiction. Therefore 𝑥𝔪=𝐴⟹𝔪=𝑥−1𝐴. This shows that 𝑥−1=𝑥−1⋅1∈𝐴 and that 𝔪 is a principal ideal.

c) ⟹ h). By assumption 𝐴 is a Noetherian local ring with dim𝐴=1. So it remains to show that dim𝔪/𝔪2=1. As 𝔪 is principal then dim𝔪/𝔪2≤1. If it is zero then 𝔪=𝔪2 and dim𝐴=0 by (3.29.1), a contradiction.

h) ⟹ c). By assumption dim𝔪/𝔪2=1 and so by (3.21.7) 𝔪=(𝜋) for some 𝜋∈𝔪∖𝔪2.

The final statements have already been proven. ∎

Lemma 3.31.3 (Valuation and Powers of the Maximal Ideal)

Let (𝐴,𝔪) be a DVR with valuation 𝑣, and 𝑘≥0 an integer. For 0≠𝑎∈𝐴:

  1. 𝑣(𝑎)≥𝑘⟺𝑎∈𝔪𝑘;
  2. 𝑣(𝑎)=𝑘⟺𝑎∈𝔪𝑘∖𝔪𝑘+1.

Proof. Drafted by Claude Sonnet 5, reviewed by David Rufino.

Fix a uniformiser 𝜋 (so 𝔪=(𝜋), hence 𝔪𝑘=(𝜋𝑘) (3.31.2)), and write 𝑎=𝑢𝜋𝑣(𝑎) for a unit 𝑢∈𝐴⋆ (3.31.2).

a). 𝑎∈𝔪𝑘=(𝜋𝑘)⟺𝜋𝑘∣𝑎=𝑢𝜋𝑣(𝑎)⟺𝜋𝑘∣𝜋𝑣(𝑎) (as 𝑢 is a unit) ⟺𝑣(𝑎)≥𝑘 (as the ideals (𝜋𝑛) are strictly decreasing (3.31.2)).

b). By a), 𝑎∈𝔪𝑘∖𝔪𝑘+1⟺𝑣(𝑎)≥𝑘and not(𝑣(𝑎)≥𝑘+1)⟺𝑣(𝑎)=𝑘. ∎

Corollary 3.31.4

Let 𝐴 be a principal ideal domain which is not a field. Then every localisation of 𝐴 at a non-zero prime ideal is a DVR.

In particular ℤ(𝑝) and 𝑘[𝑋](𝑓) are DVRs where 𝑝 is prime and 𝑓(𝑋) is an irreducible polynomial.

Lemma 3.31.5 (A DVR is Maximal Among Valuation Subrings of its Fraction Field)
Let 𝐴 be a DVR with fraction field 𝐾 and 𝐵 a valuation ring of 𝐾 with 𝐴⊆𝐵. Then 𝐴=𝐵 or 𝐵=𝐾.
Proof. Let 𝜋 be a uniformiser of 𝐴, so every 𝑎∈𝐴 is 𝑢𝜋𝑘 for a unit 𝑢∈𝐴⋆ and integer 𝑘≥0 (3.31.2). Suppose 𝐵≠𝐾. As 𝐵 is a valuation ring of 𝐾, either 𝜋∈𝐵 (already true, as 𝜋∈𝐴⊆𝐵) or 𝜋−1∈𝐵; we rule out the latter. If 𝜋−1∈𝐵, then for any 𝑥∈𝐾⋆, writing 𝑥=𝑢𝜋𝑛 (𝑢∈𝐴⋆⊂𝐵, 𝑛∈ℤ, using 𝐴 a DVR), 𝑥=𝑢𝜋𝑛∈𝐵 whether 𝑛≥0 (as 𝜋𝑛∈𝐴⊂𝐵) or 𝑛<0 (as 𝜋𝑛=(𝜋−1)−𝑛∈𝐵); so 𝐵=𝐾, contradicting assumption. Hence 𝜋−1∉𝐵, that is 𝜋∈𝔪𝐵. Then 𝔪𝐴=𝜋𝐴⊂𝜋𝐵⊂𝔪𝐵 (as 𝜋∈𝔪𝐵 and 𝐵 absorbs), so 𝐵 dominates 𝐴; as 𝐴 is a valuation ring, it is maximal under domination (3.25.13), giving 𝐴=𝐵. ∎
Lemma 3.31.6 (Directed Union of Valuation Rings with a Common Uniformiser)
Let (𝐾𝑖)𝑖∈𝐼 be a directed system of fields with union 𝐾≔⋃𝑖𝐾𝑖, and for each 𝑖 let 𝑅𝑖⊂𝐾𝑖 be a valuation ring with 𝑅𝑖⊆𝑅𝑙 whenever 𝐾𝑖⊆𝐾𝑙. Suppose in addition every 𝔪𝑅𝑖 is principal, generated by a single common element 0≠𝜋∈⋂𝑖𝑅𝑖. Then 𝑅≔⋃𝑖𝑅𝑖 is a valuation ring of 𝐾, with 𝔪𝑅=𝜋𝑅≠0; in particular 𝑅⊊𝐾.

Proof. For 𝐾𝑖⊆𝐾𝑙 (so 𝑅𝑖⊆𝑅𝑙), 𝔪𝑅𝑖=𝜋𝑅𝑖⊆𝜋𝑅𝑙=𝔪𝑅𝑙, giving 𝑅𝑙 dominates 𝑅𝑖 (3.25.4) so (3.25.7) applies, and 𝔪𝑅=⋃𝑖𝔪𝑅𝑖=⋃𝑖𝜋𝑅𝑖=𝜋𝑅.

As 𝜋∈𝔪𝑅𝑖 is a non-unit of every 𝑅𝑖, 𝜋−1∉𝑅𝑖 for every 𝑖 (well-defined, as 0≠𝜋∈𝐾𝑖⊂𝐾), hence 𝜋−1∉𝑅=⋃𝑖𝑅𝑖; as 𝜋−1∈𝐾, 𝑅⊊𝐾. ∎

Proposition 3.31.7

Let 𝐴 be a discrete valuation ring with unique maximal ideal 𝔪. Then there exists a canonical group isomorphism

(𝔪)−:ℤ⟶∼ℐ︀𝐴

which is the unique group homomorphism mapping 1 to 𝔪 (and 0 to 𝐴. In particular every fractional ideal is invertible.

Write the inverse group isomorphism by 𝑣𝔪, which satisfies

  1. 𝔪𝑣𝔪(𝔞)=𝔞
  2. 𝑣𝔪(𝔪𝑘)=𝑘

A fractional ideal 𝐼 is contained in 𝐴 if and only if 𝑣𝔪(𝐼)≥0.

Proof. By (3.31.2) we may suppose 𝔪=(𝜋). Evidently (𝜋𝑘) is a fractional ideal for every integer 𝑘 and (𝜋𝑘)(𝜋𝑙)=(𝜋𝑘+𝑙), which gives the required homomorphism 𝑘→(𝜋𝑘).

Suppose the kernel is non-trivial then there exists 𝑘 which maps to 𝐴, and we may assume that 𝑘 is positive (by considering the inverse). This means precisely that 𝔪𝑘=𝐴, which may only happen when 𝑘=0 because 𝔪 is proper.

Let 𝐼 be an arbitrary fractional ideal then 𝑎𝐼⊆𝐴 for some 𝑎∈𝐴. By (3.31.2) 𝑎=𝑢𝜋𝑙 and 𝑎𝐼=(𝜋𝑘) for some positive integers 𝑙 and 𝑘. This means (𝜋𝑙)𝐼=(𝜋𝑘) and therefore 𝐼=(𝜋𝑘)(𝜋−𝑙)=(𝜋𝑘−𝑙). This shows the homomorphism is surjective and therefore an isomorphism.

Evidently (𝜋𝑘)⊂𝐴⟺𝑘≥0 since 𝜋∈𝐴 and 𝜋−1∉𝐴⟹𝜋−𝑘∉𝐴. ∎

Proposition 3.31.8

Let 𝐴 be a discrete valuation ring and 𝐼,𝐽 fractional ideals. Then the following are equivalent

  1. 𝐼⊂𝐽
  2. 𝐽 divides 𝐼 (that is there exists an ideal 𝔞 such that 𝔞𝐽=𝐼)
  3. 𝑣𝔪(𝐼)≥𝑣𝔪(𝐽)

Proof. Using the results of (3.31.7) we may show (eliding the subscript 𝔪)

a) ⟺ c) 𝐼⊂𝐽⟺𝐽−1𝐼⊂𝐴⟺𝑣(𝐽−1𝐼)≥0⟺𝑣(𝐼)≥𝑣𝔪(𝐽)

b) ⟹ c) 𝑣(𝐼)=𝑣(𝔞)+𝑣(𝐽)≥𝑣(𝐽)

c) ⟹ b) Define 𝔞=𝔪𝑣(𝐼)−𝑣(𝐽) then evidentally 𝑣(𝔞𝐽)=𝑣(𝐼) whence 𝐼=𝐽. ∎

Lemma 3.31.9 (Intersection of the Powers of the Maximal Ideal)
Let 𝐴 be a DVR with maximal ideal 𝔪. Then ⋂𝑛≥0𝔪𝑛=0.

Proof. Drafted by Claude Sonnet 5, reviewed by David Rufino.

Let 0≠𝑎∈𝐴, and write 𝑎=𝑢𝜋𝑣(𝑎) for a unit 𝑢 and uniformiser 𝜋 (3.31.2). If 𝑎∈𝔪𝑣(𝑎)+1=(𝜋𝑣(𝑎)+1) then 𝑎=𝑢′𝜋𝑣(𝑎)+1 for some 𝑢′∈𝐴, so 𝑢𝜋𝑣(𝑎)=𝑢′𝜋𝑣(𝑎)+1, giving 𝑢=𝑢′𝜋 by cancellation – contradicting 𝑢 a unit. So 𝑎∉𝔪𝑣(𝑎)+1, in particular 𝑎∉⋂𝑛𝔪𝑛. ∎

Lemma 3.31.10 (Ramification and Injectivity/Surjectivity of Local Homomorphisms)

Let (𝐴,𝔪), (𝐵,𝔫) be DVRs with valuations 𝑣𝐴,𝑣𝐵 (3.31.2), and 𝜙:𝐴→𝐵 a local ring homomorphism (that is 𝜙(𝔪)⊆𝔫). Fix a uniformiser 𝜋 of 𝐴 and set 𝑒≔𝑣𝐵(𝜙(𝜋))∈ℕ∪{∞} (the ramification index of 𝜙; independent of the choice of 𝜋, with 𝑒=∞ iff 𝜙(𝜋)=0). Then:

  1. 𝜙 is injective if and only if 𝑒<∞;
  2. if 𝜙 is surjective, then 𝑒=1; in particular, by a), 𝜙 is then also injective – so 𝜙 is surjective if and only if it is bijective.

Proof. Drafted by Claude Sonnet 5, reviewed by David Rufino.

a). ker𝜙 is a prime ideal of 𝐴 (the kernel of a ring homomorphism into the domain 𝐵), so ker𝜙∈{0,𝔪} (3.31.2). If 𝑒=∞: 𝜙(𝜋)=0, so 𝜋∈ker𝜙, and, as 𝔪=(𝜋), 𝔪⊆ker𝜙; as ker𝜙≠𝐴 (𝜙(1)=1≠0), ker𝜙=𝔪≠0: 𝜙 is not injective. If 𝑒<∞: 𝜙(𝜋)≠0, so 𝜋∉ker𝜙, giving ker𝜙≠𝔪, so ker𝜙=0: 𝜙 is injective.

b). Suppose 𝜙 is surjective. Let 𝜛 be a uniformiser of 𝐵; by surjectivity, 𝜛=𝜙(𝑎) for some 𝑎∈𝐴, necessarily 𝑎≠0 (as 𝜛≠0). Write 𝑎=𝑢𝜋𝑘 for a unit 𝑢∈𝐴⋆ and 𝑘≔𝑣𝐴(𝑎)≥0 (3.31.2). If 𝑒=∞: 𝜙(𝑎)=𝜙(𝑢)𝜙(𝜋)𝑘 is 𝜙(𝑢) (a unit, as units map to units) if 𝑘=0, or 0 if 𝑘≥1; neither equals 𝜛 (𝑣𝐵(𝜛)=1≠0, 𝜛≠0), a contradiction. So 𝑒<∞, and

1=𝑣𝐵(𝜛)=𝑣𝐵(𝜙(𝑢))+𝑘⋅𝑣𝐵(𝜙(𝜋))=0+𝑘𝑒=𝑘𝑒,

forcing 𝑒=1. ∎
