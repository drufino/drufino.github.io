Proof. Let (0)≠𝔞 be an ideal and let 𝑓∈𝔞 be a polynomial of minimal degree. We may assume it is monic. Any 𝑔∈𝔞 may be represented as 𝑓=𝑞𝑔+𝑟 by the division algorithm. Clearly 𝑟∈𝔞, therefore by minimality 𝑟=0, whence 𝑔∈(𝑓). ∎

Lemma 3.17.34 (Roots and Multiplicity)

Proposition 3.17.37 (Criteria for Multiple Roots)

Let 𝑓(𝑋)∈𝑘[𝑋] be a polynomial, 𝑟 a positive integer and suppose that either char(𝑘)=0 or 𝑟<char(𝑘). Then 𝛼∈𝑘 is a root of 𝑓 with multiplicity 𝑟 precisely when

𝑓(𝛼)=𝑓(1)(𝛼)=…=𝑓(𝑟−1)(𝛼)=0

and 𝑓(𝑟)(𝛼)≠0.

Therefore the multiple roots are precisely the common roots of 𝑓(𝑋) and 𝑓′(𝑋) (irrespective of the characteristic).

Proof. Note that by (3.17.34) and (3.8.9)

The second statement is simply the case 𝑟=1. ∎

Definition 3.17.38 (Separable Polynomial)
A polynomial 𝑓∈𝑘[𝑋] is separable if 𝑓 and 𝑓′ are co-maximal, that is (𝑓,𝑓′)=(1). Otherwise it is inseparable.
Proposition 3.17.39
A separable polynomial 𝑓∈𝑘[𝑋] has no multiple roots (and 𝑓 and 𝑓′ have no common roots) in any extension field 𝐾/𝑘.
Proof. Since (𝑓,𝑓′)=(1) we have 𝑎𝑓+𝑏𝑓′=1 for some 𝑎,𝑏∈𝑘[𝑋]. Clearly 𝑓 and 𝑓′ can have no common roots, and therefore 𝑓 has no multiple roots by (3.17.37). ∎
Proposition 3.17.40
Suppose 𝑓,𝑔∈𝑘[𝑋], 𝑔 is separable and 𝑓|𝑔, then 𝑓 is separable.
Proof. Suppose 𝑓 is not separable then by (3.16.5) 𝑓 and 𝑓′ have a common divisor 𝑑 such that deg(𝑑)>0. Since 𝑔=𝑓ℎ, so 𝑔′=𝑓′ℎ+𝑓ℎ′. Therefore 𝑑 is also a non-trivial common divisor of 𝑔 and 𝑔′ contradicting (3.16.5). ∎
