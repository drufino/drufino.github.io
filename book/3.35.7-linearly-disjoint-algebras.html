3.35.7 Linearly Disjoint Algebras

Definition 3.35.52

Let 𝐴/𝑘, 𝐵/𝑘 be 𝑘-algebras and homomorphisms 𝑖:𝐴/𝑘→Ω/𝑘, 𝑗:𝐵/𝑘→Ω/𝑘 (typically being inclusion). Then we say that 𝐴 and 𝐵 are linearly disjoint in Ω if the canonical map

𝐴⊗𝑘𝐵→Ω𝑎⊗𝑏→𝑖(𝑎)𝑗(𝑏)

is injective. As the canonical maps 𝐴,𝐵→𝐴⊗𝑘𝐵 are injective by (3.4.41) this implies 𝑖,𝑗 must be injective. In the case that Ω is integral (resp. reduced) then 𝐴,𝐵 are also necessarily integral (resp. reduced).

Recall that every 𝑘-module is free (3.3.125), moreover by (3.4.23) a basis of 𝐴⊗𝑘𝐵 may be formed by tensor product of bases for 𝐴 and 𝐵.

Example 3.35.53
Let 𝐾/𝑘 be a non-trivial field extension then 𝐾 is not linearly disjoint with itself. For {1,𝑥} a linearly independent subset of 𝐾 which may be extended to a basis. Given the remark on the basis of the tensor product we see 1⊗𝑥≠𝑥⊗1. On the other hand the image of 1⊗𝑥−𝑥⊗1 in Ω is clearly 0. The same consideration shows that 𝐴∩𝐵=𝑘.
Remark 3.35.54
The notion of linear disjointness in general depends on the embeddings in Ω/𝑘. For example 𝑘(𝑡) and 𝑘(𝑠) are linearly disjoint in 𝑘(𝑠,𝑡), but not when both are identified with 𝑘(𝑥).

In cases where both algebras are fields and one is algebraic then linear disjointness can be characterized by a property of the tensor product. In particular it does not depend on the ambient algebra Ω/𝑘.

Lemma 3.35.56
Let 𝐴 be a finite-dimensional 𝑘-algebra with no zero-divisors. Then 𝐴 is a field.
Proof. By assumption multiplication by a non-zero element 𝑥 is injective and 𝑘-linear. Therefore by (3.3.136) it is surjective and 𝑥 has an inverse as required. ∎
Proposition 3.35.57

Let 𝐾/𝑘 be a field extension and 𝑘′/𝑘 an algebraic field extension. Then the following are equivalent

  1. 𝐾 and 𝑘′ are linearly disjoint with respect to every embedding into an algebra Ω/𝑘
  2. 𝐾 and 𝑘′ are linearly disjoint with respect to some common field extension Ω/𝑘
  3. 𝐾⊗𝑘𝑘′ is an integral domain
  4. 𝐾⊗𝑘𝑘′ is a field

Proof. 𝑎)⟹𝑏) We may take Ω≔(𝐾⊗𝑘𝑘′)/𝔪.

𝑏)⟹𝑐) As Ω is assumed to be a field it is certainly integral, and therefore so is 𝐾⊗𝑘𝑘′ being a subring.

𝑐)⟹𝑑) As every element of the tensor product is a finite linear combination of elementary tensors we may reduce to the case 𝑘′ is finitely generated. Then by (3.19.44) 𝑘′ is finite over 𝑘 and therefore by (3.4.23) 𝐾⊗𝑘𝑘′ is finite over 𝐾. By (3.35.56) it is a field.

𝑑)⟹𝑎) This is immediate because the kernel is an ideal which must be (0). ∎
