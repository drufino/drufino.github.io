Lemma 3.35.30
Let 𝐴,𝐵 be 𝑘-algebras. If 𝑎⊗1∈𝐴⊗𝑘𝐵 is a zero-divisor then so is 𝑎.
Proof. Consider a basis {𝑎𝑖}𝑖∈𝐼 for 𝐴 such that 𝑎𝑖0=𝑎. If 𝑎 is not a zero-divisor then we may show directly that {𝑎𝑎𝑖}𝑖∈𝐼 is linearly independent. Then by extending to a basis of 𝐴 we may use (3.4.26) to show that {(𝑎𝑎𝑖)⊗1}𝑖∈𝐼 is 𝐵-linearly independent. We see immediately that 𝑎⊗1 is not a zero-divisor as required. ∎
Lemma 3.35.31
Let 𝐴 be a ring and 𝔞⊂𝑁(𝐴) a locally nilpotent ideal. Then 𝐴 is irreducible iff 𝐴/𝔞 is irreducible.
Lemma 3.35.32

Let 𝐴⊂𝐵 be a subring of an irreducible ring. Then 𝐴 is irreducible.

In particular if 𝐵 is a geometrically irreducible 𝑘-algebra, then so is any subalgebra.

Proof. By (3.22.7) every minimal prime in 𝐴 is the contraction of a minimal prime. As there is precisely one minimal prime of 𝐵 then we are done.

The final statement follows because the canonical map 𝐴⊗𝑘𝐿→𝐵⊗𝑘𝐿 is injective. ∎

Lemma 3.35.33

Let 𝐴,𝐵 be irreducible (resp. integral) 𝑘-algebras with 𝑘 algebraically closed. Then 𝐴⊗𝑘𝐵 is irreducible (resp. integral).

In particular 𝐴 is a geometrically irreducible (resp. integral) 𝑘-algebra.

Proof. Observe that 𝑁(𝐴)⊗𝑘𝐵+𝐴⊗𝑘𝑁(𝐵)⊂𝑁(𝐴⊗𝑘𝐵). For (𝑛⊗𝜆)𝑚=𝑛𝑚⊗𝜆𝑚=0 and we may use (…) to argue any linear combination of such elements is nilpotent. By (…) we have an isomorphism

𝐴/𝑁(𝐴)⊗𝑘𝐵/𝑁(𝐵)≅(𝐴⊗𝑘𝐵)/(𝑁(𝐴)⊗𝑘𝐵+𝐴⊗𝑘𝑁(𝐵))

Then by (3.35.31) 𝐴/𝑁(𝐴)⊗𝑘𝐵/𝑁(𝐵) is irreducible iff 𝐴⊗𝑘𝐵 is. Therefore we may consider only the case 𝐴,𝐵 reduced and therefore integral, and show that 𝐴⊗𝑘𝐵 is integral and a-fortiori irreducible.

If 𝐴⊗𝑘𝐵 were not integral it would contain a non-trivial zero divisor, and this would also be true for 𝐴′⊗𝑘𝐵′ for some finitely generated subalgebras 𝐴′ and 𝐵′. Therefore we may assume wlog that 𝐴 and 𝐵 are finitely generated.

To simplify the argument choose a 𝑘-basis {𝑏𝑖}𝑖∈𝐼 for 𝐵. By (…) {1⊗𝑏𝑖}𝑖∈𝐼 is an 𝐴-basis for 𝐴⊗𝑘𝐵. Therefore if we have a product of elements equal to 0 then may write it as follows

(∑𝑖𝑎𝑖⊗𝑏𝑖)(∑𝑖𝑎𝑖′⊗𝑏𝑖)=0

for some 𝑎𝑖,𝑎𝑖′∈𝐴 all but finitely many zero. Let 𝔪◃𝐴 be a maximal ideal, then by the Weak Nullstellensatz (3.34.23) the structural morphism 𝑘→𝐴/𝔪 is an isomorphism. Reduce the equation to 𝐴/𝔪⊗𝑘𝐵≅𝐵 to find

(∑𝑖𝑎―𝑖𝑏𝑖)(∑𝑖𝑎―𝑖′𝑏𝑖)=0

As 𝐵 is an integral domain, and 𝑏𝑖 are linearly independent we find 𝑎―𝑖,𝑎―𝑖′=0. As 𝔪 was arbitrary we find 𝑎𝑖,𝑎𝑖′∈⋂𝔪 for all 𝑖. By assumption (0) is prime and so by the Strong Nullstellensatz (3.36.3) we find 𝑎𝑖=𝑎𝑖′=0. This shows that 𝐴⊗𝑘𝐵 is integral as required.

The last statement follows because a field 𝐾/𝑘 is always irreducible. ∎

Lemma 3.35.35 (Purely Inseparable Extensions of Scalars are Homeomorphisms)

Let 𝑘 be a field with characteristic exponent 𝑝, 𝐴 a 𝑘-algebra and 𝑘′/𝑘 a purely inseparable field extension. Consider the structural algebra homomorphism

𝜑:𝐴→𝐴⊗𝑘𝑘′

Then 𝜑 induces an order-preserving bijection between prime ideals

𝔮𝑐←𝔮

In particular 𝐴 is irreducible iff 𝐴⊗𝑘𝑘′ is.

Furthermore a purely inseparable extension is geometrically irreducible.

Proof. If 𝑝=1 then 𝑘′/𝑘 is trivial and the statement is obvious. So we may assume wlog that 𝑝>1.

If 𝜑 were surjective then we would have equality 𝔮𝑐𝑒=𝔮 and which would imply the map of prime ideals is injective. However we note that 𝜑 is “almost” surjective, namely for every 𝑧=∑𝑖=1𝑁𝑎𝑖⊗𝜆𝑖 there exists 𝑛≥0 such that 𝑧𝑝𝑛∈𝜑(𝐴). For 𝑘′/𝑘 is purely inseparable so by definition there is some 𝑛≥0 such that 𝜆𝑖𝑝𝑛∈𝑘 for all 𝑖=1…𝑁. In this case 𝑧𝑝𝑛=∑𝑖=1𝑁𝑎𝑖𝑝𝑛𝜆𝑖𝑝𝑛⊗1 which is in the image of 𝐴 as required.

This allows us to argue the map is injective for suppose 𝔭≔𝔮𝑐=(𝔮′)𝑐 and 𝑧∈𝔮∖𝔮′. Then by the previous argument 𝑧𝑝𝑛=𝜑(𝑥)⟹𝑧𝑝𝑛∈𝔮⟹𝑧𝑝𝑛∈𝔮′⟹𝑧∈𝔮′ which is a contradiction. Therefore contraction of prime ideals is injective.

Finally we may observe that 𝜑 is integral (3.23.20) and injective (3.35.2). Therefore it satisfies the lying over property (3.23.17) and so every prime ideal in 𝐴 is contracted and the given map is surjective. ∎

The following result shows it is sufficient to ensure that 𝐴 remains irreducible under a finite separable base change.

Proposition 3.35.36

Let 𝐴 be a 𝑘-algebra. Then the following are equivalent

  1. 𝐴 is geometrically irreducible
  2. 𝐴⊗𝑘𝑘′ is irreducible for every finite separable extension 𝑘′/𝑘
  3. 𝐴⊗𝑘𝑘sep is irreducible
  4. 𝐴⊗𝑘𝑘― is irreducible

Proof. 𝑎)⟹𝑏) immediate

𝑏)⟹𝑐) Suppose 𝔮1,𝔮2 are minimal prime ideals of 𝐴⊗𝑘𝑘sep. Let 𝑘sep/𝑘′/𝑘 be a fixed finite separable extension and consider the subring

𝐴⊗𝑘𝑘′⊂𝐴⊗𝑘𝑘sep

together with the prime ideals 𝔭𝑖≔𝔮𝑖∩(𝐴⊗𝑘𝑘′). By (3.35.34) the prime ideals 𝔭𝑖 are minimal and therefore 𝔭1=𝔭2 by assumption. As 𝑘′/𝑘 was an arbitrary finite subextension we may conclude by (3.35.5) that 𝔮1=𝔮2 and 𝐴⊗𝑘𝑘sep is irreducible.

𝑐)⟹𝑑) By (…) 𝐴⊗𝑘𝑘―≅(𝐴⊗𝑘𝑘sep)⊗𝑘sep𝑘― and so is irreducible by (3.35.35), since 𝑘―/𝑘sep is purely inseparable (3.18.98).

𝑑)⟹𝑎) Let 𝐾/𝑘 be a field extension then we may identify 𝑘― and 𝐾 as subextensions of 𝐾―/𝑘. Then 𝐴⊗𝑘𝐾―≅(𝐴⊗𝑘𝑘―)⊗𝑘―𝐾―. By (3.35.33) 𝐴⊗𝑘𝐾― is irreducible. Finally from (3.35.34) we see that 𝐴⊗𝑘𝐾 is irreducible as required. ∎

Proposition 3.35.37
Let 𝐴 be a geometrically irreducible 𝐾-algebra and 𝐾/𝑘 a geometrically irreducible field extension. Then 𝐴/𝑘 is geometrically irreducible.

Proof. It is sufficient by (3.35.36) to consider base change by a finite separable extension 𝑘′/𝑘. Observe by transitivity

𝐴⊗𝑘𝑘′≅𝐴⊗𝐾(𝐾⊗𝑘𝑘′)

so it would be sufficient to show that 𝐾⊗𝑘𝑘′ is a field. By hypothesis 𝐾⊗𝑘𝑘′ is irreducible. Furthermore 𝑘′/𝑘 is geometrically reduced (3.35.18) whence 𝐾⊗𝑘𝑘′ is reduced and therefore an integral domain. Using the fact 𝑘′/𝑘 is algebraic we may conclude from (3.34.57) that 𝐾⊗𝑘𝑘′ is a field, and we are done. ∎

Proposition 3.35.38 (Primary Extension)

Let 𝐾/𝑘 be a field extension. Then 𝐾/𝑘 is geometrically irreducible if and only if it is relatively separably closed.

In this case the subfield of algebraic elements is purely inseparable over 𝑘. In particular an algebraic extension is geometrically irreducible if and only if it is purely inseparable.

We say such an extension is primary.

Proof. Suppose that 𝐾/𝑘 is relatively separably closed. By (3.35.36) it is enough to show that 𝐾⊗𝑘𝑘′ is irreducible for every finite separable extension 𝑘′/𝑘.

Let 𝐾/𝐾′/𝑘 be the relative algebraic closure then 𝐾/𝐾′ is relatively algebraically closed (3.18.46) and 𝐾′/𝑘 is purely inseparable because 𝐾𝑠=𝑘 by hypothesis (3.18.96). Observe 𝐾′/𝑘 is geometrically irreducible (3.35.35) so by (3.35.37) it is enough to show that 𝐾/𝐾′ is geometrically irreducible. This reduces to the case that 𝐾/𝑘 is relatively algebraically closed.

As before it is sufficient to consider base change by a finite separable extension 𝑘′/𝑘. By (3.18.77) 𝑘′=𝑘(𝛼) is simple. Let 𝑚(𝑋) be the minimal polynomial for 𝛼 then 𝑘(𝛼)≅𝑘[𝑋]/(𝑚). From (3.35.39) 𝑚(𝑋) remains irreducible in 𝐾[𝑋] and therefore (using 𝑘(𝛼)⊗𝑘𝐾≅(𝑘[𝑋]⊗𝑘𝐾)/(𝑚) as 𝐾-algebras, (3.4.42), and 𝑘[𝑋]⊗𝑘𝐾≅𝐾[𝑋], (3.7.8))

𝐾⊗𝑘𝑘(𝛼)≅𝐾[𝑋]/(𝑚)

is a field and in particular irreducible.

Conversely suppose that 𝐾/𝑘 is geometrically irreducible and 𝛼∈𝐾 is separable. Then 𝑘(𝛼) is geometrically irreducible by (3.35.32). Therefore, using 𝑘(𝛼)⊗𝑘𝑘―≅(𝑘[𝑋]⊗𝑘𝑘―)/(𝑚𝛼) as 𝑘―-algebras (3.4.42), then 𝑘[𝑋]⊗𝑘𝑘―≅𝑘―[𝑋] (3.7.8), then by the Chinese Remainder Theorem (3.3.62)

𝑘(𝛼)⊗𝑘𝑘―≅𝑘[𝑋]/(𝑚𝛼)⊗𝑘𝑘―≅𝑘―[𝑋]/(𝑚𝛼)≅𝑘―[𝑋]/(𝑋−𝛼1)𝑛1×⋯×𝑘―[𝑋]/(𝑋−𝛼𝑟)𝑛𝑟

is irreducible where 𝛼1,…,𝛼𝑟 are the distinct roots in 𝑘―. This implies 𝑟=1 and therefore 𝛼 is purely inseparable (3.18.88). Being both separable and purely inseparable 𝛼∈𝑘 (3.18.89) as required. ∎

Lemma 3.35.39
Let 𝐾/𝑘 be a field extension such that 𝑘 is algebraically closed in 𝐾. Then every irreducible polynomial 𝑓∈𝑘[𝑋] remains irreducible in 𝐾[𝑋].
Proof. Consider the extension 𝐾― which contains 𝑘―. And suppose 𝑔∣𝑓 in 𝐾[𝑋]. As 𝑓 splits completely in 𝑘― then by unique factorisation in 𝐾―[𝑋] the coefficients of 𝑔 lie in 𝐾∩𝑘―=𝑘. Therefore by irreducibility 𝑔=𝑓 or is constant. In particular 𝑓 is irreducible in 𝐾[𝑋]. ∎
Proposition 3.35.40
Let 𝐴 be an integral 𝑘-algebra and 𝐾≔Frac(𝐴). Then 𝐴 is geometrically irreducible iff 𝐾 is geometrically irreducible.

Proof. Suppose 𝐾 is geometrically irreducible and consider an arbitrary field extension 𝐿/𝑘. Then we have an embedding 𝐴⊗𝑘𝐿↪︎𝐾⊗𝑘𝐿, and therefore an embedding (𝐴⊗𝑘𝐿)red↪︎(𝐾⊗𝑘𝐿)red. By assumption these are integral and we conclude that (𝐴⊗𝑘𝐿) is irreducible.

Conversely suppose 𝐴⊗𝑘𝐿 is irreducible then define Ω≔Frac((𝐴⊗𝑘𝐿)red). As 𝐿↪︎(𝐴⊗𝑘𝐿) is injective and 𝐿 is reduced we have a canonical map 𝐿↪︎Ω. Similarly we have a canonical map 𝐴↪︎Ω and therefore a map 𝐾↪︎Ω. Therefore we have a well-defined ring homomorphism

𝜓:𝐾⊗𝑘𝐿→Ω𝑎𝑏−1⊗𝜆→(𝑎⊗𝜆)⋅(𝑏⊗1)−1

We must show that if 𝑥∈ker(𝜓) then 𝑥 is nilpotent. By definition

𝑥=∑𝑖=1𝑛𝜆𝑖⊗𝜇𝑖

We may find 𝑎∈𝐴 such that 𝑦=𝑎𝑥 is of the form

𝑦=∑𝑖=1𝑛𝑎𝑖⊗𝜇𝑖∈𝐴⊗𝑘𝐿

We observe 𝑦∈ker(𝐴⊗𝑘𝐿→Ω) whence 𝑦, and therefore 𝑥, is nilpotent. This means we have an embedding

(𝐾⊗𝑘𝐿)red↪︎Ω

which means (𝐾⊗𝑘𝐿)red is integral and therefore 𝐾⊗𝑘𝐿 is irreducible. ∎

Proposition 3.35.41

Let 𝐴 be an integral 𝑘-algebra and 𝐾≔Frac(𝐴). Then the following are equivalent

  1. 𝐴 is geometrically irreducible
  2. 𝐴⊗𝑘𝑘′ is irreducible for every finite separable extension 𝑘′/𝑘
