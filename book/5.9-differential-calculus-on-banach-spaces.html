5.9 Differential Calculus on Banach Spaces

The main reference for this section is [Car71], along with [Lan12], in a generality which is almost certainly not required for the purposes of these notes.

5.9.1 Total Derivatives

Definition 5.9.1

Let 𝑈⊂𝑋 be an open neighbourhood of 0 and 𝜑:𝑈→𝑌 a function. We say 𝜑 is sublinear if

∀𝜀>0∃𝛿s.t.‖ℎ‖<𝛿⟹(ℎ∈𝑈)∧(‖𝜑(ℎ)‖<𝜀‖ℎ‖)

Note sublinear functions are closed under linear combinations, and the property is unchanged under equivalent norms.

Definition 5.9.2 (Total Derivative)

Let 𝑈⊂𝑋 be an open subset of a Banach space, 𝑌 a Banach space and 𝑥∈𝑈. We say that a map 𝜑:𝑈→𝑌 is differentiable at 𝑥 if there exists a continuous linear map 𝐷𝜑(𝑥)∈𝐿(𝑋,𝑌) (derivative at 𝑥) such that

𝜑(𝑥+ℎ)−𝜑(𝑥)−𝐷𝜑(𝑥)(ℎ)is sublinear inℎ

We say it is simply differentiable if this holds for every point 𝑥∈𝑈 and write 𝐷𝜑 for the corresponding function 𝑈→𝐿(𝑋,𝑌).

If 𝐷𝜑 is also continuous then we say that 𝜑 is continuously differentiable or 𝐶1.

The following result shows that the total derivative is necessarily unique.

Proposition 5.9.3
Let 𝛼:𝑋→𝑌 be a linear map which is also sublinear. Then 𝛼=0.
Proof. Consider 𝑥∈𝑋 then for every 𝜀>0 there exists 𝛿 such that ‖ℎ‖<𝛿⟹‖𝛼(ℎ)‖<𝜀‖ℎ‖. Define 𝜆≔𝛿2‖𝑥‖. Then evidently ‖𝛼(𝜆𝑥)‖<𝜀‖𝜆𝑥‖ whence ‖𝛼(𝑥)‖<𝜀‖𝑥‖. As this holds for every 𝜀 we see ‖𝛼(𝑥)‖=0 and 𝛼(𝑥)=0. ∎
Proposition 5.9.4

Let 𝑓:𝑈→𝑌 be a differentiable map and 𝛼:𝑌→𝑍 a linear map. Then

𝐷(𝛼∘𝑓)(𝑥)=𝛼∘𝐷𝑓(𝑥)
Proposition 5.9.5

Let 𝑈⊂𝐾 an open set and 𝜑:𝑈→𝑌 a function. Then the following are equivalent

  1. 𝜑 is (continuously) differentiable in the sense of (5.9.2)
  2. 𝜑 is (continuously) differentiable in the sense of (5.5.1)

Furthermore we may identify the derivatives as follows

𝐷𝜑(𝑡)(𝜆)=𝜑′(𝑡)𝜆
Proposition 5.9.6 (Chain Rule)

Let 𝑈⊂𝑋 and 𝑉⊂𝑌 be open sets and 𝜑:𝑈→𝑌 and 𝜓:𝑉→𝑍 (continuously) differentiable functions such that 𝜑(𝑈)⊂𝑉. Then 𝜓∘𝜑 is (continuously) differentiable and

(𝐷(𝜓∘𝜑))(𝑥)=(𝐷𝜓)(𝜑(𝑥))∘(𝐷𝜑)(𝑥)

In the case 𝑋=𝐾 we have

(𝜓∘𝜑)′(𝑡)=(𝐷𝜓)(𝜑(𝑡))(𝜑′(𝑡))
Proposition 5.9.7 (Product Rule I)

Let 𝑈⊂𝑋 and 𝑉⊂𝑌 open sets, 𝜑:𝑈→𝑋′, 𝜓:𝑉→𝑌′ (continuously) differentiable maps and Ψ:𝑋′×𝑌′→𝑍 a 𝐶1 bilinear map. Then define

(𝜑×Ψ𝜓)(𝑥,𝑦)≔Ψ(𝜑(𝑥),𝜓(𝑦))

is (continuously) differentiable with derivative

𝐷(𝜑×Ψ𝜓)(𝑥,𝑦)(ℎ,𝑘)=Ψ(𝐷𝜑(𝑥)(ℎ),𝜓(𝑦))+Ψ(𝜑(𝑥),𝐷𝜓(𝑦)(𝑘))
Corollary 5.9.8

Let 𝑋,𝑌 be normed vector spaces, 𝑈⊂𝑋 an open subset and 𝜑,𝜓:𝑋→𝑌 (continuously) differentiable at 𝑥. Then both 𝜑+𝜓 and 𝜆𝜑 are (continuously) differentiable at 𝑥 and

𝐷(𝜑+𝜓)(𝑥)=𝐷(𝜑)(𝑥)+𝐷(𝜓)(𝑥)𝐷(𝜆𝜑)(𝑥)=𝜆𝐷(𝜑)(𝑥)

If 𝑌 is also a Banach algebra then 𝜑⋅𝜓 is (continuously) differentiable at 𝑥 and

𝐷(𝜑⋅𝜓)(𝑥)(ℎ)=𝐷(𝜑)(𝑥)(ℎ)⋅𝜓(𝑥)+𝜑(𝑥)⋅𝐷(𝜓)(𝑥)(ℎ)
Corollary 5.9.9 (Product Rule II)

Let 𝑈⊂𝑋 be an open set, and 𝜑:𝑈→𝐾 and 𝜓:𝑈→𝑌 differentiable maps. Then

𝐷(𝜑⋅𝜓)(𝑥)(ℎ)=𝐷𝜑(𝑥)(ℎ)𝜓(𝑥)+𝜑(𝑥)𝐷𝜓(𝑥)(ℎ)

If 𝜑,𝜓 are 𝐶1 then so is 𝜑⋅𝜓.

Definition 5.9.10 (Higher-Order Derivatives)

Let 𝑓:𝑈→𝑌 we say that 𝑓 is 𝑛-times continuously differentiable (or 𝐶𝑛) if it is differentiable and 𝐷𝑓 is 𝐶𝑛−1 (where 𝐶0 means simply continuous). In this case for 𝑘≤𝑛 we may define the 𝑘-fold derivative inductively

𝐷𝑘𝑓≔𝐷(𝐷𝑘−1𝑓):𝑈⟶𝐿(𝑋,⋯,𝐿(𝑋,𝑌))

which is by definition continuous.

Proposition 5.9.11

Let 𝑈⊂𝐾 be an open subset and 𝑓:𝑈→𝑌 a function. Then 𝑓 is 𝑛-times differentiable in the sense of (…) if and only if it is 𝑛-times differentiable in the sense of (5.9.10). In this case

(𝐷𝑛𝑓)(𝑥)(ℎ1,⋯,ℎ𝑛)=𝑓(𝑛)(𝑥)⋅ℎ1⋯ℎ𝑛
Proposition 5.9.12 (Mean Value Theorem)

Let 𝑈⊂𝑋 be an open subset and 𝑓:𝑈→𝑌 a 𝐶1 map. Suppose that ℎ∈𝐵(𝑥;𝑟)⊂𝑈. Then

𝑓(𝑥+ℎ)=𝑓(𝑥)+∫01𝐷𝑓(𝑥+𝑡ℎ)(ℎ)d𝑡
Proof. Let 𝐺(𝑡)≔𝑓(𝑥+𝑡ℎ) be defined as a function on [0,1]→𝑌. Then by (5.9.6) 𝐺′(𝑡)=𝐷𝑓(𝑥+𝑡ℎ)(ℎ) is continuous, because evidently 𝑡→𝑥+𝑡ℎ is continuously differentiable with scalar derivative ℎ. The result follows by applying (5.8.20) to 𝐺(𝑡). ∎

5.9.2 Partial Derivatives

Lemma 5.9.13

Let 𝑋1,⋯,𝑋𝑛 be Banach spaces and for every 𝑥∈𝑋 define the injection

𝜆𝑗(𝑥):𝑋𝑗⟶𝑋1×⋯×𝑋𝑛𝑥′→(𝑥1,⋯,𝑥𝑗−1,𝑥′,𝑥𝑗+1,⋯,𝑥𝑛)

Then 𝜆𝑗 is differentiable at every 𝑥𝑗∈𝑋𝑗 and (𝐷𝜆𝑗(𝑥))(𝑥𝑗)=𝜇𝑗 where

𝜇𝑗(ℎ)=(0,⋯,ℎ,⋯,0)
Proof. We have 𝜆𝑗(𝑥)(𝑥′+ℎ′)−𝜆𝑗(𝑥)=𝜇𝑗(ℎ′) and 𝜇𝑗 is linear. ∎
Proposition 5.9.14 (Criteria for Total Differentiability)

Let 𝑋1,⋯,𝑋𝑛,𝑌 be Banach spaces and 𝑈⊂𝑋≔𝑋1×⋯×𝑋𝑛 an open subset. Consider a continuous map

𝜑:𝑈→𝑌

The following are equivalent

  1. The map 𝜑 is continuously differentiable (with respect to the sup norm on the product)

  2. For every 𝑥∈𝑈 define 𝑉𝑗≔𝜆𝑗(𝑥)−1(𝑈)⊂𝑋𝑗 an open neighbourhood of 𝑥𝑗

    1. The map

      𝜑𝑗(𝑥)≔𝜑∘𝜆𝑗(𝑥):𝑉𝑗→𝑌

      is differentiable for all 𝑗=1⋯𝑛. Denote the 𝑗-th partial derivative at 𝑥∈𝑈 by

      𝐷𝑗𝜑:𝑈→𝐿(𝑋𝑗,𝑌)𝑥𝑗→ℎ→(𝐷𝜑𝑗(𝑥))(𝑥𝑗)(ℎ)
    2. The map 𝐷𝑗 is continuous for all 𝑗=1⋯𝑛

Furthermore in this case

𝐷𝜑(𝑥)(ℎ)=∑𝑗=1𝑛(𝐷𝑗𝜑)(𝑥)(ℎ𝑗)

Proof. a) ⟹ b) By the chain rule (5.9.6) and (5.9.13) we have 𝜑𝑗 is differentiable and

(𝐷𝜑𝑗(𝑥))(𝑥𝑗)=(𝐷𝜑)(𝑥)∘(𝐷𝜆𝑗(𝑥))(𝑥𝑗)=(𝐷𝜑)(𝑥)∘𝜇𝑗

b) ⟹ a) We consider the case 𝑛=2. Observe

𝜑(𝑥1+ℎ1,𝑥2+ℎ2)−𝜑(𝑥1,𝑥2)=𝜑(𝑥1+ℎ1,𝑥2+ℎ2)−𝜑(𝑥1+ℎ1,𝑥2)+𝜑(𝑥1+ℎ1,𝑥2)−𝜑(𝑥1,𝑥2)=∫01𝐷2𝜑(𝑥1+ℎ1,𝑥2+𝑡ℎ2)(ℎ2)d𝑡+∫01𝐷1𝜑(𝑥1+𝑡ℎ1,𝑥2)(ℎ1)d𝑡=𝐷2𝜑(𝑥1,𝑥2)(ℎ2)+𝐷1𝜑(𝑥1,𝑥2)(ℎ1)+∫01[𝐷2𝜑(𝑥1+ℎ1,𝑥2+𝑡ℎ2)(ℎ2)−𝐷2𝜑(𝑥1,𝑥2)(ℎ2)]d𝑡+∫01[𝐷1𝜑(𝑥1+𝑡ℎ1,𝑥2)(ℎ1)−𝐷1𝜑(𝑥1,𝑥2)(ℎ1)]d𝑡

Then

‖∫01[𝐷2𝜑(𝑥1+ℎ1,𝑥2+𝑡ℎ2)(ℎ2)−𝐷2𝜑(𝑥1,𝑥2)(ℎ2)]d𝑡‖≤∫01‖𝐷2𝜑(𝑥1+ℎ1,𝑥2+𝑡ℎ2)(ℎ2)−𝐷2𝜑(𝑥1,𝑥2)(ℎ2)‖d𝑡≤∫01‖𝐷2𝜑(𝑥1+ℎ1,𝑥2+𝑡ℎ2)−𝐷2𝜑(𝑥1,𝑥2)‖‖ℎ2‖d𝑡≤∫01‖𝐷2𝜑‖‖(ℎ1,𝑡ℎ2)‖‖ℎ2‖d𝑡≤‖𝐷2𝜑‖‖ℎ‖2

and similarly for the second integral. Therefore 𝜑 is differentiable at (𝑥1,𝑥2) with derivative

𝐷𝜑(𝑥1,𝑥2)(ℎ)=𝐷1𝜑(𝑥1,𝑥2)(ℎ1)+𝐷2𝜑(𝑥1,𝑥2)(ℎ2)

as required.

We have ℎ=∑𝑗=1𝑛𝜇𝑗(ℎ𝑗) and so by the first part

𝐷𝜑(𝑥)(ℎ)=∑𝑗=1𝑛(𝐷𝜑(𝑥))(𝜇𝑗(ℎ𝑗))=∑𝑗=1𝑛(𝐷𝜑𝑗(𝑥))(𝑥𝑗)(ℎ𝑗)=∑𝑗=1𝑛(𝐷𝑗𝜑)(𝑥)(ℎ𝑗)

 ∎

Proposition 5.9.15

5.9.3 Second Derivative

Definition 5.9.18

For a map 𝛼:𝑋×𝑋→𝑌 we say that 𝛼(𝑣,𝑤) is sublinear if for all 𝜀>0 there exists 𝛿>0 such that

‖(𝑣,𝑤)‖<𝛿⟹‖𝛼(𝑣,𝑤)‖<𝜀‖𝑣‖‖𝑤‖

where the norm on 𝑋×𝑋 is the product norm.

As before we have the following uniqueness property

Lemma 5.9.19
Let 𝛼:𝑋×𝑋→𝑌 be a bilinear map which is also sublinear. Then 𝛼=0.
Proposition 5.9.20 (Second derivative is symmetric)

Let 𝑓:𝑈→𝑌 be a 𝐶2 map. Then for all 𝑥∈𝑈 we have 𝐷2𝑓(𝑥) is the unique bilinear map 𝑋×𝑋→𝑌 such that

𝑓(𝑥+𝑣+𝑤)−𝑓(𝑥+𝑤)−𝑓(𝑥+𝑣)+𝑓(𝑥)−𝐷2𝑓(𝑥)(𝑣,𝑤)is sublinear

𝑔(𝑥)≔𝑓(𝑥+𝑣)−𝑓(𝑥)

By (…) applied to 𝑔 and 𝐷𝑓 we find

𝑔(𝑥+𝑤)−𝑔(𝑥)=∫01[𝐷𝑓(𝑥+𝑣+𝑡𝑤)(𝑤)−𝐷𝑓(𝑥+𝑡𝑤)(𝑤)]d𝑡=∫01∫01𝐷2𝑓(𝑥+𝑠𝑣+𝑡𝑤)(𝑣)d𝑠(𝑤)d𝑡=𝐷2𝑓(𝑥)(𝑣,𝑤)+∫01∫01[𝐷2𝑓(𝑥+𝑠𝑣+𝑡𝑤)(𝑣)−𝐷2𝑓(𝑥)(𝑣)]d𝑠(𝑤)d𝑡

Let 𝜓(𝑣,𝑤) denote the integral then

‖𝜓(𝑣,𝑤)‖≤∫01∫01‖𝐷2𝑓(𝑥+𝑠𝑣+𝑡𝑤)(𝑣)−𝐷2𝑓(𝑥)(𝑣)‖d𝑠‖𝑤‖d𝑡≤∫01∫01‖𝐷2𝑓(𝑥+𝑠𝑣+𝑡𝑤)−𝐷2𝑓(𝑥)‖d𝑠d𝑡⋅‖𝑣‖‖𝑤‖≤sup0≤𝑠,𝑡≤1‖𝐷2𝑓(𝑥+𝑠𝑣+𝑡𝑤)−𝐷2𝑓(𝑥)‖‖𝑣‖‖𝑤‖

Proposition 5.9.21 (Symmetry of partial derivatives)

Let 𝑋=𝑋1×⋯×𝑋𝑛, 𝜑:𝑈→𝑌 be a 𝐶2 map and 𝑥∈𝑈. Then

Proof. The first equation follows from (5.9.14) and the second from (5.9.20). ∎
Proposition 5.9.22 (Hessian Matrix)

5.9.4 Differentiation under Integral Sign

Proposition 5.9.23
