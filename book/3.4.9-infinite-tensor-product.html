3.4.9 Infinite Tensor Product

Proposition 3.4.49 (Directed Colimits of Commutative Algebras)

Let 𝐼 be a directed preorder (2.6.124), 𝐴 a commutative ring and 𝐹 a diagram of commutative 𝐴-algebras indexed by 𝐼, with transition maps 𝐹(𝑖→𝑗) for 𝑖≺𝑗. Let 𝐿≔lim⟶𝑖∈𝐼𝐹(𝑖) be the direct limit of the underlying sets (2.6.129), with 𝜇𝑖:𝐹(𝑖)→𝐿.

Then 𝐿 has a unique structure of commutative 𝐴-algebra for which every 𝜇𝑖 is an 𝐴-algebra homomorphism. Explicitly, for 𝑥,𝑦∈𝐹(𝑖) and 𝑎∈𝐴

[(𝑖,𝑥)]+[(𝑖,𝑦)]=[(𝑖,𝑥+𝑦)],[(𝑖,𝑥)][(𝑖,𝑦)]=[(𝑖,𝑥𝑦)],𝑎[(𝑖,𝑥)]=[(𝑖,𝑎𝑥)],

with 0=[(𝑖,0)] and 1=[(𝑖,1)]. Furthermore (𝐿,{𝜇𝑖}) is a direct limit of 𝐹 in the category of commutative 𝐴-algebras, that is for every cocone of 𝐴-algebra homomorphisms 𝜇𝑖′:𝐹(𝑖)→𝐿′ the unique map 𝛼:𝐿→𝐿′ with 𝛼∘𝜇𝑖=𝜇𝑖′ of (2.6.129) is an 𝐴-algebra homomorphism.

Moreover 𝐿=0 if and only if 𝐹(𝑖)=0 for some 𝑖∈𝐼, and if all the transition maps 𝐹(𝑖→𝑗) are injective then so is every 𝜇𝑖.

Definition 3.4.50 (Infinite Tensor Product)

Let (𝐵𝑖)𝑖∈𝐼 be a family of commutative 𝐴-algebras. The finite subsets 𝐽⊆𝐼 ordered by inclusion form a directed preorder. By (3.4.43) the finite tensor products ⨂𝑖∈𝐽𝐵𝑖 for finite 𝐽⊆𝐼, with the homomorphisms ⨂𝑖∈𝐽𝐵𝑖→⨂𝑖∈𝐽′𝐵𝑖, 𝑏↦𝑏⊗1 for 𝐽⊆𝐽′, form a diagram of commutative 𝐴-algebras. We define the tensor product of the family to be the directed colimit (3.4.49)

⨂𝑖∈𝐼𝐵𝑖≔lim⟶𝐽⊆𝐼finite⨂𝑖∈𝐽𝐵𝑖.

For 𝑗∈𝐼 let 𝑖𝑗:𝐵𝑗→⨂𝑖∈𝐼𝐵𝑖 be the composite of 𝐵𝑗=⨂𝑖∈{𝑗}𝐵𝑖 with the canonical map to the colimit, written 𝑏↦𝑏⊗1.

Proposition 3.4.51 (Infinite Tensor Product is Coproduct)

Let (𝐵𝑖)𝑖∈𝐼 be a family of commutative 𝐴-algebras. The 𝐴-algebra homomorphisms 𝑖𝑗:𝐵𝑗→⨂𝑖∈𝐼𝐵𝑖 are natural in 𝐵𝑗, and ⨂𝑖∈𝐼𝐵𝑖 is generated as an 𝐴-module by the pure tensors ⨂𝑖𝑏𝑖 with 𝑏𝑖=1 for all but finitely many 𝑖.

Furthermore for 𝑍 an 𝐴-algebra there is a bijection

AlgHom𝐴(⨂𝑖∈𝐼𝐵𝑖,𝑍)≅∏𝑖∈𝐼AlgHom𝐴(𝐵𝑖,𝑍)𝜓→(𝜓∘𝑖𝑖)𝑖∈𝐼

which is natural in 𝑍.

If 𝐼 is finite this is the finite tensor product (3.4.43).

Proposition 3.4.52 (Infinite Tensor Product over a Field)
Let 𝑘 be a field and (𝐵𝑖)𝑖∈𝐼 a family of non-zero commutative 𝑘-algebras. Then each 𝑖𝑗:𝐵𝑗→⨂𝑖∈𝐼𝐵𝑖 is injective. In particular ⨂𝑖∈𝐼𝐵𝑖≠0.
