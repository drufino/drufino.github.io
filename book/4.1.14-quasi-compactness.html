4.1.14 Quasi-Compactness

Definition 4.1.100 (Cluster Point of a Sequence)

Let (𝑥𝑛) be a sequence in a topological space 𝑋. We say that 𝑥∈𝑋 is

  1. a cluster point (or accumulation point) of 𝑥𝑛 if for every 𝑈∈𝒪︀𝑥 the set {𝑚∈ℕ|𝑥𝑚∈𝑈} is infinite
  2. a subsequential limit of 𝑥𝑛 if there exists some subsequence 𝑥𝑛𝑘→𝑥

In general the latter implies the former, and if 𝑋 is first countable the concepts are equivalent as we will see.

Lemma 4.1.101 (Cluster Point is Subsequential Limit Point)
Let 𝑋 be a first countable topological space and 𝑥 a cluster point of a sequence (𝑥𝑛). Then there exists some subsequence 𝑥𝑛𝑘 such that 𝑥𝑛𝑘→𝑥.

Proof. Let (𝑈𝑛) be a local base at 𝑥. By replacing it with 𝑈𝑛′≔𝑈1∩…∩𝑈𝑛 we may assume it is without loss of generality decreasing. Define 𝑛0=0 and 𝑛𝑘 inductively by

𝑛𝑘≔min{𝑚>𝑛𝑘−1|𝑥𝑚∈𝑈𝑘}

which is well-defined precisely because 𝑥 is a cluster point. Then 𝑥𝑛𝑘∈𝑈𝑘 which shows that 𝑥𝑛𝑘→𝑥 as required. ∎

Definition 4.1.102

Let 𝑋 be a topological space. We say it is

  • Quasi-Compact if every open cover of 𝑋 has a finite subcover
  • Countably Quasi-Compact if every countable open cover of 𝑋 has a finite subcover
  • Sequentially Quasi-Compact if every sequence (𝑥𝑛) in 𝑋 has a convergent subsequence

If in addition 𝑋 is Hausdorff (e.g. a metric space) we say that 𝑋 is compact (resp. countably compact, sequentially compact).

Proposition 4.1.103 (Criteria for Countable Compactness)

Let 𝑋 be a topological space. Then the following are equivalent

  1. 𝑋 is countably quasi-compact
  2. Let {𝑍𝑛}𝑛∈ℕ be a countable collection of closed subsets such that every finite set has non-empty intersection. Then the intersection ⋂𝑛∈ℕ𝑍𝑛 is non-empty

  3. For every increasing sequence of proper open subsets

    𝑈1⊆𝑈2⊆…⊆𝑈𝑛⊆…

    the union ⋃𝑛=1∞𝑈𝑛 is proper.

  4. For every decreasing sequence of non-empty closed subsets

    𝑍1⊇𝑍2⊇…⊇𝑍𝑛⊇…

    the intersection ⋂𝑛=1∞𝑍𝑛 is non-empty

Proof. 𝑎)⟺𝑏) and 𝑐)⟺𝑑) are simple consequences of De Morgan’s laws. For 𝑎)⟹𝑐) suppose ⋃𝑛=1∞𝑈𝑛=𝑋 then by assumption 𝑈𝑛1∪…∪𝑈𝑛𝑘=𝑋, and by the increasing assumption 𝑈𝑛𝑘=𝑋 which is a contradiction.

For 𝑐)⟹𝑎), suppose we have an open cover 𝑋=⋃𝑛=1∞𝑈𝑛 define the increasing open cover

𝑈𝑛′≔⋃𝑖=1𝑛𝑈𝑖

by the contrapositive 𝑈𝑛′ is not proper for some 𝑛, which means precisely that there is a finite subcover. ∎

Proposition 4.1.104
Let 𝑋 be a topological space. Then sequentially quasi-compact ⟹ countably quasi-compact. Under the assumption of first countability the converse holds.

Proof. ⟹) Consider a countable open cover 𝑋=⋃𝑛=1∞𝑈𝑛 with no finite subcover. Then there exists 𝑥𝑛∈𝑋∖⋃𝑖=1𝑛𝑈𝑖. By assumption there is a convergent subsequence 𝑥𝑛𝑘→𝑥. We have 𝑥∈𝑈𝑁 for some 𝑁 but 𝑛𝑘≥𝑁⟹𝑥𝑛𝑘∉𝑈𝑁.

⟸) Consider a sequence (𝑥𝑛) and define

𝑍𝑛≔{𝑥𝑘:𝑘>𝑛}―

which is a decreasing sequence of non-empty closed subsets. By the countable compactness criteria (4.1.103) 𝑍≔⋂𝑛=1∞𝑍𝑛 is non-empty. However we may show this is precisely the set of cluster points of (𝑥𝑛), i.e. there exists a cluster point 𝑥∈𝑍. By (4.1.101) there exists a subsequence such that 𝑥𝑛𝑘→𝑥 as required. ∎

Lemma 4.1.105 (Lindelof Lemma)
Let 𝑋 be a second countable topological space. Then every open cover has a countable subcover.
Proof. Let ℬ︀ be a countable base and {𝑈𝛼}𝛼∈𝒜︀ an open cover of 𝑋. Note for every 𝑈𝛼 we have some subfamily ℬ︀𝛼⊂ℬ︀ such that 𝑈𝛼=⋃ℬ︀𝛼. Consider ℬ︀′≔⋃{𝐵𝛼|𝛼∈𝒜︀}⊂ℬ︀. By definition for every 𝑉∈ℬ︀′ we have 𝑉⊂𝑈𝛼 for some 𝛼∈𝒜︀. In other words there exists a map 𝑓:ℬ︀′→𝒜︀ such that 𝑉⊂𝑈𝑓(𝑉). We may verify that {𝑈𝛼}𝛼∈Im(𝑓) is a countable subcover. ∎

Proposition 4.1.107 (Compact subsets of Hausdorff Spaces are Closed)
Let 𝑋 be a Hausdorff topological space and 𝑌⊂𝑋 a subset compact with respect to the subspace topology. Then 𝑌 is closed.

Proof. Fix 𝑥∈𝑋∖𝑌. Then for every 𝑦∈𝑌 by assumption there exists disjoint open subsets 𝑥∈𝑈𝑦 and 𝑦∈𝑉𝑦. Then {𝑉𝑦∩𝑌|𝑦∈𝑌} is an open cover for 𝑌, so there exists a finite set of points 𝑦1,…,𝑦𝑛 such that 𝑌⊆𝑉𝑦1∪…∪𝑉𝑦𝑛. Furthermore

𝑈≔𝑈𝑦1∩…∩𝑈𝑦𝑛

is an open neighbourhood of 𝑥 disjoint from each 𝑉𝑦𝑖 and therefore disjoint from 𝑌. By (4.1.19) we see that 𝑥∉𝑌―, which shows 𝑌=𝑌―, i.e. 𝑌 is closed. ∎

Proposition 4.1.108 (Closed subsets of Compact Spaces are Compact)
Let 𝑋 be a quasi-compact topological space and 𝑌⊂𝑋 a closed subset. Then 𝑌 is quasi-compact under the subspace topology.

Proof. Let 𝒰︀≔{𝑈𝛼}𝛼∈𝒜︀ be an open cover for 𝑌. Then by definition 𝑈𝛼=𝑈𝛼′∩𝑌, where 𝑈𝛼′⊂𝑋 are open. Evidently

𝒰︀′:{𝑈𝛼′}𝛼∈𝒜︀∪{𝑋∖𝑌}

is an open cover of 𝑋. Therefore by assumption it has a finite subcover, which immediately yields a finite subcover of 𝑌 as required. ∎

Proposition 4.1.109 (Image of a Compact Space is Compact)

Let 𝑓:𝑋→𝑌 be a continuous map of topological spaces where 𝑋 is quasi-compact. Then the image 𝑓(𝑋) is quasi-compact.

Further if 𝑌 is Hausdorff then 𝑓 is an open map (i.e. 𝑓(𝑈) is open for every open 𝑈⊂𝑋). If in addition 𝑓 is injective then it is a homeomorphism onto its image.

Proof. We may assume without loss of generality that 𝑓(𝑋)=𝑌. Let {𝑈𝛼}𝛼∈𝒜︀ be an open cover of 𝑌. In other words 𝑓(𝑋)⊆⋃𝛼∈𝒜︀𝑈𝛼⟹𝑋⊆⋃𝛼∈𝒜︀𝑓−1(𝑈𝛼). By assumption we have a finite subcover

𝑋⊆𝑓−1(𝑈𝛼1)∪…∪𝑓−1(𝑈𝛼𝑛)

which implies

𝑓(𝑋)⊆𝑈𝛼1∪…∪𝑈𝛼𝑛

since 𝑓 is assumed to be surjective, and we see that 𝑓(𝑋) is compact. ∎

Analogously we have similar results for sequentially compact spaces, which may be conceptually simpler.

Proposition 4.1.110
Let 𝑋 be a first countable Kolmogorov space and 𝑌⊂𝑋 a subset of a sequentially compact with respect to the subspace topology. Then 𝑌 is closed.
Proof. Consider 𝑥∈𝑌― then by (4.1.19) there is some sequence 𝑥𝑛→𝑥. Further by assumption there is some subsequence 𝑥𝑛𝑘→𝑦∈𝑌. By uniqueness of limits (4.1.51) we have 𝑥=𝑦, and in particular 𝑌―=𝑌 is closed. ∎
Proposition 4.1.111
Let 𝑋 be a sequentially compact topological space, and 𝑌⊂𝑋 a closed subset. Then 𝑌 is sequentially compact.
Proof. Let 𝑥𝑛∈𝑌 be a sequence. By assumption it has a convergent subsequence 𝑥𝑛𝑘→𝑥 where 𝑥∈𝑋. By (4.1.18) 𝑥 is an adherent point of 𝑌 and therefore lies in 𝑌 by (4.1.19). ∎
Proposition 4.1.112
Let 𝑓:𝑋→𝑌 be a continuous map of topological spaces where 𝑋 is sequentially compact. Then the image 𝑓(𝑋) is sequentially compact.
Proof. Let 𝑦𝑛=𝑓(𝑥𝑛) be a sequence in 𝑓(𝑋). Then by assumption 𝑥𝑛𝑘→𝑥, and therefore by (4.1.50) 𝑦𝑛𝑘→𝑓(𝑥). ∎
Proposition 4.1.113
Let 𝑋 be a topological space with an open cover 𝑋=⋃𝑖=1𝑛𝑈𝑖 such that 𝑈𝑖 is quasi-compact under the subspace topology. Then 𝑋 is quasi-compact.

Proof. Let 𝑋=⋃𝛼∈𝒜︀𝑉𝛼 then 𝑈𝑖=⋃𝛼∈𝒜︀(𝑉𝛼∩𝑈𝑖). By assumption there is some finite subset 𝒜︀𝑖⊂𝒜︀ such that 𝑈𝑖=⋃𝛼∈𝒜︀𝑖(𝑉𝛼∩𝑈𝑖). Then

𝑋=⋃𝛼∈𝒜︀1∪…∪𝒜︀𝑛𝑉𝛼

Therefore 𝑋 is quasi-compact. ∎

Proposition 4.1.114 (Noetherian ⟺ Hereditarily Quasi-Compact)
Let 𝑋 be a topological space. Then 𝑋 is Noetherian if and only if every open subset is quasi-compact.

Proof. ⟹) By (4.1.77) every open subset is Noetherian, so it is enough to show that 𝑋 is quasi-compact. By definition the lattice of open subsets is Noetherian and by (2.1.63) this holds iff every family of open subsets has a maximal element.

Suppose that we have an open cover 𝑋=⋃𝛼∈𝒜︀𝑈𝛼. Let ℱ︀ be the family of open subsets expressible as a finite union of sets 𝑈𝛼. By assumption it has a maximal element

𝑈≔𝑈𝛼1∪…∪𝑈𝛼𝑛

Suppose that 𝑈⊊𝑋 is proper then choose 𝑥∈𝑋∖𝑈. By hypothesis there is some 𝛼𝑛+1 such that 𝑥∈𝑈𝛼𝑛+1. Then 𝑈⊊𝑈∪𝑈𝛼𝑛+1∈ℱ︀, contradicting maximality. Therefore 𝑈=𝑋 and 𝑋 is quasi-compact as required.

⟸) Omitted. ∎

Proposition 4.1.115
Let 𝑋,𝑌 be quasi-compact topological spaces. Then 𝑋×𝑌 is quasi-compact.

Proof. Let {𝑈𝜆}𝜆∈Λ be an open cover of 𝑋×𝑌. Therefore for each (𝑥,𝑦) there is a 𝜆(𝑥,𝑦) such that (𝑥,𝑦)∈𝑈𝜆(𝑥,𝑦). By definition of the product topology (4.1.95) we may find open sets 𝑉(𝑥,𝑦)⊂𝑋 and 𝑊(𝑥,𝑦)⊂𝑌 such that (𝑥,𝑦)∈𝑉(𝑥,𝑦)×𝑊(𝑥,𝑦)⊂𝑈𝜆(𝑥,𝑦).

Fix 𝑥 then the family {𝑊(𝑥,𝑦)}𝑦∈𝑌 is an open cover of 𝑌. So we may find a finite subset 𝐽𝑥⊂𝑌 such that {𝑊(𝑥,𝑦)}𝑦∈𝐽𝑥 covers 𝑌.

Consider the open sets

𝑉𝑥′≔⋂𝑦∈𝐽𝑥𝑉(𝑥,𝑦)

which evidently cover 𝑋. Then there is a finite subset 𝐼⊂𝑋 such that {𝑉𝑥′}𝑥∈𝐼 covers 𝑋.

Then we claim that the family {𝑈𝜆(𝑥,𝑦)}𝑥∈𝐼,𝑦∈𝐽𝑥 is a finite subcover of 𝑋×𝑌. For given (𝑥′,𝑦′)∈𝑋×𝑌 there is an 𝑥∈𝐼 such that 𝑥′∈𝑉𝑥′, and 𝑦∈𝐽𝑥 such that 𝑦′∈𝑊(𝑥,𝑦). Evidently 𝑥′∈𝑉(𝑥,𝑦), whence (𝑥′,𝑦′)∈𝑈𝜆(𝑥,𝑦) as required. ∎
