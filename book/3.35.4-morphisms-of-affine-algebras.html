3.35.4 Morphisms of Affine Algebras

Definition 3.35.25
Let 𝐵 be a 𝑘-algebra. We say that 𝐵 is algebraic over 𝑘 if for every maximal ideal 𝔪◃𝐵 we have 𝐵/𝔪→∼𝑘(𝔪) is algebraic over 𝑘.
Proposition 3.35.26

Let 𝐵 be a 𝑘-algebra, then it is algebraic in each of the following cases

  1. 𝐵 is a finitely generated 𝑘-algebra
  2. 𝐵 is a local 𝑘-algebra such that 𝑘(𝔪𝐵)/𝑘 is algebraic
  3. 𝐵 is an algebraic field extension of 𝑘
  4. 𝐵 is the localisation of a finitely-generated 𝑘-algebra at a maximal ideal

Proof. We prove each in turn

  1. This follows from Zariski’s Lemma in the general case (3.35.23)
  2. The only maximal ideal is 𝔪𝐵 so this is obvious
  3. This is a special case of b)
  4. This follows from a) and b) because 𝐵𝔪 is a local ring with residue field isomorphic to 𝐵/𝔪 (3.6.43).

 ∎

Proposition 3.35.27
Let 𝜙:𝐴→𝐵 be a 𝑘-algebra homomorphism, with 𝐵 algebraic. Then the inverse image of a maximal ideal is again maximal.
Proof. Let 𝔫 be a maximal ideal. By assumption 𝐵/𝔫 is integral over 𝑘, and so a-fortiori 𝐵/𝔫 is integral over 𝐴. Therefore 𝜙−1(𝔫) is maximal by (3.24.16). ∎
