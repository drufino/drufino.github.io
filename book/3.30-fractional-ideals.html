3.30 Fractional Ideals

Definition 3.30.1

Let 𝐴 be an integral domain contained in its quotient field 𝐾. An 𝐴-submodule 𝐼⊂𝐾 is called a fractional ideal if there exists 𝑎∈𝐴 such that 𝑎𝐼⊂𝐴.

We say a fractional ideal is principal if it is generated by some 𝑥∈𝐾 as an 𝐴-module.

Proposition 3.30.2 (Operations on Fractional ideals)

Let 𝐴 be an integral domain and 𝐼,𝐽⊂𝐾 fractional ideals. Then the following constructions are also fractional ideals

  1. 𝐼+𝐽={𝑖+𝑗|𝑖∈𝐼𝑗∈𝐽}
  2. 𝐼𝐽={∑𝑘=1𝑛𝑖𝑘𝑗𝑘|𝑖𝑘∈𝐼𝑗𝑘∈𝐽}
  3. (𝐼:𝐽)={𝑥∈𝐾|𝑥𝐽⊆𝐼}

Proof. It is straightforward to prove that these constructions are again 𝐴-submodules of 𝐾.

Suppose that 𝑎𝐼⊆𝐴 and 𝑏𝐽⊆𝐴, then (𝑎𝑏)(𝐼+𝐽)⊆𝐴 and (𝑎𝑏)𝐼𝐽⊆𝐴. Similarly

(𝐼:𝐽)𝐽⊆𝐼⟹𝑗(𝐼:𝐽)⊆𝐼⟹𝑎𝑏𝑗(𝐼:𝐽)⊆𝐴

for some 0≠𝑗∈𝐽 ∎

Proposition 3.30.3
Let 𝐴 be an integral domain. The collection of fractional ideals ℐ︀𝐴 forms a monoid under multiplication of ideals, with identity 𝐴.
Definition 3.30.4
Let 𝐴 be an integral domain and 𝐼 a fractional ideal. Then we say 𝐼 is invertible if there exists a fractional ideal 𝐽 such that 𝐼𝐽=𝐴.
Proposition 3.30.5
Let 𝐴 be an integral domain and 𝐼 a fractional ideal. Then 𝐼 is invertible if and only if 𝐼(𝐴:𝐼)=𝐴, in which case (𝐴:𝐼) is the unique inverse.
Proof. Uniqueness follows from general properties of monoids (…). If 𝐼(𝐴:𝐼)=𝐴 then by definition 𝐼 is invertible. Conversely suppose 𝐼 is invertible, that is 𝐼𝐽=𝐴. Then evidentally 𝐽⊆(𝐴:𝐼) so 𝐴=𝐼𝐽⊆𝐼(𝐴:𝐼)⊆𝐴. Therefore (𝐴:𝐼) is an inverse for 𝐼 as required. ∎
Corollary 3.30.6
Let 𝐴 be an integral domain. Then the invertible fractional ideals form an abelian group under multiplication.
Definition 3.30.7 (Localisation of Fractional Ideals)

Let 𝐴 be an integral domain contained in its quotient field 𝐾. Let 𝐼 be a fractional ideal and 𝑆 a multiplicatively closed subset of 𝐴. Then define

𝑆−1𝐼≔{𝑖𝑠|𝑖∈𝐼𝑠∈𝑆}

If 𝔞 is an ideal then 𝑆−1𝔞 corresponds to the usual notion (3.6.17) and is equal to the extension of 𝔞 under the inclusion homomorphism 𝐴→𝑆−1𝐴.

If 𝔭 is a prime ideal of 𝐴 then as usual define

𝐼𝔭≔(𝐴∖𝔭)−1𝐼

and we may write

𝔞𝔭=𝔞𝐴𝔭
Proposition 3.30.8 (Localisation of Fractional Ideals)

Let 𝐴 be an integral domain contained in its quotient field 𝐾. Let 𝑆 be a multiplicatively closed subset of 𝐴 and 𝐼 a fractional ideal. Then regarding as subrings of 𝐾 then 𝑆−1𝐼 is a fractional ideal of 𝑆−1𝐴. Furthermore

  1. 𝑆−1(𝐼+𝐽)=𝑆−1𝐼+𝑆−1𝐽
  2. 𝑆−1(𝐼𝐽)=(𝑆−1𝐼)(𝑆−1𝐽)
  3. 𝑆−1(𝐼:𝐽)=(𝑆−1𝐼:𝑆−1𝐽)

Proof. Evidently 𝑆−1𝐼 is again a fractional ideal of 𝑆−1𝐴.

  1. Clear because 𝑖+𝑗𝑠=𝑖𝑠+𝑗𝑠.
  2. We have 𝑖𝑗𝑠=(𝑖𝑠)(𝑗1) whence 𝑆−1(𝐼𝐽)⊆(𝑆−1𝐼)(𝑆−1𝐽). Conversely we have (𝑖𝑠)(𝑗𝑡)=𝑖𝑗𝑠𝑡 so we ahve the reverse inclusion.
  3. Suppose that 𝑥𝑠∈𝑆−1(𝐼:𝐽) and 𝑗𝑡∈𝑆−1𝐽. Then (𝑥𝑠)(𝑗𝑡)=𝑥𝑗𝑠𝑡∈𝑆−1𝐼 because 𝑥∈(𝐼:𝐽). Therefore 𝑆−1(𝐼:𝐽)⊆(𝑆−1𝐼:𝑆−1𝐽). Conversely given 𝑥∈(𝑆−1𝐼:𝑆−1𝐽) and 𝑗∈𝐽. Then by definition 𝑥(𝑗1)=𝑖𝑠 whence 𝑠𝑥∈(𝐼:𝐽). Therefore 𝑥∈𝑆−1(𝐼:𝐽) as required.

 ∎

Lemma 3.30.9

Let 𝐴 be an integral domain contained in its quotient field 𝐾. Suppose 𝐼 is a fractional ideal then

𝐼=⋂𝔪𝐼𝔪

In particular we have

𝐴=⋂𝔪𝐴𝔪
Proof. Evidently 𝐼⊆⋂𝔪𝐼𝔪. Suppose that 𝑥∈⋂𝔪𝐼𝔪. Consider the ideal 𝔞≔{𝑎∈𝐴|𝑎𝑥∈𝐼}. Suppose 𝔞 is proper then by (3.3.15) it is contained in a maximal ideal 𝔪. As 𝑥∈𝐼𝔪 we have (𝐴∖𝔪)∩𝔞≠∅ and 𝔞⊆𝔪 a contradiction. Therefore 𝔞=𝐴 and in particular 𝑥=1.𝑥∈𝐼 as required. ∎
Proposition 3.30.10 (Invertibility is a local property)

Let 𝐴 be an integral domain with quotient field 𝐾, and 𝐼 a fractional ideal of 𝐴. Then the following are equivalent

  1. 𝐼 is invertible
  2. 𝐼𝔭 is invertible for all prime ideals 𝔭
  3. 𝐼𝔪 is invertible for all prime ideals 𝔪

Proof. a) ⟹ b) By assumption and (3.30.5) 𝐼(𝐴:𝐼)=𝐴 so by (3.30.8) 𝐼𝔭(𝐴𝔭:𝐼𝔭)=𝐴𝔭, as required.

b) ⟹ c) Every maximal ideal is prime (3.3.59) so we are done.

c) ⟹ a) Let 𝐼 be a fractional ideal then

𝐼(𝐴:𝐼)=⋂𝔪(𝐼(𝐴:𝐼))𝔪(3.30.9)=⋂𝔪𝐼𝔪(𝐴:𝐼)𝔪(3.30.8)=⋂𝔪𝐼𝔪(𝐴𝔪:𝐼𝔪)(3.30.8)=⋂𝔪𝐴𝔪(3.30.5)=𝐴(3.30.9)

 ∎
