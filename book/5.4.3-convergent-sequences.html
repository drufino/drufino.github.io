5.4.3 Convergent Sequences

Proposition 5.4.11 (Convergent Sequence)

Let 𝑋 be a normed vector space and (𝑎𝑛) a sequence in 𝑋. Then the following are equivalent

  1. 𝑎𝑛→𝛼 in the topological sense (4.1.49)

  2. For all 𝜀>0 there exists 𝑁(𝜀)∈ℕ+ such that

    𝑛≥𝑁(𝜀)⟹‖𝑎𝑛−𝛼‖<𝜀

  3. The function

    ℕ∪{∞}→𝑋𝑛→𝑎𝑛∞→𝛼

    is continuous with respect to the topology on ℕ∪{∞} given by open sets of the form {𝑁,𝑁+1,⋯}∪{∞} and arbitrary subsets of ℕ.

In this case we say the sequence (𝑎𝑛) is convergent. If this doesn’t hold it is divergent.

Proposition 5.4.12 (Uniqueness of Limits)
Let 𝑋 be a normed vector space. Then it is Hausdorff and limits are unique.
Proposition 5.4.13 (Continuous Image of Convergent Sequence)

Let 𝑋,𝑌 be normed vector spaces, 𝑆⊂𝑋 and 𝑓:𝑆→𝑌 a map. Then the following are equivalent

  1. 𝑓 is continuous in the topological sense
  2. For every convergent sequence 𝑎𝑛→𝛼 we have 𝑓(𝑎𝑛)→𝑓(𝛼).
Proof. Recall that 𝑋 is first-countable and so the equivalence follows from (4.1.50) and (4.1.52). ∎
Proposition 5.4.14 (Arithmetic of Convergent Sequences in an NVS)

Let 𝑋 be a normed vector space and (𝑎𝑛) and (𝑏𝑛) be convergent sequences in 𝑋. Then

lim𝑛→∞(𝑎𝑛+𝑏𝑛)=lim𝑛→∞𝑎𝑛+lim𝑛→∞𝑏𝑛lim𝑛→∞𝜆𝑎𝑛=𝜆lim𝑛→∞𝑎𝑛

In particular 𝑎𝑛→𝛼⟺𝑎𝑛−𝛼→0.

Proposition 5.4.15 (Arithmetic of Convergent Sequences in a valued field)

Let 𝐾 be a valued field and (𝑎𝑛) and (𝑏𝑛) be convergent sequences. Then

lim𝑛→∞𝑎𝑛𝑏𝑛=(lim𝑛→∞𝑎𝑛)(lim𝑛→∞𝑏𝑛)

Further if both 𝑏𝑛 and lim𝑛→∞𝑏𝑛 is non-zero then

lim𝑛→∞𝑎𝑛𝑏𝑛=lim𝑛→∞𝑎𝑛lim𝑛→∞𝑏𝑛
Proposition 5.4.16 (Arithmetic of Convergent Sequences in an ordered field)
Let 𝐾 be an ordered field and (𝑏𝑛) a sequence of non-negative elements. Then 𝑏𝑛→∞⟺𝑏𝑛−1→0.

Let 𝐾 be a valued field and 𝑥∈𝐾 such that |𝑥|<1. Then

Definition 5.4.18 (Cauchy Sequence)

Let 𝑋 be a normed vector space and (𝑎𝑛)𝑛∈ℕ a sequence in 𝑋. We say (𝑎𝑛) is a cauchy sequence if for all 𝜀>0 there exists 𝑁(𝜀) such that

𝑛,𝑚≥𝑁(𝜀)⟹‖𝑎𝑛−𝑎𝑚‖<𝜀

Every convergent sequence is cauchy. If the converse holds then we say 𝑋 is complete.
