3.32.1 Exponential and Logarithm

Definition 3.32.23 (Formal Exponential and Logarithm)

Let 𝐴 be an integral domain with ℚ⊆𝐴. Define

exp(𝑋)≔∑𝑛≥0𝑋𝑛𝑛!∈𝐴[[𝑋]]log(1+𝑋)≔∑𝑛≥1(−1)𝑛+1𝑋𝑛𝑛∈𝑋𝐴[[𝑋]]

For 𝑓∈𝑋𝐴[[𝑋]] and 𝑔∈1+𝑋𝐴[[𝑋]] define exp(𝑓)≔exp(𝑋)∘𝑓 and log(𝑔)≔log(1+𝑋)∘(𝑔−1), using substitution (3.32.20) (note 𝑔−1∈𝑋𝐴[[𝑋]]).

Lemma 3.32.24 (Zero Derivative)
Let 𝐴 be a commutative ring with ℚ⊆𝐴 and 𝐹∈𝐴((𝑋)) with 𝐹′=0. Then 𝐹 is a constant, that is 𝐹∈𝐴.

Proof. Drafted by Claude Sonnet, reviewed by David Rufino.

(𝐹′)𝑛=(𝑛+1)𝐹𝑛+1=0 for every 𝑛∈ℤ, and 𝑛+1 is invertible in 𝐴 for 𝑛≠−1, so 𝐹𝑛+1=0 for all 𝑛≠−1, that is 𝐹=𝐹0. ∎

Proposition 3.32.25 (Derivatives of exp and log)

Let 𝐴 be an integral domain with ℚ⊆𝐴, 𝑓∈𝑋𝐴[[𝑋]] and 𝑔∈1+𝑋𝐴[[𝑋]]. Then exp(𝑓)∈1+𝑋𝐴[[𝑋]] and log(𝑔)∈𝑋𝐴[[𝑋]], and

exp(𝑓)′=exp(𝑓)𝑓′log(𝑔)′=𝑔′𝑔−1

where 𝑔 is a unit of 𝐴[[𝑋]] by (3.32.13).

exp. Directly exp(𝑋)′=∑𝑛≥1𝑋𝑛−1(𝑛−1)!=exp(𝑋). Since 𝑣(𝑓𝑛)≥𝑛, only the term 𝑛=0 contributes to the constant coefficient of exp(𝑓)=∑𝑛𝑓𝑛𝑛!, so exp(𝑓)∈1+𝑋𝐴[[𝑋]]. By the chain rule (3.32.21) exp(𝑓)′=(exp′∘𝑓)𝑓′=exp(𝑓)𝑓′.

log. Likewise log(𝑔)∈𝑋𝐴[[𝑋]], as each term of ∑𝑛≥1(−1)𝑛+1(𝑔−1)𝑛𝑛 has order at least 𝑛. Directly log(1+𝑋)′=∑𝑛≥1(−1)𝑛+1𝑋𝑛−1=∑𝑚≥0(−𝑋)𝑚=(1+𝑋)−1, as (1+𝑋)∑𝑚≥0(−𝑋)𝑚=1 coefficientwise. By the chain rule

log(𝑔)′=(log(1+𝑋)′∘(𝑔−1))𝑔′=((1+𝑋)−1∘(𝑔−1))𝑔′=𝑔−1𝑔′

since 𝑓↦𝑓∘(𝑔−1) is a ring homomorphism (3.32.22) and (1+𝑋)∘(𝑔−1)=𝑔. ∎

Proposition 3.32.26 (Functional Equations of exp and log)
Let 𝐴 be an integral domain with ℚ⊆𝐴. Then exp(𝑓+𝑔)=exp(𝑓)exp(𝑔) for 𝑓,𝑔∈𝑋𝐴[[𝑋]], and log(𝑔ℎ)=log(𝑔)+log(ℎ) for 𝑔,ℎ∈1+𝑋𝐴[[𝑋]].

Proof. Drafted by Claude Sonnet, reviewed by David Rufino.

exp. Write 𝐸≔exp(𝑓+𝑔) and 𝑈≔exp(𝑓)exp(𝑔), a unit of 𝐴[[𝑋]] with constant coefficient 1. By the product rule and (3.32.25), 𝐸′=𝐸(𝑓′+𝑔′) and 𝑈′=𝑈(𝑓′+𝑔′). Hence for 𝑅≔𝐸𝑈−1, using the power rule (3.32.19) for 𝑈−1,

𝑅′=𝐸′𝑈−1−𝐸𝑈′𝑈−2by the product and power rules=𝐸(𝑓′+𝑔′)𝑈−1−𝐸𝑈(𝑓′+𝑔′)𝑈−2=0

so 𝑅 is constant by (3.32.24), equal to 𝑅(0)=1, that is 𝐸=𝑈.

log. By the product rule and (3.32.25), log(𝑔ℎ)′=(𝑔′ℎ+𝑔ℎ′)𝑔−1ℎ−1=𝑔′𝑔−1+ℎ′ℎ−1=(log(𝑔)+log(ℎ))′. So log(𝑔ℎ)−log(𝑔)−log(ℎ) has derivative 0 and constant coefficient 0, hence is 0 by (3.32.24). ∎

Proposition 3.32.27 (exp and log are Inverse Isomorphisms)
Let 𝐴 be an integral domain with ℚ⊆𝐴. Then log(exp(𝑓))=𝑓 for 𝑓∈𝑋𝐴[[𝑋]] and exp(log(𝑔))=𝑔 for 𝑔∈1+𝑋𝐴[[𝑋]]. Hence exp and log are mutually inverse isomorphisms of groups between (𝑋𝐴[[𝑋]],+) and (1+𝑋𝐴[[𝑋]],×).

Proof. Drafted by Claude Sonnet, reviewed by David Rufino.

By (3.32.25) log(exp(𝑓))′=exp(𝑓)′exp(𝑓)−1=𝑓′, so log(exp(𝑓))−𝑓 has derivative 0 and constant coefficient 0, and is 0 by (3.32.24). For the other composite let 𝐸≔exp(log(𝑔)), so 𝐸′=𝐸log(𝑔)′=𝐸𝑔′𝑔−1 by the same result. Then
