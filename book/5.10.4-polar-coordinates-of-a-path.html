5.10.4 Polar Coordinates of a Path

We would like to show that any path 𝛾:[𝑎,𝑏]→ℂ∖{0} has a continuous parameterisation (𝑟(𝑡),𝜃(𝑡)) in polar coordinates, and that this is essentially unique.

Definition 5.10.11

Suppose 𝑋 is a Hausdorff space. A continuous surjective map 𝑝:𝑋̃→𝑋 is a covering map if every 𝑥∈𝑋 has a neighbourhood 𝑈𝑥 such that

  1. 𝑝−1(𝑈𝑥)=⨆𝑖∈𝐼𝑥𝑉𝑥,𝑖
  2. 𝑝|𝑉𝑥,𝑖:𝑉𝑥,𝑖→𝑈𝑥 is a homeomorphism for all 𝑖∈𝐼𝑥

We may show that 𝑋̃ is necessarily Hausdorff.

Proposition 5.10.12 (Punctured Plane Covering Map)

The map

ℝ>0×ℝ→ℝ2∖{(0,0)}(𝑟,𝜃)→(𝑟cos(𝜃),𝑟sin(𝜃))

is a covering map.

Proof. Define

𝑈1≔ℝ2∖{(𝑥,0)|𝑥≥0}𝑈2≔ℝ2∖{(𝑥,0)|𝑥≤0}

We claim that

𝑝−1(𝑈1)=⨆𝑛ℝ>0×(2𝑛𝜋,(2𝑛+2)𝜋)𝑝−1(𝑈2)=⨆𝑛ℝ>0×((2𝑛−1)𝜋,(2𝑛+1)𝜋)

Denote the open sets by 𝑉𝑖,𝑛 for 𝑖=1,2. Then the maps

𝑝|𝑉𝑖,𝑛→𝑈𝑖

are injective by (5.10.10). We may define explicit continuous inverses by

𝑈1→𝑉1,𝑛(𝑥,𝑦)→(𝑥2+𝑦2,𝜃(𝑥,𝑦)+2𝑛𝜋)𝑈2→𝑉2,𝑛(𝑥,𝑦)→(𝑥2+𝑦2,−𝜃(−𝑥,𝑦)+2(𝑛−1)𝜋)

 ∎

Proposition 5.10.13 (Path lifting)
Let 𝑝:𝑋̃→𝑋 be a covering map and 𝛾:[𝑎,𝑏]→𝑋 a continuous path. Consider any 𝑥̃0∈𝑋̃ such that 𝑝(𝑥̃0)=𝛾(𝑎). Then there exists a unique lifting 𝛾̃:[𝑎,𝑏]→𝑋̃ such that 𝑝∘𝛾̃=𝛾 and 𝛾̃(𝑎)=𝑥̃0.

Proof. We first prove uniqueness. Consider two liftings 𝛾̃1,𝛾̃2:[𝑎,𝑏]→𝑋̃ and define

𝑋≔{𝑡∈[𝑎,𝑏]|𝛾̃1(𝑡)=𝛾̃2(𝑡)}

Fix 𝑡∈𝑋 and let 𝑈 be a neighbourhood of 𝛾(𝑡) such that 𝑝−1(𝑈)=⨆𝑖∈𝐼𝑉𝑖. Suppose 𝛾̃1(𝑡)=𝛾̃2(𝑡)∈𝑉𝑖. By continuity there exists a neighbourhood 𝑊 of 𝑡 such that 𝛾̃1(𝑊)⊆𝑉𝑖 and 𝛾̃2(𝑊)⊆𝑉𝑖. By definition of 𝑋 this means 𝑊⊆𝑋. Therefore 𝑋 is open. By (4.1.99) 𝑋 is also closed. By (5.7.9) then 𝑋 is either empty or [𝑎,𝑏]. However we have 𝑎∈𝑋 and the liftings must be equal.

Define

𝐿≔{𝑥∈[𝑎,𝑏]|∃𝛾̃:[𝑎,𝑥]→𝑋̃s.t.𝑝∘𝛾̃=𝛾and𝛾̃(𝑎)=𝑥̃0}

To show that 𝐿 is closed consider a sequence 𝑥𝑛∈𝐿 such that 𝑥𝑛→𝑥∈[𝑎,𝑏]. Choose 𝛾(𝑥)∈𝑈 such that 𝑝−1(𝑈)=⨆𝑖𝑉𝑖 and 𝑝|𝑉𝑖 is a homeomorphism. For some 𝜀 we have (𝑥−𝜀,𝑥+𝜀)∩[𝑎,𝑏]⊂𝛾−1(𝑈), and for some 𝑁, 𝑥𝑁∈(𝑥−𝜀,𝑥+𝜀)∩[𝑎,𝑏]. In particular there is a lift

𝛾̃′:[𝑎,𝑥𝑁]→𝑋̃.

and 𝛾̃′(𝑥𝑁)∈𝑉𝑖 for some 𝑖. If 𝑥𝑁≥𝑥 then we are done, otherwise assume 𝑥𝑁<𝑥 and define the lift

𝛾̃(𝑡)≔{𝛾̃′(𝑡)𝑡≤𝑥𝑁(𝑝|𝑉𝑖)−1(𝛾(𝑡))𝑥𝑁≤𝑡≤𝑥

Therefore 𝑥∈𝐿, and 𝐿 is closed. By (5.7.9) we conclude 𝐿=[𝑎,𝑏] and we are done. ∎

Proposition 5.10.14 (Almost uniqueness of polar coordinates)

Let 𝑝:ℝ>0×ℝ→ℝ2∖{(0,0)} be the covering map of the punctured plane (5.10.12) and 𝛾:[𝑎,𝑏]→ℝ2∖{(0,0)} a continuous path. Suppose (𝑟1,𝜃1) and (𝑟2,𝜃2) are liftings. Then

𝑟1(𝑡)=𝑟2(𝑡)𝜃1(𝑡)=𝜃2(𝑡)+2𝑛𝜋

for some 𝑛∈ℤ.

Proof. By (5.10.10) we have 𝜃2(𝑎)−𝜃1(𝑎)=2𝑛𝜋. Then (𝑟2(𝑡),𝜃2(𝑡)−2𝑛𝜋) is also a lifting and so by uniqueness equals (𝑟1(𝑡),𝜃1(𝑡)) as required. ∎
Definition 5.10.15 (Winding Number)

Let 𝛾:[𝑎,𝑏]→ℂ∖{0} be a continuous path. We define the angle traversed by the path 𝛾 to be

𝜃(𝑏)−𝜃(𝑎)

where (𝑟(𝑡),𝜃(𝑡)) is any lifting of 𝛾. Note when 𝛾 is a closed path then this equals 2𝑛𝜋 for a unique integer 𝑛. We call this the winding number of the closed path 𝛾, which we denote by 𝑊(𝛾;0). In general we define

𝑊(𝛾;𝑧0)≔𝑊(𝛾−𝑧0;0)

for any 𝑧0∉𝛾([𝑎,𝑏]).
