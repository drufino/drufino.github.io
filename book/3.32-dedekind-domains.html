3.32 Dedekind Domains

We develop the theory of Dedekind Domains. These occur both as number fields and regular affine curves (over any field). The important results are the finiteness of integral closure in a finite extension, and the ramification formula of a prime ideal. In the case of regular curves over a finite field further results are needed, which we prove later in the context of affine algebras.

Proposition 3.32.1

Let 𝐴 be a Noetherian integral domain of dimension 1. Then the following are equivalent

  1. 𝐴 is integrally closed
  2. 𝐴𝔭 is integrally closed for every prime ideal 𝔭
  3. 𝐴𝔭 is a discrete valuation ring for every non-zero prime ideal 𝔭

In this case we say that 𝐴 is a Dedekind Domain.

Proof. Let 𝔭 be non-zero prime ideal of 𝐴. Then firstly 𝐴𝔭 is Noetherian by (…) and an integral domain by (…). Then dim𝐴𝔭=ht(𝔭)≤dim𝐴=1. However (0)⊊𝔭 so dim𝐴𝔭=ht(𝔭)=1.

a) ⟺ b) This is (3.24.10).

b) ⟹ c) We have shown that 𝐴𝔭 is a Noetherian integral domain of dimension 1. Then it is a discrete valuation ring by (3.31.2).

c) ⟹ b) Also follows from (3.31.2), since 𝐴(0) is a field and therefore automatically integrally closed. ∎

Every principal ideal domain which is not a field is a Dedekind Domain. For example 𝑘[𝑋] and ℤ.

Proposition 3.32.3 (Correspondence between Prime Ideals and Valuation Rings)

Let 𝐴 be an integral domain with Krull dimension 1 and quotient field 𝐾. Then there is a bijection

{𝔭◃𝐴|𝔭non-zero prime}⟷{𝐴⊊𝑅⊊𝐾local rings}𝔭⟶𝐴𝔭𝔪𝑅∩𝐴⟵𝑅

When 𝐴 is a Dedekind domain then all such local rings (and valuation rings) are discrete valuation rings.

Proof. Suppose that 𝑥∈𝔭1∖𝔭2. Then 𝑥−1∈𝐴𝔭2 but we claim 𝑥−1∉𝐴𝔭1. For suppose 𝑥−1∈𝐴𝔭1 then 𝑎𝑥𝑏=1 for 𝑎∈𝐴 and 𝑏∉𝔭1 whence 𝑎𝑥=𝑏 a contradiction. Therefore the given map is injective.

Let 𝑅 be a local ring with maximal ideal 𝔪𝑅. Then 𝔪𝑅∩𝐴 is a prime ideal, and so either (0) or a non-zero prime 𝔭. We have 0≠𝑥𝑦∈𝔪𝑅 for some 𝑥,𝑦∈𝐴 whence 0≠𝑥∈𝔪𝑅. Therefore 𝔪𝑅∩𝐴=𝔭. Suppose 𝑥∈𝐴∖𝔭 then 𝑥∈𝑅∖𝔪𝑅⟹𝑥∈𝑅⋆. This shows that 𝐴𝔭⊂𝑅 and 𝔭𝐴𝔭⊂𝔪𝑅, and we are done by (3.25.13). ∎

Proposition 3.32.4
Let 𝐴 be a Dedekind domain. Then every fractional ideal is invertible.
Proof. Let 𝐼 be an invertible ideal. By (3.30.10) it is sufficient to show that 𝐼𝔭 is an invertible fractional ideal of 𝐴𝔭 for all prime ideals 𝔭. By (3.32.1) 𝐴𝔭 is a discrete valuation ring and the result follows from (3.31.7). ∎
Lemma 3.32.5

Let 𝐴 be a Dedekind domain and 𝔞 a proper ideal. Then there is a well-defined group homomorphism

(𝔞)−:ℤ⟶ℐ︀𝐴

mapping 1 to 𝔞.

Proof. By (3.32.4) the monoid ℐ︀𝐴 is a group, and therefore the first homomorphism exists by (3.2.6). ∎
Lemma 3.32.6

Let 𝐴 be a Dedekind domain and 𝔭, 𝔮 non-zero prime ideals. Then

(𝔭𝑘)𝔮={𝐴𝔮𝔭≠𝔮(𝔮𝐴𝔮)𝑘𝔭=𝔮

Proof. When 𝑘=0 then by definition 𝔭𝑘=𝐴 and (𝔮𝐴𝔮)𝑘=𝐴𝔮 and so we are done.

We suppose that 𝑘=1. If 𝔭≠𝔮 then as dim𝐴=1 we have 𝔭⊈𝔮. Therefore by definition 𝔭𝔮 contains 1 and equals 𝐴𝔮 as required. The case 𝔭=𝔮 follows from (3.30.7).

By (3.30.8) and (3.32.5) both left and right hand sides define group homomorphisms ℤ→ℐ︀𝐴𝔮 which agree at 1 and are therefore equal. ∎

Proposition 3.32.7 (Unique Factorisation)

Let 𝐴 be a Dedekind domain. There is a well-defined abelian group isomorphism

⨁𝔭∈𝒫︀ℤ⟶∼ℐ︀𝐴(𝑒𝔭)⟶∏𝔭:𝑒𝔭≠0𝔭𝑒𝔭

where 𝒫︀ is the set of prime ideals of 𝐴. Write (𝑣𝔭) for the inverse, then it is given explicitly by

𝑣𝔭(𝐼)=𝑣𝔭(𝐼𝔭)

Further a fractional ideal 𝐼 is contained in 𝐴 if and only if 𝑣𝔭(𝐼)≥0 for all prime ideals 𝔭.

Proof. The homomorphisms exists by (3.32.6) and the universal property of the sum of abelian groups (…).

Let 𝐼 be a fractional ideal and define 𝑣𝔭(𝐼)≔𝑣𝔭(𝐼𝔭) using the notation of (3.31.7). This determines a group homomorphism 𝑣𝔭:ℐ︀𝐴→ℤ by (3.30.8) b). We claim for every 𝐼 that 𝑣𝔭(𝐼) is zero for all but finitely many prime ideals 𝔭. As 𝐼=(𝑎)−1𝔞 for some ideal 𝐽 of 𝐴 it is sufficient by multiplicativity to demonstrate this for ideals 𝔞. By (…) if 𝔞⊄𝔭 then 𝔞𝔭=𝐴𝔭 which has valuation 0. Therefore we may assume that 𝔞⊂𝔭. However by (3.22.6) there are only finitely many such primes, since every non-zero prime containing 𝔞 must be minimal over (0), and a-fortiori over 𝔞.

Observe by (3.32.6) that for distinct prime ideals 𝔭1,…,𝔭𝑛 we have the identity

(𝔭1𝑟1…𝔭𝑛𝑟𝑛)𝔮={(𝔭𝑘𝐴𝔮)𝑟𝑘𝔮=𝔭𝑘𝐴𝔮𝔮≠𝔭𝑘for all𝑘=1…𝑛

By the first claim there is a well-defined homomorphism ℐ︀𝐴→⨁𝔭∈𝒫︀ℤ. We claim that this is a left inverse for the given map, that is to say

∏𝔭:𝑣𝔭(𝐼)≠0𝔭𝑣𝔭(𝐼)=𝐼

Denote the left hand side by 𝐼′. When 𝑣𝔭(𝐼)≠0 then by the identity above we have, using (3.31.7) for the final equality,

𝐼𝔭′=(𝔭𝐴𝔭)𝑣𝔭(𝐼)=(𝔭𝐴𝔭)𝑣𝔭(𝐼𝔭)=𝐼𝔭

Similarly if 𝑣𝔭(𝐼)=0 by (3.31.7)

𝐼𝔭′=𝐴𝔭=𝐼𝔭

We conclude by (3.30.9) that 𝐼=𝐼′ as required. Finally the relation above also shows that this is a right inverse.

For the final statement by (3.30.9) we have 𝐼⊂𝐴⟺𝐼𝔭⊂𝐴𝔭 for all prime ideals 𝔭, then we may use the equivalent statement over DVRs (3.31.7). ∎

Proposition 3.32.8

Let 𝐴 be a Dedekind with 𝐼,𝐽 fractional ideals. Then the following are equivalent

  1. 𝐼⊂𝐽
  2. 𝐽 divides 𝐼 (that is there exists an ideal 𝔞 such that 𝔞𝐽=𝐼)
  3. 𝑣𝔭(𝐼)≥𝑣𝔭(𝐽) for all prime ideals 𝔭

Proof. a) ⟺ c) By (3.30.9) we have 𝐼⊂𝐽⟺𝐼𝔭⊂𝐽𝔭 for all prime ideals 𝔭. Then we may use the corresponding statement for DVRs (3.31.8).

b) ⟹ c) Follows because 𝑣𝔭(𝔞)≥0 (3.32.7).

c) ⟹ b) We may define 𝔞=∏𝔭𝑣𝔭(𝐼)−𝑣𝔭(𝐽) which is contained in 𝐴 and by multiplicativity 𝑣𝔭(𝔞𝐽)=𝑣𝔭(𝐼) for all prime ideals 𝔭 whence 𝔞𝐽=𝐼 by the correspondence in (3.32.7). ∎

Proposition 3.32.9 (Integral Closure is a Dedekind Domain)

Let 𝐿/𝐾 be a finite separable extension and 𝐴⊂𝐾 a subring which is a Dedekind domain with quotient field 𝐾. Let 𝐵 be the integral closure of 𝐴 in 𝐿, then 𝐵 is a finite 𝐴-module and a Dedekind domain.

If in addition 𝐴 is a principal ideal domain (e.g. a discrete valuation ring) then 𝐵 is a free 𝐴-module of rank [𝐿:𝐾].

Moreover 𝐵⊗𝐴𝐾≅𝐿, via 𝑏⊗𝑥↦𝑏𝑥 (regarding 𝐵,𝐾⊆𝐿).

Proof. From (3.24.24) we know 𝐵 is integrally closed and dim𝐵=1. Then the rest follows from (3.24.26).

Base change to 𝐾. Writing 𝑆≔𝐴∖{0}, (3.6.31) gives an isomorphism 𝐵⊗𝐴𝐾≅𝑆−1𝐵, 𝑏⊗𝑥↦𝑏𝑥, where 𝑆−1𝐵 is naturally a subring of 𝐿 (as 𝑆⊂𝐵∖{0} too). By (3.24.15) (applied to the integral map 𝐴↪︎𝐵, and 𝑆), 𝐾=𝑆−1𝐴→𝑆−1𝐵 is integral; as 𝑆−1𝐵 is an integral domain (a subring of 𝐿) and 𝐾 is a field, (3.24.14) gives that 𝑆−1𝐵 is itself a field. Being a field containing 𝐵, 𝑆−1𝐵 contains Frac(𝐵)=𝐿; but 𝑆−1𝐵⊆𝐿 already; so 𝑆−1𝐵=𝐿. ∎

Definition 3.32.10 (AKLB Situation)
Let 𝐿/𝐾 be a finite field extension, 𝐴⊂𝐾, 𝐵⊂𝐿 Dedekind domains for which 𝐴⊂𝐵 is integral (equivalently 𝐵 is the integral closure of 𝐴 in 𝐿), Frac(𝐴)=𝐾 and Frac(𝐵)=𝐿. We refer to this situation as “AKLB”.

Proposition 3.32.12

Using the notation in (3.32.10). Let 𝔭 be a prime ideal of 𝐴. There exists at least one prime ideal 𝔮∣𝔭, and only finitely many. For every such prime ideal define the integers 𝑒𝔮/𝔭≔𝑣𝔮(𝔭𝐵), then

𝔭𝐵=∏𝔮∣𝔭𝔮𝑒𝔮/𝔭=⋂𝔮∣𝔭𝔮𝑒𝔮/𝔭
Proof. The existence of a prime 𝔮 follows from the Lying Over property of integral extensions (3.24.17). The factorisation exists by (3.32.7) which also shows that there are only finitely many divisors of 𝔭, and the second equality holds by the Chinese Remainder Theorem (3.3.62). ∎
Definition 3.32.13 (Ramification Index / Inertial Degree)

Using the notation in (3.32.10), suppose that 𝔮∣𝔭. Then we define the ramification index by

𝑒𝔮/𝔭=𝑣𝔮(𝔭𝐵)

which by (…) is a positive integer. Similarly define the inertial degree

𝑓𝔮/𝔭=[𝐵/𝔮:𝐴/𝔭]

which we presently show to be finite.

Proposition 3.32.16 (Ramification Formula)

Using the notation of (3.32.10). For every prime ideal 𝔭 of 𝐴 we have the identity

∑𝔮∣𝔭𝑒𝔮/𝔭𝑓𝔮/𝔭=[𝐵/𝔭𝐵:𝐴/𝔭]≤[𝐿:𝐾]

Suppose in addition that 𝐵 is a finite 𝐴-module then we have equality. In particular this holds when 𝐿/𝐾 is separable.

Proof. Recall from (3.32.12) we have the factorisation

𝔭𝐵=∏𝔮∣𝔭𝔮𝑒𝔮/𝔭

By the Chinese Remainder Theorem (3.3.62) we have a canonical isomorphism

𝐵/𝔭𝐵⟶∼∏𝔮∣𝔭𝐵/𝔮𝑒𝔮

Both 𝐵/𝔭𝐵 and 𝐵/𝔮𝑒𝔮 are 𝐴/𝔭-modules, and so equating vector space dimensions we have

[𝐵/𝔭𝐵:𝐴/𝔭]=∑𝔮∣𝔭[𝐵/𝔮𝑒𝔮:𝐴/𝔭]

As 𝔭𝐵⊂𝔮𝑒 we have a decreasing sequence of 𝐴/𝔭-submodules

𝐵/𝔮𝑒⊇𝔮1/𝔮𝑒⊇…⊇𝔮𝑒/𝔮𝑒=(0)

so by (3.3.130) we have

[𝐵/𝔮𝑒:𝐴/𝔭]=∑𝑖=1𝑒[(𝔮𝑖−1/𝔮𝑒)/(𝔮𝑖/𝔮𝑒):𝐴/𝔭]

We claim there is an 𝐴/𝔭-module isomorphism

(𝔮𝑖−1/𝔮𝑒)/(𝔮𝑖/𝔮𝑒)→𝔮𝑖−1/𝔮𝑖

This exists as an 𝐴-module isomorphism by (3.3.105), and therefore as an 𝐴/𝔭-module isomorphism as the action factors through the quotient map 𝐴→𝐴/𝔭. Therefore by (3.19.29)

[𝐵/𝔮𝑒:𝐴/𝔭]=∑𝑖=1𝑒[𝔮𝑖−1/𝔮𝑖:𝐴/𝔭]=∑𝑖=1𝑒[𝐵/𝔮:𝐴/𝔭][𝔮𝑖−1/𝔮𝑖:𝐵/𝔮]=∑𝑖=1𝑒𝑓𝔮/𝔭[𝔮𝑖−1/𝔮𝑖:𝐵/𝔮]

We claim that [𝔮𝑖−1/𝔮𝑖:𝐵/𝔮]=1. Any non-trivial subspace of 𝔮𝑖−1/𝔮𝑖 is a-fortiori a 𝐵-submodule, and by the isomorphism theorem yields an ideal between 𝔮𝑖−1 and 𝔮𝑖. Therefore it is enough to show there is no such ideal. By (3.6.42) we may localize at 𝔮 to consider the ring 𝐵𝔮. By definition this is a DVR and the result follows from (3.31.2). Therefore

[𝐵/𝔭𝐵:𝐴/𝔭]=∑𝔮∣𝔭𝑒𝔮/𝔭𝑓𝔮/𝔭.

The inequality follows from (3.32.14).

It remains to show this equals [𝐵/𝔭𝐵:𝐴/𝔭]=[𝐿:𝐾] in the case 𝐵 is a finite 𝐴-module. By (3.32.15) and (3.32.14) we may reduce to the case of 𝐴 a discrete valuation ring, and in a particular principal ideal domain. Then by (3.24.25) we have 𝐵 is a free 𝐴-module of rank 𝑛=[𝐿:𝐾]. Let 𝑥1,…,𝑥𝑛 be such a basis, then we claim that 𝑥1―,…,𝑥1― is linearly independent over 𝐴/𝔭 and therefore a basis for 𝐵/𝔭𝐵. For suppose

0=∑𝑖=1𝑛𝜆𝑖𝑥𝑖―𝜆𝑖∈𝐴

then we may show that 𝔭𝐵=𝔭𝑥1+…+𝔭𝑥𝑛 whence by definition

∑𝑖=1𝑛𝜆𝑖𝑥𝑖=∑𝑖=1𝑛𝜇𝑖𝑥𝑖

for some 𝜇𝑖∈𝔭, from which it follows 𝜆𝑖=𝜇𝑖 and 𝜆𝑖―=0, and we are done.

The case 𝐿/𝐾 is separable is then (3.24.26). ∎

3.32.1 Local Monogenicity

Lemma 3.32.17 (A Uniformiser Generates a Totally Ramified Extension)

Using the notation of (3.32.10), with 𝐿/𝐾 separable, let 𝔭 be a prime of 𝐴 with a unique prime 𝔮 of 𝐵 over it, of residue degree 𝑓𝔮/𝔭=1 (so 𝐵𝔭=𝐵𝔮 is itself a DVR, totally ramified: 𝑒𝔮/𝔭=𝑛≔[𝐿:𝐾]). Then for any uniformiser 𝜋 of 𝐵𝔭,

𝐵𝔭=𝐴𝔭[𝜋],

and 𝜋’s minimal polynomial 𝐹∈𝐴𝔭[𝑌] over 𝐾 has degree exactly 𝑛.

Proof. Drafted by Claude Sonnet 5, reviewed by David Rufino.

For 0≤𝑗<𝑛, 𝔮𝑗/𝔮𝑗+1 is 1-dimensional over 𝐵𝔭/𝔮=𝑘 (as 𝐵𝔭 is a DVR), generated by the image of 𝜋𝑗; residue degree 1 means 𝐴𝔭/𝔭=𝑘 too, so by downward induction on 𝑗, {1,𝜋,…,𝜋𝑛−1} spans 𝐵𝔭/𝔭𝐵𝔭=𝐵𝔭/𝔮𝑛 (as 𝔭𝐵𝔭=𝔮𝑛, totally ramified) as an 𝐴𝔭/𝔭-module. By (3.21.6), applied to the finite 𝐴𝔭-module 𝐵𝔭, a spanning set of 𝐵𝔭/𝔭𝐵𝔭 already spans 𝐵𝔭 itself, so

𝐵𝔭=𝐴𝔭[𝜋].

Let 𝐹∈𝐴𝔭[𝑌] be 𝜋’s minimal polynomial over 𝐾, of degree 𝑑. As 𝐵𝔭=𝐴𝔭[𝜋] and 𝐹(𝜋)=0, 𝐵𝔭 is already spanned by 1,𝜋,…,𝜋𝑑−1 over 𝐴𝔭, so rank𝐴𝔭𝐵𝔭≤𝑑; as 𝐵𝔭 is 𝐴𝔭-free of rank 𝑛 (3.24.25), 𝑑≥𝑛, while 𝑑=[𝐾(𝜋):𝐾]≤[𝐿:𝐾]=𝑛 as 𝐾(𝜋)⊆𝐿. So 𝑑=𝑛 exactly. ∎
