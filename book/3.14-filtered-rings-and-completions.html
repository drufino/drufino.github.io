3.14 Filtered Rings and Completions

Both DVRs and complete DVRs sit naturally in the category of filtered rings developed in this subsection: a DVR such as 𝐴(𝑝) is filtered by the powers of its maximal ideal, and a complete DVR such as ℤ𝑝 or 𝑘[[𝑋]] is exactly such a filtered ring whose canonical map to its own completion happens to be an isomorphism (3.14.20). Working at this level of generality – filtered rings and continuous homomorphisms between them, rather than DVRs and local homomorphisms specifically – gives a category closed under more operations than DVRs themselves support: for instance, an arbitrary product of filtered rings is again filtered, with completion computed factorwise (3.14.22), even though a product of DVRs is essentially never itself a DVR. This is the kind of fact the filtered-ring framework makes easy to state: the completion of a semi-local Dedekind domain (such as the integral closure 𝒪︀𝐿 of a DVR in a finite extension) agrees with the product of the completions at its finitely many primes, by the Chinese Remainder Theorem factorwise – though the trace/residue theory developed later avoids needing this, working directly with Laurent series instead (see the Extensions of Power Series Rings section).

Filtered rings, at this level of generality, are the algebraic core of the admissible rings of [Gro60] (0I, §7) (linearly topologized rings with a fundamental system of open ideals, not necessarily powers of a single ideal).

Definition 3.14.1 (Filtered Ring)
A filtered ring (𝐴,𝔞•) is a ring 𝐴 together with a decreasing chain of ideals 𝔞•=(𝔞𝑛)𝑛∈ℤ of 𝐴, that is 𝔞𝑛+1⊆𝔞𝑛 for every 𝑛∈ℤ, with 𝔞𝑛=𝐴 for every 𝑛<0. We say (𝐴,𝔞•) is separated if ⋂𝑛𝔞𝑛=0.
Definition 3.14.2 (Filtered Module)

Let 𝐴 be a ring and 𝑀 an 𝐴-module. A filtration of 𝑀 (as an 𝐴-module) is a decreasing chain ℳ︀•=(ℳ︀𝑛)𝑛∈ℤ of 𝐴-submodules of 𝑀, that is ℳ︀𝑛+1⊆ℳ︀𝑛 for every 𝑛∈ℤ. We call (𝑀,ℳ︀•) a filtered 𝐴-module, and say it is separated if ⋂𝑛ℳ︀𝑛=0, and exhaustive if ⋃𝑛ℳ︀𝑛=𝑀.

Every positively filtered 𝐴-module (3.14.3) is exhaustive, trivially (ℳ︀𝑛=𝑀 already for 𝑛<0); the fractional-ideal filtration of 𝐾=Frac(𝐴), 𝐴 a DVR, is a genuinely exhaustive example that is not positively filtered.

When (𝐴,𝔞•) is itself a filtered ring, every 𝐴-module 𝑀 carries a canonical filtration 𝔞•𝑀≔(𝔞𝑛𝑀)𝑛∈ℤ, the induced filtration (with 𝔞𝑛𝑀=𝑀 for 𝑛<0, as 𝔞𝑛=𝐴 there; so (𝑀,𝔞•𝑀) is positively filtered, hence exhaustive).

Definition 3.14.3 (Positively Filtered)
A filtered ring (3.14.1), or more generally a filtered 𝐴-module (𝑀,ℳ︀•) (3.14.2), is positively filtered if ℳ︀𝑛 equals the whole module for every 𝑛<0. Taking 𝑀≔𝐴, ℳ︀𝑛≔𝔞𝑛 exhibits every filtered ring as a positively filtered 𝐴-module over itself.
Definition 3.14.4 (𝔞-adic Filtration)
Let 𝐴 be a ring and 𝔞◃𝐴 an ideal. The 𝔞-adic filtration is 𝔞𝑛≔𝔞𝑛+1 for 𝑛≥0 and 𝔞𝑛≔𝐴 for 𝑛<0, a filtration of 𝐴 (3.14.1).
Definition 3.14.5 (Continuous Homomorphism)
Let (𝐴,𝔞•), (𝐵,𝔟•) be filtered rings. A ring homomorphism 𝜙:𝐴→𝐵 is continuous if, for every 𝑚≥0, there is 𝑛≥0 with 𝜙(𝔞𝑛)⊆𝔟𝑚.
Remark 3.14.6
Continuity is exactly continuity of 𝜙 at 0 in the usual metric-space sense, for the metrics 𝑑𝐴(𝑥,𝑦)≔2−sup{𝑛:𝑥−𝑦∈𝔞𝑛} on 𝐴 and 𝑑𝐵(𝑥,𝑦)≔2−sup{𝑛:𝑥−𝑦∈𝔟𝑛} on 𝐵 (with sup∅≔−1, so 𝑑𝐴,𝑑𝐵 vanish exactly on equal pairs): a ball of radius 2−𝑚 about 0∈𝐵 is exactly 𝔟𝑚+1, so “𝜙(𝔞𝑛+1)⊆𝔟𝑚+1 for some 𝑛, for every 𝑚” is exactly continuity of 𝜙 at 0. As 𝑑𝐴,𝑑𝐵 are translation-invariant (𝑑(𝑥,𝑦)=𝑑(𝑥−𝑦,0), immediate as 𝔞𝑛,𝔟𝑛 are subgroups), this already gives continuity of 𝜙 at every point of 𝐴: given 𝑥0∈𝐴 and 𝜀>0, pick 𝑚 with 2−𝑚≤𝜀 and 𝑛 with 𝜙(𝔞𝑛+1)⊆𝔟𝑚+1; then 𝑑𝐴(𝑥,𝑥0)<2−𝑛 gives 𝑥−𝑥0∈𝔞𝑛+1, so 𝜙(𝑥)−𝜙(𝑥0)=𝜙(𝑥−𝑥0)∈𝔟𝑚+1, that is 𝑑𝐵(𝜙(𝑥),𝜙(𝑥0))≤2−𝑚≤𝜀. (This is the same translation-invariance trick used for the identity map in (3.14.13), which compares two filtrations of a single ring this way.)

For the remainder of this subsection, fix filtered rings (𝐴,𝔞•), (𝐴′,𝔞•′), a ring homomorphism 𝜌:𝐴→𝐴′, and filtered 𝐴-, 𝐴′-modules (𝑀,ℳ︀•), (𝑀′,ℳ︀•′) (3.14.2): this notation is reused without restatement in every definition and proposition below phrased in terms of a map 𝜓:𝑀→𝑀′.

Definition 3.14.7 (Continuous Homomorphism, Module Case)

An additive map 𝜓:𝑀→𝑀′ is a 𝜌-linear map (or 𝜌-homomorphism) if 𝜓(𝑎⋅𝑥)=𝜌(𝑎)⋅𝜓(𝑥) for every 𝑎∈𝐴, 𝑥∈𝑀, and is continuous if, for every 𝑚∈ℤ, there is 𝑛∈ℤ with 𝜓(ℳ︀𝑛)⊆ℳ︀𝑚′.

When 𝐴=𝐴′ and 𝜌=id𝐴, a continuous 𝜌-homomorphism is exactly an ordinary continuous 𝐴-module homomorphism.

Definition 3.14.8 (Dense Homomorphism)
Let (𝐴,𝔞•), (𝐵,𝔟•) be filtered rings. A ring homomorphism 𝜙:𝐴→𝐵 is dense if 𝐵=𝜙(𝐴)+𝔟𝑛 for every 𝑛≥0.
Definition 3.14.9 (Dense Homomorphism, Module Case)
A 𝜌-homomorphism 𝜓:𝑀→𝑀′ (3.14.7) is dense if 𝑀′=𝜓(𝑀)+ℳ︀𝑛′ for every 𝑛≥0. (Taking 𝑀≔𝐴, 𝑀′≔𝐴′ as filtered 𝐴-, 𝐴′-modules over themselves and 𝜓≔𝜌 recovers (3.14.8).)
Definition 3.14.10 (Taut Homomorphism)

Let (𝐴,𝔞•), (𝐵,𝔟•) be filtered rings and 𝜙:𝐴→𝐵 a ring homomorphism with 𝜙(𝔞𝑛)⊆𝔟𝑛 for every 𝑛≥0. Then 𝜙 is taut if there is a strictly increasing sequence 𝑛0<𝑛1<𝑛2<⋯ in ℕ such that

𝐵=𝜙(𝐴)+𝔟𝑛0and𝔟𝑛𝑁=𝜙(𝔞𝑛𝑁)+𝔟𝑛𝑁+1for every𝑁≥0.
Definition 3.14.11 (Taut Homomorphism, Module Case)

A 𝜌-homomorphism 𝜓:𝑀→𝑀′ with 𝜓(ℳ︀𝑛)⊆ℳ︀𝑛′ for every 𝑛≥0 is taut if there is a strictly increasing sequence 𝑛0<𝑛1<𝑛2<⋯ in ℕ such that

𝑀′=𝜓(𝑀)+ℳ︀𝑛0′andℳ︀𝑛𝑁′=𝜓(ℳ︀𝑛𝑁)+ℳ︀𝑛𝑁+1′for every𝑁≥0.

(Taking 𝑀≔𝐴, 𝑀′≔𝐴′ as filtered 𝐴-, 𝐴′-modules over themselves and 𝜓≔𝜌 recovers (3.14.10).)

Remark 3.14.12
The name is borrowed, by analogy, from the taut ring maps of linearly topologized rings in [Stacks] (Tag 0AMX) – itself flagged there as nonstandard terminology – where an analogous (though not identical) condition, combined with density and completeness, is used to conclude surjectivity via an open mapping theorem ([Stacks] Tag 0APT) rather than the direct successive-approximation argument used here.
Proposition 3.14.13 (Refinement Criterion)

Let 𝔞•, 𝔞•′ be filtrations of a ring 𝐴 (3.14.1), with metrics 𝑑1(𝑥,𝑦)≔2−sup{𝑛:𝑥−𝑦∈𝔞𝑛} and 𝑑2(𝑥,𝑦)≔2−sup{𝑛:𝑥−𝑦∈𝔞𝑛′} on 𝐴 (sup∅≔−1). The following are equivalent:

  1. 1𝐴:(𝐴,𝔞•)→(𝐴,𝔞•′) is continuous (3.14.5)
  2. 𝔞• is cofinal in 𝔞•′: for every 𝑚≥0 there is 𝑛≥0 with 𝔞𝑛⊆𝔞𝑚′;
  3. 1𝐴:(𝐴,𝑑1)→(𝐴,𝑑2) is continuous at 0 in the metric-space sense;
  4. the topology on 𝐴 induced by 𝑑2 is weaker than the one induced by 𝑑1: every 𝑑2-open subset of 𝐴 is 𝑑1-open.

Proof. Drafted by Claude Sonnet, reviewed by David Rufino.

a) ⟺ b) is (3.14.5) applied to 𝜙≔1𝐴.

b) ⟹ c): given 𝜀>0, pick 𝑚≥0 with 2−𝑚≤𝜀; by b) at 𝑚+1, pick 𝑛≥0 with 𝔞𝑛⊆𝔞𝑚+1′, and set 𝛿≔2−(𝑛−1) (any 𝛿>0 if 𝑛=0). If 𝑑1(𝑥,0)<𝛿 then 𝑥∈𝔞𝑛⊆𝔞𝑚+1′, so 𝑑2(𝑥,0)≤2−𝑚≤𝜀.

c) ⟹ b): given 𝑚≥0, apply c) with 𝜀≔2−𝑚 to get 𝛿>0 with 𝑑1(𝑥,0)<𝛿⟹𝑑2(𝑥,0)≤2−𝑚; picking 𝑛≥0 with 2−(𝑛−1)<𝛿 (any 𝑛≥1 if 𝛿 exceeds every such bound) gives 𝔞𝑛⊆{𝑥:𝑑1(𝑥,0)<𝛿}⊆{𝑥:𝑑2(𝑥,0)≤2−𝑚}=𝔞𝑚′.

c) ⟹ d): both 𝑑1,𝑑2 are translation-invariant (𝑑𝑖(𝑥+𝑧,𝑦+𝑧)=𝑑𝑖(𝑥,𝑦), immediate as (𝑥+𝑧)−(𝑦+𝑧)=𝑥−𝑦), so continuity of 1𝐴:(𝐴,𝑑1)→(𝐴,𝑑2) at 0 gives continuity at every point 𝑥0∈𝐴: given 𝜀>0, take 𝛿>0 as in c); if 𝑑1(𝑥,𝑥0)<𝛿 then 𝑑1(𝑥−𝑥0,0)<𝛿, so 𝑑2(𝑥−𝑥0,0)<𝜀, that is 𝑑2(𝑥,𝑥0)<𝜀. Continuity of 1𝐴 at every point is exactly d) (the preimage, under the identity map, of a 𝑑2-open set is itself, and it is 𝑑1-open).

d) ⟹ c): a 𝑑2-ball around 0 is 𝑑2-open, hence 𝑑1-open by d), hence contains a 𝑑1-ball around 0 (as 0 lies in it); this is exactly continuity of 1𝐴 at 0. ∎

Proposition 3.14.14 (Equivalence Criterion)

Let 𝔞•, 𝔞•′ be filtrations of a ring 𝐴 (3.14.1), with metrics 𝑑1, 𝑑2 as in (3.14.13). The following are equivalent:

  1. 1𝐴:(𝐴,𝔞•)→(𝐴,𝔞•′) is bicontinuous, that is 1𝐴 is continuous both as a map (𝐴,𝔞•)→(𝐴,𝔞•′) and as a map (𝐴,𝔞•′)→(𝐴,𝔞•) (3.14.5)
  2. 𝔞• and 𝔞•′ are cofinal in each other (3.14.13)
  3. 𝑑1 and 𝑑2 are equivalent metrics on 𝐴: 1𝐴 is bicontinuous at 0 as a map (𝐴,𝑑1)→(𝐴,𝑑2);
  4. 𝑑1 and 𝑑2 induce the same topology on 𝐴.

Proof. Drafted by Claude Sonnet, reviewed by David Rufino.

Applying (3.14.13) to the pair (𝔞•,𝔞•′) gives the equivalence of “𝔞• cofinal in 𝔞•′”, “1𝐴:(𝐴,𝔞•)→(𝐴,𝔞•′) continuous”, “1𝐴:(𝐴,𝑑1)→(𝐴,𝑑2) continuous at 0”, and “every 𝑑2-open set is 𝑑1-open”; applying it to the reversed pair (𝔞•′,𝔞•) gives the equivalence of the four corresponding statements with the roles of 1,2 exchanged. Conjoining each pair of equivalences (an “if and only if” together with its reverse) gives exactly a) ⟺ b) ⟺ c) ⟺ d), the last as “every 𝑑2-open set is 𝑑1-open, and every 𝑑1-open set is 𝑑2-open” is exactly the two topologies having the same open sets. ∎

Lemma 3.14.15 (Continuity Criterion for Ideal-Power Filtrations)
Let 𝐴, 𝐵 be rings with the 𝔞-adic and 𝔟-adic filtrations (3.14.4), for an ideal 𝔞◃𝐴 and a radical ideal 𝔟◃𝐵 (3.3.36). Then a ring homomorphism 𝜙:𝐴→𝐵 is continuous (3.14.5) if and only if 𝜙(𝔞)⊆𝔟.

Proof. Drafted by Claude Sonnet, reviewed by David Rufino.

If 𝜙(𝔞)⊆𝔟: for every 𝑘≥1, 𝜙(𝔞𝑘) is generated (as an ideal of 𝐵) by products of 𝑘 elements of 𝜙(𝔞)⊆𝔟, hence 𝜙(𝔞𝑘)⊆𝔟𝑘; so, given 𝑚, taking 𝑛≔𝑚 gives 𝜙(𝔞𝑛)=𝜙(𝔞𝑛+1)⊆𝔟𝑚+1=𝔟𝑚: 𝜙 is continuous.

Conversely, suppose 𝜙 is continuous; taking 𝑚≔0 gives some 𝑛 with 𝜙(𝔞𝑛+1)⊆𝔟. If 𝜙(𝔞)¬⊆𝔟, pick 𝑎∈𝔞 with 𝜙(𝑎)∉𝔟; as 𝔟 is radical, its defining property 𝑥𝑛+1∈𝔟⟹𝑥∈𝔟 contraposes to 𝜙(𝑎)∉𝔟⟹𝜙(𝑎)𝑛+1∉𝔟. But 𝜙(𝑎)𝑛+1=𝜙(𝑎𝑛+1)∈𝜙(𝔞𝑛+1)⊆𝔟, as 𝑎𝑛+1∈𝔞𝑛+1: a contradiction. So 𝜙(𝔞)⊆𝔟. ∎

By (3.14.27), there is a unique continuous 𝜌-homomorphism Ψ:𝑀̂→𝑀̂′ with Ψ∘𝜄=𝜄𝑀′∘𝜓; set 𝜓̂≔𝜄𝑀′−1∘Ψ, continuous as a composite, with 𝜓̂∘𝜄=𝜄𝑀′−1∘Ψ∘𝜄=𝜄𝑀′−1∘𝜄𝑀′∘𝜓=𝜓: the triangle commutes.

Uniqueness. If 𝜓̂1,𝜓̂2:𝑀̂→𝑀′ are both continuous 𝜌-homomorphisms with 𝜓̂𝑖∘𝜄=𝜓, then 𝜄𝑀′∘𝜓̂𝑖:𝑀̂→𝑀̂′ are both continuous with (𝜄𝑀′∘𝜓̂𝑖)∘𝜄=𝜄𝑀′∘𝜓; by uniqueness in (3.14.27), 𝜄𝑀′∘𝜓̂1=𝜄𝑀′∘𝜓̂2, and as 𝜄𝑀′ is injective, 𝜓̂1=𝜓̂2.

Surjectivity criterion. If 𝜓 is taut, (3.14.27)‘s own surjectivity criterion gives Ψ surjective, hence 𝜓̂=𝜄𝑀′−1∘Ψ surjective (𝜄𝑀′−1 an isomorphism).

Injectivity criterion. Under the stated hypotheses, (3.14.27)‘s own injectivity criterion gives Ψ injective, hence 𝜓̂=𝜄𝑀′−1∘Ψ injective. ∎

Remark 3.14.29
