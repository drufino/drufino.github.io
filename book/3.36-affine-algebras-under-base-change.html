3.36 Affine Algebras under Base Change

This section covers what happens to a 𝑘-algebra 𝐴 under base change, that is 𝐴(𝐿) for 𝐿/𝑘 a field extension. Principally given an integral 𝑘-algebra 𝐴 we would like to ensure that 𝐴(𝐿) is reduced (which is related to separability), irreducible and therefore integral. We follow [Stacks] but large parts of exposition are from [Bou89].

Definition 3.36.1 (Geometrically Reduced / Integral Algebra)

A 𝑘-algebra 𝐴 is

  • geometrically integral (resp. irreducible, reduced) if the ring 𝐴⊗𝑘𝐿 is integral (resp. irreducible, reduced) for every field extension 𝐿/𝑘.
  • algebraically integral (resp. irreducible, reduced) if the ring 𝐴⊗𝑘𝑘― is integral (resp. irreducible, reduced).

A few remarks about the important results and notation in the literature

  • As in (3.3.65) we see that integral ⟺ irreducible and reduced.
  • These are shown to be equivalent (3.36.26), (3.36.36) and (3.36.44). In fact it is sufficient to check the properties for all finite extensions.
  • [Liu06] uses the “algebraic” form
  • [Bou89] uses the term “separable” for “geometrically reduced”, as this coincides with the notion of separable for algebraic extensions (3.36.19)
  • Similarly [Bou89] and [Lan11] use the term “regular” for geometrically integral

Proposition 3.36.3 (A Subspace is Recovered by Intersection After Base Change)

Let 𝑉⊆𝑊 be 𝑘-vector spaces and 𝑘′/𝑘 a field extension. Then, identifying 𝑊 with its image in 𝑊⊗𝑘𝑘′ via 𝑤↦𝑤⊗1 (injective, (3.4.31)),

(𝑉⊗𝑘𝑘′)∩𝑊=𝑉

inside 𝑊⊗𝑘𝑘′.

Proof. Drafted by Claude Sonnet 5, reviewed by David Rufino.

“⊇” is clear. For “⊆”: choose a 𝑘-subspace complement 𝑉′ of 𝑉 in 𝑊 ((3.3.138), 𝑘 being a field), so 𝑊=𝑉⊕𝑉′; tensoring, 𝑊⊗𝑘𝑘′≅(𝑉⊗𝑘𝑘′)⊕(𝑉′⊗𝑘𝑘′) (3.4.21). Given 𝑤∈𝑊 with 𝑤⊗1∈𝑉⊗𝑘𝑘′: writing 𝑤=𝑣+𝑣′ (𝑣∈𝑉, 𝑣′∈𝑉′) for its decomposition, 𝑤⊗1=(𝑣⊗1)+(𝑣′⊗1) under this direct sum splitting; as 𝑤⊗1 lies in the first summand, the second component vanishes, that is 𝑣′⊗1=0; as 𝑣′↦𝑣′⊗1 is injective (3.4.31), 𝑣′=0, giving 𝑤=𝑣∈𝑉. ∎

[𝐴:𝑘]𝑠≔#︎AlgHom𝑘(𝐴,𝑘―)

We say that 𝐴 is etale if this equals the dimension of 𝐴 as a 𝑘-vector space

Recall a finite field extension 𝐾/𝑘 is etale if and only if it is separable (3.19.92).

Lemma 3.36.7

Let 𝐴 be a finite-dimensional 𝑘-algebra and 𝐾/𝑘 a field extension. Then

[Hom𝑘(𝐴,𝐾):𝐾]=[𝐴∨:𝑘]=[𝐴:𝑘]

More precisely if {𝑎1,…,𝑎𝑛} is a 𝑘-basis for 𝐴 then {𝑎1∨,…,𝑎𝑛∨} is a 𝐾-basis for Hom𝑘(𝐴,𝐾).

Proof. This is simply a special case of (3.4.27). ∎

Let 𝐴 be a finite-dimensional 𝑘-algebra then #︎AlgHom𝑘(𝐴,𝐾)≤[𝐴:𝑘] for every field extension 𝐾/𝑘.
Proposition 3.36.9 (Image of $A$ is finite-dimensional)

Let 𝐴 be a finite-dimensional 𝑘-algebra and Ω/𝑘 a field extension. Then there exists a finite subextension 𝐾/𝑘 such that

AlgHom𝑘(𝐴,𝐾)=AlgHom𝑘(𝐴,Ω)

If Ω contains 𝑘― then these are both equal to AlgHom𝑘(𝐴,𝑘―).

Proof. By (…) 𝜙(𝐴) has finite degree over 𝑘 and therefore finitely-generated and algebraic as a 𝑘-algebra. Therefore 𝜙(𝐴)=𝑘[𝑎1,…,𝑎𝑛]=𝑘(𝑎1,…,𝑎𝑛) is a finite-dimensional field by (3.19.58).

We may take the compositum of all these images (since there are only finitely many) to obtain the finite extension 𝐾/𝑘.

If Ω contains 𝑘― then trivially

AlgHom𝑘(𝐴,𝐾)⊆AlgHom𝑘(𝐴,𝑘―)⊆AlgHom𝑘(𝐴,Ω)

whence they are all equal. ∎

Proposition 3.36.10 (Etale-ness is preserved under base extension)

Let 𝐴 be a finite-dimensional 𝑘-algebra and 𝐾/𝑘 a field extension then

[𝐴⊗𝑘𝐾:𝐾]=[𝐴:𝑘][𝐴⊗𝑘𝐾:𝐾]𝑠=[𝐴:𝑘]𝑠

Proof. The first equality follows from (3.4.26) and (3.3.108).

For the second equality we see that by (3.36.9) and (3.4.38)

AlgHom𝑘(𝐴,𝑘―)=AlgHom𝑘(𝐴,𝐾―)≅AlgHom𝐾(𝐴⊗𝑘𝐾,𝐾―)

 ∎

Let 𝐴=𝐴1×⋯×𝐴𝑑 be a finite product of finite-dimensional 𝑘-algebras. Then 𝐴 is etale if and only if each 𝐴𝑖 is.

Proof. This follows because

and a similar consideration for the dimension over 𝑘. ∎

Lemma 3.36.12

Let 𝐴 be a finite-dimensional 𝑘-algebra. The following are equivalent

  1. 𝐴 is reduced
  2. 𝐴≅𝐿1×⋯×𝐿𝑛 for some finite extensions 𝐿𝑖/𝑘

Further 𝐴 is etale if and only if each 𝐿𝑖/𝑘 is separable. If 𝑘 is perfect then 𝐴 is automatically etale.

Proof. One direction is obvious. Suppose conversely that 𝐴 is reduced. If dim𝑘𝐴=1 then any proper ideal must have strictly lower dimension and therefore 𝐴 is a field. It is therefore sufficient to show (by induction on dimension) that if 𝐴 is not a field then 𝐴≅𝐴1×𝐴2 for two (non-zero) 𝑘-algebras 𝐴1,𝐴2.

Suppose 𝐴 is not a field and let 𝔞 be a non-zero proper ideal of 𝐴 which has minimal dimension over 𝑘 as a vector space. As 𝐴 is reduced, and by minimality, we have 𝔞2=𝔞. By Nakayama’s Lemma (3.21.5) there is 𝑒∈𝔞 such that 𝑒𝑥=𝑥 for all 𝑥∈𝔞 and in particular 𝑒2=𝑒 and 𝔞=𝐴𝑒. By assumption 𝑒≠0,1. Define 𝑓≔1−𝑒≠0,1 then evidently 𝑓2=𝑓 and 𝑒𝑓=0. The homomorphism of 𝐴-modules

𝐴→𝐴𝑒×𝐴𝑓𝑎→(𝑎𝑒,𝑎𝑓)

is injective because 𝑎𝑒+𝑎𝑓=𝑎 and surjective because the image of 𝑎1𝑒+𝑎2𝑓 is (𝑎1𝑒,𝑎2𝑓). The sub-modules 𝐴𝑒 and 𝐴𝑓 are actually sub-rings by the idempotence property and the given map is an isomorphism of rings.

The last statement follows from (3.36.11) and (3.19.117). ∎

Corollary 3.36.13 (Primes of a Finite Product of Fields)
Let 𝐴≅𝐿1×⋯×𝐿𝑛 be a finite product of fields (in particular, any reduced finite-dimensional 𝑘-algebra, (3.36.12)). Then every prime ideal of 𝐴 is maximal, and there are exactly 𝑛 of them.

Proof. For each 𝑗, 𝔪𝑗≔ker(𝜋𝑗) (the kernel of the 𝑗-th projection 𝜋𝑗:𝐴↠𝐿𝑗) is maximal, being the kernel of a surjection onto a field (3.3.58), and the 𝔪𝑗 are pairwise distinct (the element with 1 in coordinate 𝑗 and 0 elsewhere lies in every 𝔪𝑖, 𝑖≠𝑗, but not 𝔪𝑗), giving 𝑛 maximal ideals.

Let 𝔭 be any prime. As ⋂𝑗𝔪𝑗=ker(𝜋1,…,𝜋𝑛)=0⊆𝔭, (3.3.39) gives 𝔪𝑗⊆𝔭 for some 𝑗; as 𝔪𝑗 is already maximal, 𝔭=𝔪𝑗. ∎

  2. 𝐴⊗𝑘Ω≅Ω𝑛 as an Ω-algebra

Proposition 3.36.15 (A Field Splits Completely Over Any Normal Extension it Embeds Into)

Let 𝐾/𝑘 be a finite separable field extension and 𝐿/𝑘 a field extension containing (a copy of) a normal closure of 𝐾/𝑘. Fix a primitive element 𝜃 for 𝐾/𝑘 ((3.19.119)), with minimal polynomial 𝑚∈𝑘[𝑋] of degree 𝑛≔[𝐾:𝑘], and write 𝑚=∏𝑖=1𝑛(𝑋−𝛽𝑖) for its (pairwise distinct) roots 𝛽𝑖∈𝐿. Then there is a canonical isomorphism of 𝐿-algebras

𝐾⊗𝑘𝐿≅𝐿𝑛,𝑝(𝜃)⊗𝑐↦(𝑐𝑝(𝛽1),…,𝑐𝑝(𝛽𝑛))(𝑝∈𝑘[𝑋],𝑐∈𝐿)

In particular 𝐾⊗𝑘𝐿 has exactly 𝑛 maximal ideals, 𝔪𝑖 the kernel of the 𝑖-th coordinate.

Proof. As 𝐾/𝑘 is separable, 𝑚 is separable (3.19.88). A normal closure 𝑁 of 𝐾/𝑘 is, by definition, the splitting field of 𝑚 over 𝑘 (3.19.87), so 𝑚 splits completely already over 𝑁; as 𝑁⊆𝐿 (by hypothesis), the same factorisation exhibits 𝑚 splitting completely over 𝐿 too, giving the 𝛽𝑖∈𝑁⊆𝐿, pairwise distinct as 𝑚 is separable. As 𝐾≅𝑘[𝑋]/(𝑚) (3.19.54) (𝜃 corresponding to the class of 𝑋), base-changing along 𝑘↪︎𝐿 (using 𝐾⊗𝑘𝐿≅(𝑘[𝑋]⊗𝑘𝐿)/(𝑚) as 𝐿-algebras, (3.4.42), and 𝑘[𝑋]⊗𝑘𝐿≅𝐿[𝑋], (3.8.7)) gives 𝐾⊗𝑘𝐿≅𝐿[𝑋]/(𝑚); as the (𝑋−𝛽𝑖) are pairwise comaximal (distinct monic linear factors), the Chinese Remainder Theorem (3.3.61) gives

𝐾⊗𝑘𝐿≅𝐿[𝑋]/(𝑚)≅∏𝑖=1𝑛𝐿[𝑋]/(𝑋−𝛽𝑖)≅𝐿𝑛

Tracing the elementary tensor 𝑝(𝜃)⊗𝑐 through: it corresponds to 𝑐⋅𝑝(𝑋)mod𝑚∈𝐿[𝑋]/(𝑚) ((3.4.42), (3.8.7)), which under the Chinese Remainder Theorem’s canonical map – reduction modulo each (𝑋−𝛽𝑖), that is evaluation at 𝛽𝑖 – corresponds to (𝑐𝑝(𝛽1),…,𝑐𝑝(𝛽𝑛)). The maximal ideals of 𝐿𝑛 are exactly the 𝑛 coordinate kernels (3.36.13). ∎

Lemma 3.36.35 (Purely Inseparable Extensions of Scalars are Homeomorphisms)

Let 𝑘 be a field with characteristic exponent 𝑝, 𝐴 a 𝑘-algebra and 𝑘′/𝑘 a purely inseparable field extension. Consider the structural algebra homomorphism

𝜑:𝐴→𝐴⊗𝑘𝑘′

𝔮𝑐←𝔮

If 𝜑 were surjective then we would have equality 𝔮𝑐𝑒=𝔮 and which would imply the map of prime ideals is injective. However we note that 𝜑 is “almost” surjective, namely for every 𝑧=∑𝑖=1𝑁𝑎𝑖⊗𝜆𝑖 there exists 𝑛≥0 such that 𝑧𝑝𝑛∈𝜑(𝐴). This allows us to argue the map is injective for suppose 𝔭≔𝔮𝑐=(𝔮′)𝑐 and 𝑧∈𝔮∖𝔮′. Then by the previous argument 𝑧𝑝𝑛=𝜑(𝑥)⟹𝑧𝑝𝑛∈𝔮⟹𝑧𝑝𝑛∈𝔮′⟹𝑧∈𝔮′ which is a contradiction. Therefore contraction of prime ideals is injective.

Every 𝑠∈𝒪︀𝑘′∖𝔭𝑗 then satisfies 𝑠∉𝔮=𝔪𝑅∩𝒪︀𝑘′, that is 𝑠∉𝔪𝑅, so 𝑠 is a unit of 𝑅; by the universal property of localisation, 𝒪︀𝑗 then embeds in 𝑅. As 𝔪𝒪︀𝑗=𝜋𝒪︀𝑗 and 𝜋∈𝔪𝑅, 𝔪𝒪︀𝑗⊆𝔪𝑅∩𝒪︀𝑗; conversely 𝔪𝑅∩𝒪︀𝑗 is a proper ideal of the local ring 𝒪︀𝑗, hence ⊆𝔪𝒪︀𝑗: so 𝔪𝒪︀𝑗=𝔪𝑅∩𝒪︀𝑗, that is 𝑅 dominates 𝒪︀𝑗 (3.25.4). As 𝒪︀𝑗 is a valuation ring, it is maximal under domination (3.25.13), so 𝑅=𝒪︀𝑗.

e) 𝒪︀𝑗 restricts to 𝒪︀. As 𝒪︀𝑘′⊂𝐹𝑘′ is a domain, 𝒪︀𝑗 is the subring of 𝐹𝑘′ of fractions 𝑎/𝑠 with 𝑎∈𝒪︀𝑘′, 𝑠∈𝒪︀𝑘′∖𝔭𝑗, so 𝒪︀⊂𝒪︀𝑘′⊂𝒪︀𝑗. Moreover 𝔪⊆𝔪𝒪︀𝑘′=𝔟⊆𝔭𝑗 (a)), so 𝔪⊆𝔭𝑗𝒪︀𝑗=𝔪𝒪︀𝑗 (3.6.42): and 𝔭𝑗𝒪︀𝑗 is a proper ideal. Since 𝒪︀ is a valuation ring of 𝐹, (3.25.6) (with 𝐵=𝒪︀𝑗, 𝔫=𝔭𝑗𝒪︀𝑗) gives 𝒪︀𝑗∩𝐹=𝒪︀ and 𝔭𝑗𝒪︀𝑗∩𝐹=𝔪. Combined with c) this shows that the valuation rings 𝑅 of 𝐹𝑘′ with 𝑘′⊂𝑅, 𝒪︀⊂𝑅 and 𝑅∩𝐹=𝒪︀ are precisely 𝒪︀1,…,𝒪︀𝑟. ∎

Remark 3.36.48 (Relation to the Stacks Project)
