6.3.3 Meromorphic Differentials in the case 𝑘=ℂ

In this section we aim to provide a post-hoc motivation for the introduction of Weil Differentials, by showing that they are canonically isomorphic to the classical space of Meromorphic Differentials in the case 𝑘=ℂ. This relies on some techniques which are specific to the complex-analytic structure of Riemann Surfaces.

Proposition 6.3.18 (Residue Theorem for 𝑘=ℂ)

Let 𝑝 be a point of a Riemann surface and 𝜋 a local holomorphic coordinate centered at 𝑝. For 𝑓 meromorphic near 𝑝, define

Res𝜋(𝑓)≔Res𝑝(𝑓d𝜋)≔12𝜋𝑖∮𝛾𝑓d𝜋

for a small loop 𝛾 around 𝑝 – the classical residue of the 1-form 𝑓d𝜋. Writing 𝑔≔𝑓∘𝜓 for the re-expansion of 𝑓 relative to 𝜋′=𝜓−1(𝜋), and 𝑓=∑𝑛𝑎𝑛𝜋𝑛 for the Laurent series expansion of 𝑓 at 𝑝, then

  1. Res𝜋 is 𝑘-linear in 𝑓;
  2. Res𝜋(𝑓)=0 whenever 𝑓 is holomorphic at 𝑝 (i.e. has no pole there);
  3. Res𝜋(𝑓)=𝑎−1;
  4. Res𝜋′(𝑔⋅𝜓′)=Res𝜋(𝑓);

  5. if 𝑝 ranges over a compact Riemann surface 𝑋 and 𝜔 is a meromorphic 1-form on 𝑋 (so has only finitely many poles), then, writing 𝜔𝜋 for the coefficient of 𝜔 relative to a local coordinate 𝜋 at 𝑝 (so 𝜔=𝜔𝜋d𝜋 near 𝑝, and Res𝜋(𝜔𝜋)=Res𝑝(𝜔) by definition – independent of the choice of 𝜋 at each 𝑝, by d)),

    ∑𝑝Res𝜋(𝜔𝜋)=0

Proof. Drafted by Claude Sonnet 5, reviewed by David Rufino.

a). Immediate from linearity of the contour integral, ∮𝛾(𝛼𝑓+𝛽ℎ)d𝜋=𝛼∮𝛾𝑓d𝜋+𝛽∮𝛾ℎd𝜋 for 𝛼,𝛽∈𝑘.

b). Immediate from c) below: 𝑓 holomorphic at 𝑝 means 𝑎𝑛=0 for every 𝑛<0, in particular 𝑎−1=0, so Res𝜋(𝑓)=𝑎−1=0.

c). Term-by-term integration (valid by uniform convergence of the Laurent series on 𝛾) gives ∮𝛾𝑓d𝜋=∑𝑛𝑎𝑛∮𝛾𝜋𝑛d𝜋, and ∮𝛾𝜋𝑛d𝜋=2𝜋𝑖𝛿𝑛,−1 (𝑛≠−1: exact, 𝜋𝑛d𝜋=d(𝜋𝑛+1𝑛+1), so integrates to 0 around a closed loop; 𝑛=−1: winding number 1 around 𝜋=0), so Res𝜋(𝑓)=12𝜋𝑖∮𝛾𝑓d𝜋=𝑎−1.

d). By the chain rule, 𝑓d𝜋=𝑓(𝜓(𝜋′))𝜓′(𝜋′)d𝜋′=(𝑔⋅𝜓′)d𝜋′: these are the same 1-form 𝜔, written in the two coordinates 𝜋 and 𝜋′. The contour integral ∮𝛾𝜔 depends only on 𝜔 and 𝛾, not on the coordinate used to write it down – reparametrising 𝛾 by 𝜋′ instead of 𝜋 changes nothing about which loop is being integrated over – so Res𝑝(𝜔) computed from either expression gives the same number:

Res𝜋′(𝑔⋅𝜓′)=Res𝑝((𝑔⋅𝜓′)d𝜋′)=Res𝑝(𝜔)=Res𝑝(𝑓d𝜋)=Res𝜋(𝑓)

e). This uses two facts special to the compact setting, neither purely local (unlike a)–d) above): 𝜔 is closed away from its poles (𝜔=𝑓d𝑧 locally with 𝑓 holomorphic, so d𝜔=𝜕𝑓𝜕𝑧―d𝑧―∧d𝑧=0 by the Cauchy–Riemann equations), and 𝑋 is compact. Removing a small disk 𝐷𝑝 around each pole leaves 𝑋0≔𝑋∖⋃𝑝𝐷𝑝, on which 𝜔 is holomorphic, so d𝜔=0 there; Stokes’ theorem gives

0=∫𝑋0d𝜔=∫𝜕𝑋0𝜔=−∑𝑝∮𝜕𝐷𝑝𝜔=−∑𝑝2𝜋𝑖Res𝜋(𝜔𝜋)

(the sign, as 𝜕𝑋0 traverses each 𝜕𝐷𝑝 oppositely to the usual counterclockwise orientation used to define Res𝜋, and ∮𝜕𝐷𝑝𝜔=2𝜋𝑖Res𝜋(𝜔𝜋) by definition), giving ∑𝑝Res𝜋(𝜔𝜋)=0. ∎

Proposition 6.3.19 (Classical Differentials are Weil Differentials)

Let 𝑋 be a compact Riemann surface with function field 𝐾 (so 𝑘=ℂ), and let ℳ︀1(𝑋) denote the ℂ-vector space of meromorphic 1-forms on 𝑋. For 𝜔∈ℳ︀1(𝑋), define 𝜑𝜔∈Hom𝑘(𝒜︀𝐾,𝑘) by

𝜑𝜔(𝛼)≔∑𝑃Res𝜋(𝛼𝑃⋅𝜔𝜋),𝛼=(𝛼𝑃)𝑃∈𝒜︀𝐾

(a finite sum, by b): 𝛼𝑃𝜔𝜋 is holomorphic, hence contributes 0, at all but finitely many 𝑃). Then 𝜑𝜔∈Ω𝐾, and

ℳ︀1(𝑋)⟶Ω𝐾,𝜔↦𝜑𝜔

is a canonical ℂ-linear isomorphism.

Proof. Drafted by Claude Sonnet 5, reviewed by David Rufino.

𝜑𝜔∈Ω𝐾. Let 𝐷≔(𝜔), the divisor of 𝜔 (well-defined: 𝑣𝑃(𝜔𝜋) is independent of the choice of 𝜋 at 𝑃, as 𝜔𝜋′=(𝜔𝜋∘𝜓)⋅𝜓′ by the chain rule – the same identity underlying d) – and 𝜓′ is a unit, so composition with 𝜓 and multiplication by 𝜓′ both preserve order of vanishing). We show 𝒜︀𝐾(𝐷)+𝐾⊂ker(𝜑𝜔). For 𝛼∈𝒜︀𝐾(𝐷), 𝑣𝑃(𝛼𝑃)≥−𝑣𝑃(𝐷)=−𝑣𝑃(𝜔𝜋) for every 𝑃, so 𝑣𝑃(𝛼𝑃𝜔𝜋)≥0: each 𝛼𝑃𝜔𝜋 is holomorphic at 𝑃, so Res𝜋(𝛼𝑃𝜔𝜋)=0 by b), giving 𝜑𝜔(𝛼)=0. For 𝑓∈𝐾 (embedded diagonally, 𝛼𝑃=𝑓 for every 𝑃), 𝜑𝜔(𝑓,𝑓,…)=∑𝑃Res𝜋(𝑓⋅𝜔𝜋)=∑𝑃Res𝜋((𝑓𝜔)𝜋)=0 by e) (the Residue Theorem), applied to the meromorphic 1-form 𝑓𝜔∈ℳ︀1(𝑋). So 𝜑𝜔 vanishes on 𝒜︀𝐾(𝐷)+𝐾, that is 𝜑𝜔∈Ω𝐾(𝐷)⊂Ω𝐾.

ℂ-linearity. Immediate from a): 𝜑𝜆𝜔1+𝜇𝜔2(𝛼)=∑𝑃Res𝜋(𝛼𝑃(𝜆𝜔1+𝜇𝜔2)𝜋)=𝜆𝜑𝜔1(𝛼)+𝜇𝜑𝜔2(𝛼) for 𝜆,𝜇∈ℂ, using that (𝜆𝜔1+𝜇𝜔2)𝜋=𝜆(𝜔1)𝜋+𝜇(𝜔2)𝜋 (coefficients relative to a fixed 𝜋 add linearly) and linearity of Res𝜋 in its argument.

Injectivity. Suppose 𝜑𝜔=0; we show 𝜔=0. Fix 𝑃 and, for 𝑛∈ℤ, let 𝛼(𝑛)∈𝒜︀𝐾 be the repartition with 𝛼𝑃(𝑛)≔𝜋−𝑛 and 𝛼𝑄(𝑛)≔0 for 𝑄≠𝑃 (a valid repartition: it is holomorphic away from the single place 𝑃). Writing 𝜔𝜋=∑𝑚𝑎𝑚𝜋𝑚, 0=𝜑𝜔(𝛼(𝑛))=Res𝜋(𝜋−𝑛𝜔𝜋)=𝑎𝑛−1 by c). As 𝑛 was arbitrary, every coefficient of 𝜔𝜋 vanishes, so 𝜔 is 0 near 𝑃; as 𝑃 was arbitrary, 𝜔≡0 on 𝑋.

Surjectivity. Sketch. This needs the classical Mittag-Leffler theorem for meromorphic differentials: given principal parts (polar Laurent tails) prescribed at finitely many points of 𝑋, satisfying the residue-sum-zero constraint of e), there exists a global meromorphic 1-form on 𝑋 realising them – solved via the 𝜕―-equation (Hodge theory / finite-dimensionality of 𝐻1(𝑋,𝒪︀)), genuinely global analytic content with no counterpart among a)–e) above. Given 𝜓∈Ω𝐾(𝐷), read off, at each pole 𝑃 of 𝐷, the local principal part 𝜓 demands by testing against the single-place repartitions 𝛼𝑃(𝑛)=𝜋−𝑛 used in the injectivity argument above; since 𝜓 vanishes on 𝐾 by construction, these prescribed principal parts automatically satisfy the residue-sum-zero compatibility condition Mittag-Leffler requires, and it produces a global 𝜔 realising them, with 𝜑𝜔=𝜓. ∎
