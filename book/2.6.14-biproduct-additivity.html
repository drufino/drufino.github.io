2.6.14 Biproduct Additivity

Following [Fre64] [Lan98], our eventual goal is to show that in a pointed category with (binary) biproducts, each hom-set Mor(𝑋,𝑌) carries a canonical addition, making composition bilinear – so that additivity, usually imposed as an axiom, is instead a theorem, derived here purely from the existence of biproducts, via matrix notation.

Proposition 2.6.114 (Composition Without Cross Terms)

Let 𝒞︀ be a pointed category with binary biproducts, hence all finite biproducts (2.6.105), 𝐼,𝐽 finite sets, 𝜎:𝐼→𝐽 a function, and 𝑀:⨁𝑗∈𝐽𝑋𝑗→⨁𝑖∈𝐼𝑌𝑖 a morphism whose matrix satisfies 𝑀𝑖𝑗=0 for every 𝑖∈𝐼, 𝑗≠𝜎(𝑖). Then for any 𝑁:⨁𝑘∈𝐾𝑊𝑘→⨁𝑗∈𝐽𝑋𝑗 with matrix (𝑁𝑗𝑘)

(𝑀∘𝑁)𝑖𝑘=𝑀𝑖,𝜎(𝑖)∘𝑁𝜎(𝑖),𝑘for all𝑖∈𝐼,𝑘∈𝐾

Dually, if 𝜏:𝐼→𝐽 and 𝑀′:⨁𝑖∈𝐼𝑋𝑖→⨁𝑗∈𝐽𝑌𝑗 has matrix satisfying 𝑀𝑗𝑖′=0 for every 𝑖∈𝐼, 𝑗≠𝜏(𝑖), then for any 𝑁:⨁𝑗∈𝐽𝑌𝑗→⨁𝑘∈𝐾𝑍𝑘 with matrix (𝑁𝑘𝑗)

(𝑁∘𝑀′)𝑘𝑖=𝑁𝑘,𝜏(𝑖)∘𝑀𝜏(𝑖),𝑖′for all𝑘∈𝐾,𝑖∈𝐼

Proof. Drafted by Claude Sonnet 5, reviewed by David Rufino.

For 𝑖∈𝐼 and any 𝑗∈𝐽, 𝑎𝑖∘𝜋𝜎(𝑖)∘𝑠𝑗=𝑎𝑖∘𝛿𝜎(𝑖),𝑗 (using 𝜋𝜎(𝑖)∘𝑠𝑗=𝛿𝜎(𝑖),𝑗 (2.6.103)), which is 𝑎𝑖=𝑀𝑖,𝜎(𝑖) if 𝑗=𝜎(𝑖) and 0=𝑀𝑖𝑗 otherwise (by hypothesis on 𝑀); this matches 𝜋𝑖∘𝑀∘𝑠𝑗=𝑀𝑖𝑗 for every 𝑗∈𝐽, so by the uniqueness clause of (2.6.93) (applied to maps out of ⨁𝑗∈𝐽𝑋𝑗), 𝜋𝑖∘𝑀=𝑎𝑖∘𝜋𝜎(𝑖).

Hence, for every 𝑖∈𝐼, 𝑘∈𝐾

𝜋𝑖∘𝑀∘𝑁∘𝑠𝑘=𝑎𝑖∘𝜋𝜎(𝑖)∘𝑁∘𝑠𝑘=𝑎𝑖∘𝑁𝜎(𝑖),𝑘

giving the matrix of 𝑀∘𝑁.

Dually, for 𝑖∈𝐼 and any 𝑗∈𝐽, 𝜋𝑗∘𝑠𝜏(𝑖)∘𝑐𝑖=𝛿𝑗,𝜏(𝑖)𝑐𝑖, which matches 𝜋𝑗∘𝑀′∘𝑠𝑖=𝑀𝑗𝑖′ for every 𝑗∈𝐽 (equal to 𝑐𝑖 if 𝑗=𝜏(𝑖) and 0 otherwise, by hypothesis), so by the uniqueness clause of (2.6.81) (applied to maps into ⨁𝑗∈𝐽𝑌𝑗), 𝑀′∘𝑠𝑖=𝑠𝜏(𝑖)∘𝑐𝑖; hence, for every 𝑘∈𝐾, 𝑖∈𝐼

𝜋𝑘∘𝑁∘𝑀′∘𝑠𝑖=𝜋𝑘∘𝑁∘𝑠𝜏(𝑖)∘𝑐𝑖=𝑁𝑘,𝜏(𝑖)∘𝑐𝑖

giving the matrix of 𝑁∘𝑀′. ∎

Definition 2.6.116 (Diagonal and Codiagonal)

Let 𝒞︀ be a pointed category with binary biproduct 𝑋⊕𝑋, with injections 𝑠1,𝑠2 and projections 𝜋1,𝜋2. Regarding 𝑋 as the trivial biproduct of {𝑋}, (2.6.108) identifies Mor(𝑋,𝑋⊕𝑋) with 2×1 matrices and Mor(𝑋⊕𝑋,𝑋) with 1×2 matrices over Mor(𝑋,𝑋). The diagonal Δ𝑋:𝑋→𝑋⊕𝑋 and codiagonal (or summation map) ∇𝑋:𝑋⊕𝑋→𝑋 are the morphisms with matrices

Δ𝑋=(1𝑋1𝑋)∇𝑋=(1𝑋1𝑋)

that is, the unique morphisms with 𝜋1∘Δ𝑋=𝜋2∘Δ𝑋=1𝑋 and ∇𝑋∘𝑠1=∇𝑋∘𝑠2=1𝑋 respectively.

Remark 2.6.117
In 𝐀𝐛𝐆𝐫𝐩, where 𝑋⊕𝑋 is the direct sum 𝑋×𝑋 with 𝑠1(𝑥)=(𝑥,0), 𝑠2(𝑥)=(0,𝑥), 𝜋1(𝑥,𝑦)=𝑥, 𝜋2(𝑥,𝑦)=𝑦, the codiagonal is exactly the addition map: ∇𝑋(𝑥,0)=𝑥 and ∇𝑋(0,𝑦)=𝑦 force, by additivity of the homomorphism ∇𝑋, ∇𝑋(𝑥,𝑦)=∇𝑋(𝑥,0)+∇𝑋(0,𝑦)=𝑥+𝑦. Dually the diagonal is Δ𝑋(𝑥)=(𝑥,𝑥).
Definition 2.6.118 (Sum of Parallel Morphisms)

Let 𝒞︀ be a pointed category with binary biproducts and 𝑓,𝑔:𝑋→𝑌. We define

𝑓+𝑔≔(𝑓𝑔)∘Δ𝑋=∇𝑌∘(𝑓00𝑔)∘Δ𝑋=∇𝑌∘(𝑓𝑔):𝑋→𝑌

the three expressions agreeing by (2.6.114), since (𝑓00𝑔) has no cross terms.

Proposition 2.6.119 (Interchange Law)
Let 𝒞︀ be a pointed category with binary biproducts and 𝑤,𝑥,𝑦,𝑧:𝑋→𝑌. Then (𝑤+𝑥)+(𝑦+𝑧)=(𝑤+𝑦)+(𝑥+𝑧).

Proof. Drafted by Claude Sonnet 5, reviewed by David Rufino. Following [Fre64] Theorem 2.38.

Let 𝑀≔(𝑤𝑥𝑦𝑧):𝑋⊕𝑋→𝑌⊕𝑌 (2.6.108). Then

𝑀∘Δ𝑋=(2.6.112)((𝑤𝑥)(𝑦𝑧))∘Δ𝑋=(2.6.113)((𝑤𝑥)∘Δ𝑋(𝑦𝑧)∘Δ𝑋)=(2.6.118)(𝑤+𝑥𝑦+𝑧)∇𝑌∘𝑀=(2.6.112)∇𝑌∘((𝑤𝑦)(𝑥𝑧))=(2.6.113)(∇𝑌∘(𝑤𝑦)∇𝑌∘(𝑥𝑧))=(2.6.118)(𝑤+𝑦𝑥+𝑧)

so, applying ∇𝑌∘− to the first display and −∘Δ𝑋 to the second (each a further instance of (2.6.118))

(𝑤+𝑥)+(𝑦+𝑧)=∇𝑌∘(𝑀∘Δ𝑋)=(∇𝑌∘𝑀)∘Δ𝑋=(𝑤+𝑦)+(𝑥+𝑧)

 ∎

Remark 2.6.120 (Eckmann--Hilton Phenomenon)
The Interchange Law is an instance of the classical Eckmann–Hilton argument: whenever a set carries two unital binary operations ∗,· satisfying the interchange law (𝑎∗𝑏)·(𝑐∗𝑑)=(𝑎·𝑐)∗(𝑏·𝑑) and sharing a common unit, the two operations coincide and are commutative and associative. Here the two operations are the row-first and column-first ways of combining the block (𝑤𝑥𝑦𝑧) into a single morphism via Δ𝑋,∇𝑌; (2.6.118) already records that they coincide (as a single well-defined +), and (2.6.119) is exactly the interchange law for it. Commutativity and associativity, proved below, are the usual Eckmann–Hilton consequences, recovered by the substitutions 𝑤=𝑧≔0 and 𝑦≔0 respectively.
Proposition 2.6.121 (Mor(𝑋,𝑌) is a Commutative Monoid, and Composition is Bilinear)

Let 𝒞︀ be a pointed category with binary biproducts. For objects 𝑋,𝑌, (Mor(𝑋,𝑌),+) is a commutative monoid with identity 0. Moreover, for 𝑓,𝑔:𝑋→𝑌, ℎ:𝑌→𝑍 and 𝑘:𝑊→𝑋

ℎ∘(𝑓+𝑔)=ℎ∘𝑓+ℎ∘𝑔(𝑓+𝑔)∘𝑘=𝑓∘𝑘+𝑔∘𝑘

Proof. Drafted by Claude Sonnet 5, reviewed by David Rufino.

Identity. Since (𝑓0) and (0𝑓) each have a single nonzero entry, (2.6.114) gives

𝑓+0=(𝑓0)∘(1𝑋1𝑋)=𝑓∘1𝑋=𝑓0+𝑓=(0𝑓)∘(1𝑋1𝑋)=𝑓∘1𝑋=𝑓

Commutativity. By (2.6.119), setting 𝑤≔0, 𝑥≔𝑓, 𝑦≔𝑔, 𝑧≔0 gives (0+𝑓)+(𝑔+0)=(0+𝑔)+(𝑓+0), that is 𝑓+𝑔=𝑔+𝑓 using the identity just proved.

Associativity. For a third morphism ℎ:𝑋→𝑌, setting 𝑤≔𝑓, 𝑥≔𝑔, 𝑦≔0, 𝑧≔ℎ in (2.6.119) gives (𝑓+𝑔)+(0+ℎ)=(𝑓+0)+(𝑔+ℎ), that is (𝑓+𝑔)+ℎ=𝑓+(𝑔+ℎ).

Bilinearity.

ℎ∘(𝑓+𝑔)=(2.6.118)ℎ∘(𝑓𝑔)∘Δ𝑋=(2.6.113)(ℎ∘𝑓ℎ∘𝑔)∘Δ𝑋=(2.6.118)ℎ∘𝑓+ℎ∘𝑔

and dually

(𝑓+𝑔)∘𝑘=(2.6.118)∇𝑌∘(𝑓𝑔)∘𝑘=(2.6.113)∇𝑌∘(𝑓∘𝑘𝑔∘𝑘)=(2.6.118)𝑓∘𝑘+𝑔∘𝑘

 ∎

Proposition 2.6.122 (Composition is Matrix Multiplication)

Let 𝒞︀ be a pointed category with binary biproducts, 𝐼,𝐽,𝐾 finite sets, 𝑀:⨁𝑗∈𝐽𝑋𝑗→⨁𝑖∈𝐼𝑌𝑖 with matrix (𝑀𝑖𝑗) and 𝑁:⨁𝑘∈𝐾𝑊𝑘→⨁𝑗∈𝐽𝑋𝑗 with matrix (𝑁𝑗𝑘). Then

(𝑀∘𝑁)𝑖𝑘=∑𝑗∈𝐽𝑀𝑖𝑗∘𝑁𝑗𝑘for all𝑖∈𝐼,𝑘∈𝐾

Proof. Drafted by Claude Sonnet 5, reviewed by David Rufino.

We first show that any column 𝐶:𝑍→⨁𝑗∈𝐽𝑋𝑗, with entries 𝐶𝑗≔𝜋𝑗∘𝐶, satisfies 𝐶=∑𝑗∈𝐽𝑠𝑗∘𝐶𝑗. Indeed, for each 𝑖∈𝐽, by (2.6.121) (bilinearity, iterated over the finite sum)

𝜋𝑖∘∑𝑗∈𝐽𝑠𝑗∘𝐶𝑗=∑𝑗∈𝐽𝜋𝑖∘𝑠𝑗∘𝐶𝑗=∑𝑗∈𝐽𝛿𝑖𝑗𝐶𝑗=𝐶𝑖

using 𝜋𝑖∘𝑠𝑗=𝛿𝑖𝑗 (2.6.103) and that every term with 𝑗≠𝑖 vanishes (composing with the zero morphism), so this matches the matrix of 𝐶 entrywise, whence ∑𝑗∈𝐽𝑠𝑗∘𝐶𝑗=𝐶 by (2.6.108). Dually, any row 𝑅:⨁𝑖∈𝐼𝑌𝑖→𝑍 satisfies 𝑅=∑𝑖∈𝐼𝑅𝑖∘𝜋𝑖, where 𝑅𝑖≔𝑅∘𝑠𝑖.

Now fix 𝑖∈𝐼, 𝑘∈𝐾. The 𝑘-th column of 𝑁 is 𝑁∘𝑠𝑘, with entries 𝑁𝑗𝑘=𝜋𝑗∘𝑁∘𝑠𝑘, so by the column decomposition, 𝑁∘𝑠𝑘=∑𝑗∈𝐽𝑠𝑗∘𝑁𝑗𝑘. Hence, using bilinearity to distribute 𝑀∘− and then 𝜋𝑖∘− over the finite sum

(𝑀∘𝑁)𝑖𝑘=𝜋𝑖∘𝑀∘𝑁∘𝑠𝑘=𝜋𝑖∘𝑀∘∑𝑗∈𝐽𝑠𝑗∘𝑁𝑗𝑘=∑𝑗∈𝐽𝜋𝑖∘𝑀∘𝑠𝑗∘𝑁𝑗𝑘=∑𝑗∈𝐽𝑀𝑖𝑗∘𝑁𝑗𝑘

 ∎
