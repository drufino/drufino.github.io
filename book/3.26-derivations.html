3.26 Derivations

Definition 3.26.1 (Module of Derivations)

Let 𝐵 be a commutative ring and 𝑀 an (𝐵,𝐶)-bimodule, then we say a derivation is a map 𝐷:𝐵→𝑀 satisfying the following properties

  • 𝐷(𝑥+𝑦)=𝐷(𝑥)+𝐷(𝑦) linearity
  • 𝐷(𝑥𝑦)=𝑥𝐷(𝑦)+𝑦𝐷(𝑥) product rule

We denote the family of such derivations by Der(𝐵,𝑀), and it is an (𝐵,𝐶)-bimodule. We may consider the case 𝑀=𝐶 and 𝜙:𝐵→𝐵 a ring homomorphism and in this case the product rule becomes

𝐷(𝑥𝑦)=𝜙(𝑥)𝐷(𝑦)+𝐷(𝑥)𝜙(𝑦)

We observe that by induction we have 𝐷(𝑛⋅1𝐵)=0 for all 𝑛∈ℤ.

Suppose that 𝐵 is an 𝐴-algebra then we claim the following are equivalent

  • 𝐷(𝑎)=0∀𝑎∈𝐴
  • 𝐷(𝑎𝑏)=𝑎𝐷(𝑏)∀𝑎∈𝐴

In this case we denote the set of 𝐴-linear derivations by

Der𝐴(𝐵,𝑀)

Note every derivation is ℤ-linear so we may work with 𝐴=ℤ in these cases. Finally we write

Der𝐴(𝐵)≔Der𝐴(𝐵,𝐵)
