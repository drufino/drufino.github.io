3.35.1 Etale Algebras

Definition 3.35.6

Let 𝐴 be a finite-dimensional 𝑘-algebra. Then define the separable degree as follows

[𝐴:𝑘]𝑠≔#︎AlgHom𝑘(𝐴,𝑘―)

We say that 𝐴 is etale if this equals the dimension of 𝐴 as a 𝑘-vector space

[𝐴:𝑘]𝑠=[𝐴:𝑘]

Recall a finite field extension 𝐾/𝑘 is etale if and only if it is separable (3.18.78).

Lemma 3.35.7

Let 𝐴 be a finite-dimensional 𝑘-algebra and 𝐾/𝑘 a field extension. Then

[Hom𝑘(𝐴,𝐾):𝐾]=[𝐴∨:𝑘]=[𝐴:𝑘]

More precisely if {𝑎1,…,𝑎𝑛} is a 𝑘-basis for 𝐴 then {𝑎1∨,…,𝑎𝑛∨} is a 𝐾-basis for Hom𝑘(𝐴,𝐾).

Proof. This is simply a special case of (3.4.27). ∎
Corollary 3.35.8 (Separable degree is finite)
Let 𝐴 be a finite-dimensional 𝑘-algebra then #︎AlgHom𝑘(𝐴,𝐾)≤[𝐴:𝑘] for every field extension 𝐾/𝑘.
Proposition 3.35.9 (Image of $A$ is finite-dimensional)

Let 𝐴 be a finite-dimensional 𝑘-algebra and Ω/𝑘 a field extension. Then there exists a finite subextension 𝐾/𝑘 such that

AlgHom𝑘(𝐴,𝐾)=AlgHom𝑘(𝐴,Ω)

If Ω contains 𝑘― then these are both equal to AlgHom𝑘(𝐴,𝑘―).

Proof. By (…) 𝜙(𝐴) has finite degree over 𝑘 and therefore finitely-generated and algebraic as a 𝑘-algebra. Therefore 𝜙(𝐴)=𝑘[𝑎1,…,𝑎𝑛]=𝑘(𝑎1,…,𝑎𝑛) is a finite-dimensional field by (3.18.44).

We may take the compositum of all these images (since there are only finitely many) to obtain the finite extension 𝐾/𝑘.

If Ω contains 𝑘― then trivially

AlgHom𝑘(𝐴,𝐾)⊆AlgHom𝑘(𝐴,𝑘―)⊆AlgHom𝑘(𝐴,Ω)

whence they are all equal. ∎

Proposition 3.35.10 (Etale-ness is preserved under base extension)

Let 𝐴 be a finite-dimensional 𝑘-algebra and 𝐾/𝑘 a field extension then

[𝐴⊗𝑘𝐾:𝐾]=[𝐴:𝑘][𝐴⊗𝑘𝐾:𝐾]𝑠=[𝐴:𝑘]𝑠

In particular 𝐴 is etale if and only if 𝐴⊗𝑘𝐾 is.

Proof. The first equality follows from (3.4.26) and (3.3.109).

For the second equality we see that by (3.35.9) and (3.4.38)

AlgHom𝑘(𝐴,𝑘―)=AlgHom𝑘(𝐴,𝐾―)≅AlgHom𝐾(𝐴⊗𝑘𝐾,𝐾―)

 ∎

Lemma 3.35.11
Let 𝐴=𝐴1×⋯×𝐴𝑑 be a finite product of finite-dimensional 𝑘-algebras. Then 𝐴 is etale if and only if each 𝐴𝑖 is.

Proof. This follows because

AlgHom𝑘(𝐴1×⋯×𝐴𝑑,𝑘―)≅AlgHom𝑘(𝐴1,𝑘―)×⋯×AlgHom𝑘(𝐴𝑑,𝑘―)

and a similar consideration for the dimension over 𝑘. ∎

Lemma 3.35.12

Let 𝐴 be a finite-dimensional 𝑘-algebra. The following are equivalent

  1. 𝐴 is reduced
  2. 𝐴≅𝐿1×⋯×𝐿𝑛 for some finite extensions 𝐿𝑖/𝑘

Further 𝐴 is etale if and only if each 𝐿𝑖/𝑘 is separable. If 𝑘 is perfect then 𝐴 is automatically etale.

Proof. One direction is obvious. Suppose conversely that 𝐴 is reduced. If dim𝑘𝐴=1 then any proper ideal must have strictly lower dimension and therefore 𝐴 is a field. It is therefore sufficient to show (by induction on dimension) that if 𝐴 is not a field then 𝐴≅𝐴1×𝐴2 for two (non-zero) 𝑘-algebras 𝐴1,𝐴2.

Suppose 𝐴 is not a field and let 𝔞 be a non-zero proper ideal of 𝐴 which has minimal dimension over 𝑘 as a vector space. As 𝐴 is reduced, and by minimality, we have 𝔞2=𝔞. By Nakayama’s Lemma (3.20.5) there is 𝑒∈𝔞 such that 𝑒𝑥=𝑥 for all 𝑥∈𝔞 and in particular 𝑒2=𝑒 and 𝔞=𝐴𝑒. By assumption 𝑒≠0,1. Define 𝑓≔1−𝑒≠0,1 then evidently 𝑓2=𝑓 and 𝑒𝑓=0. The homomorphism of 𝐴-modules

𝐴→𝐴𝑒×𝐴𝑓𝑎→(𝑎𝑒,𝑎𝑓)

is injective because 𝑎𝑒+𝑎𝑓=𝑎 and surjective because the image of 𝑎1𝑒+𝑎2𝑓 is (𝑎1𝑒,𝑎2𝑓). The sub-modules 𝐴𝑒 and 𝐴𝑓 are actually sub-rings by the idempotence property and the given map is an isomorphism of rings.

The last statement follows from (3.35.11) and (3.18.103). ∎

Corollary 3.35.13 (Primes of a Finite Product of Fields)
Let 𝐴≅𝐿1×⋯×𝐿𝑛 be a finite product of fields (in particular, any reduced finite-dimensional 𝑘-algebra, (3.35.12)). Then every prime ideal of 𝐴 is maximal, and there are exactly 𝑛 of them.

Proof. For each 𝑗, 𝔪𝑗≔ker(𝜋𝑗) (the kernel of the 𝑗-th projection 𝜋𝑗:𝐴↠𝐿𝑗) is maximal, being the kernel of a surjection onto a field (3.3.59), and the 𝔪𝑗 are pairwise distinct (the element with 1 in coordinate 𝑗 and 0 elsewhere lies in every 𝔪𝑖, 𝑖≠𝑗, but not 𝔪𝑗), giving 𝑛 maximal ideals.

Let 𝔭 be any prime. As ⋂𝑗𝔪𝑗=ker(𝜋1,…,𝜋𝑛)=0⊆𝔭, (3.3.40) gives 𝔪𝑗⊆𝔭 for some 𝑗; as 𝔪𝑗 is already maximal, 𝔭=𝔪𝑗. ∎

Lemma 3.35.14

Let 𝐴 be a finite-dimensional 𝑘-algebra and Ω/𝑘 a field extension containing 𝑘―. Then the following are equivalent

  1. 𝐴 is etale
  2. 𝐴⊗𝑘Ω≅Ω𝑛 as an Ω-algebra

Proof. 𝑎)⟹𝑏) By (3.35.9) #︎AlgHom𝑘(𝐴,Ω)=#︎AlgHom𝑘(𝐴,𝑘―)≕[𝐴:𝑘]𝑠=[𝐴:𝑘] with the last equality because 𝐴 is assumed etale. Then defining 𝐵≔𝐴⊗𝑘Ω

#︎AlgHomΩ(𝐵,Ω)=(3.4.38)#︎AlgHom𝑘(𝐴,Ω)=[𝐴:𝑘]=(3.4.16)[𝐵:Ω]=[𝐵∨:Ω]

By (3.18.130) and (3.3.127) we see that AlgHomΩ(𝐵,Ω) is a Ω-basis for 𝐵∨. Denote this basis by 𝜑1,…,𝜑𝑛 then by (3.3.109) this corresponds to a basis 𝑒1,…,𝑒𝑛 of 𝐵. Then the algebra isomorphism is exhibited explicitly by

𝐵≅Ω𝑛𝑏→(𝜑1(𝑏),…,𝜑𝑛(𝑏))

𝑏)⟹𝑎) We may show that the algebra homomorphisms are just the projections 𝜋𝑖:Ω𝑛→Ω, so that #︎AlgHomΩ(𝐵,Ω)=[𝐵:Ω]. As before then [𝐴:𝑘]𝑠=#︎AlgHom𝑘(𝐴,𝑘―)=#︎AlgHom𝑘(𝐴,Ω)=#︎AlgHomΩ(𝐵,Ω)=[𝐵:Ω]=[𝐴:𝑘] which shows that 𝐴 is etale. ∎

Proposition 3.35.15 (A Field Splits Completely Over Any Normal Extension it Embeds Into)

Let 𝐾/𝑘 be a finite separable field extension and 𝐿/𝑘 a field extension containing (a copy of) a normal closure of 𝐾/𝑘. Fix a primitive element 𝜃 for 𝐾/𝑘 (3.18.105), with minimal polynomial 𝑚∈𝑘[𝑋] of degree 𝑛≔[𝐾:𝑘], and write 𝑚=∏𝑖=1𝑛(𝑋−𝛽𝑖) for its (pairwise distinct) roots 𝛽𝑖∈𝐿. Then there is a canonical isomorphism of 𝐿-algebras

𝐾⊗𝑘𝐿≅𝐿𝑛,𝑝(𝜃)⊗𝑐↦(𝑐𝑝(𝛽1),…,𝑐𝑝(𝛽𝑛))(𝑝∈𝑘[𝑋],𝑐∈𝐿)

In particular 𝐾⊗𝑘𝐿 has exactly 𝑛 maximal ideals, 𝔪𝑖 the kernel of the 𝑖-th coordinate.

Proof. As 𝐾/𝑘 is separable, 𝑚 is separable (3.18.74). A normal closure 𝑁 of 𝐾/𝑘 is, by definition, the splitting field of 𝑚 over 𝑘 (3.18.73), so 𝑚 splits completely already over 𝑁; as 𝑁⊆𝐿 (by hypothesis), the same factorisation exhibits 𝑚 splitting completely over 𝐿 too, giving the 𝛽𝑖∈𝑁⊆𝐿, pairwise distinct as 𝑚 is separable. As 𝐾≅𝑘[𝑋]/(𝑚) (3.18.40) (𝜃 corresponding to the class of 𝑋), base-changing along 𝑘↪︎𝐿 (using 𝐾⊗𝑘𝐿≅(𝑘[𝑋]⊗𝑘𝐿)/(𝑚) as 𝐿-algebras, (3.4.42), and 𝑘[𝑋]⊗𝑘𝐿≅𝐿[𝑋], (3.7.8)) gives 𝐾⊗𝑘𝐿≅𝐿[𝑋]/(𝑚); as the (𝑋−𝛽𝑖) are pairwise comaximal (distinct monic linear factors), the Chinese Remainder Theorem (3.3.62) gives

𝐾⊗𝑘𝐿≅𝐿[𝑋]/(𝑚)≅∏𝑖=1𝑛𝐿[𝑋]/(𝑋−𝛽𝑖)≅𝐿𝑛

Tracing the elementary tensor 𝑝(𝜃)⊗𝑐 through: it corresponds to 𝑐⋅𝑝(𝑋)mod𝑚∈𝐿[𝑋]/(𝑚) (3.4.42), (3.7.8), which under the Chinese Remainder Theorem’s canonical map – reduction modulo each (𝑋−𝛽𝑖), that is evaluation at 𝛽𝑖 – corresponds to (𝑐𝑝(𝛽1),…,𝑐𝑝(𝛽𝑛)). The maximal ideals of 𝐿𝑛 are exactly the 𝑛 coordinate kernels (3.35.13). ∎

Proposition 3.35.16 (Etale Criteria)

Let 𝐴 be a finite-dimensional 𝑘-algebra. The following are equivalent

  1. 𝐴 is etale
  2. 𝐴 is geometrically reduced
  3. 𝐴⊗𝑘𝑘― is reduced
  4. 𝐴⊗𝑘𝐾 is reduced for some perfect field extension 𝐾/𝑘
  5. 𝐴≅𝐿1×⋯×𝐿𝑛 for 𝐿𝑖/𝑘 finite separable extensions

In particular 𝐴 is reduced.

Proof. 𝑎)⟹𝑏) Consider a field extension 𝐿/𝑘 and the tower of extensions 𝐿―/𝐿/𝑘. Then by (3.35.14) 𝐴⊗𝑘𝐿―≅𝐿―𝑛 is evidently reduced. By (…) 𝐴⊗𝑘𝐿⊂𝐴⊗𝑘𝐿― is a subring and therefore also reduced.

𝑏)⟹𝑐) immediate.

𝑐)⟹𝑑) immediate as 𝑘― is perfect.

𝑑)⟹𝑎) By (3.35.12) 𝐴⊗𝑘𝐾 is an etale 𝐾-algebra so by (3.35.10) 𝐴 is etale.

𝑒)⟹𝑎) Follows immediately from (3.35.11).

𝑎)⟹𝑒) We know that 𝐴 is reduced so the result follows from (3.35.12). ∎

Corollary 3.35.17

Let 𝐴 be a finite-dimensional 𝑘-algebra and 𝑘 perfect. Then the following are equivalent

  1. 𝐴 is etale
  2. 𝐴 is reduced
  3. 𝐴 is geometrically reduced
  4. 𝐴⊗𝑘𝑘― is reduced.

For completeness we summarize the relationship between the different notions of separability

Corollary 3.35.18 (Equivalent definitions of separability)

Let 𝐾/𝑘 be a finite extension. Then the following are equivalent

  1. 𝐾/𝑘 is separable algebraic (3.18.51)
  2. 𝐾/𝑘 is etale
  3. 𝐾/𝑘 is geometrically reduced
  4. 𝐾⊗𝑘𝑘― is reduced

When 𝑘 is perfect every field extension is separable.

Proof. We have already shown 𝑏)⟺𝑐)⟺𝑑). Furthermore 𝑎)⟺𝑏) is (3.18.78). ∎
Corollary 3.35.19 (Equivalent definitions of separability)

Let 𝐾/𝑘 be an algebraic extension. Then the following are equivalent

  1. 𝐾/𝑘 is separable algebraic (3.18.51)
  2. Every finite subextension of 𝐾/𝑘 is etale
  3. 𝐾/𝑘 is geometrically reduced
  4. 𝐾⊗𝑘𝑘― is reduced

In particular an algebraic extension of a perfect field 𝑘 is geometrically reduced.

Proof. This follows directly from (3.35.18) because each property has “finite character”, i.e. holds if and only if it holds for every finite subextension.

The last statement follows from (3.18.103). ∎
