5.8.6 Product Measure

Definition 5.8.32 (Product of 𝜎-algebra)

Let (Ω1,ℱ︀1),…,(Ω𝑛,ℱ︀𝑛) be a family of measurable spaces. Then define the product measurable space (Ω1×…×Ω𝑛,ℱ︀1⊗…⊗ℱ︀𝑛) to be given by

ℱ︀1⊗…⊗ℱ︀𝑛≔𝜎(ℱ︀1×…×ℱ︀𝑛)

and

ℱ︀1×…×ℱ︀𝑛≔{𝐴1×…×𝐴𝑛|𝐴𝑖∈ℱ︀𝑖}
Definition 5.8.33 (Product Measure)

Let (Ω𝑖,ℱ︀𝑖,𝜇𝑖) be a family of 𝜎-finite measures for 𝑖=1…𝑛. Then we say a measure

𝜇1⊗…⊗𝜇𝑛:ℱ︀1⊗…⊗ℱ︀𝑛→ℝ♯

such that

𝜇(𝐴1×…×𝐴𝑛)=𝜇1(𝐴1)⋅…⋅𝜇𝑛(𝐴𝑛)

is called the product measure space.

We defer proof of existence and uniqueness as it relies on theory of integration, but nevertheless prove the finitely additive case and then the case of ℝ𝑛 in the next section.

Lemma 5.8.34

Let ℛ︀1,…,ℛ︀𝑛 be semi-rings. Then the family

ℛ︀1×…×ℛ︀𝑛={𝐴1×…×𝐴𝑛|𝐴𝑖∈ℛ︀𝑖}

is a semi-ring.

Proof. We may reduce to the case of 𝑛=2. Then observe that

(𝐴1×𝐵1)∖(𝐴2×𝐵2)=(𝐴1∖𝐴2)×(𝐵1∖𝐵2)⊔(𝐴1∩𝐴2)×(𝐵1∖𝐵2)⊔(𝐴1∖𝐴2)×(𝐵1∩𝐵2)

 ∎

Lemma 5.8.35

Let ℛ︀ be a semi-ring and 𝐴1,…,𝐴𝑛∈ℛ︀ a family of subsets. Then there exists a family of disjoint subsets 𝒮︀≔{𝐵1,…,𝐵𝑁}⊂ℛ︀ such that 𝒮︀=𝒮︀1∪…∪𝒮︀𝑛 and

𝐴𝑖=⊔𝐵∈𝒮︀𝑖𝐵

Proof. For a tuple 𝜀≔(𝜀1,…,𝜀𝑛) for which 𝜀𝑖=±1 define the family of disjoint sets

𝐴𝜀≔𝐴1𝜀1∩…∩𝐴𝑛𝜀𝑛

where 𝐴𝑖1≔𝐴𝑖 and 𝐴𝑖−1≔𝐴𝑖𝑐. By (5.8.6) 𝐴𝜀 may be expressed as the disjoint union of some sets in ℛ︀, say 𝒮︀𝜀. Then 𝒮︀=∪𝜀𝒮︀𝜀 consists of pairwise disjoint sets, where we consider tuples 𝜀 for which at least one entry is positive. Further

𝐴𝑖=⊔𝜀:𝜀𝑖=1𝐴𝜀

therefore we may define 𝒮︀𝑖≔∪𝜀:𝜀𝑖=1𝒮︀𝜀. Note throughout we may omit 𝜀 for which 𝐴𝜀=∅. ∎

Lemma 5.8.36

Let {(Ω𝑖,ℛ︀𝑖,𝜇𝑖)}𝑖=1…𝑛 be a collection of finitely additive set functions on semi-rings. The set function

𝜇:ℛ︀1×…×ℛ︀𝑛→ℝ∪{∞}𝐴1×…×𝐴𝑛→𝜇1(𝐴1)…𝜇𝑛(𝐴𝑛)

is finitely additive, and we write 𝜇≕𝜇1×…×𝜇𝑛. Moreover if each 𝜇𝑖 is 𝜎-finite then so is 𝜇.

Proof. By induction it’s enough to consider the case 𝑛=2. Suppose that

𝐴𝑖=𝐵𝑖×𝐶𝑖𝑖=0,…,𝑛𝐴0=⊔𝑖=1𝑛𝐴𝑖

for 𝐵𝑖∈Ω1 and 𝐶𝑖∈Ω2. By (5.8.35) we may write

𝐵𝑖≔⊔𝐵̂∈𝒮︀𝑖𝐵̂𝐶𝑖≔⊔𝐶̂∈𝒯︀𝑖𝐶̂

where 𝒮︀𝑖 and 𝒯︀𝑖 are finite subsets of ℛ︀1 and ℛ︀2 respectively. Evidently

𝐵𝑖×𝐶𝑖=⊔(𝐵̂,𝐶̂)∈𝒮︀𝑖×𝒯︀𝑖𝐵̂×𝐶̂

and 𝒮︀0=∪𝑖=1𝑛𝒮︀𝑖 and 𝒯︀0≔∪𝑖=1𝑛𝒯︀𝑖. As 𝜇1 and 𝜇2 are finitely additive then

𝜇1(𝐵𝑖)=∑𝐵̂∈𝒮︀𝑖𝜇1(𝐵̂)𝜇2(𝐶𝑖)=∑𝐶̂∈𝒯︀𝑖𝜇2(𝐶̂)∑𝑖=1𝑛𝜇(𝐴𝑖)=∑𝑖=1𝑛𝜇1(𝐵𝑖)𝜇2(𝐶𝑖)=∑𝑖=1𝑛∑(𝐵̂,𝐶̂)∈𝒮︀𝑖×𝒯︀𝑖𝜇1(𝐵̂)𝜇2(𝐶̂)𝜇(𝐴0)=𝜇1(𝐵0)𝜇2(𝐶0)=∑(𝐵̂,𝐶̂)∈𝒮︀0×𝒯︀0𝜇1(𝐵̂)𝜇2(𝐶̂)

As the sets 𝐵𝑖×𝐶𝑖 are disjoint we may deduce that

𝒮︀0×𝒯︀0=⊔𝑖=1𝑛𝒮︀𝑖×𝒯︀𝑖

from which the result follows. ∎
