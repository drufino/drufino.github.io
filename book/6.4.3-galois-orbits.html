6.4.3 Galois Orbits

Proposition 6.4.17

Let 𝑋=𝑉+(𝔞)⊂ℙ𝑘𝑛 be a projective variety and 𝐾/𝑘 a normal algebraic extension (e.g. 𝑘―). Then there is a well-defined group action

Aut(𝐾/𝑘)×𝑋(𝐾)→𝑋(𝐾)(𝜎,(𝑥0:…:𝑥𝑛))→(𝜎(𝑥0):…:𝜎(𝑥𝑛))

which commutes with the affine charts

𝜎(𝜃𝑖(𝑥))=𝜃𝑖(𝜎(𝑥))∀𝑥∈𝑈𝑖(𝐾)∩𝑋(𝐾)

Write 𝑋0(𝐾) for the set of equivalence classes under this action. The projection map

𝜋:𝑋(𝐾)→𝑋0(𝐾)

is the Kolmogorov Quotient (4.1.36). In particular points 𝑥,𝑦∈𝑋(𝐾) are topologically indistinguishable iff they are Galois conjugate. Further closed (resp. open) subsets are Galois-equivariant.

Proof. First consider the case 𝑋=ℙ𝑘𝑛. If (𝑥0:…:𝑥𝑛)=(𝑥0′:…:𝑥𝑛′) then 𝑥𝑖=𝜆𝑖𝑥𝑖′ for 𝜆𝑖∈𝐾⋆. Then 𝜎(𝑥𝑖)=𝜎(𝜆𝑖)𝜎(𝑥𝑖′) so (𝜎(𝑥0):…:𝜎(𝑥𝑛))∼(𝜎(𝑥0′):…:𝜎(𝑥𝑛′)) and the action is well-defined. For 𝑥∈𝑋(𝐾) write 𝜎(𝑥). Then 𝜏(𝜎(𝑥))=(𝜏∘𝜎)(𝑥) so it is a group action.

For the general case if 𝐹(𝑥)=0 for 𝐹∈𝑘[𝑋0,…,𝑋𝑛] then 𝐹(𝜎(𝑥))=0. Therefore 𝑥∈𝑉(𝔞)(𝐾)⟹𝜎(𝑥)∈𝑉(𝔞)(𝐾) and the action is well-defined on 𝑋(𝐾).

We need to show that two points 𝑥,𝑦∈𝑋(𝐾) are conjugate by Aut(𝐾/𝑘) iff they are topologically indistinguishable. Suppose 𝑥=𝜎(𝑦) and 𝑥∈𝑉(𝔟). Then evidently 𝑦∈𝑉(𝔟). Conversely if they are topologically indistinguishable then we may suppose 𝑥,𝑦∈𝑈𝑖. Then 𝜃𝑖(𝑥),𝜃𝑖(𝑦) are also indistinguishable, so by (6.1.9) they are Galois conjugate, and so 𝜃𝑖(𝑥)=𝜃𝑖(𝜎(𝑦)). As 𝜃𝑖 is bijective we deduce 𝑥=𝜎(𝑦) as required. ∎
