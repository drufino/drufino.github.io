6.3.2 Algebraic Function Fields

We develop the basic theory of algebraic function fields of one variable, the field-theoretic counterpart to a regular algebraic curve.

Proposition 6.3.5
Let 𝐾/𝑘 be a function field and 𝑘⊊𝒪︀𝑃⊊𝐾 a valuation ring. Then 𝑘̃⊂𝒪︀𝑃 and 𝑘̃∩𝔪𝑃=(0)
Proof. By definition 𝑘̃ is the integral closure of 𝑘 in 𝐾, so 𝑘̃⊂𝒪︀𝑃 by (3.23.14). Suppose 0≠𝑥∈𝑘̃ then 𝑥 is invertible in 𝒪︀𝑃 and therefore 𝑥 is not contained in any proper ideal, and in particular 𝑥∉𝔪𝑃. ∎
Proposition 6.3.6 (Residue Degree of a Place)

Let 𝐾/𝑘 be an algebraic function field and 𝑓∈𝐾∖𝑘̃. Then

  1. 𝐾/𝑘(𝑓) is finite.

  2. If 𝒪︀𝑃 is a place with 𝑓∈𝒪︀𝑃, then 𝔭≔𝔪𝑃∩𝑘[𝑓] is a maximal ideal of 𝑘[𝑓], the field 𝑘(𝔭)≔𝑘[𝑓]/𝔭 is finite over 𝑘, and

    [𝑘(𝔪𝑃):𝑘(𝔭)]≤[𝐾:𝑘(𝑓)]
  3. Consequently the residue field 𝑘(𝔪𝑃) of every place 𝑃 of 𝐾/𝑘, whether or not it contains 𝑓, is a finite extension of 𝑘.

Proof. Drafted by Claude Sonnet, reviewed by David Rufino.

a) As 𝑓 is not algebraic over 𝑘 and trdeg(𝐾/𝑘)=1 (6.3.1), 𝑓 is a transcendence base (3.17.142), so 𝐾/𝑘(𝑓) is algebraic and finitely generated, hence finite (3.17.44).

b) The ring 𝑘[𝑓] is a polynomial ring, hence a PID (3.14.4), and 𝔭 is a prime ideal of it contained in 𝔪𝑃. As 𝒪︀𝑃≠𝐾 is not a field, 𝔪𝑃≠0, and every element of 𝒪︀𝑃⊂𝐾 is algebraic over 𝑘(𝑓) by a), so 𝔭≠0 (3.22.17). So 𝔭 is a non-zero prime ideal of a PID, hence maximal, and 𝔭=(𝑔(𝑓)) for an irreducible polynomial 𝑔. Thus 𝑘(𝔭)≅𝑘[𝑋]/(𝑔) is finite over 𝑘 (3.17.40). Further 𝑘[𝑓]𝔭 is a discrete valuation ring (3.29.4), in particular a PID, and 𝒪︀𝑃 is an overring of 𝑘[𝑓] with quotient field 𝐾 containing the proper ideal 𝔪𝑃⊇𝔭. So (3.30.18) applied to 𝑘[𝑓]⊂𝒪︀𝑃 gives the inequality.

c) As 𝒪︀𝑃≠𝐾 is not a field, there is 0≠𝑥∈𝔪𝑃. Then 𝑥∉𝑘̃ by (6.3.5), so b) applies to 𝑥, and [𝑘(𝔪𝑃):𝑘]=[𝑘(𝔪𝑃):𝑘(𝔭)][𝑘(𝔭):𝑘] is finite. ∎

In order to use the theory of Dedekind domains and affine algebras we establish a connection between valuation rings and a certain “affine” subring.

Proposition 6.3.7

Let 𝐾/𝑘 be an algebraic function field and 𝑓∈𝐾∖𝑘̃ transcendental. Then the integral closure 𝑘[𝑓]― in 𝐾 is a Dedekind domain with quotient field 𝐾 and a finite 𝑘[𝑓]-module. There is a correspondence

{𝔅◃𝑘[𝑓]―|𝔅non-zero prime}⟷{𝒪︀𝑃⊊𝐾|𝑓∈𝒪︀𝑃}𝔅⟶𝑘[𝑓]―𝔅𝔪𝑃∩𝑘[𝑓]―⟵𝒪︀𝑃

In particular every such 𝒪︀𝑃 is a discrete valuation ring, and 𝑘(𝔪𝑃)=𝑘(𝔅). Moreover every place 𝑃 of 𝐾/𝑘 (whether or not 𝑓∈𝒪︀𝑃) is a discrete valuation ring, so we may associate to it a discrete valuation

𝑣𝑃:𝐾⟶ℤ∪{∞}

which is zero on 𝑘 and 𝑣𝑃(𝑓)<0⟺𝑓∉𝒪︀𝑃⟺𝑓−1∈𝔪𝑃.

Proof. As 𝑓∈𝐾∖𝑘̃ is transcendental over 𝑘, 𝑘[𝑓] is a polynomial ring and so a PID (3.14.4), hence an affine Dedekind domain (3.30.2), which has Krull dimension 1 (3.30.1). Also 𝐾/𝑘(𝑓) is finite (6.3.6).a). By (3.32.60) 𝐵≔𝑘[𝑓]―𝐾 is therefore a Dedekind domain and finite 𝑘[𝑓]-module, with quotient field 𝐾 as 𝐾/𝑘(𝑓) is algebraic.

By (3.30.4) applied to the Dedekind domain 𝐵 there is a bijection between the non-zero primes 𝔅 of 𝐵 and the local rings 𝑅 with 𝐵⊆𝑅⊊𝐾, given by 𝔅↦𝐵𝔅 and 𝑅↦𝔪𝑅∩𝐵; each such 𝑅 is a discrete valuation ring with 𝔪𝑅=𝔅𝐵𝔅. A place 𝒪︀𝑃 with 𝑓∈𝒪︀𝑃 is a valuation ring, so integrally closed (3.23.2), and contains 𝑘[𝑓], hence 𝐵; as it is local and 𝒪︀𝑃≠𝐾 it is one of these rings 𝑅. Conversely each such 𝑅 is a discrete valuation ring with 𝑘⊊𝑅⊊𝐾 as 𝑓∈𝐵⊆𝑅 but 𝑓∉𝑘, so it is a place with 𝑓∈𝑅. Thus 𝑃↦𝔪𝑃∩𝐵 and 𝔅↦𝐵𝔅 are mutually inverse bijections as displayed. Finally 𝔅 is maximal, as 𝐵 is a Dedekind domain, so 𝑘(𝔪𝑃)=𝐵𝔅/𝔅𝐵𝔅≅Frac(𝐵/𝔅)=𝑘(𝔅) (3.5.43).

This covers every place 𝑃 with 𝑓∈𝒪︀𝑃. For a place 𝑃 with 𝑓∉𝒪︀𝑃 instead, 𝑓−1∈𝔪𝑃⊂𝒪︀𝑃 (3.23.2), and 𝑓−1 is transcendental too (else 𝑓=(𝑓−1)−1 would be algebraic); applying the above with 𝑓−1 in place of 𝑓 shows 𝒪︀𝑃 is again a discrete valuation ring. So every place 𝑃 of 𝐾/𝑘 is a discrete valuation ring, giving the associated valuation 𝑣𝑃. It vanishes on 𝑘, as non-zero elements of 𝑘 are units of 𝒪︀𝑃, and 𝑣𝑃(𝑓)<0⟺𝑓∉𝒪︀𝑃⟺𝑓−1∈𝔪𝑃 by (3.23.2). ∎

Corollary 6.3.8
Let 𝐾/𝑘 be an algebraic function field. Then the field of constants 𝑘̃/𝑘 has finite degree.
Proof. For any place 𝑃 we have an embedding 𝑘̃/𝑘↪︎𝑘(𝔪𝑃)/𝑘 which is finite (6.3.6).c). ∎

We prove a number of facts in analogy with algebraic curves.

Lemma 6.3.9 (Zeros and Poles of a Non-constant Function)
Let 𝐾/𝑘 be an algebraic function field and 0≠𝑓∈𝐾. If 𝑓∈𝑘̃ (i.e. 𝑓 is algebraic), then 𝑣𝑃(𝑓)=0 for every place 𝑃, so 𝑓 has no zeros or poles; otherwise (𝑓 transcendental) 𝑓 has between 1 and [𝐾:𝑘(𝑓)] distinct zeros (resp. poles), that is places 𝑃 with 𝑣𝑃(𝑓)>0 (resp. <0).

Proof. If 𝑓∈𝑘̃, then 𝑓≠0 gives 𝑓∈𝑘̃∖{0}, so 𝑓∉𝔪𝑃 for every place 𝑃 (6.3.5), that is 𝑣𝑃(𝑓)=0.

Otherwise, clearly 𝑘[𝑓] is a polynomial ring with maximal ideal (𝑓). Therefore by (3.23.12) there is a valuation ring 𝒪︀𝑃⊃𝑘[𝑓] such that 𝔪𝑃∩𝑘[𝑓]=(𝑓), which means 𝑓 has at least one zero.

Recall by (6.3.7) that zeros of 𝑓 correspond to the maximal ideals of 𝑘[𝑓]― containing 𝑓. These are precisely the prime divisors of (𝑓) (see (3.30.13)), so the bound follows from (3.30.20).

The statement about poles of 𝑓 follows immediately because these are precisely zeros of 𝑓−1 and 𝑘(𝑓)=𝑘(𝑓−1). ∎

Proposition 6.3.10 (Places of the Rational Function Field)

Let 𝐾=𝑘(𝑥) with 𝑥 transcendental over 𝑘. Then the places of 𝐾/𝑘, that is the valuation rings 𝑘⊊𝒪︀⊊𝐾, are exactly the following pairwise distinct discrete valuation rings

  1. 𝒪︀𝑝(𝑥)≔𝑘[𝑥](𝑝(𝑥)) where 𝑝∈𝑘[𝑋] is an irreducible monic polynomial, with uniformiser 𝑝(𝑥) and residue field 𝑘[𝑋]/(𝑝), and
  2. 𝒪︀∞≔{𝑓(𝑥)𝑔(𝑥)|𝑔≠0,deg𝑓≤deg𝑔}, with uniformiser 𝑥−1 and residue field 𝑘.

If 𝑘 is algebraically closed then the irreducible monic polynomials are the 𝑋−𝜃 with 𝜃∈𝑘, so every place is rational, with uniformiser 𝑥−𝜃 or 𝑥−1.

Proof. Let 𝑡≔𝑥−1, which is again transcendental over 𝑘 with 𝑘(𝑡)=𝐾.

The listed rings are discrete valuation rings. As 𝑘[𝑥]≅𝑘[𝑋] is a PID (3.14.4), 𝒪︀𝑝(𝑥) is a discrete valuation ring (3.29.4), with maximal ideal 𝑝(𝑥)𝒪︀𝑝(𝑥) and residue field 𝑘[𝑥]/(𝑝(𝑥)) (3.5.27).

Next 𝒪︀∞=𝑘[𝑡](𝑡): if 𝑓,𝑔∈𝑘[𝑋] with 𝑔(0)≠0 and 𝑁≔max(deg𝑓,deg𝑔) then 𝑓(𝑡)/𝑔(𝑡)=𝐹(𝑥)/𝐺(𝑥) with 𝐹(𝑥)≔𝑥𝑁𝑓(1/𝑥), 𝐺(𝑥)≔𝑥𝑁𝑔(1/𝑥), deg𝐹≤𝑁=deg𝐺; conversely 𝐹(𝑥)/𝐺(𝑥) with deg𝐹≤deg𝐺≕𝑁 equals 𝑓(𝑡)/𝑔(𝑡) with 𝑓(𝑡)≔𝑡𝑁𝐹(1/𝑡) and 𝑔(𝑡)≔𝑡𝑁𝐺(1/𝑡), where 𝑔(0) is the leading coefficient of 𝐺. So 𝒪︀∞ is a discrete valuation ring with uniformiser 𝑡 and residue field 𝑘[𝑡]/(𝑡)=𝑘, as above.

Every place is listed. Let 𝒪︀ be a place with maximal ideal 𝔪. Suppose firstly that 𝑥∈𝒪︀. Then 𝑘[𝑥]⊂𝒪︀ and the ideal 𝑘[𝑥]∩𝔪≕(𝑝(𝑥)) is principal. There is an injective 𝑘-algebra homomorphism 𝑘[𝑥]/(𝑝(𝑥))↪︎𝑘(𝔪) whence (𝑝(𝑥)) is prime (3.3.59). As 𝒪︀≠𝐾 is not a field, 𝔪≠0, and 𝐾=𝑘(𝑥) is algebraic over 𝑘[𝑥], so (𝑝(𝑥))≠0 (3.22.17); we take 𝑝 monic, and therefore 𝑝 is irreducible (3.15.11). Suppose 𝑔(𝑥)∉(𝑝(𝑥)) then 𝑔(𝑥)∉𝔪⟹𝑔(𝑥)−1∈𝒪︀ (3.23.2). Therefore 𝒪︀𝑝(𝑥)⊂𝒪︀, and as 𝒪︀𝑝(𝑥) is a discrete valuation ring and 𝒪︀≠𝐾 we conclude that they are equal (3.29.5).

Alternatively suppose that 𝑥∉𝒪︀ then by (3.23.2) 𝑡=𝑥−1∈𝔪⊂𝒪︀. Applying the previous part with 𝑡 in place of 𝑥 gives 𝒪︀=𝒪︀𝑝(𝑡) where 𝔪∩𝑘[𝑡]=(𝑝(𝑡)). As 𝑡∈𝔪 we conclude that 𝑝(𝑡)=𝑡, and 𝒪︀=𝑘[𝑡](𝑡)=𝒪︀∞.

Distinctness. 𝑥∈𝒪︀𝑝(𝑥) but 𝑥∉𝒪︀∞, as deg𝑥>deg1. For distinct monic irreducible 𝑝≠𝑝′, 𝑝∉(𝑝′), so 𝑝(𝑥) is a unit of 𝒪︀𝑝′(𝑥) but not of 𝒪︀𝑝(𝑥).

Finally if 𝑘 is algebraically closed the irreducible monic polynomials are the 𝑋−𝜃 (3.17.60), with residue field 𝑘[𝑋]/(𝑋−𝜃)=𝑘. ∎

Lemma 6.3.11 (Restricting a Place is Proper)
Let 𝐿/𝑘′ be an algebraic function field, 𝑃′ a place of 𝐿/𝑘′, 𝑘⊆𝑘′ with 𝑘′/𝑘 algebraic, and 𝐾⊆𝐿 a subfield with trdeg𝑘(𝐾)≥1 (e.g. 𝐾/𝑘 itself an algebraic function field). Then 𝒪︀𝑃′∩𝐾 is a proper valuation ring of 𝐾 (and therefore a place.)

Proof. Drafted by Claude Sonnet, reviewed by David Rufino.

𝒪︀𝑃′∩𝐾 is a valuation ring of 𝐾 (3.23.3). Suppose it equals 𝐾, so 𝐾⊂𝒪︀𝑃′. Nonzero elements of 𝐾 are units of 𝒪︀𝑃′, hence not in 𝔪𝑃′, so 𝐾→𝒪︀𝑃′/𝔪𝑃′=𝑘′(𝔪𝑃′) is injective, and trdeg𝑘(𝐾)≤trdeg𝑘(𝑘′(𝔪𝑃′)) (3.17.139). But 𝑘′(𝔪𝑃′)/𝑘′ is algebraic (6.3.6).c) and 𝑘′/𝑘 is algebraic, so the right-hand side is 0, contradicting trdeg𝑘(𝐾)≥1. ∎

Lemma 6.3.12 (Places Restrict Correctly)
Let 𝐾/𝑘 be an algebraic function field, 𝑃 a place of 𝐾/𝑘, and 𝐿/𝐾 a field extension. Let 𝑘⊆𝑘″⊂𝐿 with 𝑘″/𝑘 algebraic and 𝐿/𝑘″ itself an algebraic function field, and 𝑃′ a place of 𝐿/𝑘″ with 𝒪︀𝑃⊂𝒪︀𝑃′. Then 𝒪︀𝑃′∩𝐾=𝒪︀𝑃; in particular 𝔪𝑃⊂𝔪𝑃′, and 𝜋𝑃∉𝑘″.

Proof. Drafted by Claude Sonnet, reviewed by David Rufino.

𝒪︀𝑃′∩𝐾 is a valuation ring of 𝐾 containing 𝒪︀𝑃 (3.23.3), and proper (as 𝐾/𝑘 is an algebraic function field, so trdeg𝑘(𝐾)=1≥1) (6.3.11). By (3.29.5) (𝒪︀𝑃 being a DVR, (6.3.7)), a valuation ring of 𝐾 containing the DVR 𝒪︀𝑃 is either 𝒪︀𝑃 itself or all of 𝐾; as 𝒪︀𝑃′∩𝐾≠𝐾, this forces 𝒪︀𝑃′∩𝐾=𝒪︀𝑃.

For 0≠𝑥∈𝔪𝑃 we have 𝑥−1∈𝐾∖𝒪︀𝑃=𝐾∖𝒪︀𝑃′, so 𝑥∈𝔪𝑃′ by (3.23.2): thus 𝔪𝑃⊂𝔪𝑃′. In particular 𝜋𝑃∈𝔪𝑃′ is a non-unit of 𝒪︀𝑃′, so 𝜋𝑃∉𝑘″ (nonzero elements of the field 𝑘″⊂𝒪︀𝑃′ are units). ∎

We can now prove (6.3.4). Recall that it states that 𝑋↦𝑘(𝑋) is an anti-equivalence between complete, regular, geometrically integral curves over 𝑘 with non-constant morphisms and algebraic function fields 𝐾/𝑘 in which 𝑘 is relatively separably closed with 𝑘-embeddings, and that under it the points of 𝑋, up to the Kolmogorov quotient 𝑋0, correspond to the places of 𝑘(𝑋)/𝑘, compatibly with morphisms and embeddings, as in a), b) and c) of that statement.

Proof of (6.3.4). Drafted by Claude Sonnet, reviewed by David Rufino.

A complete regular curve 𝑋 as above has 𝑘(𝑋)/𝑘 finitely generated of transcendence degree dim𝑋=1 (6.1.58), so 𝑘(𝑋) is an algebraic function field (6.3.1), in which 𝑘 is relatively separably closed as 𝑋 is geometrically integral (6.2.9). The bijection between morphisms and embeddings, together with the degree, is (6.2.124). The map 𝑥0↦𝒪︀𝑋,𝑥 is a bijection from 𝑋0 to the valuation rings 𝑘⊊𝑅⊊𝑘(𝑋) (6.2.121), which are exactly the places of 𝑘(𝑋)/𝑘; it is well-defined on 𝑋0 as points of 𝑋 with the same local ring are identified there (6.2.23).

For the last statement in b), the morphism induces a local homomorphism 𝒪︀𝑌,𝜙(𝑥)→𝒪︀𝑋,𝑥, so 𝜙⋆(𝒪︀𝑌,𝜙(𝑥))⊂𝒪︀𝑋,𝑥; if [𝑦]=[𝜙(𝑥)] then 𝒪︀𝑌,𝑦=𝒪︀𝑌,𝜙(𝑥) (6.2.23) and the inclusion follows. Conversely, if 𝜙⋆(𝒪︀𝑌,𝑦)⊂𝒪︀𝑋,𝑥 then 𝑅≔𝒪︀𝑋,𝑥∩𝑘(𝑌) is a valuation ring of 𝑘(𝑌) containing the discrete valuation rings 𝒪︀𝑌,𝑦 and 𝒪︀𝑌,𝜙(𝑥). It is proper, as 𝜙 is non-constant: a uniformiser of 𝒪︀𝑌,𝜙(𝑥) lies in 𝔪𝑋,𝑥 and so is not a unit of 𝑅. By (3.29.5) 𝑅=𝒪︀𝑌,𝑦=𝒪︀𝑌,𝜙(𝑥), so [𝑦]=[𝜙(𝑥)] (6.2.23).

For the converse we construct, for every algebraic function field 𝐾/𝑘 in which 𝑘 is relatively separably closed, a complete regular geometrically integral curve 𝑋𝐾 with 𝑘(𝑋𝐾)≅𝐾.

An affine open cover of 𝑋𝐾. Choose 𝑓∈𝐾∖𝑘̃, which is non-empty as 𝐾 has transcendence degree 1, so 𝑓 is transcendental over 𝑘, and put 𝐵≔𝑘[𝑓]―𝐾 and 𝐵′≔𝑘[𝑓−1]―𝐾, subrings of 𝐾. These are Dedekind domains with quotient field 𝐾, finite over 𝑘[𝑓] and 𝑘[𝑓−1] respectively (6.3.7). As normalisation commutes with localisation (3.22.9), both 𝐵[𝑓−1] and 𝐵′[𝑓] are the integral closure of 𝑘[𝑓,𝑓−1] in 𝐾, so they are equal as subrings of 𝐾.
