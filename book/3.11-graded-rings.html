3.11 Δ-Graded Rings

References:

  • [Bou98a] (Chap. II §11)

Let Δ be a commutative monoid. By convention we write the monoid operation additively and 0 for the identity. Further we assume it is cancellable: 𝜆+𝜇=𝜆+𝜇′⟹𝜇=𝜇′. Typically Δ=ℤ, but for example we may also consider ℕ𝑘 for some positive integer 𝑘.

Definition 3.11.1 (Graded Abelian Groups)

Let Δ be a cancellable commutative monoid. An abelian group 𝐺 is Δ-graded if it may be written as internal direct sum (3.3.93) of ℤ-modules

𝐺=⨁𝜆∈Δ𝐺𝜆

for subgroups 𝐺𝜆≤𝐺. In particular the subgroups 𝐺𝜆 are disjoint. This means for every 𝑔∈𝐺 there is a unique decomposition into a finite sum

𝑔=∑𝜆∈Δ𝑔𝜆

If 𝑔∈𝐺𝜆 then we say 𝑔 is homogenous. In this case we write 𝛿(𝑔)≔𝜆 for the degree of 𝑔.

We may write 𝜋𝜆:𝐺→𝐺𝜆 and 𝑖𝜆:𝐺𝜆→𝐺 for projection and inclusion respectively. For 𝑔∈𝐺 we may abbreviate 𝜋𝜆(𝑔) by 𝑔𝜆.

Definition 3.11.2 (Graded Ring)

Let Δ be a cancellable commutative monoid. A commutative ring 𝐴 is graded of type Δ if the additive group (𝐴,+) is Δ-graded, and the multiplication is compatible in the sense that

𝑎∈𝐴𝜆,𝑏∈𝐴𝜇⟹𝑎⋅𝑏∈𝐴𝜆+𝜇
Lemma 3.11.3

Let 𝐴 be a Δ-ring. Then 𝐴0 is a subring.

In the case Δ=ℕ the additive subgroups

𝐴≥𝑘≔∑𝑙≥𝑘𝐴𝑙

are homogenous ideals. We write 𝐴+≔𝐴≥1 which we call the irrelevant ideal.

Proof. We need only show 1∈𝐴0. By hypothesis there is a unique decomposition

1=∑𝜆∈Δ𝑒𝜆𝑒𝜆∈𝐴𝜆

For every 𝑥∈𝐴𝜇 we have

𝑥=𝑥⋅1=∑𝜆∈Δ𝑥⋅𝑒𝜆

By the cancellable assumption we have 𝜆≠0⟹𝜆+𝜇≠𝜇. Therefore 𝑥⋅𝑒0=𝑥. By distributivity this then holds for all 𝑥∈𝐴 and in particular 1=1⋅𝑒0=𝑒0. ∎

Definition 3.11.4 (Graded Module)

Let 𝐴 be a Δ-graded ring and 𝑀 a left (resp. right) 𝐴-module which is a Δ-graded as an abelian group. Then 𝑀 is a graded module if the 𝐴-module structure is compatible with the grading, namely

𝑎∈𝐴𝜆,𝑚∈𝑀𝜇⟹𝑎⋅𝑚∈𝑀𝜆+𝜇

Elements of 𝑀𝜆 are known as homogenous.

Definition 3.11.5 (Graded Homomorphisms)

Let 𝐴,𝐵 be Δ-graded rings and 𝜙:𝐴→𝐵 a ring homomorphism. It is a graded ring homomorphism if

𝜙(𝐴𝜆)⊆𝐵𝜆∀𝜆∈Δ

Let 𝑀,𝑀′ be graded 𝐴-modules and 𝜙:𝑀→𝑀′ an 𝐴-module homomorphism. Then 𝜙 is a graded module homomorphism if

𝜙(𝑀𝜆)⊆𝑀𝜆′∀𝜆∈Δ
Definition 3.11.6 (Graded Submodules)

Let 𝑀 be a graded 𝐴-module, and 𝑁≤𝑀 an 𝐴-submodule. Then the following are equivalent

  1. 𝑛∈𝑁,𝜆∈Δ⟹𝜋𝜆(𝑛)∈𝑁
  2. 𝑁 is generated by homogenous elements

  3. 𝑁 is equal to the internal direct sum

    ⨁𝜆∈Δ𝑁∩𝑀𝜆

We say such a submodule is graded or homogenous and we write 𝑁𝜆≔𝑁∩𝑀𝜆.

Proposition 3.11.7

Let 𝑀 be a graded 𝐴-module and {𝑁𝛼}𝛼∈𝐼 be a family of submodules. Then both

∑𝛼∈𝐼𝑁𝛼

and

⋂𝛼∈𝐼𝑁𝛼

are graded submodules.

Proposition 3.11.8
Let 𝑀 be a finitely-generated graded 𝐴-module. Then 𝑀 has a finite generating set of homogenous elements.
Definition 3.11.9 (Homogenous Ideal)

Let 𝐴 be a commutative graded ring. An ideal 𝔞◃𝐴 which is homogenous as an 𝐴-submodule of 𝐴 is a homogenous ideal. We write 𝔞𝜆≔𝔞∩𝐴, then by definition

𝔞=⨁𝜆∈Δ𝔞𝜆

Note if 𝐴 is Noetherian then every homogenous ideal has a finite generating set of homogenous elements.

Lemma 3.11.10
Let 𝜙:𝐴→𝐵 be a graded ring and 𝔟◃𝐵 a homogenous ideal. Then 𝜙−1(𝔟) is homogenous.
Example 3.11.11 (Polynomial Ring)

The polynomial ring 𝑘[𝑋0,…,𝑋𝑛] is a graded ring over ℕ with

𝑘[𝑋0,…,𝑋𝑛]𝑑=𝑘⟨{𝑋1𝛼1⋅…⋅𝑋𝑛𝛼𝑛|𝛼1+…+𝛼𝑛=𝑑}⟩

Furthermore

⨁𝑖≥𝑑𝑘[𝑋0,…,𝑋𝑛]𝑖=(𝑋0,…,𝑋𝑛)𝑑
Proposition 3.11.12 (Quotient by a Homogenous Ideal)

Suppose Δ is a commutative cancellable monoid. Let 𝐴 be a Δ-graded ring and 𝔞◃𝐴 a homogenous ideal. Then 𝐴/𝔞 is a Δ-graded ring by considering the internal direct sum of abelian groups

𝐴/𝔞=⨁𝜆∈Δ(𝐴/𝔞)𝜆(𝐴/𝔞)𝜆≔{𝑎―|𝑎∈𝐴𝜆}

Furthermore there is an isomorphism of 𝐴-modules

(𝐴/𝔞)𝜆→∼𝐴𝜆/(𝔞∩𝐴𝜆)

For every homogenous ideal 𝔟⊇𝔞 the quotient ideal 𝔟/𝔞 is homogenous with

𝔟/𝔞=⨁𝜆∈Δ(𝔟/𝔞)𝜆

Proof. Evidently the (𝐴/𝔞)𝜆 are abelian subgroups and span 𝐴/𝔞. Observe that

0=∑𝜆∈Δ𝑎𝜆―⟹∑𝜆∈Δ𝑎𝜆∈𝔞

Then as 𝔞 is homogenous we conclude 𝑎𝜆∈𝔞 and therefore 𝑎𝜆―=0. Therefore the family (𝐴/𝔞)𝜆 satisfy the criteria of (3.3.94) and the direct sum is well-defined. Evidently the ring multiplication on 𝐴/𝔞 is compatible with the given Δ-grading.

The isomorphism is induced by 𝜋𝜆.

Suppose that 𝑏―∈𝔟/𝔞 then we require to prove that (𝑏―)𝜆∈(𝔟/𝔞). By assumption 𝑏𝜆∈𝔟 so 𝑏𝜆―∈𝔟/𝔞. However by the first part 𝑏𝜆―=(𝑏―)𝜆. ∎

Proposition 3.11.13 (Localization at Homogenous Elements)

Let Δ be an abelian group, 𝐴 a Δ-graded ring and 𝑆⊂𝐴 a multiplicative set of homogenous elements. Then we may define a Δ-gradation on 𝑆−1𝐴 by

(𝑆−1𝐴)𝜆≔{𝑎𝑠|𝛿(𝑎)−𝛿(𝑠)=𝜆}

For a homogenous ideal 𝔞◃𝐴 then the ideal 𝑆−1𝔞 is homogenous.

Proof. We first show that (𝑆−1𝐴)𝜆 is an additive subgroup, for given homogenous 𝑎,𝑎′∈𝐴 and 𝑠,𝑠′∈𝑠 by definition

𝑎𝑠+𝑎′𝑠′=𝑎𝑠′+𝑎′𝑠𝑠𝑠′

Suppose 𝛿(𝑎)−𝛿(𝑠)=𝜆=𝛿(𝑎′)−𝛿(𝑠′) then 𝛿(𝑎𝑠′)=𝛿(𝑎′𝑠). Therefore 𝑎𝑠′+𝑎′𝑠 is homogenous and 𝛿(𝑎𝑠′+𝑎′𝑠)=𝛿(𝑎𝑠′)=𝜆+𝛿(𝑠)+𝛿(𝑠′)=𝜆+𝛿(𝑠𝑠′) whence (𝑆−1𝐴)𝜆 is an additive subgroup. Similarly for the multiplicative structure suppose 𝛿(𝑎)=𝛿(𝑠)+𝜆 and 𝛿(𝑎′)=𝛿(𝑠′)+𝜆′ then

𝛿(𝑎𝑎′)=𝛿(𝑎)+𝛿(𝑎′)=𝛿(𝜆)+𝛿(𝜆′)+(𝜆+𝜆′)=𝛿(𝜆𝜆′)+(𝜆+𝜆′)

so that 𝑎𝑠⋅𝑎′𝑠′∈(𝑆−1𝐴)𝜆+𝜆′ as required. Evidently 𝑆−1𝐴 is the internal sum of (𝑆−1𝐴)𝜆. To show it is direct suppose that

0=∑𝑖=1𝑛𝑎𝑖𝑠𝑖𝛿(𝑎𝑖)=𝛿(𝑠𝑖)+𝜆𝑖

for distinct 𝜆1,…,𝜆𝑛. Then

0=∑𝑖=1𝑛𝑎𝑖𝑡∏𝑗≠𝑖𝑠𝑗

for some 𝑡∈𝑆. Furthermore

𝛿(𝑎𝑖𝑡∏𝑗≠𝑖𝑠𝑗)=𝛿(𝑎𝑖)+∑𝑗≠𝑖𝛿(𝑠𝑗)+𝛿(𝑡)=𝜆𝑖+∑𝑗=1𝑛𝛿(𝑠𝑗)+𝛿(𝑡)

As Δ is cancellable the elements 𝜆̂𝑖≔𝜆𝑖+∑𝑗=1𝑛𝛿(𝑠𝑗)+𝛿(𝑡) are distinct. By assumption we conclude that 𝑎𝑖𝑡∏𝑗≠𝑖𝑠𝑗=0 and so 𝑎𝑖𝑠𝑖=0. Therefore 𝑆−1𝐴 is the direct sum of (𝑆−1𝐴)𝜆 by (3.3.94).

For 𝑎𝑠∈𝑆−1𝔞 we wish to show that (𝑎𝑠)𝜆∈𝑆−1𝔞 for all 𝜆∈Δ. Here 𝑎∈𝔞 so by assumption 𝑎𝜆∈𝔞 for all 𝜆∈Δ and 𝑎=∑𝜆∈Δ𝑎𝜆 so

𝑎𝑠=∑𝜆∈Δ𝑎𝜆𝑠=∑𝜆∈Δ𝑎𝜆+𝛿(𝑠)𝑠

and so (𝑎𝑠)𝜆=𝑎𝜆+𝛿(𝑠)𝑠∈𝑆−1𝔞 as required. ∎

Recall for any Δ-graded ring 𝐴 the additive subgroup 𝐴0 is also a subring.

Definition 3.11.14

Let Δ be a commutative abelian group, 𝐴 a Δ-graded ring and 𝑆 be a multiplicative subset of homogenous elements. Define the subring of 𝑆−1𝐴

𝐴(𝑆)≔(𝑆−1𝐴)0={𝑎𝑠|𝛿(𝑠)=𝛿(𝑎)}

Similarly for 𝔞 a homogenous ideal define the homogenous ideal of 𝑆−1𝐴

𝔞(𝑆)≔{𝑎𝑠|𝑎∈𝔞,𝑠∈𝑆,𝛿(𝑠)=𝛿(𝑎)}

In the case 𝔭 is a homogenous prime ideal define the local ring

𝐴(𝔭)≔𝐴({𝑥∉𝔭})

and for 𝑓∈𝐴 homogenous define

𝐴(𝑓)≔𝐴({1,𝑓,𝑓2,…})
Lemma 3.11.15

Let Δ be an abelian group, 𝐴 a Δ-graded ring and 𝔞◃𝐴 a homogenous ideal. Then

𝔞(𝑆)=𝐴(𝑆)∩𝑆−1𝔞

as subsets of 𝑆−1𝐴.

Lemma 3.11.16

Let Δ be an abelian group, 𝐴 a Δ-graded ring, 𝑎∈𝐴𝜇 homogenous and 𝑥∈𝐴. Then

(𝑎𝑥)𝜆=𝑎⋅𝑥𝜆−𝜇

In particular 𝑎𝑥 is homogenous iff 𝑥 is homogenous.

Proposition 3.11.17 (Homogenous localisation comutes with quotients)

Let Δ be an abelian group, 𝐴 a Δ-graded ring, 𝔞 a homogenous ideal and 𝑆⊂𝐴 a multiplicatively closed set of homogenous elements such that 𝑆∩𝔞=∅. Then there is a canonical isomorphism of Δ-graded rings

(𝑆−1𝐴)/(𝑆−1𝔞)→∼𝜋(𝑆)−1(𝐴/𝔞)𝑎𝑠+𝑆−1𝐴→𝑎+𝔞𝑠+𝔞

where 𝜋:𝐴→𝐴/𝔞 is the canonical surjection. In particular there is an isomorphism of rings

𝐴(𝑆)/𝔞(𝑆)⟶∼(𝑆−1𝐴/𝑆−1𝔞)0⟶∼(𝐴/𝔞)(𝜋(𝑆))

Proof. The first isomorphism of rings is from (3.5.25), and it is obviously compatible with the Δ-grading. For suppose 𝑥∈(𝑆−1𝐴)/(𝑆−1𝔞) with 𝛿(𝑥)=𝜆. Then 𝑥=𝑎𝑠 with 𝛿(𝑎)−𝛿(𝑠)=𝜆. By definition 𝛿(𝑎+𝔞)−𝛿(𝑠+𝔞)=𝜆 as required.

The third isomorphism follows immediately. For the second consider the ring homomorphism

𝜙:𝐴(𝑆)↪︎𝑆−1𝐴→𝑆−1𝐴/𝑆−1𝔞

Then evidently the image is (𝑆−1𝐴/𝑆−1𝔞)0 as the quotient map is graded. The kernel is 𝐴(𝑆)∩𝑆−1𝔞 which equals 𝔞(𝑆) by (3.11.15). ∎

Proposition 3.11.18

Let Δ be an abelian group, 𝐴 a Δ-graded ring, 𝔞 a homogenous ideal and 𝔭⊇𝔞 a homogenous prime ideal. Then there is a canonical isomorphism of Δ-graded rings

𝐴𝔭/𝔞𝔭⟶∼(𝐴/𝔞)𝔭/𝔞𝑎𝑠+𝔞𝔭→𝑎+𝔞𝑠+𝔞

In particular there is an isomorphism of rings

𝐴(𝔭)/𝔞(𝔭)⟶∼(𝐴𝔭/𝔞𝔭)0⟶∼(𝐴/𝔞)(𝔭/𝔞)

given by

𝑎𝑠+𝔞(𝔭)→𝑎+𝔞𝑠+𝔞
Proof. Follows from (3.11.17) and the observation that 𝐴/𝔞∖𝔭/𝔞=𝜋(𝐴∖𝔭). ∎
