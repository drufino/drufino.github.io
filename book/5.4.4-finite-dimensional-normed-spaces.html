5.4.4 Finite-Dimensional Normed Spaces

Definition 5.4.19 (Equivalent Norms)

Let 𝑋 be a vector space over a valued field 𝐾. Consider two norms ‖⋅‖1 and ‖⋅‖2. We say that they are equivalent if there exists constants 𝑐1,𝑐2>0 such that

𝑐1‖𝑥‖1≤‖𝑥‖2≤𝑐2‖𝑥‖1

We may prove easily that this is an equivalence relation on norms.

Proposition 5.4.20

Let 𝑋 be a vector space over a valued field 𝐾. Suppose that ‖⋅‖1 and ‖⋅‖2 are equivalent norms for 𝑋. Then the following derived quantities are the same

  • Induced topology
  • Convergent sequences
  • Cauchy sequences
Proposition 5.4.21 (Supremum Norm)

Let 𝑋 be a finite-dimensional vector space with basis (𝑒1,⋯,𝑒𝑛). Then the supremum norm defined by

‖∑𝑖=1𝑛𝜆𝑖𝑒𝑖‖∞≔max(|𝜆1|,⋯,|𝜆𝑛|)

is well-defined (it is the product norm on 𝑋≅𝐾×⋯×𝐾). If 𝐾 is complete then so is 𝑋.

Proposition 5.4.22 (Equivalence of Norms)
Let 𝑋 be a non-zero finite-dimensional vector space over a complete valued field 𝐾. Then every norm is equivalent to the supremum norm (and therefore complete).

Proof. Fix a basis (𝑒1,⋯,𝑒𝑛). Then for any norm ‖⋅‖ we have by the triangle inequality

‖∑𝑖=1𝑛𝜆𝑖𝑒𝑖‖≤∑𝑖=1𝑛|𝜆𝑖|‖𝑒𝑖‖≤(∑𝑖=1𝑛‖𝑒𝑖‖)‖∑𝑖=1𝑛𝜆𝑖𝑒𝑖‖∞

The reverse inequality we prove by induction. The case 𝑛=1 we may take 𝑐1=‖𝑒1‖. Suppose 𝑛>1 and there is no such constant 𝐶. Then there are vectors 𝑣𝑘∈𝑋 such that

‖𝑣𝑘‖<1𝑘‖𝑣𝑘‖∞

Suppose

𝑣𝑘=∑𝑖=1𝑛𝜆𝑘𝑖𝑒𝑖

Consider the sets of integers

𝑆𝑖≔{𝑘∈ℕ|‖𝑣𝑘‖∞=|𝜆𝑘𝑖|}𝑖=1⋯𝑛

Then at least one of 𝑆1,⋯,𝑆𝑛 is infinite, since the union is ℕ. Without loss of generality we assume it is 𝑆𝑛.

Therefore by passing to a subsequence, and scaling, we may assume that ‖𝑣𝑘‖∞=𝜆𝑘𝑛=1 and ‖𝑣𝑘‖<1𝑘. Define the vector 𝑤𝑘≔𝑣𝑘−𝑒𝑛. Then by definition 𝑤𝑘→𝑒𝑛 with respect to ‖⋅‖.

Let 𝑊 be the (𝑛−1)-dimensional subspace spanned by {𝑒1,⋯,𝑒𝑛−1}. We prove also that 𝑤𝑘→𝑤∈𝑊 with respect to ‖⋅‖, which contradicts uniqueness of limits. Evidently 𝑤𝑘 is a cauchy sequence with respect to ‖⋅‖ restricted to 𝑊. By induction ‖⋅‖∞ is equivalent to ‖⋅‖ on 𝑊, and therefore 𝑤𝑘 is cauchy with respect to ‖⋅‖∞. As 𝑊 is complete (5.4.21) we see that 𝑤𝑘→𝑤 with respect to ‖⋅‖∞, and therefore with respect to ‖⋅‖. ∎

Corollary 5.4.23

Let 𝑋 be a finite dimensional normed vector space with basis {𝑥1,⋯,𝑥𝑛}. The projection map

𝜋𝑖:𝑋→⟨𝑥𝑖⟩→∼𝐾
