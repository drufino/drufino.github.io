5.1.6 Limsup and Liminf

Proposition 5.1.33 (Limsup and Liminf)

Let 𝐾 be a complete ordered field and (𝑎𝑛) a sequence. Define the associated sequences in 𝐾♯

𝑎𝑛+≔sup𝑛′≥𝑛𝑎𝑛′𝑎𝑛−≔inf𝑛′≥𝑛𝑎𝑛′

which are decreasing and increasing sequences respectively. Define

lim sup𝑎𝑛=lim𝑛→∞𝑎𝑛+lim inf𝑎𝑛≔lim𝑛→∞𝑎𝑛−

Then

  1. 𝑎𝑛is bounded above⟺𝑎𝑛+is bounded above⟺lim sup𝑎𝑛+<∞
  2. 𝑎𝑛is bounded below⟺𝑎𝑛−is bounded above⟺lim inf𝑎𝑛−>−∞
  3. lim sup𝑎𝑛≥lim inf𝑎𝑛
  4. 𝑎𝑛→𝛼⟺lim sup𝑎𝑛=lim inf𝑎𝑛=𝛼

Proof. Clearly 𝑎𝑛+ is decreasing and 𝑎𝑛− is increasing. Therefore by (5.1.23) the definition of lim sup𝑎𝑛 and lim inf𝑎𝑛 is well-defined, the same result also demonstrates a) and b). By definition 𝑎𝑛+≥𝑎𝑛≥𝑎𝑛− so the inequality c) follows from (5.1.21).

Suppose 𝛼→∞ then for every 𝐿>0 there exists 𝑁 such that

𝑛≥𝑁⟹𝑎𝑛>𝐿⟹𝑎𝑛−≥𝐿

whence lim inf𝑎𝑛=∞. Conversely suppose lim inf𝑎𝑛=∞ we see by definition that 𝑎𝑛→∞ as required. The case of −∞ follows similarly.

Suppose that 𝛼=lim sup𝑎𝑛=lim inf𝑎𝑛 is finite, then there exists 𝑁1 such that

𝑛≥𝑁1⟹𝑎𝑛≥𝑎𝑛−>𝛼−𝜀2

and 𝑁2 such that

𝑛≥𝑁2⟹𝑎𝑛≤𝑎𝑛+<𝛼+𝜀2

Then

𝑛≥max(𝑁1,𝑁2)⟹|𝑎𝑛−𝛼|<𝜀

and we conclude lim𝑛→∞𝑎𝑛=𝛼.

Conversely suppose 𝑎𝑛→𝛼. Then for every 𝜀>0 there exists some 𝑁 such that

𝑛≥𝑁⟹𝛼−𝜀<𝑎𝑛<𝛼+𝜀

and therefore

𝑛≥𝑁⟹𝛼−𝜀≤𝑎𝑛−≤𝑎𝑛≤𝑎𝑛+≤𝛼+𝜀

Evidently then

𝛼−𝜀≤lim inf𝑎𝑛≤lim sup𝑎𝑛≤𝛼+𝜀

As 𝜀 was arbitrary then this shows 𝛼=lim inf𝑎𝑛=lim sup𝑎𝑛. ∎

Proposition 5.1.34 (Arithmetic Properties of limsup and liminf)

Let 𝐾 be a complete ordered field and (𝑎𝑛),(𝑏𝑛) bounded sequences. Then

  1. lim sup(𝑎𝑛+𝑏𝑛)≤lim sup𝑎𝑛+lim sup𝑏𝑛
  2. lim inf(𝑎𝑛+𝑏𝑛)≤lim inf𝑎𝑛+lim inf𝑏𝑛

If 𝑏𝑛→𝑏 then

  1. lim sup(𝑎𝑛+𝑏𝑛)=lim sup𝑎𝑛+𝑏
  2. lim inf(𝑎𝑛+𝑏𝑛)=lim inf𝑎𝑛+𝑏

and if in addition 𝑏≥0 then

  1. lim sup(𝑎𝑛𝑏𝑛)=𝑏lim sup𝑎𝑛
  2. lim inf(𝑎𝑛𝑏𝑛)=𝑏lim inf𝑎𝑛

Proof. We prove each in turn

  1. By definition 𝑛′≥𝑛⟹𝑎𝑛′+𝑏𝑛′≤𝑎𝑛++𝑏𝑛+, whence (𝑎𝑛+𝑏𝑛)+≤𝑎++𝑏𝑛+. The result follows from (5.1.21).
  2. Similarly
  3. By a) we have lim sup(𝑎𝑛+𝑏𝑛)≤lim sup𝑎𝑛+𝑏. Furthermore lim sup(𝑎𝑛)=lim sup(𝑎𝑛+𝑏𝑛+(−𝑏𝑛))≤lim sup(𝑎𝑛+𝑏𝑛)+lim sup(−𝑏𝑛)=lim sup(𝑎𝑛+𝑏𝑛)−𝑏, and the result follows.
  4. Similarly

e), f) Suppose first that 𝑏=0. Then for sufficiently large 𝑛

0≤𝑏𝑛≤𝜀⟹−𝜀|𝑎𝑛−|≤(𝑎𝑛𝑏𝑛)+≤|𝑎𝑛+|𝜀⟹|(𝑎𝑛𝑏𝑛)+|≤max(|sup𝑘𝑎𝑘+|,|inf𝑘𝑎𝑘−|)𝜀

as (𝑎𝑛) is assumed bounded then this shows that (𝑎𝑛𝑏𝑛)+→0 as required. In this case f) follows similarly. For the general case then

𝑎𝑛𝑏𝑛=𝑎𝑛(𝑏𝑛−𝑏)+𝑎𝑛𝑏

Obviously 𝑏𝑛−𝑏→0, so by the case already proven we have

lim sup(𝑎𝑛(𝑏𝑛−𝑏))=lim inf(𝑎𝑛(𝑏𝑛−𝑏))=0

and in particular 𝑎𝑛(𝑏𝑛−𝑏)→0 by (5.1.33).d). Consequently by c)

lim sup(𝑎𝑛𝑏𝑛)=lim sup(𝑎𝑛𝑏)=𝑏lim sup(𝑎𝑛)

as required. The case f) follows similarly. ∎

Proposition 5.1.35

Let 𝐾 be a complete ordered field and (𝑎𝑛) a bounded sequence. Then every subsequence (𝑎𝑛𝑘) satisfies

lim sup𝑘𝑎𝑛𝑘≤lim sup𝑛𝑎𝑛

and there in fact exists a convergent subsequence 𝑎𝑛𝑘 such that

𝑎𝑛𝑘↓lim sup𝑛𝑎𝑛

A similar statement applies to lim inf𝑎𝑛.

Proof. Define 𝑏𝑘≔sup𝑘′≥𝑘𝑎𝑛𝑘′, then by definition

𝑏𝑘+≤𝑎𝑛𝑘+

Taking limits as 𝑘→∞ and considering we find that

lim sup𝑘𝑎𝑛𝑘≤(5.1.21)lim𝑘→∞𝑎𝑛𝑘+=(5.1.22)lim𝑛→∞𝑎𝑛+≕lim sup𝑛𝑎𝑛

Recall that 𝑎𝑛+↓lim sup𝑎𝑛. Therefore for every 𝑘>0 there exists 𝑁𝑘 such that 𝑎𝑁𝑘+≤lim sup𝑎𝑛+12𝑘. Then by definition there exists some 𝑛𝑘≥𝑁𝑘 such that 𝑎𝑛𝑘≤𝑎𝑁𝑘++12𝑘≤lim sup𝑎𝑛+1𝑘. We see that 𝑎𝑛𝑘 has the required property. ∎
