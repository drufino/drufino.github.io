3.22 Lying over, Incomparability, Going Up and Going Down

Definition 3.22.1 (Lying over / Going up)

Let 𝜙:𝐴→𝐵 be a ring map and 𝔭 and 𝔮 primes of 𝐴 and 𝐵 respectively

  1. 𝔮 lies over 𝔭, or 𝔭 lies under 𝔮 if 𝔭=𝜙−1(𝔮)=𝔮𝑐. When 𝐴⊆𝐵 and 𝜙 is the identity then this is equivalent to saying 𝔭=𝔮∩𝐴.
Definition 3.22.2 (Lying Over / Going Up / Incomparability)

Let 𝜙:𝐴→𝐵 be a ring map. We say that it has the

  1. lying over property if every prime ideal 𝔭⊇ker(𝜙) has a prime 𝔮 lying over it. NB ker(𝜙)⊆𝔭 is a necessary condition for 𝔭 to be a contraction and is equivalent to 𝐵𝔭≠0.
  2. going up property if for every pair of prime ideals 𝔭⊊𝔭′ in 𝐴 and 𝔮◃𝐵 lying over 𝔭, there exists a prime ideal 𝔮′ such that 𝔮⊊𝔮′ and 𝔮′ lies over 𝔭′.
  3. incomparability property if for every pair of prime ideals 𝔮,𝔮′◃𝐵 then 𝔮⊊𝔮′⟹𝜙−1(𝔮)⊊𝜙−1(𝔮′)
  4. going down property if for every pair of prime ideals 𝔭′⊊𝔭 in 𝐴 and 𝔮◃𝐵 lying over 𝔭, there exists a prime ideal 𝔮′ such that 𝔮′⊊𝔮 and 𝔮′ lies over 𝔭′.
Remark 3.22.3

It’s possible to interpret these geometrically in terms of the map 𝜙⋆:Spec(𝐵)→Spec(𝐴).

  • Lying over - 𝜙⋆ is surjective onto 𝑉(ker(𝜙))
  • Going up - 𝜙⋆ is closed
  • Incomparability - fibres have dimension 0
  • Going down (and finite presentation) - 𝜙⋆ is open

The main result of this section is the correspondence between primes lying over 𝔭 and primes of the ring 𝐵𝔭/𝔭𝐵𝔭 (3.22.5). As a preliminary result we consider conditions under which we can strengthen 𝔭⊆𝔮𝑐 to 𝔭=𝔮𝑐, as the former condition is somewhat easier to satisfy.

Lemma 3.22.4 (Lying over criteria)

Let 𝜙:𝐴→𝐵 be a ring map, 𝔭◃𝐴 prime such that ker(𝜙)⊆𝔭 and 𝔮◃𝐵. Then

𝔭=𝔮𝑐⟺𝔭𝑒⊆𝔮and𝔮∩𝜙(𝐴∖𝔭)=∅

In particular

𝔭=𝔭𝑒𝑐is contracted⟺𝔭𝑒∩𝜙(𝐴∖𝔭)=∅

Proof. Recall (3.3.52) that in general 𝔭⊆𝔮𝑐⟺𝔭𝑒⊆𝔮. The first equivalence is then clear because 𝑥∈𝔮𝑐∖𝔭⟺𝜙(𝑥)∈𝔮∩𝜙(𝐴∖𝔭). For 𝜙(𝑥)∈𝔮∩𝜙(𝐴∖𝔭)⟹𝜙(𝑥)=𝜙(𝑦) for 𝑦∉𝔭 and 𝑥∈𝔮𝑐. This implies 𝑥−𝑦∈ker(𝜙)⊆𝔭 whence 𝑥∉𝔭 as required.

The final statement follows by considering 𝔮=𝔭𝑒. ∎

Proposition 3.22.5 (Lying over correspondence)

Let 𝜙:𝐴→𝐵 be a ring map and 𝔭◃𝐴 a prime ideal s.t. ker(𝜙)⊆𝔭. Then there is a order-preserving correspondence of prime ideals

{𝔮|𝔮lies above𝔭}⟷{𝔮′◃𝐵𝔭|𝔭𝐵𝔭⊆𝔮′}⟷{𝔮″◃𝐵𝔭/𝔭𝐵𝔭}𝔮⟶𝔮𝐵𝔭⟶𝔮𝐵𝔭/𝔭𝐵𝔭

In particular TFAE

  1. 𝔭 lies under a prime 𝔮
  2. 𝐵𝔭/𝔭𝐵𝔭≠0
  3. 𝔭=𝔭𝑒𝑐 is contracted

NB c) is a-priori weaker than a).

Proof. Recall 𝐵𝔭≔𝑆−1𝐵 and 𝔭𝐵𝔭≔𝔭𝑒𝐵𝔭=𝑆−1𝔭𝑒 where 𝑆≔𝜙(𝐴∖𝔭).

By (3.22.4) 𝔮 lies above 𝔭 if and only if 𝔭𝑒⊆𝔮 and 𝔮∩𝑆=∅. The first correspondence then follows from (3.5.19) and the second from (3.3.56).

a) ⟺ b) This follows from the correspondence, since a ring without any non-zero prime ideals is simply the zero-ring.

b) ⟺ c) Observe 𝔭=𝔭𝑒𝑐⟺𝔭𝑒∩𝑆=∅ by (3.22.4), which holds if and only if 𝔭𝐵𝔭≠𝐵𝔭 by (3.5.18) e). ∎
