4.1.9 Connected Topological Spaces

Proposition 4.1.53 (Connected Topological Space)

Let 𝑋 be a topological space. Then the following are equivalent

  1. 𝑋 cannot be written as the disjoint union 𝑈⊔𝑉 of two non-empty open subsets 𝑈,𝑉⊂𝑋
  2. There are no proper non-empty subsets which are both open and closed

In this case we say that 𝑋 is connected.

Proposition 4.1.54 (Connected Subset)

Let 𝑋 be a topological space and 𝑍⊂𝑋. Then the following are equivalent

  1. 𝑍 is connected in the subspace topology
  2. 𝑍 cannot be written as a disjoint (𝑍∩𝑈)⊔(𝑍∩𝑉) with both 𝑍∩𝑈,𝑍∩𝑉≠∅
Proposition 4.1.55
Let 𝑋 be a topological space and {𝑍𝑖}𝑖∈𝐼 a family of connected subsets such that ⋂𝑖∈𝐼𝑍𝑖≠∅. Then ⋃𝑖∈𝐼𝑍𝑖 is connected.
Definition 4.1.56 (Connected Component)
Let 𝑋 be a topological space. We say 𝑍⊂𝑋 is a connected component if it is both connected and not properly contained in a connected subset.
Proposition 4.1.57
Let 𝑋 be a topological space. Then every connected subset 𝑍 is contained in a unique connected component.

Proposition 4.1.58

Let 𝑋 be a topological space, then the connected components partition 𝑋.

We denote this collection by 𝜋0(𝑋).

Proof. Evidently {𝑥} is connected so that the connected components cover 𝑋 by (4.1.57).

Suppose we have two connected components 𝑍1,𝑍2 are not disjoint. Then by (4.1.55) 𝑍1∪𝑍2 is connected. By maximality we have 𝑍1∪𝑍2=𝑍1=𝑍2 as required. ∎
