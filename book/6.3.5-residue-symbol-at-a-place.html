6.3.5 Residue Symbol at a Place

The residue symbol Res𝑃(𝑧d𝑡) built in this subsection is defined via the Laurent coefficients of 𝑧𝜕𝜋(𝑡) relative to a choice of uniformiser 𝜋, and shown independent of that choice (6.3.31) – exactly the invariance a genuine differential-form residue ought to have. This invariance is not straightforward in characteristic 𝑝: it reduces (6.3.31) to computing (𝜓𝑛𝜓′)−1 for the change-of-uniformiser series 𝜓, an identity which the naive derivative-of-a-power argument only settles when 𝑛+1 is invertible – automatic in characteristic 0, but not when 𝑝∣(𝑛+1). We settle it uniformly in every characteristic by proving the identity once, universally, over ℤ[𝑎1,𝑎1−1,𝑎2,𝑎3,…] (indeterminate coefficients, torsion-free over ℤ) and specialising (6.3.26) – the same universal-ℤ-polynomial philosophy used, e.g., in [Sti09] (Chap. 4, Prop. 4.2.9); another route entirely avoiding a characteristic-𝑝 detour is via finite-potent operators [Tat68] (§§1–2).

Throughout this subsection 𝑘 is perfect, so the residue field 𝑘(𝑃) of every place 𝑃 of 𝐾/𝑘 is a finite separable extension of 𝑘 (3.18.103) and the coefficient expansion (3.32.17) embeds 𝐾 in 𝑘(𝑃)((𝑋)). The residue of 𝑧d𝑡 at 𝑃 is first a 𝑘(𝑃)-valued Laurent coefficient, and the residue symbol Res𝑃(𝑧d𝑡)∈𝑘 is its image under the trace Tr𝑘(𝑃)/𝑘. At a rational place, that is 𝑘(𝑃)=𝑘, the trace is the identity and this is the residue of the classical case, a 𝑘-valued Laurent coefficient.

The map 𝐿𝜋 below is the algebraic analogue of the Laurent expansion 𝑓=∑𝑗≥−𝑁𝑎𝑗(𝑧−𝑝)𝑗 of a meromorphic function 𝑓 at a point 𝑝 of a Riemann surface, in a local coordinate 𝑧−𝑝 (the case 𝑘=ℂ and 𝑃 a rational place). Here 𝐾 plays the role of the meromorphic functions on the curve, 𝒪︀𝑃 of those holomorphic at 𝑃 and the uniformiser 𝜋 of the local coordinate, and the coefficients lie in the residue field 𝑘(𝑃), which is ℂ at every point of a Riemann surface. The residue of a differential 𝑧d𝑡 at 𝑃 will be the trace Tr𝑘(𝑃)/𝑘 of the coefficient 𝑎−1∈𝑘(𝑃) of the expansion of 𝑧𝜕𝜋(𝑡), so that it lands in 𝑘; at a rational place this is 𝑎−1 itself, as in the classical case.

Proposition 6.3.23 (Laurent Coefficients at a Place)

Let 𝑃 be a place of 𝐾/𝑘, 𝜋 a uniformiser of 𝒪︀𝑃 and 𝑘(𝑃)≔𝒪︀𝑃/𝔪𝑃 (a finite separable extension of 𝑘, as 𝑘 is perfect). For 𝑢∈𝒪︀𝑃 write [𝑢]𝑁 for its class in 𝒪︀𝑃/𝔪𝑃𝑁+1, and let 𝑠𝑁:𝑘(𝑃)→𝒪︀𝑃/𝔪𝑃𝑁+1 be the 𝑘-algebra sections of (3.25.18), which do not depend on 𝜋. There is a unique 𝑘-algebra homomorphism

𝐿𝜋:𝐾→𝑘(𝑃)((𝑋))

with 𝐿𝜋(𝜋)=𝑋 such that

  • 𝐿𝜋 preserves valuation: 𝑣𝑋(𝐿𝜋(𝑧))=𝑣𝑃(𝑧) for every 𝑧∈𝐾⋆ (𝑣𝑋 being the valuation of the DVR 𝑘(𝑃)[[𝑋]] (3.32.16));
  • 𝐿𝜋 induces the identity on the residue field: 𝐿𝜋(𝑢)≡𝑢―(mod𝑋) for 𝑢∈𝒪︀𝑃.

It has the following further properties.

  1. 𝐿𝜋 is injective, and 𝑧∈𝒪︀𝑃⟺𝐿𝜋(𝑧)∈𝑘(𝑃)[[𝑋]] for every 𝑧∈𝐾.
  2. For 𝑧∈𝐾⋆, 𝐿𝜋(𝑧)=∑𝑗≥𝑣𝑃(𝑧)𝑎𝑗𝑋𝑗 with 𝑎𝑣𝑃(𝑧)≠0 (the Laurent coefficients 𝑎𝑗∈𝑘(𝑃) of 𝑧 at 𝑃 relative to 𝜋).

  3. If 𝑢∈𝒪︀𝑃 and 𝐿𝜋(𝑢)=∑𝑖𝑐𝑖𝑋𝑖, then for every 𝑁≥0

    [𝑢]𝑁=∑𝑖≤𝑁𝑠𝑁(𝑐𝑖)[𝜋𝑖]𝑁.
  4. For 𝑐∈𝑘(𝑃) and 𝑐′∈𝒪︀𝑃 with [𝑐′]𝑁=𝑠𝑁(𝑐) we have 𝐿𝜋(𝑐′)≡𝑐(mod𝑋𝑁+1), for every uniformiser 𝜋.

If 𝑃 is rational then 𝑘(𝑃)=𝑘, the 𝑠𝑁 are induced by 𝑘⊆𝒪︀𝑃 and 𝐿𝜋:𝐾→𝑘((𝑋)).

Proof. Drafted by Claude Sonnet, reviewed by David Rufino.

As 𝑘(𝑃)/𝑘 is finite (6.2.114).c) and 𝑘 is perfect, 𝑘(𝑃)/𝑘 is separable algebraic (3.18.103), so (3.32.17) applies to 𝐴≔𝒪︀𝑃, with the sections 𝑠𝑁 of (3.25.18). It gives an injective 𝑘-algebra homomorphism 𝐿𝜋:𝐾→𝑘(𝑃)((𝑋)) with 𝐿𝜋(𝜋)=𝑋 which on 𝒪︀𝑃 is given by the digits of the 𝜋-adic expansion, that is the unique coefficients 𝑐𝑖(𝑢)∈𝑘(𝑃) of (3.32.17) with [𝑢]𝑁=∑𝑖≤𝑁𝑠𝑁(𝑐𝑖(𝑢))[𝜋𝑖]𝑁 for every 𝑁, so that 𝐿𝜋(𝑢)=∑𝑖𝑐𝑖(𝑢)𝑋𝑖.

Valuation and residue. 𝑣𝑋(𝐿𝜋(𝑢))=𝑣𝑃(𝑢) for 0≠𝑢∈𝒪︀𝑃 is the valuation formula of that proposition, and for 𝑧=𝑎/𝑠∈𝐾⋆ (𝑎,𝑠∈𝒪︀𝑃), 𝐿𝜋(𝑧)=𝐿𝜋(𝑎)𝐿𝜋(𝑠)−1, so 𝑣𝑋(𝐿𝜋(𝑧))=𝑣𝑃(𝑎)−𝑣𝑃(𝑠)=𝑣𝑃(𝑧) (multiplicativity of 𝑣𝑋 (3.32.15)). In particular 𝑧∈𝒪︀𝑃⟺𝑣𝑃(𝑧)≥0⟺𝐿𝜋(𝑧)∈𝑘(𝑃)[[𝑋]], and 𝑎𝑗=0 for 𝑗<𝑣𝑃(𝑧) with 𝑎𝑣𝑃(𝑧)≠0. For 𝑢∈𝒪︀𝑃 the case 𝑁=0 of the displayed identity reads [𝑢]0=𝑠0(𝑐0(𝑢))=𝑐0(𝑢) (𝑠0 is the identity of 𝑘(𝑃)), that is 𝑐0(𝑢)=𝑢―: 𝐿𝜋 induces the identity on residue fields.

Compatibility with the sections. Let 𝑐∈𝑘(𝑃) and 𝑐′∈𝒪︀𝑃 with [𝑐′]𝑁=𝑠𝑁(𝑐). Then (𝑐,0,…,0) satisfies the defining identity for 𝑐′ at level 𝑁, so by uniqueness of the digits 𝑐0(𝑐′)=𝑐 and 𝑐𝑖(𝑐′)=0 for 1≤𝑖≤𝑁, that is 𝐿𝜋(𝑐′)≡𝑐(mod𝑋𝑁+1). The 𝑠𝑁 are unique, hence independent of 𝜋.

Uniqueness. Let 𝜙:𝐾→𝑘(𝑃)((𝑋)) be a valuation-preserving 𝑘-algebra homomorphism with 𝜙(𝜋)=𝑋 inducing the identity on the residue field. For 𝑁≥0 it maps 𝒪︀𝑃 into 𝑘(𝑃)[[𝑋]] and 𝔪𝑃𝑁+1 into 𝑋𝑁+1𝑘(𝑃)[[𝑋]], so induces a 𝑘-algebra homomorphism 𝜙𝑁:𝒪︀𝑃/𝔪𝑃𝑁+1→𝑘(𝑃)[𝑋]/(𝑋𝑁+1). Then 𝜙𝑁∘𝑠𝑁:𝑘(𝑃)→𝑘(𝑃)[𝑋]/(𝑋𝑁+1) is a 𝑘-algebra homomorphism which reduces to the identity of 𝑘(𝑃) modulo 𝑋 (as 𝜙 induces the identity on residue fields), as does the inclusion of the constants. 𝜙(𝑢)≡∑𝑖≤𝑁𝜙𝑁(𝑠𝑁(𝑐𝑖))𝑋𝑖=∑𝑖≤𝑁𝑐𝑖𝑋𝑖≡𝐿𝜋(𝑢)(mod𝑋𝑁+1)

for every 𝑁, so 𝜙=𝐿𝜋 on 𝒪︀𝑃 and hence on 𝐾=Frac(𝒪︀𝑃). ∎

Definition 6.3.24 (Residue Symbol of a Laurent Series)

Let 𝐴 be a commutative ring, 𝜋 an indeterminate, and 𝑧,𝑡∈𝐴((𝜋)). Write 𝜕𝜋 for the formal derivative, 𝜕𝜋(∑𝑛𝑎𝑛𝜋𝑛)≔∑𝑛𝑛𝑎𝑛𝜋𝑛−1, and define

Res𝜋(𝑧d𝑡)≔(𝑧𝜕𝜋(𝑡))−1∈𝐴,

the coefficient of 𝜋−1 in 𝑧𝜕𝜋(𝑡).

Definition 6.3.25 (Residue Symbol at a Place)

Let 𝑃 be a place of 𝐾/𝑘, 𝜋 a uniformiser, and 𝑡,𝑧∈𝐾. Let 𝐿𝜋:𝐾↪︎𝑘(𝑃)((𝑋)) be the Laurent coefficient embedding (6.3.23). Define the 𝑘(𝑃)-valued residue

Res𝑃𝑘(𝑃)(𝑧d𝑡)≔Res𝜋(𝐿𝜋(𝑧)d𝐿𝜋(𝑡))∈𝑘(𝑃),

where Res𝜋 is the residue of (6.3.24) with 𝐴≔𝑘(𝑃), and d𝐿𝜋(𝑡)≔𝜕𝜋(𝐿𝜋(𝑡))d𝜋, and the residue symbol

Res𝑃(𝑧d𝑡)≔Tr𝑘(𝑃)/𝑘(Res𝑃𝑘(𝑃)(𝑧d𝑡))∈𝑘.

Since 𝐿𝜋 commutes with the uniformiser derivation, 𝐿𝜋(𝜕𝜋(𝑡))=𝜕𝜋(𝐿𝜋(𝑡)) (6.3.29), one has explicitly

Res𝑃𝑘(𝑃)(𝑧d𝑡)=𝐿𝜋(𝑧𝜕𝜋(𝑡))−1=(𝐿𝜋(𝑧)𝜕𝜋(𝐿𝜋(𝑡)))−1.

If 𝑃 is rational then 𝑘(𝑃)=𝑘, the trace is the identity and Res𝑃=Res𝑃𝑘 is the 𝑘-valued Laurent coefficient. We show that both are independent of the choice of uniformiser 𝜋 below (6.3.31).

Lemma 6.3.26 (Residue of 𝜓𝑛𝜓′)

Let 𝐹 be a field and 𝜓∈𝑋𝐹[[𝑋]] with 𝜓′(0)≠0 (equivalently, 𝜓 has order exactly 1). For every 𝑛∈ℤ,

(𝜓𝑛𝜓′)−1=𝛿𝑛,−1
Remark 6.3.27
The simplest proof using the identity (𝑛+1)𝜓𝑛𝜓′=(𝜓𝑛+1)′ fails when char(𝑘)=𝑝∣𝑛+1, so some additional work is required.

Proof. Drafted by Claude Sonnet, reviewed by David Rufino.

𝑛≥0. 𝜓∈𝑋𝑘[[𝑋]] has no negative powers, so neither do 𝜓𝑛 or 𝜓′; their product has no 𝑋−1 term, giving 0, matching 𝛿𝑛,−1=0.

𝑛=−1. Write 𝜓(𝑋)=𝑋𝑣(𝑋) with 𝑣∈𝑘[[𝑋]]⋆ a unit (𝑣(0)=𝜓′(0)≠0). Then 𝜓′=𝑣+𝑋𝑣′, so

𝜓−1𝜓′=𝑣+𝑋𝑣′𝑋𝑣=1𝑋+𝑣′𝑣

The second term is a genuine power series (product of 𝑣′ and the unit 𝑣−1, both in 𝑘[[𝑋]]), so has zero −1-coefficient; the first contributes (𝑋−1)−1=1. Total: 1, matching 𝛿−1,−1.

𝑛≤−2: a universal identity. Fix 𝑛≤−2; we prove the identity over a universal coefficient ring first, then specialise (see the remark above for why). Let 𝑅≔ℤ[𝑎1,𝑎2,𝑎3,…][𝑎1−1], the polynomial ring over ℤ in countably many indeterminates 𝑎1,𝑎2,…, localised to invert 𝑎1 – an integral domain of characteristic 0 (a localisation of the domain ℤ[𝑎1,𝑎2,…], itself a domain as a polynomial ring over the domain ℤ, (3.7.3), applied to finitely many variables at a time since any product only ever involves finitely many of them). Let Ψ≔∑𝑖≥1𝑎𝑖𝑋𝑖∈𝑋𝑅[[𝑋]], so Ψ=𝑎1𝑋⋅𝑈 for 𝑈≔1+∑𝑖≥2(𝑎𝑖/𝑎1)𝑋𝑖−1∈1+𝑋𝑅[[𝑋]]; as 𝑈 has constant coefficient 1∈𝑅⋆, (3.32.13) gives 𝑈∈𝑅[[𝑋]]⋆, and hence Ψ∈𝑅((𝑋))⋆ too (𝑎1∈𝑅⋆, and 𝑋 a unit of 𝑅((𝑋)) directly by construction of the formal Laurent series ring, (3.32.1)). So Ψ𝑛Ψ′∈𝑅((𝑋)) is well-defined, with a well-defined coefficient of 𝑋−1, which we still write (Ψ𝑛Ψ′)−1∈𝑅.

As before (the power rule and vanishing of a derivative’s 𝑋−1-coefficient being valid over any commutative ring, not just 𝑘), (𝑛+1)Ψ𝑛Ψ′=(Ψ𝑛+1)′ (3.32.20) (Ψ being a unit) has vanishing 𝑋−1-coefficient:

Specialising. Write 𝜓=∑𝑖≥1𝑐𝑖𝑋𝑖, so 𝑐1=𝜓′(0)≠0 by hypothesis. As 𝑐1∈𝑘⋆, the assignment 𝑎1↦𝑐1, 𝑎𝑖↦𝑐𝑖 (𝑖≥2) extends, by the universal property of localisation (3.5.5) (applied to ℤ[𝑎1,𝑎2,…]→𝑘, 𝑎1↦𝑐1 a unit), to a ring homomorphism 𝜙:𝑅→𝑘. (𝜓𝑛𝜓′)−1=𝜙̂(Ψ𝑛Ψ′)−1=𝜙((Ψ𝑛Ψ′)−1)=𝜙(0)=0
