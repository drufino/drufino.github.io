3.34 Complete Discrete Valuation Rings

We isolate the algebraic (topology-free) notion of completeness for a DVR, needed to make sense of “formal” local models such as 𝑘[[𝑡]], and prove the key structural fact used throughout: a complete DVR has a unique extension of its valuation to any finite separable extension of its fraction field.

This generalises the previous section, which proved the case 𝒪︀𝐾≅𝑘[[𝑡]] by direct computation in power series, without this general completion machinery: (3.33.4) and (3.33.5) there are the special cases of (3.34.19) and (3.34.20) here.

Definition 3.34.1 (Complete DVR)
A DVR 𝐴 with maximal ideal 𝔪 is complete if every sequence (𝑦―𝑛)𝑛≥0 with 𝑦―𝑛∈𝐴/𝔪𝑛+1, compatible under reduction, is the image of some element of 𝐴 (equivalently, 𝐴→lim⟵𝑛𝐴/𝔪𝑛+1 is surjective; see (3.34.5) for the general criterion).
Proposition 3.34.2 (The Formal Power Series Ring is a Complete DVR)
Let 𝑘 be a field. Then 𝑘[[𝑡]] is a complete DVR.

Proof. Drafted by Claude Sonnet 5, reviewed by David Rufino.

By (3.10.7), 𝑘[[𝑡]] is a discrete valuation ring, so it remains to show completeness.

Let (𝑦―𝑛)𝑛≥0 with 𝑦―𝑛∈𝑘[[𝑡]]/(𝑡)𝑛+1 be compatible under reduction. Each 𝑦―𝑛 has a unique representative 𝑓𝑛=∑𝑖≤𝑛𝑐𝑖(𝑛)𝑡𝑖 of degree at most 𝑛. Compatibility says 𝑓𝑛≡𝑓𝑛−1(mod𝑡𝑛), so 𝑐𝑖(𝑛)=𝑐𝑖(𝑛−1) for 𝑖<𝑛. Hence for each 𝑖 the coefficient 𝑐𝑖≔𝑐𝑖(𝑛) is independent of 𝑛≥𝑖. Put 𝑓≔∑𝑖𝑐𝑖𝑡𝑖∈𝑘[[𝑡]]. For each 𝑛, the coefficients of 𝑓 up to degree 𝑛 are those of 𝑓𝑛, so 𝑓≡𝑓𝑛(mod𝑡𝑛+1), that is 𝑦―𝑛 is the reduction of 𝑓. Thus 𝑘[[𝑡]] is complete. ∎

Proposition 3.34.3 (A Complete DVR with a Coefficient Field is a Power Series Ring)
Let (𝐴,𝔪) be a complete DVR, 𝜋 a uniformiser, and 𝑘⊆𝐴 a coefficient field (𝑘(𝔪)=𝑘). Then there is a 𝑘-algebra isomorphism 𝑘[[𝑌]]⟶∼𝐴, 𝑌↦𝜋.

Proof. Drafted by Claude Sonnet 5, reviewed by David Rufino.

Coefficients. Since 𝐴/𝔪=𝑘 and 𝔪=(𝜋), every 𝑎∈𝐴 can be written 𝑎≡∑𝑖<𝑁𝑐𝑖𝜋𝑖(mod𝜋𝑁) with 𝑐𝑖∈𝑘, and the 𝑐𝑖 are uniquely determined by 𝑎 (apply 𝑣). Indeed 𝑎−𝑐0∈𝔪=𝜋𝐴 gives 𝑎=𝑐0+𝜋𝑎1, and iterate.

Definition of Φ. Let 𝑓=∑𝑛𝑐𝑛𝑌𝑛∈𝑘[[𝑌]] with truncations 𝑠𝑁≔∑𝑛<𝑁𝑐𝑛𝜋𝑛. Since 𝑠𝑁+1−𝑠𝑁=𝑐𝑁𝜋𝑁∈𝜋𝑁𝐴, the sequence (𝑠𝑁mod𝜋𝑁)𝑁 is compatible under reduction, so by completeness of 𝐴 (3.34.1) there is Φ(𝑓)∈𝐴 with Φ(𝑓)≡𝑠𝑁(mod𝜋𝑁) for every 𝑁. It is unique, as ⋂𝑁𝜋𝑁𝐴=0.

Homomorphism. Φ is 𝑘-linear, and Φ(1)=1. For 𝑓,𝑔, the truncations satisfy 𝑠𝑁(𝑓)𝑠𝑁(𝑔)≡𝑠𝑁(𝑓𝑔)(mod𝜋𝑁) (the products agree modulo 𝜋𝑁, since the difference only involves coefficients of degree ≥𝑁 in 𝑌, sent to multiples of 𝜋𝑁). Hence Φ(𝑓)Φ(𝑔)≡𝑠𝑁(𝑓)𝑠𝑁(𝑔)≡𝑠𝑁(𝑓𝑔)≡Φ(𝑓𝑔)(mod𝜋𝑁) for all 𝑁, so Φ(𝑓)Φ(𝑔)=Φ(𝑓𝑔). Additivity is the same argument.

Injective. If Φ(𝑓)=0 and 𝑓≠0, let 𝑛0 be minimal with 𝑐𝑛0≠0. Then 𝜋𝑛0𝑐𝑛0≡Φ(𝑓)−∑𝑛>𝑛0𝑐𝑛𝜋𝑛≡0(mod𝜋𝑛0+1), so 𝑐𝑛0𝜋𝑛0∈𝜋𝑛0+1𝐴, contradicting the uniqueness of the coefficients above.

Surjective. Given 𝑎∈𝐴, take its coefficients 𝑐𝑖∈𝑘 from the first paragraph. These are compatible (the expansion modulo 𝜋𝑁 is the truncation of the expansion modulo 𝜋𝑁+1), so 𝑓≔∑𝑖𝑐𝑖𝑌𝑖 satisfies Φ(𝑓)≡𝑎(mod𝜋𝑁) for every 𝑁, whence Φ(𝑓)=𝑎. ∎

Let (𝐴,𝔪) be a local ring with ⋂𝑛𝔪𝑛=0 (automatic if 𝐴 is a DVR (3.31.9)), and 𝐴̂, 𝜄 as in (3.34.4). Then the following are equivalent:

  1. 𝜄:𝐴→𝐴̂ is surjective;

This is the special case 𝔞≔𝔪 of (3.14.21), noting 𝐴̂𝔪=𝐴̂, 𝜄𝔪=𝜄. ∎

Let (𝐴,𝔪) be a local ring, and 𝐴̂, 𝜆𝑛 as in (3.34.4). Then 𝐴̂ is local, with units, for (𝑎𝑛)𝑛≥0∈𝐴̂, characterised by

Proposition 3.34.8 (The Completion is a DVR)

𝜋̂ is a non-unit; 𝐴̂ is not a field. As 𝜄 is injective and 𝜋≠0, 𝜋̂=𝜄(𝜋)≠0. Writing 𝜋̂=(𝜋―𝑚)𝑚, 𝜋―0=𝜋mod𝔪=0 (as 𝜋∈𝔪), so 𝜋̂∉𝐴̂⋆ by d). Proposition 3.34.10 (Taut Criterion, DVR Case)
Let (𝐴,𝔪), (𝐵,𝔫) be DVRs, 𝜙:𝐴→𝐵 a local ring homomorphism, and 𝑒 its ramification index (3.31.10). Then 𝜙 is taut (3.14.10) if and only if 𝑒=1 and 𝐵=𝜙(𝐴)+𝔫.

Proposition 3.34.19 (Uniqueness of the Prime Above a Complete Base)
Let (𝒪︀𝐾,𝔪) be a complete DVR, 𝐾≔Frac(𝒪︀𝐾), 𝐿/𝐾 a finite separable extension, and 𝒪︀𝐿 the integral closure of 𝒪︀𝐾 in 𝐿. Then 𝒪︀𝐿 has a unique prime over 𝔪.

Write 𝔮1,…,𝔮𝑠 for the primes of 𝒪︀𝐿 over 𝔪 (there is at least one, by Lying Over (3.24.17), and – 𝒪︀𝐿 being Dedekind and integral over the DVR 𝒪︀𝐾 – these are exactly the maximal ideals of 𝒪︀𝐿), with ramification indices 𝑒𝑙; by the Ramification Formula (3.32.16), 𝔪𝒪︀𝐿=∏𝑙𝔮𝑙𝑒𝑙.
