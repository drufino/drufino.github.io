3.20 Local Rings

Local rings arise quite naturally when localizing at a prime ideal (see (3.6.42) and (3.20.5)) so we recall some basic properties here.

Definition 3.20.1 (Local Ring)

A ring 𝐴 is a local ring if it has a unique maximal ideal 𝔪. The field 𝐴/𝔪 is called the residue field of 𝐴 and sometimes denoted 𝜅(𝔪).

When 𝐴 is a 𝑘-algebra we may denote the residue field by 𝑘(𝔪).

Definition 3.20.2 (Local Homomorphism)

Let (𝐴,𝔪𝐴) and (𝐵,𝔪𝐵) be local rings. A ring homomorphism 𝜙:𝐴→𝐵 is said to be a local homomorphism if

𝜙(𝔪𝐴)⊆𝔪𝐵

Recall that the group of units 𝐴⋆ of a ring is a saturated multiplicative set, that is

𝑥𝑦∈𝐴⋆⟺𝑥∈𝐴⋆∧𝑦∈𝐴⋆
Proposition 3.20.3 (Criteria for Local Rings)

Let 𝐴 be a ring. Then the following are equivalent

  1. 𝐴 is a local ring
  2. 𝐴∖𝐴⋆ is an additive subgroup of 𝐴

In this case 𝔪=𝐴∖𝐴⋆ is the unique maximal ideal of 𝐴.

Proof. a) ⟹ b) Let 𝔪 be the unique maximal ideal then, because it’s proper, 𝔪∩𝐴⋆=∅⟹𝔪⊆𝐴∖𝐴⋆ by (3.3.13). Conversely given 𝑥∈𝐴∖𝐴⋆ then (𝑥) is a proper ideal by (3.3.33), and therefore contained in a maximal ideal (3.3.15) which by uniqueness means 𝑥∈𝔪.

b) ⟹ a) Define 𝔪=𝐴∖𝐴⋆ it’s a (prime) ideal because it is an additive subgroup and 𝐴⋆ is a saturated multiplicative set. Let 𝔞 be a proper ideal then 𝔞∩𝐴⋆=∅⟹𝔞⊆𝔪. Therefore 𝔪 is the unique maximal ideal. ∎

Proposition 3.20.4 (Quotient of a Local Ring is Local)
Let (𝐴,𝔪) be a local ring and 𝔞◃𝐴 a proper ideal. Then 𝐴/𝔞 is a local ring, with maximal ideal 𝔪/𝔞.

Proof. Drafted by Claude Sonnet, reviewed by David Rufino.

As 𝔞 is proper, it is contained in some maximal ideal (3.3.15), which must be 𝔪 by uniqueness; so 𝔞⊆𝔪, and by the ideal correspondence of (3.3.56), the maximal ideals of 𝐴/𝔞 correspond to the maximal ideals of 𝐴 containing 𝔞 – of which 𝔪 is the only one (as 𝐴 is local) – so 𝐴/𝔞 has the unique maximal ideal 𝔪/𝔞, that is, is local. ∎

Example 3.20.5

Let 𝐴 be a ring and 𝔭◃𝐴 a prime ideal. Then 𝐴𝔭 is a local ring with unique maximal ideal 𝔭𝐴𝔭.

When 𝐴⊂𝐾 is a subring of a field then 𝐴⊂𝐴𝔭⊂𝐾 in a natural way.

We may use this to provide another criteria

Lemma 3.20.6 (Criteria for Local Domain)

Let 𝐴⊂𝐾 be a subring of a field with a prime ideal 𝔭◃𝐴. Then 𝐴⊂𝐴𝔭.

𝐴 is a local ring with unique maximal ideal 𝔭 if and only if 𝐴=𝐴𝔭,

Proof. We’ve observed that 𝐴𝔭 is a local ring with unique maximal ideal 𝔭𝐴𝔭.

If 𝐴=𝐴𝔭 then it is a local ring and 𝔭⊂𝔭𝐴𝔭. For 𝑦∉𝔭, then 1𝑦∈𝐴𝔭⟹1𝑦∈𝐴. So we see that 𝑥∈𝔭,𝑦∉𝔭 we have 𝑥𝑦∈𝔭 and 𝔭=𝔭𝐴𝔭.

Conversely suppose 𝐴 is a local ring with unique maximal ideal 𝔭. Then 𝑦∉𝔭⟹𝑦∈𝐴⋆ and 𝐴𝔭=𝐴 as required. ∎
