2.6.9 Adjoint Functors

Some universal constructions may be expressed as an adjoint pair of functors. My view is the most illustrative are the module Tensor-Hom adjunction and the sheafification construction. Using this concept we can simplify the verification of universal properties by appealing to general criteria for adjoint functors as below.

Definition 2.6.69 (Adjoint Pair)

Let 𝐹:𝒞︀→𝒟︀ and 𝐺:𝒟︀→𝒞︀ be functors. We say that 𝐹 is left adjoint to 𝐺 if there is a bijection

𝜓𝐶,𝐷:Mor(𝐹(𝐶),𝐷)⟶Mor(𝐶,𝐺(𝐷))

which is natural in 𝐶 and 𝐷 in the following sense. Let 𝛼:𝐶′→𝐶, 𝛽:𝐷→𝐷′, then for all 𝑓:𝐹(𝐶)→𝐷 we have

𝜓𝐶′,𝐷′(𝛽∘𝑓∘𝐹(𝛼))=𝐺(𝛽)∘𝜓𝐶,𝐷(𝑓)∘𝛼(1)

or equivalently for all 𝑔:𝐶→𝐺(𝐷)

𝛽∘𝜓𝐶,𝐷−1(𝑔)∘𝐹(𝛼)=𝜓𝐶′,𝐷′−1(𝐺(𝛽)∘𝑔∘𝛼)(2)

Equivalently 𝐹(−) represents the bifunctor Mor(−,𝐺(−)) (2.6.65).

Proposition 2.6.70 (Adjoint⇒unit, counit)

Let 𝐹:𝒞︀→𝒟︀ and 𝐺:𝒟︀→𝒞︀ be adjoint functors with relationship

𝜓𝐶,𝐷:Mor(𝐹(𝐶),𝐷)⟶∼Mor(𝐶,𝐺(𝐷))

Then we have two natural transformations (unit and counit respectively)

𝜂:id𝒞︀⇒𝐺∘𝐹𝜖:𝐹∘𝐺⇒id𝒟︀

defined by

𝜂𝐶=𝜓𝐶,𝐹(𝐶)(1𝐹(𝐶))𝜖𝐷=𝜓𝐺(𝐷),𝐷−1(1𝐺(𝐷))

Furthermore we may recover the adjoint relationship via

𝜓𝐶,𝐷(𝑓)=𝐺(𝑓)∘𝜂𝐶𝜓𝐶,𝐷−1(𝑔)=𝜖𝐷∘𝐹(𝑔)

Proof. We show that the transformations given are natural. Suppose 𝛼:𝐶→𝐶′ then

𝐺(𝐹(𝛼))∘𝜂𝐶=𝐺(𝐹(𝛼))∘𝜓𝐶,𝐹(𝐶)(1𝐹(𝐶))=𝜓𝐶,𝐹(𝐶′)(𝐹(𝛼)∘1𝐹(𝐶))(1)=𝜓𝐶,𝐹(𝐶′)(1𝐹(𝐶′)∘𝐹(𝛼))=𝜓𝐶′,𝐹(𝐶′)(1𝐹(𝐶′))∘𝛼(1)=𝜂𝐶′∘𝛼

so 𝜂 is a natural transformation. Furthermore

𝜓𝐶,𝐷(𝑓)=𝜓𝐶,𝐷(𝑓∘1𝐹(𝐶))=𝐺(𝑓)∘𝜓𝐶,𝐹(𝐶)(1)=𝐺(𝑓)∘𝜂𝐶

as required. Similarly for 𝛽:𝐷→𝐷′

𝛽∘𝜖𝐷=𝛽∘𝜓𝐺(𝐷),𝐷−1(1𝐺(𝐷))=𝜓𝐺(𝐷),𝐷′−1(𝐺(𝛽)∘1𝐺(𝐷))(2)=𝜓𝐺(𝐷),𝐷′−1(1𝐺(𝐷′)∘𝐺(𝛽))=𝜓𝐺(𝐷′),𝐷′−1(1𝐺(𝐷′))∘𝐹(𝐺(𝛽))(2)=𝜖𝐷′∘𝐹(𝐺(𝛽))

 ∎

Given two natural transformations we may recover a corresponding adjoint

Proposition 2.6.71 (Adjoint from unit and counit)

Let 𝐹:𝒞︀→𝒟︀ and 𝐺:𝒟︀→𝒞︀ be functors with two natural transformations

𝜂:id𝒞︀⇒𝐺∘𝐹𝜖:𝐹∘𝐺⇒id𝒟︀

Then the following are equivalent

  1. 𝐹 is left adjoint to 𝐺 with unit and counit 𝜂, 𝜖, and

  2. the so-called triangular identities are satisfied

    1𝐺(𝐷):𝐺(𝐷)⟶𝜂𝐺(𝐷)𝐺𝐹𝐺(𝐷)⟶𝐺(𝜖𝐷)𝐺(𝐷)1𝐹(𝐶):𝐹(𝐶)⟶𝐹(𝜂𝐶)𝐹𝐺𝐹(𝐶)⟶𝜖𝐹(𝐶)𝐹(𝐶)

More precisely the adjunction is given by

Mor(𝐹(𝐶),𝐷)⇌𝜙𝜓Mor(𝐶,𝐺(𝐷))𝑓⟶𝐺(𝑓)∘𝜂𝐶𝜖𝐷∘𝐹(𝑔)⟵𝑔

Proof. Let 𝜓,𝜙 denote the proposed adjunction maps. We will use the triangular identities to show that these are mutually inverse. First observe by naturality of 𝜂 and 𝜖 that

𝜓𝜙(𝑔)=𝐺(𝜖𝐷∘𝐹(𝑔))∘𝜂𝐶=𝐺(𝜖𝐷)∘𝜂𝐺(𝐷)∘𝑔(3)𝜙𝜓(𝑓)=𝜖𝐷∘𝐹(𝐺(𝑓)∘𝜂𝐶)=𝑓∘𝜖𝐹(𝐶)∘𝐹(𝜂𝐶)

It’s then immediate that these are mutually inverse maps if and only if the triangular identities are satisfied (one way is obvious, the other way consider 𝑓=1𝐹(𝐶) and 𝑔=1𝐺(𝐷)).

Further one may easily verify that 𝜓,𝜙 so-defined are natural in 𝐶 and 𝐷

𝜓(𝛽∘𝑓∘𝐹(𝛼))=𝐺(𝛽)∘𝐺(𝑓)∘𝐺𝐹(𝛼)∘𝜂𝐶=𝐺(𝛽)∘𝐺(𝑓)∘𝜂𝐶′∘𝛼=𝐺(𝛽)∘𝜓(𝑓)∘𝛼

 ∎

Proposition 2.6.72 (Criteria for right adjoint to be full and faithful)

Let 𝒞︀⇌𝐺𝐹𝒟︀ be adjoint functors with 𝜂,𝜖 unit and counit transformations. Then

  • 𝐺 is faithful if and only if 𝜖 is pointwise epic
  • 𝐺 is full if and only if 𝜖 is pointwise split-monic
  • 𝐺 is full and faithful if and only if 𝜖 is a pointwise isomorphism

Proof. Consider the composite map

Mor(𝐷′,𝐷)⟶Mor(𝜖𝐷′,𝐷)Mor(𝐹(𝐺(𝐷′)),𝐷)→𝜓𝐺(𝐷′),𝐷Mor(𝐺(𝐷′),𝐺(𝐷))

which is natural in 𝐷 and 𝐷′. Note that image of 𝛼∈Mor(𝐷′,𝐷) is

𝜓𝐺(𝐷′),𝐷(𝛼∘𝜖𝐷′)=𝐺(𝛼)∘𝜓𝐺(𝐷′),𝐷′(𝜖𝐷′)=𝐺(𝛼)

so the composite is just 𝐺(−). The second map is bijective by the adjoint assumption. Therefore the first map is injective (resp. surjective) if and only if 𝐺 is faithful (resp. full).

By (2.6.44) Mor(𝜖𝐷′,𝐷) is injective (resp. surjective) for all 𝐷,𝐷′ if and only if 𝜖𝐷′ is epic (resp. split-monic) for all 𝐷′.

Then the first two statements follow easily. The last statement follows from the previous two, combined with (2.6.36). ∎

Proposition 2.6.73 (Reflective Sub-Category)

Let 𝒞︀⇌𝐺𝐹𝒟︀ be adjoint functors such that 𝐺 is fully faithful. Then for 𝐶∈𝒞︀ we have 𝜂𝐶 is an isomorphism iff 𝐶 is in the essential image of 𝐺 (i.e. is isomorphic to 𝐺(𝐷) for some 𝐷).

In particular let 𝒞︀′ denote the essential image of 𝒟︀ under 𝐺. Then this restricts to an equivalence of categories 𝒞︀′⇌𝐺𝐹𝒟︀

Proof. If 𝜂𝐶:𝐶→𝐺𝐹(𝐶) is an isomorphism define 𝐷≔𝐹(𝐶), and we are done.

Conversely we first show that 𝜂𝐺(𝐷) is an isomorphism. By the triangular identities (2.6.71) we have

𝐺(𝜖𝐷)∘𝜂𝐺(𝐷)=1𝐺(𝐷)

so 𝜂𝐺(𝐷) has a left inverse. Recall by (2.6.72) 𝜖𝐷 is an isomorphism, whence so is 𝐺(𝜖𝐷) (2.6.42). Therefore we may show directly that it is also a right inverse, and therefore 𝜂𝐺(𝐷) is an isomorphism.

Now assume that we have an isomorphism 𝜓:𝐶→∼𝐺(𝐷) for some 𝐷∈𝒟︀. Then by naturality we have a commutative diagram

observing that 𝐺𝐹(𝜓) is also an isomorphism. Then we conclude 𝜂𝐶 is an isomorphism as required. ∎

Proposition 2.6.74 (Adjoints can be constructed pointwise)

Let 𝐺:𝒟︀→𝒞︀ be a functor. Suppose that for all 𝐶∈ob(𝒞︀) there exists 𝐹(𝐶)∈ob(𝒟︀) such that there is a bijection natural in 𝐷

𝜓𝐶,𝐷:Mor(𝐹(𝐶),𝐷)→∼Mor(𝐶,𝐺(𝐷))

Then there exists a unique functor 𝐹 which ensures this is natural in 𝐶, more precisely for 𝑔:𝐶→𝐶′ this is given by

𝐹(𝑔)=𝜓𝐶,𝐹(𝐶′)−1(𝜂𝐶′∘𝑔)𝜂𝐶≔𝜓𝐶,𝐹(𝐶)(1𝐹(𝐶))

This is also the unique functor which ensures 𝜂𝐶 is a natural transformation.

Proof. The hypothesis is precisely that, for each 𝐶∈𝒞︀, (𝐹(𝐶),𝜓𝐶,−) represents the functor Mor(𝐶,𝐺(−)):𝒟︀→𝒮︀; equivalently, this is the hypothesis of (2.6.66) applied to the (already well-defined, since 𝐺 is a functor) bifunctor Mor(−,𝐺(−)):𝒞︀op×𝒟︀→𝒮︀, with the roles of the two categories there swapped so that 𝐹(𝐶) plays the role of the representing object varying over 𝒞︀. That proposition’s conclusion is precisely that 𝐹 can be made into a functor uniquely such that 𝜓 is a natural isomorphism of bifunctors, giving the first uniqueness; its explicit formula for 𝑔:𝐶→𝐶′ reads

𝐹(𝑔)=𝜓𝐶,𝐹(𝐶′)−1(𝜓𝐶′,𝐹(𝐶′)(1𝐹(𝐶′))∘𝑔)=𝜓𝐶,𝐹(𝐶′)−1(𝜂𝐶′∘𝑔)

using the definition of 𝜂𝐶′ for the second equality.

We have already shown that if 𝜓𝐶,𝐷 is natural in 𝐶 then 𝜂𝐶 is a natural transformation (2.6.70). Conversely suppose that 𝜂𝐶 is a natural transformation. Since we have assumed that 𝜓𝐶,𝐷 is natural in 𝐷, applying this to 𝛽≔𝐹(𝑔) and 𝑓≔1𝐹(𝐶) gives

𝜓𝐶,𝐹(𝐶′)(𝐹(𝑔))=𝜓𝐶,𝐹(𝐶′)(𝐹(𝑔)∘1𝐹(𝐶))=𝐺(𝐹(𝑔))∘𝜓𝐶,𝐹(𝐶)(1𝐹(𝐶))=𝐺𝐹(𝑔)∘𝜂𝐶=𝜂𝐶′∘𝑔

using naturality of 𝜂 for the last equality, whence 𝐹(𝑔)=𝜓𝐶,𝐹(𝐶′)−1(𝜂𝐶′∘𝑔). ∎

Proposition 2.6.75

Let 𝒞︀⇌𝐺𝐹𝒟︀ be a pair of adjoint functors. Then 𝐹 preserves initial objects and 𝐺 preserves terminal objects.

If 𝒞︀,𝒟︀ are pointed categories then 𝐹 and 𝐺 also preserve zero objects and zero morphisms.

Proof. We only prove that 𝐹 preserves initial objects, as the second statement follows dually. By hypothesis there is a bijection natural in 𝐷

Mor(𝐹(∅),𝐷)⟶∼Mor(∅,𝐺(𝐷))

Therefore if ∅ is initial, then so is 𝐹(∅).

The last statement follows from (2.6.55) and (2.6.57). ∎
