6.2.19 Weil Divisors

The notion of regular curves can be generalised in one direction.

Definition 6.2.104 (Prime Divisors of an Integral Variety)

Let 𝑋 be an integral, separated variety. We say it is regular in codimension one if for every irreducible closed subset 𝑌⊂𝑋 for which codim(𝑌,𝑋)=1 the local ring 𝒪︀𝑋,𝑌 is regular (equivalently integrally closed or a DVR (3.31.2)).

In this case, we say an irreducible closed subset of codimension 1 is a prime divisor.

We denote by Div𝑋 the free abelian group generated by the prime divisors. We write a prime divisor 𝐷 by a formal sum

𝐷=∑𝑌⊂𝑋𝑛𝑌𝑌

where 𝑛𝑌∈ℤ and all but finitely many are zero. We define the degree of a divisor 𝐷 by

deg𝐷=∑𝑌⊂𝑋𝑛𝑌[𝑘(𝔪𝑋,𝑌):𝑘]
Proposition 6.2.105

An integral, separated variety 𝑋 is regular in codimension one for either of the following special cases

  1. 𝑋 is normal
  2. 𝑋 is non-singular and 𝑘 is perfect.
Proof. See (3.31.2) and (6.2.82). ∎
Proposition 6.2.106 (Valuation Associated to Prime Divisor)

Let 𝑋 be a variety satisfying (6.2.104) and 𝑌⊂𝑋 a prime divisor. Then 𝒪︀𝑋,𝑌 is a discrete valuation ring.

We denote by 𝑣𝑌:𝑘(𝑋)⋆→ℤ the corresponding valuation.

Proof. By (6.2.55) dim𝒪︀𝑋,𝑌=codim(𝑌,𝑋)=1 and is integral, so the result follows from (3.31.2). ∎
Proposition 6.2.107 (Prime Divisors on a Curve)
Let 𝑋 be a regular curve. Then 𝑋 satisfies (6.2.104) and the prime divisors are precisely irreducible closed subsets of the form {𝑥}―. These are in bijective correspondence with the valuation rings of 𝑘(𝑋), via the map 𝑥→𝒪︀𝑋,𝑥.
Proof. By the codimension formula (6.2.55) the irreducible closed subsets of codimension 1 are precisely those of dimension 0. Therefore the result follows from (6.2.124). ∎
Proposition 6.2.108
Let 𝑋 be a variety satisfying (6.2.104) and 𝑓∈𝑘(𝑋)⋆ a rational function. Then 𝑣𝑌(𝑓)=0 for all but finitely many prime divisors 𝑌.

Proof. By definition 𝑓 is regular on some open subset 𝑈 of 𝑋, which we may assume to be affine. Define 𝑍≔𝑋∖𝑈. Then by (…) 𝑍=𝑍1∪…∪𝑍𝑛 is a decomposition into irreducible components. By (4.1.83).f) codim(𝑍,𝑋)>0 whence codim(𝑍𝑖,𝑋)>0 for all 𝑖=1…𝑛. Suppose 𝑌⊂𝑍 is a prime divisor then by definition codim(𝑌,𝑋)=1 and by (4.1.66) 𝑌⊂𝑍𝑖 for some 𝑖. Therefore by (4.1.83).c) codim(𝑌,𝑍𝑖)≤codim(𝑌,𝑋)−codim(𝑍𝑖,𝑋)≤0 and therefore by (4.1.83).f) 𝑌=𝑍𝑖.

We conclude all but finitely many 𝑌 intersect 𝑈, so we may without loss of generality assume 𝑌∩𝑈≠∅. Evidently 𝑈 also satisfies (6.2.104) and by (4.1.79) there is a bijection between prime divisors of 𝑋 meeting 𝑈, and prime divisors of 𝑈.

Therefore we may assume that 𝑋 is affine and 0≠𝑓∈𝒪︀𝑋(𝑋). Then 𝑣𝑌(𝑓)>0⟺𝑓∈𝔐𝑋,𝑌⟺𝑓∈𝐼(𝑌)⟺𝑌⊂𝑉(𝑓). We claim 𝑉(𝑓) is a proper subset of 𝑋 (for 𝑉(𝑓)=𝑋⟺(0)=𝑓⟺𝑓=0). Therefore codim(𝑉(𝑓),𝑋)>0 and

codim(𝑌,𝑉(𝑓))≤codim(𝑌,𝑋)−codim(𝑉(𝑓),𝑋)≤0

so 𝑌 is an irreducible component of 𝑉(𝑓), of which there are only finitely many (4.1.66). ∎

Definition 6.2.109 (Principal Divisor)

Let 𝑋 be a variety satisfying (6.2.104) then define the homomorphism

𝑘(𝑋)⋆⟶Div(𝑋)𝑓⟶(𝑓)

where we define a formal sum over all prime divisors

(𝑓)≔∑𝑌⊂𝑋𝑣𝑌(𝑓)𝑌.

The co-kernel of this map is known as the divisor class group of 𝑋, and denoted Cl(𝑋). We say that two divisors are equivalent 𝐷∼𝐷′ if they become equal as elements of the divisor class group. Equivalently if 𝐷−𝐷′=(𝑓) for some 𝑓∈𝑘(𝑋)⋆.
