6.2.16 Projective Space

We introduce the notion of projective varieties by showing that projective space can naturally be given the structure of an abstract variety. A more low-tech approach is taken in the Projective Varieties section, where the constructions are shown to be equivalent.

We show later that all projective closed subvarieties are complete, but here we demonstrate a simpler case more directly.

Proposition 6.2.95
ℙ𝑘1 is complete.
Proof. Let 𝑍⊂ℙ𝑘1 be an irreducible closed subvariety. Then by (4.1.83) (6.2.56) it is either the (closure of) a single point or the whole space. In the former case the function field is algebraic over 𝑘 and has no non-trivial valuation rings. Therefore we may assume wlog that 𝑍=ℙ𝑘1 and consider a valuation ring 𝑅⊂𝑘(ℙ𝑘1)=𝑘(𝑥) where 𝑥=𝑋1𝑋0.
