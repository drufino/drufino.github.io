6.3.6 The Algebraic Residue Theorem over an Algebraically Closed Field

Assume throughout this subsection that 𝑘 is algebraically closed. Then every place of 𝐾/𝑘 is rational (6.2.114), so the entire theory of (6.3.25) applies directly, with no need for the base-change-to-𝑘― machinery of the following subsections (which is needed only once 𝑘 is no longer assumed algebraically closed).

Proposition 6.3.34 (Algebraic Residue Theorem, over an Algebraically Closed Field)

Assume 𝑘 is algebraically closed. For 𝑡,𝑓∈𝐾,

∑𝑃Res𝑃(𝑓d𝑡)=0,

summed over all places 𝑃 of 𝐾/𝑘 (finitely many terms nonzero, as 𝑓d𝑡 has only finitely many poles).

Roadmap. The proof, given immediately below, proceeds in the following steps.

  • Reduce to a differential 𝑓d𝑥. Choose a separating transcendental 𝑥∈𝐾; then 𝑓d𝑡=𝑓𝜕𝑥(𝑡)d𝑥 at every place (6.3.33).b).
  • The case 𝐾=𝑘(𝑥). Partial fractions over 𝑘=𝑘― (6.3.35) write a rational function as a sum of terms 𝑐𝑖,𝑗(𝑥−𝜃𝑖)−𝑗; the finite residues are the 𝑐𝑖,1, and the place at infinity has residue −∑𝑖𝑐𝑖,1 (6.3.39).
  • Trace formula. For a finite separable 𝐾/𝑘(𝑥) and a place 𝑄 of 𝑘(𝑥), ∑𝑃∣𝑄Res𝑃(𝑓d𝑥)=Res𝑄(Tr𝐾/𝑘(𝑥)(𝑓)d𝑥) (6.3.42). This is proved locally, after completing at 𝑄: the completed field splits as ∏𝑖𝑘((𝜋𝑖)) over the places above 𝑄, reducing the identity to the local trace formula at each place, which is computed monomial by monomial via the roots of unity in 𝑘, following Lang.
  • Assemble. Group the places 𝑃 of 𝐾 by the place 𝑄 of 𝑘(𝑥) below them. The trace formula turns each inner sum into Res𝑄(Tr𝐾/𝑘(𝑥)(𝑓′)d𝑥), and summing over 𝑄 gives 0 by the case 𝐾=𝑘(𝑥).

Proof of (6.3.34). Drafted by Claude Sonnet, reviewed by David Rufino.

Fix any transcendental 𝑥∈𝐾 with 𝐾/𝑘(𝑥) finite and separable (exists as 𝐾/𝑘 is a function field, separably generated), and let 𝜕𝑥∈Der𝑘(𝐾,𝐾) be the distinguished element of (6.3.20) with 𝜕𝑥(𝑥)=1. By (6.3.33).b), for every place 𝑃,

Res𝑃(𝑓d𝑡)=Res𝑃((𝑓⋅𝜕𝑥(𝑡))d𝑥)

this handles 𝑡∈𝑘 automatically too, as 𝜕𝑥(𝑘)=0 then makes both sides 0, matching d𝑡=0. So it suffices to prove ∑𝑃Res𝑃(𝑓′d𝑥)=0 for 𝑓′≔𝑓⋅𝜕𝑥(𝑡)∈𝐾.

Every place 𝑃 of 𝐾/𝑘 lies over a unique place 𝑄 of 𝑘(𝑥)/𝑘, so (finitely many terms being nonzero) the sum may be regrouped:

∑𝑃of𝐾/𝑘Res𝑃(𝑓′d𝑥)=∑𝑄of𝑘(𝑥)/𝑘∑𝑃∣𝑄Res𝑃(𝑓′d𝑥).

By (6.3.42), the inner sum is Res𝑄(Tr𝐾/𝑘(𝑥)(𝑓′)d𝑥) for every 𝑄, so

∑𝑃Res𝑃(𝑓′d𝑥)=∑𝑄Res𝑄(Tr𝐾/𝑘(𝑥)(𝑓′)d𝑥)=0

by (6.3.39), applied to Tr𝐾/𝑘(𝑥)(𝑓′)∈𝑘(𝑥). ∎

This proof uses only (6.3.20) (already available, with no need for the module of differentials Ω𝐾/𝑘 to have been formally constructed).

Lemma 6.3.41 (Places over 𝑃 and Irreducible Factors)
Let 𝑘 be algebraically closed, 𝐾/𝑘(𝑥) a finite separable extension of degree 𝑛, 𝑃 the place (𝑥) of 𝑘(𝑥), 𝐾𝑃≔𝑘((𝑥)), and 𝑦∈𝐾 a primitive element with minimal polynomial 𝑔 over 𝑘(𝑥). Factor 𝑔=𝑔1⋯𝑔𝑟 into distinct monic irreducibles over 𝐾𝑃, with 𝑑𝑗≔deg𝑔𝑗, and let 𝐸𝑗≔𝐾𝑃[𝑌]/(𝑔𝑗) with 𝜄𝑗:𝐾↪︎𝐸𝑗 sending 𝑦↦𝑌. Then 𝑗↦𝑄𝑗, defined below, is a bijection from {1,…,𝑟} onto the places of 𝐾/𝑘 over 𝑃, and 𝑒𝑄𝑗=𝑑𝑗.

Proof. Drafted by Claude Sonnet, reviewed by David Rufino.

The place 𝑄𝑗. 𝐸𝑗/𝐾𝑃 is finite separable (as 𝑔𝑗 is separable), so by (3.32.34) there is an isomorphism Ψ𝑗:𝐸𝑗→∼𝑘((𝜋𝑗)) over 𝐾𝑃 with Ψ𝑗(𝑥) of order 𝑑𝑗. Pulling back the valuation of 𝑘((𝜋𝑗)) along Ψ𝑗∘𝜄𝑗 gives a discrete valuation of 𝐾 extending 𝑣𝑃 with ramification index 𝑑𝑗; let 𝑄𝑗 be the corresponding place.

Every place is some 𝑄𝑗. Let 𝑄 be a place of 𝐾/𝑘 over 𝑃. As 𝑘 is algebraically closed, 𝑄 is rational, so (6.3.23) gives a valuation-preserving embedding 𝐿𝑄:𝐾↪︎𝑘((𝜋𝑄)). Since 𝑔(𝑦)=0, the element 𝐿𝑄(𝑦) is a root of 𝑔 in 𝑘((𝜋𝑄)). The 𝑔𝑗 are pairwise coprime in 𝐾𝑃[𝑌], so 𝐿𝑄(𝑦) is a root of exactly one 𝑔𝑗 (a common root of 𝑔𝑗,𝑔𝑗′ would be a root of 1, by Bézout). Call this 𝑗=𝑗(𝑄). Then 𝐿𝑄 factors through 𝐸𝑗, and 𝐿𝑄(𝑥) has ramification index 𝑒𝑄 over 𝐾𝑃; the valuation induced on 𝐸𝑗 extends 𝑣𝑃, so by the uniqueness of extension ((3.32.35), applied to 𝐸𝑗/𝐾𝑃) it equals the valuation of 𝑄𝑗. Hence 𝑄=𝑄𝑗(𝑄).

Injectivity. If 𝑄𝑗=𝑄𝑗′ then 𝑗(𝑄𝑗)=𝑗 and 𝑗(𝑄𝑗′)=𝑗′ are determined by the same valuation, and the minimal factor of 𝐿(𝑦) is unique, so 𝑗=𝑗′. ∎

Proposition 6.3.42 (Trace Formula for Residues)

Assume 𝑘 is algebraically closed. Let 𝒪︀≔𝑘[𝑥], ℬ︀≔𝒪︀―𝐾 the integral closure of 𝒪︀ in a finite separable extension 𝐾/𝑘(𝑥), 𝑃 a place of 𝑘(𝑥)/𝑘 corresponding to a maximal ideal 𝔭◃𝒪︀, and 𝑓∈𝐾. Then

∑𝑄∣𝑃Res𝑄(𝑓d𝑥)=Res𝑃(Tr𝐾/𝑘(𝑥)(𝑓)d𝑥),

with no hypothesis on char(𝑘).

Proof. Drafted by Claude Sonnet, reviewed by David Rufino.

As 𝑘 is algebraically closed every place is rational, so 𝔭=(𝑥−𝜃) for some 𝜃∈𝑘; replacing 𝑥 by 𝑥−𝜃 we may assume 𝔭=(𝑥).

By (3.31.19) the places 𝑄1,…,𝑄𝑟 of 𝐾/𝑘 over 𝑃 have ramification indices 𝑒1,…,𝑒𝑟 with ∑𝑖𝑒𝑖=𝑛≔[𝐾:𝑘(𝑥)] (residue degree 1, as 𝑘 is algebraically closed). Let 𝐾𝑃≔𝑘((𝑥)), with 𝑘(𝑥)↪︎𝐾𝑃 the expansion at 𝑃 (6.3.23).

Factoring the minimal polynomial. Let 𝑦∈ℬ︀ be a primitive element with minimal polynomial 𝑔∈𝒪︀[𝑌], monic of degree 𝑛. Factor 𝑔=𝑔1⋯𝑔𝑟 over 𝐾𝑃 as in (6.3.41), with 𝐸𝑗=𝐾𝑃[𝑌]/(𝑔𝑗), 𝜄𝑗:𝐾↪︎𝐸𝑗, and Ψ𝑗:𝐸𝑗→∼𝑘((𝜋𝑗)) the isomorphisms given there, so that Ψ𝑗(𝑥) has order 𝑑𝑗=deg𝑔𝑗. Put 𝜆𝑗≔Ψ𝑗∘𝜄𝑗:𝐾↪︎𝑘((𝜋𝑗)) and 𝐿𝜋𝑗≔𝜆𝑗. By (6.3.41), the places 𝑄𝑗 attached to the 𝑔𝑗 are exactly the places of 𝐾/𝑘 over 𝑃, with 𝑒𝑄𝑗=𝑑𝑗, so after relabelling 𝑄𝑖 is the place attached to 𝑔𝑖 and 𝑒𝑖=𝑑𝑖.

Trace additivity. By the Chinese remainder theorem applied to 𝑔=∏𝑗𝑔𝑗,

𝐾⊗𝑘(𝑥)𝐾𝑃⟶∼∏𝑖=1𝑟𝐸𝑖,𝑓⊗1↦(𝜄𝑖(𝑓))𝑖,

and composing with the isomorphisms Ψ𝑖 identifies the right side with ∏𝑖𝑘((𝜋𝑖)), with 𝑓⊗1↦(𝐿𝜋𝑖(𝑓))𝑖. The trace is unchanged by base change and additive over a finite product, so

Tr𝐾/𝑘(𝑥)(𝑓)=∑𝑖=1𝑟Tr𝑘((𝜋𝑖))/𝐾𝑃(𝐿𝜋𝑖(𝑓))(𝑓∈𝐾).

Local identities. The residue at 𝑄𝑖 is the residue symbol of (6.3.25), computed in 𝑘((𝜋𝑖)) through 𝐿𝜋𝑖, so Res𝑄𝑖(𝑓d𝑥)=Res𝜋𝑖(𝐿𝜋𝑖(𝑓)d𝑥) (Laurent residue of (6.3.24)), and Res𝑃(𝑧d𝑥)=Res𝑥(𝑧d𝑥) for 𝑧∈𝐾𝑃=𝑘((𝑥)). Applying (6.3.40) to 𝐸=𝑘((𝜋𝑖)) over 𝐹=𝐾𝑃 (with 𝑡=𝑥 and Ψ𝑖(𝑥)=𝜏𝑖) gives, for every 𝑓∈𝐾,

Res𝑄𝑖(𝑓d𝑥)=Res𝑃(Tr𝑘((𝜋𝑖))/𝐾𝑃(𝐿𝜋𝑖(𝑓))d𝑥).

Assembly. Summing over 𝑖 and using 𝑘-linearity of Res𝑃 and trace additivity,

∑𝑄∣𝑃Res𝑄(𝑓d𝑥)=∑𝑖=1𝑟Res𝑃(Tr𝑘((𝜋𝑖))/𝐾𝑃(𝐿𝜋𝑖(𝑓))d𝑥)=Res𝑃(Tr𝐾/𝑘(𝑥)(𝑓)d𝑥).

 ∎
