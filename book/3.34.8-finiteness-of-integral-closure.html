3.34.8 Finiteness of Integral Closure

Remark 3.34.58
In what follows we are interested in the integral closure of affine domains in finite extensions of the quotient field. Similar results are obtained for Dedekind Domains (3.31.11) with the restriction that the field extension is separable. Although this holds over number fields where the characteristic is zero, this will not in general be the case for varieties over finite fields, as for example 𝔽𝑝(𝑡) admits the inseparable extension 𝔽𝑝(𝑡1/𝑝). However the finiteness still holds, because it’s easy to see that the integral closure of 𝔽𝑝[𝑡] is simply 𝔽𝑝[𝑡1/𝑝]. This generalises because every purely inseparable extension is contained in an extension obtained by adjoining 𝑝-th roots of unity.

Proposition 3.34.60 (Affine Domain are Japanese Rings)

Let 𝐴 be an affine domain over 𝑘 with 𝐾=Frac(𝐴) and 𝐿/𝐾 a finite extension. Then 𝐴―𝐿 is a finite 𝐴-module and Noetherian with quotient field 𝐿.

Furthermore dim𝐴―𝐿=dim𝐴.

where 𝑧1,…,𝑧𝑑 are algebraically independent over 𝑘. We claim it is enough to show that the 𝐴′―𝐿 is a finite 𝐴′-module. For 𝐴 is finite and integral over 𝐴′. Therefore by (3.23.28) 𝐴―𝐿=𝐴′―𝐿 is finite over 𝐴′, and a-fortiori over 𝐴.

Therefore without loss of generality we may assume that 𝑥1,…,𝑥𝑛 are algebraically independent over 𝑘 and 𝐴 is integrally closed (3.23.12).

By (3.18.73) we may find a finite extension 𝐿′/𝐿 such that 𝐿′/𝐾 is normal. So by (3.23.29) we may assume without loss of generality that 𝐿/𝐾 is normal.

By (…) we may decompose this into a tower 𝑀/𝐿/𝐾 such that 𝑀/𝐿 is Galois and 𝐿/𝐾 is purely inseparable.

We first show that the integral closure of 𝐴 in 𝐿 is a finite 𝐴-module. When 𝐿=𝐾 this is trivial because 𝐴 is already integrally closed in its quotient field 𝐾. Therefore we may assume 𝐿≠𝐾 and the characteristic 𝑝 is non-zero (3.18.103). 𝐿=𝐾(𝑦1,…,𝑦𝑛) is finitely generated and by definition 𝑦𝑖𝑞∈𝐾 for some 𝑝-th power 𝑞. Therefore 𝐿 is contained in 𝐿′≔𝑘(𝑥11/𝑞,…,𝑥𝑛1/𝑞) which is again a finite purely inseparable extension of 𝐾. The elements 𝑥11/𝑞,…,𝑥𝑛1/𝑞 are again algebraically independent so that the ring 𝐴′≔𝑘[𝑥11/𝑞,…,𝑥𝑛1/𝑞] is integrally closed (3.23.12) with quotient field 𝐿′. Evidently 𝐴′ is finite and integral over 𝐴 (3.23.4) therefore (3.23.28) 𝐴―𝐿′=𝐴′―𝐿′=𝐴′ is finite over 𝐴. By (3.23.29) then 𝐴―𝐿 is finite over 𝐴 and 𝐴―𝐿 has quotient field 𝐿 (3.23.24). As 𝐴 is Noetherian then so is 𝐴―𝐿.

By transitivity we may then reduce to the case 𝐿/𝐾 is separable which was proven in (3.23.26). ∎

Corollary 3.34.61

Let 𝐴 be an affine domain of Krull dimension 1, with quotient field 𝐾 and 𝐿/𝐾 a finite extension. Then the integral closure 𝐴―𝐿 is a finite 𝐴-module and a Dedekind domain.

Further for every prime ideal 𝔭 of 𝐴 we have the ramification formula

∑𝔅∣𝔭𝑒𝔅/𝔭𝑓𝔅/𝔭=[𝐿:𝐾]

Proof. By (3.34.60) 𝐵≔𝐴―𝐿 is a finite 𝐴-module with quotient field 𝐿 and Noetherian. By (…) it is integrally closed and by (3.23.22) has Krull Dimension 1. Therefore by definition 𝐵 is also a Dedekind domain.
