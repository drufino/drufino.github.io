4.2.9 Coequaliser Sheaf

Proposition 4.2.33 (Coequaliser Presheaf)

Let 𝛼,𝛽:ℱ︀⇉𝒢︀ be morphisms of presheaves with a family of pointwise coequalisers {𝑝𝑈:𝒢︀(𝑈)→coeq(𝛼𝑈,𝛽𝑈)}𝑈⊂𝑋. Then we may define

coeq(𝛼,𝛽)(𝑈)≔coeq(𝛼𝑈,𝛽𝑈)

and this may be made into a presheaf uniquely such that 𝑝 is a morphism of presheaves. Furthermore (𝑝,coeq(𝛼,𝛽)) is a coequaliser in the category of presheaves.

In particular if 𝒞︀ has all coequalisers then so does 𝐏𝐒𝐡(𝑋;𝒞︀).

Proof. Drafted by Claude Sonnet 5, reviewed by David Rufino.

We require to show that for all open subsets 𝑉⊂𝑈 there is a unique restriction morphism 𝜌𝑈𝑉:coeq(𝛼𝑈,𝛽𝑈)→coeq(𝛼𝑉,𝛽𝑉) ensuring the following diagram commutes

We have that

𝑝𝑉∘𝜌𝑈𝑉∘𝛼𝑈=𝑝𝑉∘𝛼𝑉∘𝜌𝑈𝑉=𝑝𝑉∘𝛽𝑉∘𝜌𝑈𝑉=𝑝𝑉∘𝜌𝑈𝑉∘𝛽𝑈

whence by the universal property applied to coeq(𝛼𝑈,𝛽𝑈) there exists a unique factorisation of 𝑝𝑉∘𝜌𝑈𝑉 through 𝑝𝑈. The uniqueness then shows that this satisfies the conditions to be a presheaf (𝜌𝑈𝑈=1 and 𝜌𝑉𝑊∘𝜌𝑈𝑉=𝜌𝑈𝑊 both follow from uniqueness of the factorisation of 𝑝𝑊∘𝜌𝑈𝑊 through 𝑝𝑈), and 𝑝 is a morphism of presheaves.

We now show that 𝑝 is a coequaliser of (𝛼,𝛽) in the category of presheaves. Evidently 𝑝∘𝛼=𝑝∘𝛽, since this holds pointwise by hypothesis. Suppose that 𝑝′:𝒢︀→ℋ︀ is a morphism of presheaves with 𝑝′∘𝛼=𝑝′∘𝛽, then 𝑝𝑈′∘𝛼𝑈=𝑝𝑈′∘𝛽𝑈 whence there is a unique factorisation ℎ𝑈 such that ℎ𝑈∘𝑝𝑈=𝑝𝑈′.

The large square and left-hand square commute, which we may use to show that

ℎ𝑉∘𝜌𝑈𝑉∘𝑝𝑈=ℎ𝑉∘𝑝𝑉∘𝜌𝑈𝑉=𝑝𝑉′∘𝜌𝑈𝑉=𝜌𝑈𝑉∘𝑝𝑈′=𝜌𝑈𝑉∘ℎ𝑈∘𝑝𝑈

As 𝑝𝑈 is a coequaliser it is an epimorphism (2.6.135), whence ℎ𝑉∘𝜌𝑈𝑉=𝜌𝑈𝑉∘ℎ𝑈, which shows that the right-hand square commutes. This means precisely that ℎ is a morphism of presheaves, and by construction ℎ∘𝑝=𝑝′.

Finally suppose ℎ′ is another morphism of presheaves with ℎ′∘𝑝=𝑝′. Then for every 𝑈, ℎ𝑈′∘𝑝𝑈=𝑝𝑈′=ℎ𝑈∘𝑝𝑈, so by the uniqueness of the pointwise factorisation ℎ𝑈′=ℎ𝑈, that is ℎ′=ℎ.

For the final statement, if 𝒞︀ has all coequalisers then the family of pointwise coequalisers {𝑝𝑈:𝒢︀(𝑈)→coeq(𝛼𝑈,𝛽𝑈)}𝑈⊂𝑋 exists for every pair 𝛼,𝛽:ℱ︀⇉𝒢︀ of morphisms of presheaves, so the above applies to every such pair and 𝐏𝐒𝐡(𝑋;𝒞︀) has all coequalisers. ∎

Proposition 4.2.34

Let 𝛼,𝛽:ℱ︀⇉𝒢︀ be morphisms of sheaves and 𝑝:𝒢︀→coeqpsh(𝛼,𝛽) be a coequaliser in 𝐏𝐒𝐡(𝑋;𝒞︀). Then

𝜂∘𝑝:𝒢︀→coeq(𝛼,𝛽)≔(coeqpsh(𝛼,𝛽))+

is a coequaliser in 𝐒𝐡(𝑋;𝒞︀).

In particular if 𝒞︀ has all coequalisers then so does 𝐒𝐡(𝑋;𝒞︀).

Proof. Drafted by Claude Sonnet 5, reviewed by David Rufino.

By (4.2.13) and (4.2.14), (−)+ is left adjoint to the inclusion 𝐒𝐡(𝑋;𝒞︀)↪︎𝐏𝐒𝐡(𝑋;𝒞︀) with unit 𝜂, and this inclusion is (trivially) fully faithful, since a morphism of sheaves is by definition just a morphism of the underlying presheaves. Hence this is an instance of (2.6.143), applied to this reflective subcategory with the coequaliser 𝑝 of (𝛼,𝛽) in 𝐏𝐒𝐡(𝑋;𝒞︀).

For the final statement, if 𝒞︀ has all coequalisers then by (4.2.33) 𝐏𝐒𝐡(𝑋;𝒞︀) has all coequalisers, so the hypotheses of this proposition are satisfied for every pair (𝛼,𝛽) of morphisms of sheaves, and 𝐒𝐡(𝑋;𝒞︀) has all coequalisers. ∎
