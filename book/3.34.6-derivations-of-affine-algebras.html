3.34.6 Derivations of Affine Algebras

The notion of derivations is a useful as a coordinate-free construction of the “tangent space” for both differentiable manifolds and algebraic varieties. We review the theory of derivations here primarily focusing on fields and f.g. 𝑘-algebras.

Proposition 3.34.40 (Derivations of Polynomial Algebra)

Let 𝐴=𝑘[𝑋1,…,𝑋𝑛] be a polynomial algebra and 𝑀 an (𝐴,𝐵)-bimodule. Then we have an (𝐴,𝐵)-bimodule isomorphism

Der𝑘(𝐴,𝑀)≅𝑀𝑛𝐷→(𝐷(𝑋1),…,𝐷(𝑋𝑛))∑𝑖=1𝑛𝜕𝜕𝑋𝑖⋅𝑣𝑖←𝑣

When 𝑀 has a 𝐵-basis {𝑚1,…,𝑚𝑟} then Der𝑘(𝐴,𝑀) also has a 𝐵-basis

{𝜕𝜕𝑋𝑖⋅𝑚𝑗}𝑖=1…𝑛,𝑗=1…𝑟

In particular dim𝐾Der𝑘(𝐴,𝐾)=𝑛.

Proof. The first map is clearly well-defined and by (…) injective. The inverse map is well-defined because we have shown 𝜕𝜕𝑋𝑖∈Der𝑘(𝐴,𝐴). The chain rule (3.25.8) shows that the maps are mutually inverse (the other direction following simply from orthogonality of the partial derivatives).

The final statement is straightforward. ∎

We may generalise this as follows, so that we may interpret derivations as tangent vectors

Proposition 3.34.41 (Derivations = Tangent Vectors)

Let 𝐴=𝑘[𝑋1,…,𝑋𝑛]/𝔞 be a f.g. 𝑘-algebra and 𝑀 an 𝐴-module. Then we have an 𝐴-module isomorphism

Der𝑘(𝐴,𝑀)≅{𝑣∈𝑀𝑛|∑𝑖=1𝑛𝜕𝐹𝜕𝑋𝑖―𝑣𝑖=0∀𝐹∈𝔞}⊂𝑀𝑛𝐷⟶(𝐷(𝑋―1),…,𝐷(𝑋―𝑛))∑𝑖=1𝑛𝜕𝜕𝑋𝑖―⋅𝑣𝑖⟵𝑣

If 𝔞=⟨𝐹1,…,𝐹𝑚⟩ then this is equal to the kernel of the 𝐴-module homomorphism

(𝜕𝐹1𝜕𝑋1―…𝜕𝐹1𝜕𝑋𝑛―⋮⋱⋮𝜕𝐹𝑚𝜕𝑋1―…𝜕𝐹𝑚𝜕𝑋𝑛―):𝑀𝑛→𝑀𝑚

When 𝑀 is an (𝐴,𝐵)-bimodule then this is also an (𝐴,𝐵)-bimodule isomorphism.

Proof. According to (3.25.8) we have the following relationship for all 𝐹∈𝑘[𝑋1,…,𝑋𝑛]

𝐷(𝐹―)=𝐷(𝐹(𝑋―1,…,𝑋―𝑛))=∑𝑖=1𝑛𝜕𝐹𝜕𝑋𝑖(𝑋―𝑖)𝐷(𝑋―𝑖)=∑𝑖=1𝑛𝜕𝐹𝜕𝑋𝑖―𝐷(𝑋―𝑖)

When 𝐹∈𝔞 we have 𝐹―=0⟹𝐷(𝐹―)=0, whence the first map is well-defined. Similarly for 𝑣∈RHS we see by definition that

∑𝑖=1𝑛𝜕𝜕𝑋𝑖⋅𝑣𝑖

is zero on 𝔞, so determines a well-defined derivation on 𝐴. In the same way we see that the maps are mutually inverse.

Suppose 𝑣 is in the kernel of the given matrix. For any 𝐺∈𝔞 we have by hypothesis 𝐺=∑𝑗=1𝑚𝐹𝑗𝐻𝑗 for some 𝐻𝑗∈𝑘[𝑋1,…,𝑋𝑛]. Then

∑𝑖=1𝑛𝜕𝐺𝜕𝑋𝑖―𝑣𝑖=∑𝑖=1𝑛∑𝑗=1𝑚(𝐹𝑗―𝜕𝐻𝑗𝜕𝑋𝑖―+𝐻𝑗―𝜕𝐹𝑗𝜕𝑋𝑖―)𝑣𝑖=∑𝑗=1𝑚∑𝑖=1𝑛𝜕𝐹𝑗𝜕𝑋𝑖―𝑣𝑖=0

where we have used the fact that 𝐹𝑗―=0. As 𝐺 was arbitrary this shows 𝑣 is in the right-hand side of the isomorphism, and the reverse inclusion is immediate as 𝐹𝑗∈𝔞. ∎

Proposition 3.34.42 (Lifting for separable algebraic extensions)

Let 𝐿/𝐾 be a separable algebraic (resp. separably generated) extension (over 𝑘) and 𝑉 an 𝐿-module. Then there is an isomorphism (resp. epimorphism) of 𝐿-modules

Der𝑘(𝐿,𝑉)→∼Der𝑘(𝐾,𝑉)𝐷→𝐷|𝐾

Proof. Consider 𝐿[𝑉] the 𝑘-algebra with ideal 𝑁≔0×𝑉 such that 𝑁2=0 and 𝐿[𝑉]/𝑁≅𝐿. Furthermore a derivation 𝐷:𝐾→𝑉 corresponds to a unique 𝑘-algebra homomorphism 𝜙𝐷:𝐾→𝐾[𝑉]↪︎𝐿[𝑉] by (3.25.5) so we have the following commutative diagram

In the algebraic case by (3.25.16) there is a unique homomorphism 𝜙𝐷̂:𝐿→𝐿[𝑉] completing the diagram, and in particular by (3.25.5) there is a derivation 𝐷̃:𝐿→𝑉 extending 𝐷. Uniqueness follows from that of 𝜙𝐷̂.

Corollary 3.34.43

Let 𝐾/𝑘 be a separably generated extension, 𝐴 a 𝐾-algebra and 𝑀 an 𝐴-module then

Der𝑘(𝐴,𝑀)=Der𝐾(𝐴,𝑀)

Proof. Recall (3.25.1) that a derivation 𝐷 is 𝐾-linear iff it vanishes on 𝐾. So we may reduce to the case 𝐴=𝐾 by considering the restriction 𝐷|𝐾.

Therefore it’s sufficient to show that Der𝑘(𝐾,𝑀)=Der𝐾(𝐾,𝑀)=0=Der𝑘(𝑘,𝑀), but this follows immediately from (3.34.42). ∎

Proposition 3.34.44 (Transcendence Degree Controls Derivations into Any Module)

Suppose 𝐾/𝑘 is separably generated (e.g. if 𝑘 is perfect and 𝐾 finitely generated), with transcendence basis 𝜂1,…,𝜂𝑛, 𝑛=trdeg𝑘(𝐾), and let 𝑀 be a 𝐾-module. Then

Der𝑘(𝐾,𝑀)≅𝑀𝑛,𝐷↦(𝐷(𝜂1),…,𝐷(𝜂𝑛))

as 𝐾-modules. In particular, taking 𝑀≔𝐾, trdeg𝑘(𝐾)=dim𝐾Der𝑘(𝐾).

Proof. By hypothesis 𝐾/𝑘(𝜂1,…,𝜂𝑛) is algebraic and separable. Therefore we have

Der𝑘(𝐾,𝑀)≅(3.34.42)Der𝑘(𝑘(𝜂1,…,𝜂𝑛),𝑀)≅(3.25.10)Der𝑘(𝑘[𝜂1,…,𝜂𝑛],𝑀)≅(3.34.40)𝑀𝑛

the last isomorphism sending 𝐷↦(𝐷(𝜂1),…,𝐷(𝜂𝑛)), matching the same map once restricted back along the two previous isomorphisms (both restriction maps, 𝐷↦𝐷|𝑘(𝜂1,…,𝜂𝑛) and 𝐷↦𝐷|𝑘[𝜂1,…,𝜂𝑛], fix the value at each 𝜂𝑖). Taking 𝑀≔𝐾 gives dim𝐾Der𝑘(𝐾,𝐾)=𝑛=trdeg𝑘(𝐾). ∎

Proposition 3.34.45 (Derivations Extend Uniquely Along a Field Extension)
Let 𝐾/𝑘 be a field extension, 𝑘′/𝑘 any field extension, and 𝐷∈Der𝑘(𝐾,𝐾). There is a unique 𝐷̃∈Der𝑘′(𝐾⊗𝑘𝑘′,𝐾⊗𝑘𝑘′) with 𝐷̃(𝑥⊗1)=𝐷(𝑥)⊗1 for every 𝑥∈𝐾; explicitly, 𝐷̃(𝑥⊗𝑐)=𝐷(𝑥)⊗𝑐.

Proof. Drafted by Claude Sonnet, reviewed by David Rufino.

Existence. Define 𝐷̃(𝑥⊗𝑐)≔𝐷(𝑥)⊗𝑐, extended 𝑘′-linearly to 𝐾⊗𝑘𝑘′ (well-defined as 𝐷 is 𝑘-linear). It satisfies the Leibniz rule on simple tensors, 𝐷̃((𝑥⊗𝑐)(𝑦⊗𝑐′))=𝐷(𝑥𝑦)⊗𝑐𝑐′=(𝐷(𝑥)𝑦+𝑥𝐷(𝑦))⊗𝑐𝑐′=𝐷̃(𝑥⊗𝑐)⋅(𝑦⊗𝑐′)+(𝑥⊗𝑐)⋅𝐷̃(𝑦⊗𝑐′), hence on all of 𝐾⊗𝑘𝑘′ by bilinearity: 𝐷̃∈Der𝑘′(𝐾⊗𝑘𝑘′,𝐾⊗𝑘𝑘′).

Uniqueness. 𝐾⊗𝑘𝑘′ is 𝑘′-spanned by 𝐾⊗1 (extension of scalars along a 𝑘-basis of 𝐾), and any extension of 𝐷 is pinned down there (𝑥⊗1↦𝐷(𝑥)⊗1 by definition of “extends”), so agrees with 𝐷̃ on a 𝑘′-spanning set, hence everywhere by 𝑘′-linearity. ∎

Lemma 3.34.46

Let 𝐴 be a 𝑘-algebra and 𝔪 an ideal. Then 𝔪/𝔪2 is a 𝑘(𝔪)-module with action given by

(𝑎+𝔪)⋅(𝑏+𝔪2)=(𝑎𝑏+𝔪2)

for all 𝑎∈𝐴 and 𝑏∈𝔪.

Proof. Suppose 𝑎−𝑎′∈𝔪 and 𝑏−𝑏′∈𝔪2 then

𝑎𝑏−𝑎′𝑏′=(𝑎−𝑎′)(𝑏−𝑏′)+𝑎′(𝑏−𝑏′)+𝑏′(𝑎−𝑎′)∈𝔪2

this shows that the action is well-defined. The properties of a module are straightforward. ∎

Lemma 3.34.47

Let 𝐴 be a 𝑘-algebra and 𝔪 a maximal ideal. Then there is an isomorphism of 𝐴-algebras

Further there is an isomorphism of 𝐴-modules

Der𝑘(𝐴/𝔪2,𝑘(𝔪/𝔪2))→Der𝑘(𝐴,𝑘(𝔪))𝐷→𝑥→𝜓−1(𝐷(𝑥―))

Proof. The first statement follows from (3.3.58) with 𝔞=𝔪2 and 𝔟=𝔪.

Define 𝐷̂(𝑥)≔𝜓−1(𝐷(𝑥―)). By hypothesis 𝐷(𝑥―𝑦―)=𝜋2(𝑥)𝐷(𝑦―)+𝜋2(𝑦)𝐷(𝑥―). Therefore

𝐷̂(𝑥𝑦)=𝜋1(𝑥)𝜓−1(𝐷(𝑦―))+𝜋1(𝑦)𝜓−1(𝐷(𝑥―))=𝜋1(𝑥)𝐷̂(𝑦―)+𝜋1(𝑦)𝐷̂(𝑥―)

Lemma 3.34.48

Let 𝐴 be a 𝑘-algebra and 𝔪 an ideal. Then there is an isomorphism of 𝐴-modules

Hom𝑘(𝔪)(𝔪/𝔪2,𝑘(𝔪))→Hom𝑘(𝔪/𝔪2)((𝔪/𝔪2)/(𝔪2/𝔪2),𝑘(𝔪/𝔪2))𝜃→𝜓∘𝜃∘𝑖−1

where 𝑖:𝔪/𝔪2→(𝔪/𝔪2)/(𝔪2/𝔪2) is a ring isomorphism such that

𝑖(𝜋1(𝑎)⋅𝑥)=𝜋2(𝑎)⋅𝑖(𝑥)

Proof. Then for 𝑥∈(𝔪/𝔪2)/(𝔪2/𝔪2), 𝑎∈𝐴 and 𝜃∈Hom𝑘(𝔪)(𝔪/𝔪2,𝑘(𝔪))

(𝜓∘𝜃∘𝑖−1)(𝜋2(𝑎)⋅𝑥)=(𝜓∘𝜃)(𝜋1(𝑎)⋅𝑖−1(𝑥))=𝜓(𝜋1(𝑎)𝜃(𝑖−1(𝑥)))=𝜋2(𝑎)(𝜓∘𝜃∘𝑖−1)(𝑥)

so that the map is well-defined. Similarly if 𝜃′∈Hom𝑘(𝔪/𝔪2)((𝔪/𝔪2)/(𝔪2/𝔪2),𝑘(𝔪/𝔪2))

(𝜓−1∘𝜃′∘𝑖)(𝜋1(𝑎)⋅𝑥)=(𝜓−1∘𝜃′)(𝜋2(𝑎)⋅𝑖(𝑥))=𝜓−1(𝜋2(𝑎)𝜃′(𝑖(𝑥)))=𝜋1(𝑎)(𝜓−1∘𝜃′∘𝑖)(𝑥)

so the inverse map is well-defined. ∎

Proposition 3.34.49 (Coefficient Field mod 𝔪2)
Let 𝐴 be a 𝑘-algebra and 𝔪◃𝐴 a maximal ideal with 𝔪2=0 and residue field 𝑘(𝔪)≔𝐴/𝔪. If 𝑘(𝔪)/𝑘 is separably generated (e.g. if 𝑘 is perfect and 𝑘(𝔪)/𝑘 is finitely generated), then the quotient map 𝜋:𝐴→𝑘(𝔪) admits a 𝑘-algebra section 𝑗:𝑘(𝔪)→𝐴, that is there is a subfield 𝑘⊆𝑘̂⊆𝐴 such that 𝜋|𝑘̂:𝑘̂→𝑘(𝔪) is an isomorphism. In particular 𝐴=𝑘̂⊕𝔪 as 𝑘-vector spaces.

Proof. Drafted by Claude Sonnet, reviewed by David Rufino.

As 𝔪2=0, 𝔪 is the only prime of 𝐴, so 𝐴 is local, and 𝐴/𝔪2=𝐴; so (3.25.18) (with 𝑛=1) gives a 𝑘-algebra homomorphism 𝑗≔𝑠1:𝑘(𝔪)→𝐴 with 𝜋∘𝑗=id𝑘(𝔪).

Set 𝑘̂≔𝑗(𝑘(𝔪))⊆𝐴. As 𝑗 is a ring homomorphism from a field, it is injective (3.3.61), so 𝑗:𝑘(𝔪)→𝑘̂ is an isomorphism, and 𝜋∘𝑗=id shows 𝜋|𝑘̂ is its inverse. Finally 𝑗 is a 𝑘-linear section of the 𝑘-linear surjection 𝜋:𝐴→𝑘(𝔪) with kernel 𝔪, so 𝐴=Im(𝑗)⊕ker(𝜋)=𝑘̂⊕𝔪 as 𝑘-vector spaces. ∎

Proposition 3.34.50 (Tangent space is dual to Cotangent space)

Let 𝐴 be a 𝑘-algebra and 𝔪◃𝐴 a maximal ideal with residue field 𝑘(𝔪)≔𝐴/𝔪. There is a homomorphism of 𝑘(𝔪)-modules

Der𝑘(𝐴,𝑘(𝔪))⟶Hom𝑘(𝔪)(𝔪/𝔪2,𝑘(𝔪))𝐷⟶𝜃𝐷:𝑥―→𝐷(𝑥)

When 𝑘(𝔪)/𝑘 is separably generated then this is an isomorphism. In particular this holds when 𝑘 is perfect and 𝑘(𝔪)/𝑘 is finitely generated.

Proof. The map 𝜃𝐷 is well-defined because 𝐷 annihilates 𝔪2 by the product rule. It remains to show the map is bijective when 𝑘(𝔪)/𝑘 is separably generated.

We first claim we can reduce to the case 𝔪2=0. For consider 𝐴′≔𝐴/𝔪2 and 𝔪′=𝔪/𝔪2 and the commutative diagram of 𝐴-module homomorphisms

The horizontal maps have already been defined and the vertical maps are defined in (3.34.47) and (3.34.48). The diagram evidentally commutes, so therefore it is sufficient to show that the top map is an isomorphism.

We revert to the original notation, but with the additional assumption that 𝔪2=0. By (3.34.49) there is a subfield 𝑘⊆𝑘̂⊆𝐴 mapping isomorphically to 𝑘(𝔪) under the canonical projection 𝜋:𝐴→𝐴/𝔪=𝑘(𝔪), giving a direct sum of 𝑘-vector spaces

𝐴=𝑘̂⊕𝔪

Given 𝜃∈Hom𝑘(𝔪)(𝔪,𝑘(𝔪)) define

𝐷𝜃(𝜆+𝑥)≔𝜃(𝑥)𝜆∈𝑘̂,𝑥∈𝔪

It satisfies the product rule for

𝐷𝜃((𝜆+𝑥)(𝜆′+𝑥′))=𝜃(𝜆′𝑥+𝜆𝑥′)=𝜋(𝜆′)𝜃(𝑥)+𝜋(𝜆)𝜃(𝑥′)=𝜋(𝜆′+𝑥′)𝜃(𝑥)+𝜋(𝜆+𝑥)𝜃(𝑥′)=(𝜆′+𝑥′)⋅𝐷𝜃(𝜆+𝑥)+(𝜆+𝑥)⋅𝐷𝜃(𝜆′+𝑥′)

Therefore the given map is surjective.

As 𝑘(𝔪)/𝑘 is separably generated then by (3.34.43) any derivation 𝐷 is 𝑘̂-linear. Therefore

𝐷(𝜆+𝑥)=𝐷|𝔪(𝑥)𝜆∈𝑘̂,𝑥∈𝔪

which shows that the given map is injective. ∎

Remark 3.34.51

The proof is substantially simpler when 𝑘(𝔪)=𝑘, for example when 𝑘 is algebraically closed.

The argument follows the suggestion of [Har13] (Ex 8.1), but using the simpler result [Mat70] (Prop 28.I) to argue more directly.
