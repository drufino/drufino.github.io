6.2.1 Topological Properties

Definition 6.2.5 (Integral Variety)
We say a variety 𝑋 is integral (or irreducible) if it is irreducible as a topological space.
Proposition 6.2.6 (Criteria for Integrality)

Let 𝑋 be a variety. Then the following are equivalent

  1. 𝑋 is integral
  2. 𝒪︀𝑋(𝑈) is integral for every open subset 𝑈⊂𝑋
Proposition 6.2.7 (Varieties are Noetherian and Hereditarily Quasi-Compact)
Let 𝑋 be a variety. Then 𝑋 is Noetherian as a topological space and every open subset is quasi-compact (and Noetherian).
Proof. (4.1.78) and (4.1.114). ∎

Suppose (𝑋,𝒪︀𝑋) is a space with functions such that 𝑋=⋃𝑖=1𝑛𝑈𝑖 and (𝑈𝑖,𝒪︀𝑋|𝑈𝑖) is a variety. Then so is (𝑋,𝒪︀𝑋).
Proof. Each 𝑈𝑖 is a finite union of affine varieties and therefore so is 𝑋. ∎
Proposition 6.2.10
Let 𝑋 be a variety. The (affine) principal open subsets form a base for the topology on 𝑋.

Definition 6.2.13 (Separated Variety)

Let 𝑋 be a variety. Then we say 𝑋 is separated if for every affine variety 𝑍 and pair of morphisms 𝜙1,𝜙2:𝑍⇉𝑋 the equaliser

{𝑧∈𝑍|𝜙1(𝑧)=𝜙2(𝑧)}

is closed in 𝑍.

By (6.1.43) every affine variety is separated.

Proposition 6.2.14
Let 𝑋 be a separated variety and 𝑍 a variety. Then the equaliser of any two maps 𝜙1,𝜙2:𝑍⇉𝑋 is closed.
Proof. By definition 𝑍=⋃𝑖=1𝑛𝑈𝑖 where 𝑈𝑖 is affine. By assumption the equaliser of 𝜙1|𝑈𝑖,𝜙2|𝑈𝑖:𝑈𝑖⇉𝑋 is closed. As a finite union of closed sets is closed, we are done. ∎
