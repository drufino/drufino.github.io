3.19.11 Perfect Fields

Proposition 3.19.100 (Perfect Closure)

Let 𝑘 be a field with characteristic exponent 𝑝 contained in an algebraically closed field Ω (e.g. 𝑘―). Define

𝑘𝑝−𝑛≔{𝑥∈Ω|𝑥𝑝𝑛∈𝑘}

Then 𝑘𝑝−𝑛 is a subfield of Ω/𝑘, which is purely inseparable of height ≤𝑛. Furthermore it is a splitting field for the family of polynomials {𝑋𝑝𝑟−𝑎|𝑎∈𝑘,𝑟≤𝑛} which characterizes it up to isomorphism. Furthermore

𝑘𝑝−∞≔{𝑥∈Ω|𝑥𝑝𝑛∈𝑘some𝑛≥1}=⋃𝑛≥1𝑘𝑝−𝑛

is the smallest perfect subfield of Ω containing 𝑘 and is also a splitting field for the family of polynomials {𝑋𝑝𝑛−𝑎|𝑎∈𝑘,𝑛∈ℕ}.

Proof. Suppose 𝛼,𝛽∈𝑘𝑝−𝑛 then applying the Frobenius homomorphism (…) we see (𝛼+𝛽)𝑝𝑛=𝛼𝑝𝑛+𝛽𝑝𝑛 and so 𝑘𝑝−𝑛 is an additive subgroup. Furthermore (𝛼𝛽)𝑝𝑛=𝛼𝑝𝑛𝛽𝑝𝑛 whence it is a multiplicative subgroup as this demonstrates (𝛼−1)𝑝𝑛=(𝛼𝑝𝑛)−1∈𝑘. Therefore it is a subfield which is by definition purely inseparable of height ≤𝑛.

By definition 𝑘𝑝−𝑛 is precisely the set of roots of the given polynomials and it is therefore the corresponding splitting field by (…). This also shows that 𝑛≤𝑚⟹𝑘𝑝−𝑛⊆𝑘𝑝−𝑚 which shows that the union 𝑘𝑝−∞ is a subfield of Ω.

To demonstrate it’s perfect we need to show every element 𝑥∈𝑘𝑝−𝑛 has a 𝑝-th root. By definition 𝑥𝑝𝑛∈𝑘 and there exists a root 𝛼 to the polynomial 𝑋𝑝𝑛+1−𝑥𝑝𝑛 in 𝑘𝑝−(𝑛+1). Therefore (𝛼𝑝−𝑥)𝑝𝑛=0 whence 𝛼𝑝=𝑥 as required.

Let 𝑘′ be another perfect subfield containing 𝑘. We claim by induction that 𝑘𝑝−𝑛⊆𝑘′. For given 𝛼∈𝑘𝑝−(𝑛+1) by definition 𝛼𝑝∈𝑘𝑝−𝑛 whence 𝛼𝑝∈𝑘′ by the inductive hypothesis, and by assumption 𝛼∈𝑘′. Therefore 𝑘𝑝−∞ is the smallest perfect subfield. Furthermore by definition it is the set of roots of the given family of polynomials and therefore the corresponding splitting field. ∎

Proposition 3.19.101

Let 𝑘′/𝑘 be a purely inseparable extension of height ≤𝑛, then there exists an embedding

𝑘′/𝑘→𝑘𝑝−𝑛/𝑘

If 𝑘′/𝑘 is purely inseparable and 𝐾/𝑘 is perfect then there also exists an embedding

𝑘′/𝑘→𝐾/𝑘

Proof. For the first part let 𝛼∈𝑘′ then by (3.19.87) the minimal polynomial of 𝛼 is 𝑔(𝑋)=𝑋𝑝𝑒−𝛼𝑝𝑒 for 𝑒≤𝑛 which splits completely in 𝑘𝑝−𝑛 by (3.19.100). Then the embedding exists by (3.19.50).

Suppose 𝐾/𝑘 is perfect then we claim that 𝑋𝑝𝑛−𝑎 splits completely for every 𝑎∈𝐾. By induction there is 𝑏∈𝐾 such that 𝑏𝑝𝑛=𝑎. Then 𝑋𝑝𝑛−𝑎=(𝑋−𝑏)𝑝𝑛 splits completely. In particular for every 𝛼∈𝑘′ the minimal polynomial splits completely in 𝐾/𝑘 and the embedding follows from (3.19.50). ∎

Recall a perfect field 𝑘 satisfies 𝑘𝑝=𝑘 where 𝑝 is the characteristic exponent. We show that in this case there are no inseparable algebraic extensions. First we show that all finite fields are perfect.

Proposition 3.19.102 (Finite fields are perfect)
Any finite field is perfect.
Proof. The Frobenius homomorphism is injective and therefore surjective by counting. ∎
Proposition 3.19.103 (Perfect field criteria)

Let 𝑘 be a field with characteristic exponent 𝑝. Then the following are equivalent

  1. Every irreducible polynomial in 𝑘[𝑋] is separable
  2. Every algebraic extension 𝐾/𝑘 is separable
  3. 𝑘―/𝑘 is separable
  4. 𝑘𝑝=𝑘

In particular every field of characteristic zero is perfect, and an algebraic extension of a perfect field is separable.

Proof. We prove each in turn

𝑎)⟹𝑏) Minimal polynomials are irreducible by (3.19.36) and therefore are separable by hypothesis.

𝑏)⟺𝑐) One direction is automatic as 𝑘― is algebraic. On the other hand every algebraic extension is isomorphic to a subfield of 𝑘―, so the implication follows from (3.19.79).

𝑏)⟹𝑑) We need only show 𝑘𝑝=𝑘 in the case 𝑝>1. If there exists 𝑎∈𝑘∖𝑘𝑝 then by (3.19.86) the polynomial 𝑓(𝑋)≔𝑋𝑝−𝑎 is irreducible. Then it’s clear that the field extension 𝐾≔𝑘[𝑋]/(𝑋𝑝−𝑎) is not separable. For the minimal polynomial of 𝑋― is 𝑓 which by (3.19.91) is not separable. This contradicts the assumption that 𝐾/𝑘 is separable.

𝑑)⟹𝑎) Suppose that 𝑓 is irreducible but not separable. Then by (3.19.91) char(𝑘)=𝑝>1 and 𝑓∈𝑘[𝑋𝑝]. By assumption all the coefficients are 𝑝-th powers and therefore 𝑓=ℎ𝑝 by (3.19.6) for some ℎ∈𝑘[𝑋]. This contradicts irreducibility of 𝑓, and so 𝑓 must be separable. ∎
