4.2.8 Kernel Pair Sheaf

Corollary 4.2.27 (Kernel Pair Presheaf may be constructed pointwise)

Let 𝜇:ℋ︀→𝒢︀ be a morphism of presheaves (resp. sheaves), and suppose for every 𝑈⊂𝑋 there is a kernel pair (𝑍𝑈,𝑝1,𝑈,𝑝2,𝑈) of 𝜇𝑈.

Then we may define (ℋ︀×𝒢︀ℋ︀)(𝑈)≔𝑍𝑈 and this may be made into a presheaf (resp. sheaf) uniquely such that 𝑝1,𝑝2 are morphisms of presheaves (resp. sheaves). Furthermore (ℋ︀×𝒢︀ℋ︀,𝑝1,𝑝2) is a kernel pair of 𝜇 in the category of presheaves (resp. sheaves).

When 𝒞︀ is algebraic (2.6.184) this may be constructed in the obvious way

(ℋ︀×𝒢︀ℋ︀)(𝑈)≔{(𝜎1,𝜎2)∈ℋ︀(𝑈)×ℋ︀(𝑈)|𝜇𝑈(𝜎1)=𝜇𝑈(𝜎2)}

Proof. Drafted by Claude Sonnet 5, reviewed by David Rufino.

Let 𝐹:𝚲→𝐏𝐒𝐡(𝑋;𝒞︀) (resp. 𝐒𝐡(𝑋;𝒞︀)) be the diagram 𝐹(𝑎)≔ℋ︀, 𝐹(𝑏)≔ℋ︀, 𝐹(𝑐)≔𝒢︀, sending both morphisms 𝑎→𝑐, 𝑏→𝑐 to 𝜇 (2.6.144). Then 𝐹(−)(𝑈):𝚲→𝒞︀ is exactly the cospan 𝜇𝑈:ℋ︀(𝑈)→𝒢︀(𝑈)←ℋ︀(𝑈):𝜇𝑈, whose limit is the pullback (𝑍𝑈,𝑝1,𝑈,𝑝2,𝑈) of (𝜇𝑈,𝜇𝑈), that is a kernel pair of 𝜇𝑈, which exists by hypothesis for every 𝑈. By (4.2.23), the assignment 𝑈↦𝑍𝑈 can therefore be made into a presheaf (resp. sheaf) uniquely such that the projections 𝑝1,𝑝2 are morphisms of presheaves (resp. sheaves), and (ℋ︀×𝒢︀ℋ︀,𝑝1,𝑝2)≔(lim𝚲𝐹,𝑝1,𝑝2) is a limit for 𝐹 in the category of presheaves (resp. sheaves), that is a pullback of (𝜇,𝜇), i.e. a kernel pair of 𝜇, there, as required.

When 𝒞︀ is algebraic, this is the algebraic case of (4.2.23) specialised to 𝚲: writing (𝜎𝑎,𝜎𝑏,𝜎𝑐) for the coordinates, the compatibility condition 𝜇𝑈(𝜎𝑎)=𝜎𝑐=𝜇𝑈(𝜎𝑏) pins 𝜎𝑐 down in terms of 𝜎𝑎,𝜎𝑏, so under the projection (𝜎𝑎,𝜎𝑏,𝜎𝑐)↦(𝜎𝑎,𝜎𝑏) this recovers exactly {(𝜎1,𝜎2)∈ℋ︀(𝑈)×ℋ︀(𝑈)|𝜇𝑈(𝜎1)=𝜇𝑈(𝜎2)} noted above. ∎

Proposition 4.2.28 (Monomorphisms of Presheaves and Sheaves)

Let 𝜙:ℱ︀→𝒢︀ be a morphism of presheaves (resp. sheaves). Then the following are equivalent

  1. 𝜙 is a monomorphism
  2. 𝜙𝑈 is monic in 𝒞︀ for all 𝑈⊂𝑋

If 𝒞︀ is algebraic (2.6.184), then a), b) are further equivalent to

  1. 𝜙𝑈 is injective for all 𝑈⊂𝑋

If moreover ℱ︀,𝒢︀ are sheaves, a)–c) are equivalent to

  1. 𝜙𝑥 is injective for all 𝑥∈𝑋

Proof. Drafted by Claude Sonnet 5, reviewed by David Rufino.

𝑏)⟹𝑎): Suppose 𝛼,𝛽:ℋ︀→ℱ︀ satisfy 𝜙𝛼=𝜙𝛽. Composition of morphisms of presheaves is computed pointwise, so 𝜙𝑈𝛼𝑈=𝜙𝑈𝛽𝑈 for every 𝑈; as 𝜙𝑈 is monic, 𝛼𝑈=𝛽𝑈 for every 𝑈, that is 𝛼=𝛽.

𝑎)⟹𝑏): Since 𝒞︀ has products and equalisers, by (2.6.145) 𝜙𝑈 has a kernel pair for every 𝑈, so by (4.2.27) these assemble into a kernel pair (ℱ︀×𝒢︀ℱ︀,𝑝1,𝑝2) of 𝜙 in the category of presheaves (resp. sheaves), with 𝑈-components (ℱ︀(𝑈)×𝒢︀(𝑈)ℱ︀(𝑈),𝑝1,𝑈,𝑝2,𝑈) a kernel pair of 𝜙𝑈. As 𝜙 is monic, (2.6.147) gives 𝑝1=𝑝2; since morphisms of presheaves are equal iff equal pointwise, 𝑝1,𝑈=𝑝2,𝑈 for every 𝑈, so by (2.6.147) again (applied in 𝒞︀), 𝜙𝑈 is monic for every 𝑈.

𝑏)⟺𝑐) (when 𝒞︀ is algebraic): immediate from (2.6.194) (monic iff injective), applied at each 𝑈.

𝑐)⟹𝑑): (4.2.10).

𝑑)⟹𝑐): (4.2.10). ∎

Corollary 4.2.29 (Monomorphism of Sheaves is an Isomorphism iff Surjective on Stalks)
Let 𝒞︀ be algebraic (2.6.184) and 𝑚:ℱ︀→𝒢︀ a monomorphism of sheaves. Then 𝑚 is an isomorphism if and only if 𝑚𝑥 is surjective for every 𝑥∈𝑋.

Proof. Drafted by Claude Sonnet 5, reviewed by David Rufino.

By (4.2.28), 𝑚𝑈 is monic, hence injective (2.6.194) (as 𝒞︀ is algebraic), for every 𝑈⊂𝑋, so by (4.2.10), 𝑚𝑥 is injective for every 𝑥∈𝑋.

As 𝒞︀ is algebraic, 𝑈 reflects isomorphisms (2.6.189), so an injective morphism of 𝒞︀ is an isomorphism if and only if it is surjective; applying this to 𝑚𝑥 (already known injective) for each 𝑥, 𝑚𝑥 is an isomorphism for every 𝑥∈𝑋 if and only if 𝑚𝑥 is surjective for every 𝑥∈𝑋. The result now follows from (4.2.11) (𝑎)⟺𝑑)). ∎

Definition 4.2.30 (Subpresheaf, Subsheaf)

Let 𝒞︀ be algebraic (2.6.184) and ℱ︀ a presheaf on 𝑋. A subpresheaf of ℱ︀ is an assignment, to every open 𝑈⊂𝑋, of a subalgebra 𝒢︀(𝑈)⊆𝑈(ℱ︀(𝑈)) (2.6.192), such that 𝜌𝑈𝑉ℱ︀(𝒢︀(𝑈))⊆𝒢︀(𝑉) for every 𝑉⊆𝑈.

By (2.6.193), each 𝒢︀(𝑈) carries a unique 𝒞︀-algebra structure making the inclusion 𝜄𝑈:𝒢︀(𝑈)↪︎ℱ︀(𝑈) a morphism of 𝒞︀; since 𝜌𝑈𝑉ℱ︀ is a homomorphism carrying 𝒢︀(𝑈) into 𝒢︀(𝑉), it restricts to a morphism 𝜌𝑈𝑉𝒢︀:𝒢︀(𝑈)→𝒢︀(𝑉) of 𝒞︀, making 𝒢︀ into a presheaf and 𝜄≔{𝜄𝑈}:𝒢︀→ℱ︀ into a morphism of presheaves.

We say 𝒢︀ is a subsheaf of ℱ︀ if moreover 𝒢︀, with this presheaf structure, is a sheaf.

Remark 4.2.31
Each 𝜄𝑈 is injective, hence monic (2.6.194), so 𝜄 itself is monic (4.2.28). Conversely, if 𝜄:𝒢︀→ℱ︀ is a monomorphism of presheaves (resp. sheaves), then each 𝜄𝑈 is injective (4.2.28) and (2.6.194), and its image 𝑈(𝜄𝑈)(𝑈(𝒢︀(𝑈)))⊆𝑈(ℱ︀(𝑈)) is a subalgebra (being the image of a homomorphism), so 𝜄 identifies 𝒢︀ with a subpresheaf (resp. subsheaf) of ℱ︀.
Proposition 4.2.32 (Sheafification of a Subpresheaf)

Let 𝒞︀ be algebraic (2.6.184) and 𝜄:ℱ︀→𝒢︀ a monomorphism of presheaves with 𝒢︀ a sheaf. Define

ℱ︀+(𝑈)≔{𝜏∈𝑈(𝒢︀(𝑈))|𝑈=⋃𝑖∈𝐼𝑈𝑖for some open cover, with𝜏|𝑈𝑖∈𝑈(𝜄𝑈𝑖)(𝑈(ℱ︀(𝑈𝑖)))for every𝑖}

Then ℱ︀+ is a subsheaf of 𝒢︀, and the corestriction 𝜄′:ℱ︀→ℱ︀+ of 𝜄 is a sheafification of ℱ︀ (4.2.13) (justifying, by (2.6.64), the use of the notation ℱ︀+ for it, as usual).

Proof. Drafted by Claude Sonnet 5, reviewed by David Rufino.

ℱ︀+ is a subsheaf. For 𝜔∈Ω and 𝜏1,…,𝜏𝑛𝜔∈ℱ︀+(𝑈), choose covers 𝑈=⋃𝑖𝑈𝑖(𝑟) witnessing 𝜏𝑟∈ℱ︀+(𝑈); the common refinement {𝑈𝑖1(1)∩⋯∩𝑈𝑖𝑛𝜔(𝑛𝜔)} is again a cover of 𝑈, and on each piece 𝑊 of it, 𝜏𝑟|𝑊=𝜄𝑊(𝜎𝑟) for some 𝜎𝑟∈ℱ︀(𝑊) (restricting 𝜏𝑟|𝑈𝑖𝑟(𝑟)=𝜄𝑈𝑖𝑟(𝑟)(𝜎𝑟) further, as 𝜄 is a morphism of presheaves), so

𝜔𝒢︀(𝑈)(𝜏1,…,𝜏𝑛𝜔)|𝑊=𝜔𝒢︀(𝑊)(𝜏1|𝑊,…)=𝜔𝒢︀(𝑊)(𝜄𝑊(𝜎1),…)=𝜄𝑊(𝜔ℱ︀(𝑊)(𝜎1,…))∈𝜄𝑊(ℱ︀(𝑊))

As this holds for every 𝑊 in the cover, 𝜔𝒢︀(𝑈)(𝜏1,…,𝜏𝑛𝜔)∈ℱ︀+(𝑈), so ℱ︀+(𝑈) is a subalgebra of 𝒢︀(𝑈) (2.6.192). Restricting a witnessing cover for 𝜏∈ℱ︀+(𝑈) to 𝑉⊆𝑈 shows 𝜌𝑈𝑉𝒢︀(ℱ︀+(𝑈))⊆ℱ︀+(𝑉), so ℱ︀+ is a subpresheaf of 𝒢︀ (4.2.30).

Separation for ℱ︀+ is immediate, since ℱ︀+(𝑈)⊆𝑈(𝒢︀(𝑈)) and 𝒢︀ is separated. For gluing, let 𝜏𝑖∈ℱ︀+(𝑈𝑖) for a cover 𝑈=⋃𝑖𝑈𝑖, agreeing on overlaps; as 𝒢︀ is a sheaf there is 𝜏∈𝒢︀(𝑈) with 𝜏|𝑈𝑖=𝜏𝑖. Each 𝜏𝑖 has a witnessing cover 𝑈𝑖=⋃𝑘𝑈𝑖,𝑘 with 𝜏𝑖|𝑈𝑖,𝑘∈𝜄𝑈𝑖,𝑘(ℱ︀(𝑈𝑖,𝑘)); then {𝑈𝑖,𝑘}𝑖,𝑘 is a cover of 𝑈 with 𝜏|𝑈𝑖,𝑘=𝜏𝑖|𝑈𝑖,𝑘∈𝜄𝑈𝑖,𝑘(ℱ︀(𝑈𝑖,𝑘)), so 𝜏∈ℱ︀+(𝑈). So ℱ︀+ is a subsheaf of 𝒢︀ (4.2.30). (None of this used the germ-function construction of (4.2.16).)

𝜄′ satisfies the universal property. We show directly that for every sheaf 𝒦︀,

(−)♯:Mor(ℱ︀+,𝒦︀)→Mor(ℱ︀,𝒦︀),𝜙↦𝜙∘𝜄′

is a bijection; it is then manifestly natural in 𝒦︀, being given by post-composition with the fixed 𝜄′, so this establishes (4.2.13) for (𝜄′,ℱ︀+).

Construction of 𝜓♭. Let 𝜓:ℱ︀→𝒦︀ and 𝜏∈ℱ︀+(𝑈), with witnessing cover 𝑈=⋃𝑖𝑈𝑖, 𝜏|𝑈𝑖=𝜄𝑈𝑖(𝜎𝑖) for (unique, as 𝜄 is monic) 𝜎𝑖∈ℱ︀(𝑈𝑖). On overlaps, 𝜄𝑈𝑖∩𝑈𝑗(𝜎𝑖|𝑈𝑖∩𝑈𝑗)=𝜏|𝑈𝑖∩𝑈𝑗=𝜄𝑈𝑖∩𝑈𝑗(𝜎𝑗|𝑈𝑖∩𝑈𝑗), so as 𝜄 is monic, 𝜎𝑖|𝑈𝑖∩𝑈𝑗=𝜎𝑗|𝑈𝑖∩𝑈𝑗, whence 𝜓𝑈𝑖(𝜎𝑖)|𝑈𝑖∩𝑈𝑗=𝜓𝑈𝑗(𝜎𝑗)|𝑈𝑖∩𝑈𝑗. As 𝒦︀ is a sheaf there is therefore a unique 𝜓𝑈♭(𝜏)∈𝒦︀(𝑈) with 𝜓𝑈♭(𝜏)|𝑈𝑖=𝜓𝑈𝑖(𝜎𝑖) for every 𝑖.

Independence of the witnessing cover. If 𝑈=⋃𝑗𝑉𝑗, 𝜏|𝑉𝑗=𝜄𝑉𝑗(𝜌𝑗) is another witnessing cover, then on 𝑈𝑖∩𝑉𝑗, 𝜄𝑈𝑖∩𝑉𝑗(𝜎𝑖|𝑈𝑖∩𝑉𝑗)=𝜏|𝑈𝑖∩𝑉𝑗=𝜄𝑈𝑖∩𝑉𝑗(𝜌𝑗|𝑈𝑖∩𝑉𝑗), so (as 𝜄 monic) 𝜎𝑖|𝑈𝑖∩𝑉𝑗=𝜌𝑗|𝑈𝑖∩𝑉𝑗, whence 𝜓𝑈𝑖(𝜎𝑖)|𝑈𝑖∩𝑉𝑗=𝜓𝑉𝑗(𝜌𝑗)|𝑈𝑖∩𝑉𝑗; as {𝑈𝑖∩𝑉𝑗}𝑖,𝑗 covers 𝑈, the two candidate values for 𝜓𝑈♭(𝜏) agree on a common refinement, so are equal by separation of 𝒦︀.

𝜓𝑈♭ is a homomorphism. Given 𝜏1,…,𝜏𝑛𝜔∈ℱ︀+(𝑈), using the common refinement cover {𝑊} from the first paragraph (with local representatives 𝜎𝑟∈ℱ︀(𝑊) for 𝜏𝑟), independence of witnessing cover gives 𝜓𝑈♭(𝜏𝑟)|𝑊=𝜓𝑊(𝜎𝑟); as this same cover witnesses 𝜔ℱ︀+(𝑈)(𝜏1,…,𝜏𝑛𝜔)∈ℱ︀+(𝑈) with local representatives 𝜔ℱ︀(𝑊)(𝜎1,…) (shown above), independence of witnessing cover also gives 𝜓𝑈♭(𝜔ℱ︀+(𝑈)(𝜏1,…))|𝑊=𝜓𝑊(𝜔ℱ︀(𝑊)(𝜎1,…))=𝜔𝒦︀(𝑊)(𝜓𝑊(𝜎1),…)=𝜔𝒦︀(𝑈)(𝜓𝑈♭(𝜏1),…)|𝑊 for every 𝑊, so by separation of 𝒦︀, 𝜓𝑈♭(𝜔ℱ︀+(𝑈)(𝜏1,…,𝜏𝑛𝜔))=𝜔𝒦︀(𝑈)(𝜓𝑈♭(𝜏1),…,𝜓𝑈♭(𝜏𝑛𝜔)).
