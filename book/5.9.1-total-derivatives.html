5.9.1 Total Derivatives

Definition 5.9.1

Let 𝑈⊂𝑋 be an open neighbourhood of 0 and 𝜑:𝑈→𝑌 a function. We say 𝜑 is sublinear if

∀𝜀>0∃𝛿s.t.‖ℎ‖<𝛿⟹(ℎ∈𝑈)∧(‖𝜑(ℎ)‖<𝜀‖ℎ‖)

Note sublinear functions are closed under linear combinations, and the property is unchanged under equivalent norms.

Definition 5.9.2 (Total Derivative)

Let 𝑈⊂𝑋 be an open subset of a Banach space, 𝑌 a Banach space and 𝑥∈𝑈. We say that a map 𝜑:𝑈→𝑌 is differentiable at 𝑥 if there exists a continuous linear map 𝐷𝜑(𝑥)∈𝐿(𝑋,𝑌) (derivative at 𝑥) such that

𝜑(𝑥+ℎ)−𝜑(𝑥)−𝐷𝜑(𝑥)(ℎ)is sublinear inℎ

We say it is simply differentiable if this holds for every point 𝑥∈𝑈 and write 𝐷𝜑 for the corresponding function 𝑈→𝐿(𝑋,𝑌).

If 𝐷𝜑 is also continuous then we say that 𝜑 is continuously differentiable or 𝐶1.

The following result shows that the total derivative is necessarily unique.

Proposition 5.9.3
Let 𝛼:𝑋→𝑌 be a linear map which is also sublinear. Then 𝛼=0.
Proof. Consider 𝑥∈𝑋 then for every 𝜀>0 there exists 𝛿 such that ‖ℎ‖<𝛿⟹‖𝛼(ℎ)‖<𝜀‖ℎ‖. Define 𝜆≔𝛿2‖𝑥‖. Then evidently ‖𝛼(𝜆𝑥)‖<𝜀‖𝜆𝑥‖ whence ‖𝛼(𝑥)‖<𝜀‖𝑥‖. As this holds for every 𝜀 we see ‖𝛼(𝑥)‖=0 and 𝛼(𝑥)=0. ∎
