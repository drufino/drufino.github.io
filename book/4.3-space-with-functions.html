4.3 Space with Functions

Definition 4.3.1 (Space with functions)

Let 𝑋 be a topological space and 𝒪︀𝑋 be a subsheaf of the 𝑘―-valued functions on 𝑋 (which is a sheaf of 𝑘-algebras). Then we say (𝑋,𝒪︀𝑋) is a space with functions. Additionally it’s local if it satisfies the condition

  1. For every 𝑓∈𝒪︀𝑋(𝑈) we have 𝐷(𝑓)≔{𝑓≠0} is open and 1𝑓∈𝒪︀𝑋(𝐷(𝑓)).

Note that the sheaf restriction maps 𝜌𝑈𝑉:𝒪︀𝑋(𝑈)→𝒪︀𝑋(𝑉) correspond simply to restrictions of functions.

In the latter case 𝑋 is a locally ringed space over 𝑘 because 𝒪︀𝑋,𝑥 is a local 𝑘-algebra with maximal ideal 𝔪𝑋,𝑥 consisting of stalks vanishing at 𝑥.

A regular morphism or regular map (𝑋,𝒪︀𝑋)→(𝑌,𝒪︀𝑌) is a continuous function 𝜙:𝑋→𝑌 such that

𝑔∈𝒪︀𝑌(𝑈)⟹𝑔∘𝜙∈𝒪︀𝑋(𝜙−1(𝑈))

This determines a morphism of sheaves

𝜙♯:𝒪︀𝑌⟶𝜙⋆𝒪︀𝑋

We say a regular morphism is dominant if 𝜙(𝑋) is dense in 𝑌.

An isomorphism is a homeomorphism and induces an isomorphism of sheaves, or equivalently has a two-sided regular inverse.

Proposition 4.3.2
The composition of regular maps is well-defined and associative.

4.3.1 Local Rings

Definition 4.3.3 (Local Ring)

Let 𝑋 be a local space with functions and 𝑊⊂𝑋 an irreducible closed subset. Then we define the local ring at 𝑊 to be (see (4.2.5))

𝒪︀𝑋,𝑊≔lim⟶𝑈∩𝑊≠∅𝒪︀𝑋(𝑈)

If 𝑊―=𝑊′― then 𝒪︀𝑋,𝑊=𝒪︀𝑋,𝑊′, so for example 𝒪︀𝑋,𝑊=𝒪︀𝑋,𝑊―.

It is a local ring with unique maximal ideal

𝔪𝑋,𝑊≔{[(𝑉,𝜎)]|𝑉∩𝑊≠∅,𝜎∈𝒪︀𝑋(𝑉),𝜎(𝑥)=0∀𝑥∈𝑉∩𝑊}

𝑘(𝔪𝑋,𝑊)≔𝒪︀𝑋,𝑊/𝔪𝑋,𝑊

In the case 𝑊={𝑥} we write 𝒪︀𝑋,𝑥, 𝔪𝑋,𝑥 and 𝑘(𝔪𝑋,𝑥).

Proof. Consider (𝑉,𝜎)∈𝒪︀𝑋,𝑊∖𝔪𝑋,𝑊. Then by definition 𝐷(𝜎)∩𝑊≠∅ and (𝐷(𝜎),𝜎−1) is an explicit inverse. ∎
Corollary 4.3.4 (Field of Rational Functions)
Let 𝑋 be an irreducible space with functions. Then 𝒪︀𝑋,𝑋≕𝑘(𝑋) is a field, which we call the field of rational functions.

4.3.2 Open Immersions

Definition 4.3.11 (Open Immersion)

Let 𝑋,𝑌 be spaces with functions. Then we say a regular map 𝜙:𝑋→𝑌 is an open immersion if

  1. 𝜙 is an open embedding, and

  2. 𝜙 induces an isomorphism of sheaves

    𝜙⋆(𝒪︀𝑌|𝜙(𝑋))⟶∼𝒪︀𝑋
Proposition 4.3.12 (Open Subspace)

Let 𝑋 be a (local) space of functions and 𝑈⊂𝑋 an open subset. Then (𝑈,𝒪︀𝑋|𝑈) is a (local) space of functions which we call an open subspace. We may write 𝒪︀𝑈≔𝒪︀𝑋|𝑈.

The inclusion 𝑖:𝑈↪︎𝑋 is an open immersion and an open immersion 𝜙:𝑋→𝑌 induces an isomorphism between 𝑋 and the open subvariety 𝜙(𝑋).

Proposition 4.3.13
Let 𝑋 be a space of functions and 𝑉⊂𝑈 open subsets. Then (𝑉,𝒪︀𝑈|𝑉)=(𝑉,𝒪︀𝑋|𝑉).
Proposition 4.3.14

Let 𝜙:𝑋→𝑌 be an open embedding and 𝑍⊂𝑌 closed. Then 𝑍 is irreducible iff 𝜙−1(𝑍) is irreducible.

4.3.3 Closed Immersions

Definition 4.3.20 (Closed Immersion)

Let 𝜙:𝑋→𝑌 be a regular morphism of spaces with functions. We say that 𝜙 is a closed immersion if

  1. 𝜙 is a homeomorphism onto a closed subset 𝜙(𝑋) of 𝑌

  2. For all 𝑥∈𝑋 we have the induced map on stalks

    𝒪︀𝑌,𝜙(𝑥)→𝒪︀𝑋

    is surjective.

Proposition 4.3.21 (Closed Subset Structure Sheaf)

Let (𝑋,𝒪︀𝑋) be a space of functions and 𝑍⊂𝑋 a closed subset. Then there exists a unique sheaf of functions 𝒪︀𝑍 such that the inclusion 𝑗:𝑍→𝑋 is a closed immersion.

More precisely for 𝑈⊂𝑍 an open subset

𝒪︀𝑍(𝑈)≔{𝑓:𝑈→𝑘―|𝑓(𝑦)=𝑔𝑖(𝑦)∀𝑦∈𝑍∩𝑈𝑖for some𝑔𝑖∈𝒪︀𝑋(𝑈𝑖)where𝑍∩⋃𝑖∈𝐼𝑈𝑖=𝑈}

Proof. Evidently 𝑗 is a homeomorphism onto 𝑍. We first show that 𝑗 is regular. Suppose 𝑔∈𝒪︀𝑋(𝑈′) then by definition we have 𝑔∘𝑗∈𝒪︀𝑍(𝑗−1(𝑈′))=𝒪︀𝑍(𝑈′∩𝑍).

Given (𝑉,𝑓)∈𝒪︀𝑍,𝑧 by taking a smaller open subset we may suppose that 𝑓(𝑦)=𝑔(𝑦) for all 𝑦∈𝑉 for some 𝑔∈𝒪︀𝑋(𝑈) and 𝑈∩𝑍=𝑉. Then the image of (𝑈,𝑔) under the stalk map is (𝑈∩𝑍,𝑔∘𝑗) which is precisely (𝑉,𝑓). Therefore the stalk map 𝑗𝑧 is surjective for all 𝑧∈𝑍.

Let ℱ︀ be a sheaf of functions for which 𝑗 is a (regular) closed immersion and suppose 𝑓∈𝒪︀𝑍(𝑈). Then 𝑓|𝑈𝑖∩𝑍(𝑦)=𝑔𝑖(𝑦) for all 𝑦∈𝑍∩𝑈𝑖 and 𝑔𝑖∈𝒪︀𝑋(𝑈𝑖). By assumption 𝑓|𝑈𝑖∩𝑍=𝑔𝑖∘𝑗∈ℱ︀(𝑈𝑖∩𝑍). By the sheaf condition we have 𝑓∈ℱ︀(𝑈) which shows 𝒪︀𝑍 is a subsheaf of ℱ︀.

We need to show that the inclusion of sheaves 𝒪︀𝑍↪︎ℱ︀ is an isomorphism. By (4.2.28) c) ⟹ d) and (4.2.11) it is enough to show that it is surjective on stalks. However this follows by assumption by observing the composition 𝒪︀𝑋,𝑗(𝑧)→𝒪︀𝑍,𝑧→ℱ︀𝑧 is surjective. ∎

Remark 4.3.22
This can be defined formally as 𝑗−1(𝒪︀𝑋/ℐ︀) where ℐ︀ is the presheaf of regular functions which vanish on 𝑍. We show in the Algebraic Geometry chapter’s treatment of closed subvarieties that this is consistent with the concept of affine subvariety.
Proposition 4.3.23

Let 𝜙:𝑋→𝑌 be a regular morphism of spaces with functions. Then the following are equivalent

  1. 𝜙 is a closed immersion
  2. 𝜙(𝑋) is closed and 𝜙 determines an isomorphism 𝑋⟶∼𝜙(𝑋) of spaces with functions using the structure defined in (4.3.21)

Proof. 𝑎)⟹𝑏) By definition 𝜙(𝑋) is closed and 𝜙 determines a homeomorphism onto 𝜙(𝑋). Let 𝑍≔𝜙(𝑋) and 𝒪︀𝑍 denote the canonical structure sheaf. There is a well-defined map

𝒪︀𝑍(𝑈)→𝒪︀𝑋(𝜙−1(𝑈))

which is injective, and therefore injective on stalks (4.2.28) c) ⟹ d). By (4.2.11) we need only show that the map 𝒪︀𝑍,𝜙(𝑥)→𝒪︀𝑋,𝑥 is surjective. However by hypothesis the composite map

𝒪︀𝑌,𝜙(𝑥)→𝒪︀𝑍,𝜙(𝑥)→𝒪︀𝑥

is surjective from which the result follows.

𝑏)⟹𝑎) Evidently 𝜙 is a homeomorphism onto 𝜙(𝑋). Further we have a sequence of stalk maps

𝒪︀𝑌,𝜙(𝑥)→∼𝒪︀𝜙(𝑋),𝜙(𝑥)→𝒪︀𝑋,𝑥

where the first is surjective by (4.3.21) and the second is an isomorphism by assumption. Therefore the composite is surjective and 𝜙 is a closed immersion. ∎

Proposition 4.3.25 (Patching Topologies)

  • the identity map 1:𝑈𝑖∩𝑈𝑗→𝑈𝑖∩𝑈𝑗 is continuous with respect to the subspace topologies on 𝑈𝑖 and 𝑈𝑗 respectively.

  • the 𝑈𝑖 are open in 𝑋,
  • the topology on 𝑈𝑖 coincides with the subspace topology from 𝑋

More precisely it is given by

{𝑈⊂𝑋|𝑈∩𝑈𝑖open in𝑈𝑖for all𝑖=1…𝑛}

  • the map 𝜃𝑖𝑗≔𝜃𝑗∘𝜃𝑖−1:𝜃𝑖(𝑈𝑖∩𝑈𝑗)→𝜃𝑗(𝑈𝑖∩𝑈𝑗) is continuous.

  • 𝜃𝑖 is a homeomorphism with respect to the subspace topology on 𝑈𝑖 for all 𝑖=1…𝑛.

Proposition 4.3.27 (Glueing Regular Maps)

Proof. As a function 𝜙 is clearly well-defined and unique. It is continuous by (4.1.29). Suppose 𝑔∈𝒪︀𝑌(𝑉) then by definition (𝑔∘𝜙)|𝜙−1(𝑉)∩𝑈𝑖=𝑔∘𝜙𝑖∈𝒪︀𝑈𝑖(𝜙−1(𝑉)∩𝑈𝑖)=𝒪︀𝑋(𝜙−1(𝑉)∩𝑈𝑖). As 𝒪︀𝑋 is a sheaf this shows that 𝑔∘𝜙 is regular as required. ∎
