3.4.2 Bimodule Tensor Product

To develop the tensor product in general it is convenient to work with bimodules. We make heavy use of the notion if bimodule hom-sets (3.3.78).

Definition 3.4.9 (Bilinear maps)

Consider the bimodules 𝑀𝐵𝐴, 𝑁𝐶𝐵. We say that a map

𝜓:𝑀𝐵𝐴×𝑁𝐶𝐵→𝑍𝐶𝐴

is (𝐴,𝐶)-bilinear if it satisfies all of the following conditions

  • additive 𝜓(𝑚+𝑚′,𝑛+𝑛′)=𝜓(𝑚,𝑛)+𝜓(𝑚′,𝑛)+𝜓(𝑚,𝑛′)+𝜓(𝑛,𝑛′)
  • balanced 𝜓(𝑚𝑏,𝑛)=𝜓(𝑚,𝑏𝑛)
  • 𝐴-linear 𝜓(𝑎𝑚,𝑛)=𝑎𝜓(𝑚,𝑛)
  • 𝐶-linear 𝜓(𝑚,𝑛𝑐)=𝜓(𝑚,𝑛)𝑐

Extending the notation from earlier we denote the set of additive, balanced and (𝐴,𝐶)-bilinear maps by Bilin(𝑀,𝑁;𝑍). This determines a functor

Bilin:(𝐌𝐨𝐝𝐵𝐴×𝐌𝐨𝐝𝐶𝐵)op×𝐌𝐨𝐝𝐶𝐴→𝐀𝐛𝐆𝐫𝐩

where addition is defined pointwise. We may also consider the functor of only additive and balanced maps

Balan:(𝐌𝐨𝐝𝐵×𝐌𝐨𝐝𝐵)op×𝐀𝐛𝐆𝐫𝐩→𝐀𝐛𝐆𝐫𝐩

and the functor

RBilin:(𝐌𝐨𝐝𝐶𝐵×𝐌𝐨𝐝𝐷𝐶)op×𝐌𝐨𝐝𝐷𝐴→𝐌𝐨𝐝𝐵𝐴

where we drop the 𝐵-linear requirement but also introduce an extra (𝐴,𝐵)-module structure on the maps, as follows

(𝑎𝜓𝑏)(𝑚,𝑛)≔𝑎𝜓(𝑏𝑚,𝑛)
Proposition 3.4.10 (Existence of Bimodule Tensor Product)

Let 𝐴,𝐵,𝐶 be arbitrary rings

  • The bifunctor Balan(𝑀,𝑁;−) is represented by the balanced tensor product

    ⊗𝐵:𝐌𝐨𝐝𝐵×𝐌𝐨𝐝𝐵→𝐀𝐛𝐆𝐫𝐩

    with natural isomorphism for 𝑍 an Abelian group

    Φ:Hom(𝑀𝐵⊗𝐵𝑁𝐵,𝑍)≅Balan(𝑀𝐵,𝑁𝐵;𝑍)𝜃→𝜃∘𝑖

    where 𝑖 is a universal balanced map

    𝑖:𝑀×𝑁→𝑀𝐵⊗𝐵𝑁𝐵

    The tensor product is generated by elementary tensors of the form 𝑚⊗𝑛≔𝑖(𝑚,𝑛).

  • The bifunctor Bilin(𝑀,𝑁;−) is represented by the balanced tensor product 𝑀⊗𝐵𝑁 with an additional (𝐴,𝐶)-bimodule structure given by

    𝑎⋅(𝑚⊗𝑛)⋅𝑐≔(𝑎⋅𝑚)⊗(𝑛⋅𝑐)

    This determines a bifunctor

    ⊗𝐵:𝐌𝐨𝐝𝐵𝐴×𝐌𝐨𝐝𝐶𝐵→𝐌𝐨𝐝𝐶𝐴

    and a natural isomorphism of Abelian groups

    Hom(𝑀𝐵𝐴⊗𝐵𝑁𝐶𝐵,𝑍)≅Bilin(𝑀𝐵𝐴,𝑁𝐶𝐵;𝑍𝐶𝐴)𝜃→𝜃∘𝑖

In either case, if 𝑓:𝑀→𝑀′ and 𝑔:𝑁→𝑁′ are morphisms then there is a unique morphism

(𝑓⊗𝑔):(𝑀⊗𝑁)→(𝑀′⊗𝑁′)

such that

(𝑓⊗𝑔)(𝑚⊗𝑛)=𝑓(𝑚)⊗𝑔(𝑛)

Furthermore for maps 𝑓′:𝑀′→𝑀″ and 𝑔′:𝑁′→𝑁″ the following property is satisfised

(𝑓′⊗𝑔′)∘(𝑓⊗𝑔)=(𝑓′∘𝑓)⊗(𝑔′∘𝑔)

Proof. In light of (2.6.65) we only need to show that the functors are representable for fixed 𝑀,𝑁, as the functoriality will follow immediately by abstract nonsense.

We first show that Balan(−,−;−) is representable. For let 𝐹 be the free abelian group on 𝑀×𝑁 and let 𝑆⊂𝐹 be the subgroup generated by elements of the form

  • (𝑚+𝑚′,𝑛)−(𝑚,𝑛)−(𝑚′,𝑛)
  • (𝑚,𝑛′+𝑛)−(𝑚,𝑛′)−(𝑚,𝑛)
  • (𝑚𝑏,𝑛)−(𝑚,𝑏𝑛)

Observe there is a canonical map 𝑀×𝑁→𝐹. Suppose that 𝑍 is an abelian group and 𝜓:𝑀×𝑁→𝑍 is a balanced additive map. Then we may extend by linearity to a group homomorphism 𝜓:𝐹→𝑍. By construction 𝑆⊆ker(𝜓) whence there is a unique group homomorphism 𝜓̂:𝐹/𝑆→𝑍 such that 𝜓̂∘𝑖=𝜓 where 𝑖:𝑀×𝑁→𝐹/𝑆 is the canonical inclusion. This shows that (𝐹/𝑆,𝑖) is a universal element for Balan(−,−;−) and we denote this by 𝑀𝐵⊗𝐵𝑁𝐵. Then (2.6.62) exhibits Φ as a natural isomorphism of sets, which may readily observed to be additive.

In order to define the (𝐴,𝐶)-bimodule structure in the second case let 𝜓𝑎:𝑀→𝑀 denote multiplication by 𝑎 and 𝜓𝑐:𝑁→𝑁 multiplication by 𝑐. We note these are both 𝐵-module homomorphisms by definition so we may define the action by functoriality of the balanced tensor product case

𝑎⋅𝑣≔(𝜓𝑎⊗1𝑁)(𝑣)∀𝑣∈𝑀⊗𝑁𝑣⋅𝑐≔(1𝑀⊗𝜓𝑐)(𝑣)∀𝑣∈𝑀⊗𝑁

These are 𝐵-linear by construction and we may demonstrate (𝐴,𝐶)-bimodule actions are associative and commute by using the functoriality of the tensor product

(𝜓𝑎′⊗1𝑁)∘(𝜓𝑎⊗1𝑁)=(𝜓𝑎′∘𝜓𝑎)⊗1𝑁=(𝜓𝑎′𝑎)⊗1𝑁(1𝑀⊗𝜓𝑐)∘(1𝑀⊗𝜓𝑐′)=1𝑀⊗(𝜓𝑐∘𝜓𝑐′)=1𝑀⊗𝜓𝑐𝑐′(𝜓𝑎⊗1𝑁)∘(1𝑀⊗𝜓𝑐)=𝜓𝑎⊗𝜓𝑐=(1𝑀⊗𝜓𝑐)⊗(𝜓𝑎⊗1𝑁)

Further by construction the canonical map 𝑖 is (𝐴,𝐶)-bilinear. Consider any (𝐴,𝐶)-bilinear map 𝜓:𝑀×𝑁→𝑍 then by the previous part there is a unique group homomorphism 𝜓̂:𝑀⊗𝐵𝑁→𝑍 such that

𝜓̂(𝑚⊗𝑛)≔𝜓(𝑚,𝑛)

which we see by construction (and linearity) is (𝐴,𝐶)-bilinear. It is clearly the unique such map so that we may conclude Bilin(−,−;−) has universal element (𝑀𝐵𝐴⊗𝐵𝑁𝐶𝐵,𝑖). The (2.6.62) exhibits Φ as a natural isomorphism of sets which may be readily exhibited to be additive. ∎

Lemma 3.4.11 (Currying Lemma)

Let 𝑀,𝑁,𝑃,𝑌,𝑍 be bimodules. Then there is an isomorphism of abelian groups and (𝐴,𝐵)-bimodules respectively

Bilin(𝑀𝐵𝐴,𝑁𝐶𝐵;𝑌𝐶𝐴)≅Hom(𝑀𝐵𝐴,RHom(𝑁𝐶𝐵,𝑌𝐶𝐴))≅Hom(𝑁𝐶𝐵,LHom(𝑀𝐵𝐴,𝑌𝐶𝐴))RBilin(𝑁𝐶𝐵,𝑃𝐷𝐶;𝑍𝐷𝐴)≅RHom(𝑁𝐶𝐵,RHom(𝑃𝐷𝐶,𝑍𝐷𝐴))

contravariant in 𝑀,𝑁,𝑃 and covariant in 𝑌 and 𝑍.

Proof. The maps on abelian groups are clear, namely given a bi-additive map 𝜓 we have a well-defined homomorphism of abelian groups

𝑚→(𝑛→𝜓(𝑚,𝑛))

The verification that the maps are well-defined and bijective is tedious but mechanical. ∎

Proposition 3.4.12 (Tensor-Hom Adjunction)

Let 𝑀,𝑁,𝑃,𝑌,𝑍 be bimodules then

Hom(𝑀𝐵𝐴⊗𝑁𝐶𝐵,𝑌𝐶𝐴)≅Hom(𝑀𝐵𝐴,RHom(𝑁𝐶𝐵,𝑌𝐶𝐴))≅Hom(𝑁𝐶𝐵,LHom(𝑀𝐵𝐴,𝑌𝐶𝐴))RHom(𝑁𝐶𝐵⊗𝑃𝐷𝐶,𝑍𝐷𝐴)≅RHom(𝑁𝐶𝐵,RHom(𝑃𝐷𝐶,𝑍𝐷𝐴))

Proposition 3.4.13 (Associativity of Tensor Product)

Let 𝑀𝐵𝐴, 𝑁𝐶𝐵, 𝑃𝐷𝐶 be bimodules then there is a natural isomorphism of (𝐴,𝐷)-bimodules

(𝑀⊗𝐵𝑁)⊗𝐶𝑃≅𝑀⊗𝐵(𝑁⊗𝐶𝑃)(𝑚⊗𝑛)⊗𝑝→𝑚⊗(𝑛⊗𝑝)

Proof. This follows much as in the commutative case. Let 𝑍 be an (𝐴,𝐷)-bimodule. Then by the Tensor-Hom adjunction there are natural isomorphisms of Abelian groups

Hom((𝑀𝐵𝐴⊗𝑁𝐶𝐵)⊗𝑃𝐷𝐶,𝑍𝐷𝐴)≅Hom(𝑀𝐵𝐴⊗𝑁𝐶𝐵,RHom(𝑃𝐷𝐶,𝑍𝐷𝐴))≅Hom(𝑀𝐵𝐴,RHom(𝑁𝐶𝐵,RHom(𝑃𝐷𝐶,𝑍𝐷𝐴)))≅Hom(𝑀𝐵𝐴,RHom(𝑁𝐶𝐵⊗𝐶𝑃𝐷𝐶,𝑍𝐷𝐴))≅Hom(𝑀𝐵𝐴⊗𝐵(𝑁𝐶𝐵⊗𝐶𝑃𝐷𝐶),𝑍𝐷𝐴)

The required isomorphism is then a consequence of (2.6.68). For explicit form set 𝑍=𝑀⊗(𝑁⊗𝑃) and consider the identity map 1𝑍. We see tracing back under these natural isomorphisms this corresponds to the given map. ∎
