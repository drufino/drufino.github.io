Definition 4.1.17 (Adherent Point)

Let 𝑋 be a topological space and 𝑌⊂𝑋. We say that 𝑥∈𝑋 is an adherent point of 𝑌, if 𝑈∈𝒰︀𝑥⟹𝑈∩𝑌≠∅.

We say that 𝑥 is a sequential limit point of 𝑌 if there exists a sequence 𝑥𝑛∈𝑌 such that 𝑥𝑛→𝑥.

Proposition 4.1.18 (Adherent Point)
Let 𝑋 be a topological space. Then every sequential limit point is an adherent point. If 𝑋 is first countable the converse holds.
Proof. The first implication is straightforward. For the latter, consider a countable local base at 𝑥 given by 𝑈𝑛. We may assume wlog that it is decreasing. Then by assumption we may choose 𝑥𝑛∈𝑈𝑛∩𝑌. For any 𝑉∈ℬ︀𝑥 we have 𝑈𝑁⊂𝑉 for some 𝑁, and therefore 𝑛≥𝑁⟹𝑥𝑛∈𝑈𝑛⟹𝑥𝑛∈𝑉. Therefore 𝑥𝑛→𝑥. ∎

Proposition 4.1.21 (Closure in Subspace Topology)

Let 𝑋 be a topological space and 𝑌 a subset. For 𝑍⊂𝑌 we have

cl𝑌(𝑍)=cl𝑋(𝑍)∩𝑌
Proof. By (4.1.19) cl𝑋(𝑍) is a closed subset of 𝑋 and therefore cl𝑋(𝑍)∩𝑌 is a closed subset of 𝑌 in the subspace topology. Therefore cl𝑌(𝑍)⊂cl𝑋(𝑍)∩𝑌 by minimality. Similarly cl𝑌(𝑍)=𝑊∩𝑌 for 𝑊 closed in 𝑋. By minimality cl𝑋(𝑍)⊂𝑊⟹cl𝑋(𝑍)∩𝑌⊂𝑊∩𝑌=cl𝑌(𝑍). ∎

Let 𝑋 be a topological space with 𝑋=⋃𝑖∈𝐼𝑈𝑖 an open cover. Let 𝑌⊂𝑋 be a subset then 𝑌 is closed in 𝑋 if and only if 𝑌∩𝑈𝑖 is closed in 𝑈𝑖 for all 𝑖∈𝐼.

Proof. One direction is obvious by definition of the subspace topology. Conversely 𝑈𝑖∖(𝑌∩𝑈𝑖) is open in 𝑈𝑖 and therefore in 𝑋. We see that

𝑋∖𝑌=⋃𝑖∈𝐼𝑈𝑖∖(𝑌∩𝑈𝑖)

is open and therefore 𝑌 is closed. ∎
