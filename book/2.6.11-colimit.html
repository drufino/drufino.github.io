2.6.11 Colimit

Definition 2.6.88 (Co-cones)

Let 𝐹:𝐼→𝒞︀ be a diagram. We say that the pair (𝐿,{𝜆𝑖:𝐹(𝑖)→𝐿}𝑖∈𝐼) is a co-cone over 𝐹 if

𝜆𝑗∘𝐹(𝛼)=𝜆𝑖for all morphisms𝛼:𝑖→𝑗

The cones for 𝐹 constitute a co-cone category (𝐹↓Δ) where a morphism 𝛼:(𝐿,{𝜆𝑖}𝑖∈𝐼)→(𝐿′,{𝜆𝑖′}𝑖∈𝐼) is just a morphism 𝛼:𝐿→𝐿′ such that 𝛼∘𝜆𝑖=𝜆𝑖′ for all 𝑖∈𝐼

Definition 2.6.89 (Small Colimit)

Let 𝐹:𝐼→𝒞︀ be a diagram. A co-cone (𝐿,{𝜆𝑖}𝑖∈𝐼) over 𝐹 is a colimit for 𝐹 if it is an initial object (2.6.48) in the co-cone category.

Explicitly for every co-cone (𝐿′,{𝜆𝑖′}𝑖∈𝐼) there is a unique morphism 𝛼:𝐿→𝐿′ such that 𝜆𝑖′∘𝛼=𝜆𝑖.

We may denote this as colim𝐹.

Proposition 2.6.90

Let 𝐹:𝐼→𝒞︀ be a diagram, (𝐿,{𝜆𝑖}𝑖∈𝐼) a colimit of 𝐹 and (𝐿′,{𝜆𝑖′}𝑖∈𝐼) a co-cone. Then the following are equivalent

  1. 𝐿′ is a colimit for 𝐹
  2. there is an isomorphism of co-cones 𝐿′→∼𝐿
  3. there is a morphism of co-cones 𝐿′→𝐿 and precisely one (co-cone) endomorphism of 𝐿′.

In this case the (co-cone) isomorphism is unique.

Proof. By definition (𝐿,{𝜆𝑖}𝑖∈𝐼) is an initial object of the co-cone category (𝐹↓Δ), so this is precisely (2.6.49) applied with ∅≔(𝐿,{𝜆𝑖}𝑖∈𝐼) and 𝑋≔(𝐿′,{𝜆𝑖′}𝑖∈𝐼). ∎
Proposition 2.6.91 (Uniqueness of Colimits)
Let 𝐹:𝐼→𝒞︀ be a diagram and (𝐿,{𝜆𝑖}𝑖∈𝐼) and (𝐿′,{𝜆𝑖′}𝑖∈𝐼) colimits of 𝐹. Then there is a unique morphism 𝜓:𝐿→𝐿′ satisfying 𝜓∘𝜆𝑖=𝜆𝑖′, which is an isomorphism.
Proof. Since (𝐿′,{𝜆𝑖′}𝑖∈𝐼) is a colimit for 𝐹, (2.6.90) gives a unique isomorphism of co-cones 𝜙:𝐿′→𝐿, that is, a unique isomorphism 𝜙 with 𝜙∘𝜆𝑖′=𝜆𝑖. Setting 𝜓≔𝜙−1:𝐿→𝐿′ gives the required unique isomorphism with 𝜓∘𝜆𝑖=𝜆𝑖′. ∎
Proposition 2.6.92 (Limits and Colimits swap under 𝒞︀op)

Let 𝐼 be a small category and 𝐹:𝐼→𝒞︀ a diagram. Write 𝐹op:𝐼op→𝒞︀op for the diagram with 𝐹op(𝑖)≔𝐹(𝑖) on objects and 𝐹op(𝛼op)≔𝐹(𝛼)op for 𝛼op:𝑗→𝑖 the morphism of 𝐼op corresponding to 𝛼:𝑖→𝑗 of 𝐼. Then, regarding each 𝜆𝑖:𝐿→𝐹(𝑖) as a morphism 𝜆𝑖op:𝐹(𝑖)→𝐿 of 𝒞︀op, (𝐿,{𝜆𝑖}𝑖∈𝐼) is a limit for 𝐹 in 𝒞︀ if and only if (𝐿,{𝜆𝑖op}𝑖∈𝐼) is a colimit for 𝐹op in 𝒞︀op.

Dually, (𝐿,{𝜆𝑖}𝑖∈𝐼) is a colimit for 𝐹 in 𝒞︀ if and only if (𝐿,{𝜆𝑖op}𝑖∈𝐼) is a limit for 𝐹op in 𝒞︀op.

Proof. Drafted by Claude Sonnet, reviewed by David Rufino.

We only prove the first statement, the second following by applying it to 𝒞︀op in place of 𝒞︀, using (𝒞︀op)op=𝒞︀.

Define Σ:(Δ↓𝐹)→(𝐹op↓Δ)op, (𝐿,{𝜆𝑖}𝑖∈𝐼)↦(𝐿,{𝜆𝑖op}𝑖∈𝐼), acting on a cone morphism 𝜇:(𝐿,𝜆)→(𝐿′,𝜆′) (i.e. 𝜇:𝐿→𝐿′ with 𝜆𝑖′∘𝜇=𝜆𝑖 for all 𝑖) by Σ(𝜇)≔𝜇op. This is well-defined: upon reversing every arrow (using Mor𝒞︀op(𝑋,𝑌)=Mor𝒞︀(𝑌,𝑋) and that composition in 𝒞︀op reverses that of 𝒞︀ (2.6.4)), the cone condition 𝐹(𝛼)∘𝜆𝑖=𝜆𝑗 for 𝛼:𝑖→𝑗 of 𝐼 becomes exactly the co-cone condition 𝜆𝑖op∘𝐹op(𝛼op)=𝜆𝑗op for 𝛼op:𝑗→𝑖 of 𝐼op, so {𝜆𝑖op} is a co-cone over 𝐹op; and the cone morphism condition 𝜆𝑖′∘𝜇=𝜆𝑖 becomes 𝜇op∘(𝜆𝑖′)op=𝜆𝑖op, so 𝜇op is a morphism (𝐿′,𝜆′op)→(𝐿,𝜆op) of (𝐹op↓Δ), that is, a morphism (𝐿,𝜆op)→(𝐿′,𝜆′op) of (𝐹op↓Δ)op. Thus Σ is well-defined, and is manifestly functorial.

Dually define Σ−1:(𝐹op↓Δ)op→(Δ↓𝐹), (𝐿,{𝜇𝑖}𝑖∈𝐼)↦(𝐿,{𝜇𝑖op}𝑖∈𝐼), acting on morphisms by 𝜈↦𝜈op. Since (−)op is an involution on morphisms, Σ−1Σ=1 and ΣΣ−1=1, so Σ is an isomorphism of categories, in particular an equivalence of categories (2.6.25).

By (2.6.4), a category 𝒟︀ has 𝑇 as a terminal object if and only if 𝒟︀op has 𝑇 as an initial object (since Mor𝒟︀op(𝑋,𝑇)=Mor𝒟︀(𝑇,𝑋) for all 𝑋). So (𝐿,{𝜆𝑖}) is a limit for 𝐹, i.e. terminal in (Δ↓𝐹) (2.6.78), iff, by (2.6.52), Σ(𝐿,{𝜆𝑖})=(𝐿,{𝜆𝑖op}) is terminal in (𝐹op↓Δ)op, iff (applying the noted equivalence to 𝒟︀≔(𝐹op↓Δ)) (𝐿,{𝜆𝑖op}) is initial in (𝐹op↓Δ), that is, a colimit for 𝐹op (2.6.89). ∎

Proposition 2.6.93 (Co-product)

Let 𝐼 be a discrete small category and {𝑋𝑖}𝑖∈𝐼 be a family of objects in 𝒞︀. Then there is a unique functor 𝐹:𝐼→𝒞︀ such that 𝐹(𝑖)=𝑋𝑖.

Consider an object 𝑋∈𝒞︀ and family of morphisms 𝑠𝑖:𝑋𝑖→𝑋, then the following are equivalent

  1. The co-cone (𝑋,{𝑠𝑖}𝑖∈𝐼) is a colimit for 𝐹
  2. For any family of morphisms 𝜆𝑖:𝑋𝑖→𝑋′ there exists a unique 𝜆:𝑋→𝑋′ such that 𝜆∘𝑠𝑖=𝜆𝑖

  3. For all objects 𝑋′ the map

    Φ:Mor(𝑋,𝑋′)⟶∏𝑖∈𝐼Mor(𝑋𝑖,𝑋′)𝜆→(𝜆∘𝑠𝑖)𝑖∈𝐼

    is a bijection, i.e. (𝑋,Φ) represents the covariant functor ∏𝑖∈𝐼Mor(𝑋𝑖,−).

In this case we say that (𝑋,{𝑠𝑖}𝑖∈𝐼) is a co-product for {𝑋𝑖}𝑖∈𝐼 and denote this by ∐𝑖∈𝐼𝑋𝑖.

For any family of morphisms 𝜆𝑖:𝑋𝑖→𝑋′ we write the induced morphism 𝜆:∐𝑖∈𝐼𝑋𝑖→𝑋′ as [𝜆𝑖]𝑖∈𝐼.

Definition 2.6.94

Let 𝑈:𝒞︀→𝒟︀ be a functor and 𝐼 a small category.

We say that 𝑈 creates co-limits of shape 𝐼 if for all diagrams 𝐹:𝐼→𝒞︀ such that lim𝑈∘𝐹 exists then colim𝐹 exists and is the unique cone such that 𝑈(colim𝐹)=colim(𝑈∘𝐹).

We say that 𝑈 preserves co-limits of shape 𝐼 if whenever colim𝐹 exists in 𝒞︀ we have colim𝑈∘𝐹 exists in 𝒟︀ and is equal to 𝑈(colim𝐹).

If we restrict to the case 𝐼 is discrete (resp. 𝐼=𝟐) then we say that 𝑈 creates/preserves coproducts (resp. co-equalisers).

Proposition 2.6.95 (Creates⇒Preserves)

Let 𝑈:𝒞︀→𝒟︀ which creates colimits of shape 𝐼 and 𝒟︀ has all colimits of shape 𝐼. Then 𝑈 preserves colimits of shape 𝐼.

In particular this holds unconditionally for 𝒟︀=𝐒𝐞𝐭 since this has all colimits.
