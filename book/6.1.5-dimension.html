6.1.5 Dimension

Definition 6.1.48 (Dimension)

Let 𝑋 be an affine variety. Then we define the dimension to be

dim𝑋≔dim𝑋(𝑘―)=dim𝑋0(𝑘―)

where this is the Krull Dimension (4.1.81) of the 𝑘―-rational points with the 𝑘-Zariski topology. This is the supremum of dimension over all irreducible components by (4.1.83).

We say 𝑋 is of pure dimension 𝑛 if all irreducible components have the same dimension 𝑛. Note an integral variety is always of pure dimension.

We say 𝑋=𝑉(𝔞) is an affine curve if it is of pure dimension 1.

Proposition 6.1.49 (Dimension of subspace)

Let 𝑋=𝑉(𝔞) be an affine variety and 𝑌=𝑉(𝔟) an affine subvariety Then

  1. dim𝑌=dim(𝔟)=dim𝑘[𝑋]/𝔟=dim𝑘[𝑌]
  2. codim(𝑌,𝑋)=ht(𝔟)

Further when 𝑌=𝑉(𝔭) is integral then codim(𝑌,𝑋)=dim𝑘[𝑋]𝔭.

Proof. a) follows from (6.1.23).

b) follows similarly by observing that the definition of ideal height (3.22.1) is dual to the topological definition of codimension (4.1.81). The last statement follows from (3.22.4). ∎

Corollary 6.1.50
Let 𝑋=𝑉(𝔞) be an affine variety then dim𝑋=dim𝑘[𝑋]. In particular dim𝔸𝑘𝑛=𝑛.
Proof. The first statement is a consequence of (6.1.49) in the case 𝔟=(0). The dimension for 𝔸𝑘𝑛 then follows (3.35.28). ∎

As we showed when studying the Krull dimension of affine algebras, the lattice of irreducible subsets is particularly well behaved.

Proposition 6.1.51 (Biequidimensional Algebraic Sets)

Let 𝑋=𝑉(𝔞) be an affine variety. Then 𝑋(𝑘―) is quasi-biequidimensional.

Further 𝑋 is equidimensional (e.g. integral) iff it is biequidimensional. In this case for every closed subset 𝑌=𝑉(𝔟) the codimension formula is satisfied

dim𝑋=dim𝑌+codim(𝑌,𝑋)

or in algebraic terms

dim𝑘[𝑋]=dim𝔟+ht(𝔟)

Proof. We’ve observed that the lattice of radical (resp. prime) ideals of 𝑘[𝑋] is isomorphic to the lattice of closed (resp. irreducible) subsets of 𝑋(𝑘―). Therefore by (3.35.35) 𝑋(𝑘―) is quasi-biequidimensional.

In the equidimensional case, the codimension formula follows from (4.1.87) and the algebraic version from (6.1.49). ∎

In the integral case we recover the “classical” field-theoretic version of dimension

Proposition 6.1.52 (Dimension of Function Field)

Let 𝑋=𝑉(𝔭) be an integral affine variety. Then

dim𝑋=dim𝑘[𝑋]=trdeg(Frac(𝑘[𝑋])/𝑘)
Proof. We have already shown that dim𝑋=dim𝑘[𝑋]. The second equality follows from Noether Normalisation (3.35.28). ∎
Proposition 6.1.53

Let 𝑋 be an affine variety and 𝑍⊂𝑋 a closed subset. Then the following are equivalent

  1. dim𝑍=0
  2. 𝑍 is finite

More precisely

𝑍={𝑥1}―∪…∪{𝑥𝑛}―

for a finite number of points 𝑥1,…,𝑥𝑛∈𝑍.

Proof. This follows from (4.1.91) and (6.1.18). ∎
