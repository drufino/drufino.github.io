3.14.1 Hensel’s Lemma

Lemma 3.14.33 (Convergent Sums in a Complete Ring)

Let 𝐴 be a ring and 𝔞◃𝐴 an ideal such that 𝐴 is 𝔞-adically complete (3.14.21). Let (𝑦𝑖)𝑖≥0 be a sequence in 𝐴 with 𝑦𝑖∈𝔞𝑖 for every 𝑖≥0. Then there is a unique 𝑠∈𝐴 – written 𝑠≕∑𝑖≥0𝑦𝑖 – with

𝑠≡∑𝑖=0𝑁𝑦𝑖mod𝔞𝑁+1for every𝑁≥0.

Proof. Drafted by Claude Sonnet 5, reviewed by David Rufino.

Set 𝑠𝑁≔∑𝑖=0𝑁𝑦𝑖∈𝐴 for 𝑁≥0. As 𝑠𝑁−𝑠𝑁−1=𝑦𝑁∈𝔞𝑁 for 𝑁≥1, (𝑠―𝑁)𝑁≥0, 𝑠―𝑁≔𝑠𝑁mod𝔞𝑁+1, is compatible under reduction; by (3.14.21) item b) (𝐴 being 𝔞-adically complete), there is 𝑠∈𝐴 with 𝑠≡𝑠𝑁mod𝔞𝑁+1 for every 𝑁≥0: existence. If 𝑠,𝑠′ both satisfy this, 𝑠−𝑠′∈⋂𝑁𝔞𝑁+1=0 (separated): uniqueness. ∎

Lemma 3.14.34 (Units of a Complete Ring)
Let 𝐴 be a ring and 𝔞◃𝐴 an ideal such that 𝐴 is 𝔞-adically complete (3.14.21). If 𝑢∈𝐴⋆ and 𝑥∈𝔞, then 𝑢+𝑥∈𝐴⋆.

Proof. Drafted by Claude Sonnet 5, reviewed by David Rufino.

As 𝑢+𝑥=𝑢(1+𝑢−1𝑥) and 𝑢−1𝑥∈𝔞 (𝔞 an ideal), it suffices to treat 𝑢=1: show 1+𝑦∈𝐴⋆ for 𝑦∈𝔞.

As (−𝑦)𝑖∈𝔞𝑖 for every 𝑖≥0, (3.14.33) gives 𝑠≔∑𝑖≥0(−𝑦)𝑖∈𝐴 with 𝑠≡𝑠𝑁≔∑𝑖=0𝑁(−𝑦)𝑖mod𝔞𝑁+1 for every 𝑁≥0.

For every 𝑁≥0: (1+𝑦)𝑠𝑁=∑𝑖=0𝑁(−𝑦)𝑖+∑𝑖=0𝑁𝑦(−𝑦)𝑖=∑𝑖=0𝑁(−𝑦)𝑖−∑𝑖=1𝑁+1(−𝑦)𝑖=1−(−𝑦)𝑁+1 (telescoping), and likewise 𝑠𝑁(1+𝑦)=1−(−𝑦)𝑁+1 (𝑦 commuting with its own powers); so

(1+𝑦)𝑠−1=(1+𝑦)(𝑠−𝑠𝑁)+((1+𝑦)𝑠𝑁−1)=(1+𝑦)(𝑠−𝑠𝑁)−(−𝑦)𝑁+1∈𝔞𝑁+1

(𝔞𝑁+1 an ideal, 𝑠−𝑠𝑁∈𝔞𝑁+1, (−𝑦)𝑁+1∈𝔞𝑁+1), and likewise 𝑠(1+𝑦)−1∈𝔞𝑁+1. As 𝑁≥0 was arbitrary, (1+𝑦)𝑠−1,𝑠(1+𝑦)−1∈⋂𝑁𝔞𝑁+1=0 (separated): 1+𝑦∈𝐴⋆, with inverse 𝑠. ∎

Lemma 3.14.35 (Hensel's Lemma)
Let 𝐴 be a ring and 𝔞◃𝐴 an ideal such that 𝐴 is 𝔞-adically complete (3.14.21). Let 𝑓∈𝐴[𝑋], with formal derivative 𝑓′∈𝐴[𝑋] (3.8.9) (writing 𝑓(𝑥), 𝑓′(𝑥) for their evaluations at 𝑥∈𝐴 (3.8.5)). If 𝑎0∈𝐴 has 𝑓(𝑎0)∈𝔞 and 𝑓′(𝑎0)∈𝐴⋆, then there is 𝑎∈𝐴 with 𝑓(𝑎)=0 and 𝑎≡𝑎0mod𝔞.

Proof. Drafted by Claude Sonnet 5, reviewed by David Rufino.

Taylor expansion. By (3.8.10), 𝑓(𝑎+ℎ)−𝑓(𝑎)−𝑓′(𝑎)ℎ∈(ℎ2) for every 𝑎,ℎ∈𝐴. In particular (taking 𝑎≔𝑦, ℎ≔𝑥−𝑦 for 𝑥,𝑦∈𝐴), 𝑓(𝑥)−𝑓(𝑦)∈(𝑥−𝑦).

Construction. Define inductively 𝑎𝑛+1≔𝑎𝑛−𝑓′(𝑎𝑛)−1𝑓(𝑎𝑛) for 𝑛≥0 (well-defined once 𝑓′(𝑎𝑛)∈𝐴⋆, shown below).

Claim: 𝑓(𝑎𝑛)∈𝔞2𝑛, 𝑓′(𝑎𝑛)∈𝐴⋆, and 𝑎𝑛+1≡𝑎𝑛mod𝔞2𝑛, for every 𝑛≥0. Base case 𝑛=0: given. Inductively, suppose 𝑓(𝑎𝑛)∈𝔞2𝑛≕𝔞𝑚 and 𝑓′(𝑎𝑛)∈𝐴⋆; write 𝑢𝑛≔𝑓′(𝑎𝑛)−1, ℎ𝑛≔−𝑢𝑛𝑓(𝑎𝑛)∈𝔞𝑚 (𝔞𝑚 an ideal), so 𝑎𝑛+1=𝑎𝑛+ℎ𝑛≡𝑎𝑛mod𝔞𝑚. By the Taylor expansion,

𝑓(𝑎𝑛+1)≡𝑓(𝑎𝑛)+𝑓′(𝑎𝑛)ℎ𝑛=𝑓(𝑎𝑛)−𝑓′(𝑎𝑛)𝑢𝑛𝑓(𝑎𝑛)=𝑓(𝑎𝑛)(1−𝑓′(𝑎𝑛)𝑢𝑛)=0mod(ℎ𝑛2)

(as 𝑓′(𝑎𝑛)𝑢𝑛=1 exactly), so 𝑓(𝑎𝑛+1)∈(ℎ𝑛2)⊆𝔞2𝑚=𝔞2𝑛+1 (ℎ𝑛∈𝔞𝑚, 𝔞𝑚 an ideal). As 𝑎𝑛+1≡𝑎𝑛mod𝔞𝑚⊆𝔞 (as 𝑚=2𝑛≥1), and (telescoping) 𝑎𝑛≡𝑎0mod𝔞 for every 𝑛, the Taylor expansion’s consequence 𝑓′(𝑎𝑛+1)−𝑓′(𝑎0)∈(𝑎𝑛+1−𝑎0)⊆𝔞 gives, by (3.14.34) (𝑢≔𝑓′(𝑎0)∈𝐴⋆, 𝑥≔𝑓′(𝑎𝑛+1)−𝑓′(𝑎0)∈𝔞), 𝑓′(𝑎𝑛+1)=𝑢+𝑥∈𝐴⋆. This completes the induction.

Existence of 𝑎. The sequence 𝑛𝑁≔2𝑁−1 is strictly increasing, and the Claim gives 𝑎𝑁≡𝑎𝑁−1mod𝔞2𝑁−1=𝔞𝑛𝑁−1+1 for every 𝑁≥1, so (𝑎―𝑁)𝑁≥0, 𝑎―𝑁≔𝑎𝑁mod𝔞𝑛𝑁+1, is compatible under reduction; by (3.14.21) item c), there is 𝑎∈𝐴 with 𝑎≡𝑎𝑁mod𝔞2𝑁 for every 𝑁≥0.

𝑎 is the desired root. For every 𝑁≥0: 𝑎−𝑎𝑁∈𝔞2𝑁, so, by the Taylor expansion’s consequence, 𝑓(𝑎)−𝑓(𝑎𝑁)∈(𝑎−𝑎𝑁)⊆𝔞2𝑁; as also 𝑓(𝑎𝑁)∈𝔞2𝑁 (the Claim), 𝑓(𝑎)∈𝔞2𝑁, for every 𝑁≥0. So 𝑓(𝑎)∈⋂𝑁𝔞2𝑁=⋂𝑛𝔞𝑛=0 (separated), giving 𝑓(𝑎)=0; and, at 𝑁=0, 𝑎≡𝑎0mod𝔞20=𝔞. ∎

Corollary 3.14.36 (Idempotent Lifting)
Let 𝐴 be a ring and 𝔞◃𝐴 an ideal such that 𝐴 is 𝔞-adically complete (3.14.21). Then every idempotent 𝑒―∈𝐴/𝔞 lifts to an idempotent 𝑒∈𝐴, that is 𝑒2=𝑒 and 𝑒≡𝑒―mod𝔞.

Proof. Drafted by Claude Sonnet 5, reviewed by David Rufino.

Pick 𝑎0∈𝐴 representing 𝑒―, so 𝑓(𝑎0)≔𝑎02−𝑎0∈𝔞 for 𝑓(𝑋)≔𝑋2−𝑋∈𝐴[𝑋], with 𝑓′(𝑋)=2𝑋−1 (3.8.9). As 𝑒―2=𝑒―, (2𝑎0−1)2=4𝑎02−4𝑎0+1=4(𝑎02−𝑎0)+1≡1mod𝔞 (writing 4(𝑎02−𝑎0)=4𝑓(𝑎0)∈𝔞), so 2𝑎0−1∈𝐴⋆ by (3.14.34) (applied with 𝑢≔1). So (3.14.35) gives 𝑒∈𝐴 with 𝑒2−𝑒=0 and 𝑒≡𝑎0≡𝑒―mod𝔞: the desired lift. ∎
