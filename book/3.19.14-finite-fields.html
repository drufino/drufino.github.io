3.19.14 Finite Fields

A finite field 𝐾 necessarily has positive characteristic 𝑝, and therefore the prime subfield is isomorphic to the field 𝔽𝑝≔ℤ/𝑝ℤ. We list some necessary properties of a finite field

Proposition 3.19.108 (Properties of finite fields)

Every finite field 𝐾 is a finite-dimensional vector space over its prime subfield 𝔽𝑝. Define 𝑛=[𝐾:𝔽𝑝].

  • #︎𝐾=𝑝𝑛
  • 𝐾 is a splitting field for 𝑋𝑝𝑛−𝑋∈𝔽𝑝[𝑋], and indeed is equal to the set of roots
  • The multiplicative group of units 𝐾⋆ is cyclic.
  • 𝐾/𝔽𝑝 is simple

Proof. Since 𝐾/𝔽𝑝 is a finite-dimensional vector space it must have order 𝑝𝑛.

The group of units has order 𝑝𝑛−1, so by Lagrange’s theorem every non-zero element satisfies 𝑋𝑝𝑛−1−1=0, so therefore every element satisfies 𝑋𝑝𝑛−𝑋=0. Since this polynomial can have at most 𝑝𝑛 roots (3.17.35) the roots are exactly all the elements of 𝐾.

We note again that 𝑋𝑑−1 has at most 𝑑 roots by (3.17.35). Therefore the fact 𝐾⋆ is cyclic follows from (3.2.25). ∎

Proposition 3.19.109 (Frobenius morphism)

Given any field 𝐾/𝔽𝑝 the Frobenius map

𝜙:𝑥→𝑥𝑝

is an injective field homomorphism. In particular when 𝐾 is finite (or even algebraic) it is an automorphism over 𝔽𝑝.

Proof. The only non-trivial step is showing

(𝑥+𝑦)𝑝=𝑥𝑝+𝑦𝑝

which follows from elementary calculations on binomial coefficients.

For the final statement use (3.19.35). ∎

Further we can show existence and uniqueness of finite fields.

Proposition 3.19.110 (Existence and uniqueness of finite fields)

Consider the algebraic closure 𝔽𝑝― and let 𝔽𝑝𝑛 denote the splitting field of 𝑓(𝑋)=𝑋𝑝𝑛−𝑋 in 𝔽𝑝―. Then

  • 𝔽𝑝𝑛 is equal to the set of roots of 𝑋𝑝𝑛−𝑋
  • It is the unique subfield of order 𝑝𝑛 and every finite field of order 𝑝𝑛 is isomorphic to this.
  • 𝔽𝑝𝑚⊂𝔽𝑝𝑛⟺𝑚∣𝑛

Proof. By (3.19.109) the set of roots of 𝑓(𝑋) forms a subfield of 𝔽𝑝―.

Furthermore 𝑓′(𝑋)=−1 so 𝑓(𝑋) is separable because clearly (𝑓,𝑓′)=1. Therefore by (3.19.11) 𝑓(𝑋) has 𝑝𝑛 distinct roots and the splitting field of 𝑓(𝑋) is exactly the set of roots.

Furthermore every subfield of order 𝑝𝑛 must satisfy this polynomial by Lagrange’s Theorem (3.2.12), so it is the unique such subfield.

Since every algebraic extension of 𝔽𝑝 is isomorphic to a subfield of 𝔽𝑝― it’s also the unique algebraic extension of order 𝑝𝑛 up to isomorphism.

Clearly if 𝔽𝑝𝑚⊂𝔽𝑝𝑛 then [𝔽𝑝𝑛:𝔽𝑝]=[𝔽𝑝𝑛:𝔽𝑝𝑚][𝔽𝑝𝑚:𝔽𝑝], so we must have 𝑚∣𝑛. Conversely if 𝛼∈𝔽𝑝𝑚 then 𝛼𝑝𝑚=𝛼⟹𝛼𝑝𝑟𝑚=𝛼 for all 𝑟>0, so 𝛼∈𝔽𝑝𝑛. ∎

It is usually most convenient to work in 𝔽𝑝― and consider the subfields of order 𝑝𝑛. We’ve seen in (3.19.102) that every finite field 𝔽𝑞≔𝔽𝑝𝑛 is perfect and therefore every algebraic extension is separable (3.19.103). In fact we may show that every finite extension is Galois.

Proposition 3.19.111

The field extension 𝔽𝑝𝑛/𝔽𝑝 is Galois with

Gal(𝔽𝑝𝑛/𝔽𝑝)=⟨𝜙⟩

cyclic of order 𝑛 generated by the Frobenius automorphism.

Proof. Let 𝐺=Aut(𝔽𝑝𝑛/𝔽𝑝). We’ve observed that 𝜙∈𝐺. Let 𝑑=𝑜(𝜙), then we wish to prove that 𝑛=𝑑. Certainly Lagrange’s Theorem applied to the multiplicative group 𝔽𝑝𝑛⋆ implies 𝜙𝑛=1. Therefore 𝑑∣𝑛 by (3.2.12) applied to 𝐺. By definition 𝜙𝑑=𝑒, so every 𝛼∈𝔽𝑝𝑛 satisfies the polynomial 𝑋𝑝𝑑−𝑋=0. This polynomial has at most 𝑝𝑑 roots (3.17.35) so we must have 𝑑≥𝑛, and therefore 𝑑=𝑛. Clearly 𝜙 generates a cyclic subgroup of order 𝑛. However by (3.19.107) 𝐺 has at most order 𝑛, whence 𝐺=⟨𝜙⟩ as required. Furthermore by the same result 𝔽𝑝𝑛/𝔽𝑝 is Galois. ∎
Proposition 3.19.112 (Subfields of 𝔽𝑝𝑛)

Consider the field extension 𝔽𝑝𝑛/𝔽𝑝. Then it has a unique subfield of order 𝑝𝑚 if 𝑚∣𝑛 (and no such subfield otherwise). In this case 𝔽𝑝𝑛/𝔽𝑝𝑚 is Galois and

Gal(𝔽𝑝𝑛/𝔽𝑝𝑚)=⟨𝜙𝑚⟩

and in particular has order 𝑛/𝑚.

Proof. We’ve already shown that 𝔽𝑝𝑛 has a unique subfield of order 𝑝𝑚, by assuming an embedding in 𝔽𝑝―. Let 𝐻=Aut(𝔽𝑝𝑛/𝔽𝑝𝑚). Note 𝜙𝑚 has order 𝑛/𝑚. Furthermore from (3.19.110) every element of 𝔽𝑝𝑚 satisfies 𝑋𝑝𝑚−𝑋. In other words 𝜙𝑚 fixes 𝔽𝑝𝑚 and 𝜙𝑚∈𝐻. Therefore ⟨𝜙𝑚⟩≤𝐻 and #︎𝐻≥𝑛/𝑚. By (3.19.107) #︎𝐻≤[𝔽𝑝𝑛:𝔽𝑝𝑚]=𝑛/𝑚, whence we have equality and so the extension is Galois by (3.19.107) and 𝐻=⟨𝜙𝑚⟩. ∎
Lemma 3.19.113

Let 𝑥∈𝔽𝑝𝑛. Then deg(𝑥)=𝑛⟺Fix(𝑥)={1}⊂Gal(𝔽𝑝𝑛/𝔽𝑝).

In other words Gal(𝔽𝑝𝑛/𝔽𝑝) acts freely on the elements of degree 𝑛.
