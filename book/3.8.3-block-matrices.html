3.8.3 Block Matrices

Proposition 3.8.33

Let 𝐴=ℤ[𝑋11,…,𝑋𝑛𝑛] be a polynomial ring in 𝑛2 indeterminates. Consider the matrix (𝑋𝑖𝑗)∈Mat𝑛×𝑛(𝐴) and let 𝐷𝑛 denote the determinant of this matrix.

Then for any commutative ring 𝐵 and matrix 𝑀∈Mat𝑛×𝑛(𝐵) we have

det(𝑀)=𝐷𝑛(𝑀11,…,𝑀1𝑛,𝑀21,…,𝑀𝑛𝑛)

where the right hand side corresponds to the obvious evaluation homomorphism 𝐴→𝐵.

Proof. Drafted by Claude Sonnet, reviewed by David Rufino.

Let ev:𝐴→𝐵 be the ring homomorphism with ev(𝑋𝑖𝑗)=𝑀𝑖𝑗, so that the right hand side is ev(𝐷𝑛). By the Leibniz Formula (3.8.19) applied over 𝐴 to the matrix (𝑋𝑖𝑗)

𝐷𝑛=∑𝜎∈𝑆𝑛𝜀(𝜎)∏𝑖=1𝑛𝑋𝑖𝜎(𝑖)

and since ev preserves sums and products

ev(𝐷𝑛)=∑𝜎∈𝑆𝑛𝜀(𝜎)∏𝑖=1𝑛𝑀𝑖𝜎(𝑖)=det(𝑀)

where the last equality is the Leibniz Formula again, now over 𝐵. ∎

Proposition 3.8.34 (Block Matrices)

Let 𝐴 be a commutative ring and 𝑛,𝑚≥1. The map

Φ:Mat𝑛×𝑛(Mat𝑚×𝑚(𝐴))⟶Mat(𝑛𝑚)×(𝑛𝑚)(𝐴)

sending (𝐸𝑖𝑗) to the matrix whose entry in row (𝑖−1)𝑚+𝑎 and column (𝑗−1)𝑚+𝑏 is (𝐸𝑖𝑗)𝑎𝑏 (for 1≤𝑎,𝑏≤𝑚) is an isomorphism of 𝐴-algebras. In particular the product of block matrices is computed blockwise, that is (𝐸𝐹)𝑖𝑗=∑𝑘=1𝑛𝐸𝑖𝑘𝐹𝑘𝑗, where the blocks are multiplied as 𝑚×𝑚 matrices.

Proof. The map Φ is clearly bijective and 𝐴-linear, and sends the identity to 𝐼𝑛𝑚. Writing the rows and columns of Φ(𝐸) as pairs (𝑖,𝑎), (𝑗,𝑏) we have

(Φ(𝐸)Φ(𝐹))(𝑖,𝑎),(𝑗,𝑏)=∑𝑘=1𝑛∑𝑐=1𝑚(𝐸𝑖𝑘)𝑎𝑐(𝐹𝑘𝑗)𝑐𝑏=∑𝑘=1𝑛(𝐸𝑖𝑘𝐹𝑘𝑗)𝑎𝑏

which is the (𝑎,𝑏) entry of the (𝑖,𝑗) block of Φ(𝐸𝐹). So Φ is multiplicative. ∎

Lemma 3.8.35 (Determinant of a Monic Matrix Polynomial)
Let 𝐴 be a commutative ring, 𝑘,𝑚≥1 and 𝐸∈Mat𝑚×𝑚(𝐴[𝑍]) of the form 𝐸=𝑍𝑘𝐼𝑚+𝐹, where every entry of 𝐹 is a polynomial of degree at most 𝑘−1. Then det(𝐸)∈𝐴[𝑍] is monic of degree 𝑚𝑘.
Proof. In the Leibniz Formula (3.8.19) det(𝐸)=∑𝜎∈𝑆𝑚𝜀(𝜎)∏𝑖𝐸𝑖𝜎(𝑖) the term for 𝜎=1 is ∏𝑖(𝑍𝑘+𝐹𝑖𝑖), a product of 𝑚 monic polynomials of degree 𝑘 and so monic of degree 𝑚𝑘. If 𝜎≠1 then 𝜎 moves at least two indices 𝑖, for which 𝐸𝑖𝜎(𝑖)=𝐹𝑖𝜎(𝑖) has degree at most 𝑘−1, while the remaining factors have degree at most 𝑘. So the term of 𝜎 has degree at most 𝑚𝑘−2, and the leading term of det(𝐸) is that of the identity term. ∎
Proposition 3.8.36 (Block Matrix Determinant)

Suppose 𝑀𝑖𝑗 (1≤𝑖≤𝑛,1≤𝑗≤𝑛) are 𝑚×𝑚 matrices with coefficients in a commutative ring 𝐴 and which commute pairwise. Form the matrix 𝑀 with coefficients in 𝐴 as follows

𝑀=(𝑀11…𝑀1𝑛⋮⋮𝑀𝑛1…𝑀𝑛𝑛)

Then

det(𝑀)=det(𝐷𝑛(𝑀11,…,𝑀1𝑛,𝑀21,…,𝑀𝑛𝑛))

Proof. By (3.8.34) we may identify Mat𝑛×𝑛(Mat𝑚×𝑚(𝐴)) with Mat(𝑛𝑚)×(𝑛𝑚)(𝐴), compatibly with matrix multiplication. We proceed by induction on 𝑛, for all commutative rings 𝐴 and all 𝑚 simultaneously. For 𝑛=1 there is nothing to show, as then 𝑀=𝑀11, so let 𝑛≥2.

Let 𝐵 denote the subring of Mat𝑚×𝑚(𝐴) generated by the matrices 𝑀𝑖𝑗. It is commutative, since its generators commute pairwise by hypothesis. Then we may regard 𝑀 as an element of Mat𝑛×𝑛(𝐵), and denote this by 𝑀̃ to disambiguate. As 𝐵 is commutative we may consider its determinant det(𝑀̃)∈𝐵, itself an 𝑚×𝑚 matrix over 𝐴 (so det(det(𝑀̃))∈𝐴 makes sense), and its adjugate matrix 𝑀̃ad. Further consider the matrix 𝑀′ given in block form by

𝑀′=((𝑀̃ad)110…0(𝑀̃ad)21𝐼𝑚…0⋮⋮⋱⋮(𝑀̃ad)𝑛10…𝐼𝑚)

Recall by (3.8.24) and (3.8.33) that

𝑀̃11ad=𝐷𝑛−1(𝑀22,…,𝑀2𝑛,𝑀31,…,𝑀𝑛𝑛)

and

𝑀̃𝑀̃ad=(det(𝑀̃)…0⋮⋱⋮0…det(𝑀̃))

as elements of Mat𝑛×𝑛(𝐵). Here, and in 𝐷𝑛−1 and 𝐷𝑛 below, the order of the factors in each product of the 𝑀𝑖𝑗 is immaterial, as they commute pairwise. Therefore computing the matrix product block-wise we find

𝑀𝑀′=(det(𝑀̃)𝑀12…𝑀1𝑛0𝑀22…𝑀2𝑛⋮⋮⋮0𝑀𝑛2…𝑀𝑛𝑛)

Then 𝑀𝑀′ is block upper triangular with first diagonal block det(𝑀̃), so by (3.8.21) and multiplicativity of the determinant (3.8.20)

det(𝑀)⋅det(𝑀′)=det(𝑀𝑀′)=det(det(𝑀̃))⋅det(𝑀[𝑚+1,𝑚𝑛,𝑚+1,𝑚𝑛])

By induction on 𝑛 we may then assume det(𝑀[𝑚+1,𝑚𝑛,𝑚+1,𝑚𝑛])=det(𝐷𝑛−1(𝑀22,…,𝑀2𝑛,…,𝑀𝑛𝑛))=det((𝑀̃ad)11). Similarly 𝑀′ is block lower triangular with first diagonal block (𝑀̃ad)11 and the others identities, so det(𝑀′)=det((𝑀̃ad)11) by (3.8.21). When this is not a zero-divisor in 𝐴 then we conclude that

det(𝑀)=det(det(𝑀̃))=det(𝐷𝑛(𝑀11,…,𝑀1𝑛,…,𝑀𝑛𝑛))

as required.

In order to handle the case where det((𝑀̃ad)11) is a zero divisor we may replace the ring 𝐴 with the polynomial ring 𝐴[𝑍] and consider the matrix 𝑁 for which

𝑁̃=𝑀̃+(𝑍⋅𝐼𝑚)⋅𝐼𝑛.

Then the blocks 𝑁𝑖𝑗=𝑀𝑖𝑗+𝛿𝑖𝑗𝑍𝐼𝑚 still commute pairwise, as 𝑍𝐼𝑚 is central. Moreover (𝑁̃ad)11=𝐷𝑛−1(𝑁22,…,𝑁𝑛𝑛)=∑𝜏∈𝑆𝑛−1𝜀(𝜏)∏𝑖=2𝑛𝑁𝑖𝜏(𝑖). The term for 𝜏=1 is ∏𝑖=2𝑛(𝑀𝑖𝑖+𝑍𝐼𝑚)=𝑍𝑛−1𝐼𝑚+(terms of degree≤𝑛−2), while for 𝜏≠1 only the indices fixed by 𝜏 contribute a factor of degree one in 𝑍 (as 𝑁𝑖𝜏(𝑖)=𝑀𝑖𝜏(𝑖) for 𝜏(𝑖)≠𝑖), so the term has degree at most 𝑛−3. Hence (𝑁̃ad)11=𝑍𝑛−1𝐼𝑚+𝐹 with every entry of 𝐹 of degree at most 𝑛−2, and as 𝑛−1≥1 its determinant is monic (3.8.35), and therefore not a zero-divisor of 𝐴[𝑍] (3.7.4). We conclude from the previous case that

det(𝑁)=det(𝐷𝑛(𝑁11,…,𝑁1𝑛,…,𝑁𝑛𝑛))

Finally we may apply the ring homomorphism 𝐴[𝑍]→𝐴 (evaluation at 0) to deduce the general case. ∎
