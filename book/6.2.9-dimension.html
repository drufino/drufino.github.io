6.2.9 Dimension

Definition 6.2.53 (Dimension of a Variety)
Let 𝑋 be a variety. Then we define the dimension to be the Krull Dimension of 𝑋 as a topological space.
Proposition 6.2.54

Let 𝑋 be an integral variety. Then it is biequidimensional and

dim𝑋=trdeg𝑘(𝑘(𝑋))

The dimension equals dim𝑈 for every open subset.

Proof. Let 𝑈 be an affine open subset then by (6.1.52) dim𝑈=trdeg𝑘(𝑘(𝑈)) which equals trdeg𝑘(𝑘(𝑋)) by (4.3.16). Further by (6.1.51) 𝑈 is biequidimensional and so by (4.1.73) we conclude that 𝑋 is biequidimensional with the same dimension as 𝑈.

Every open subset can be covered by affines so we are done by (4.1.88). ∎

Corollary 6.2.55

Let 𝑋 be a variety. Then it is quasi-biequidimensional. Moreover every closed subset 𝑊⊂𝑋 satisfies the codimension formula

dim𝑋=dim𝑊+codim(𝑊,𝑋)

If 𝑊 is irreducible then we also have the identity

dim𝒪︀𝑋,𝑊=codim(𝑊,𝑋)=dim𝑋−dim𝑊

Proof. Every irreducible component is an integral closed subvariety and therefore biequidimensional (6.2.54). So by definition 𝑋 is quasi-biequidimensional. The codimension formula is (4.1.87).

Let 𝑈 be an affine open subset of 𝑋 meeting 𝑊. Then there is an isomorphism 𝒪︀𝑋,𝑊→∼𝒪︀𝑈,𝑊∩𝑈 (4.3.17). Further by (4.1.73) we have codim(𝑊,𝑋)=codim(𝑊∩𝑈,𝑈). Therefore we may deduce from the affine case (6.1.57) that

dim𝒪︀𝑋,𝑊=dim𝒪︀𝑈,𝑊∩𝑈=codim(𝑊∩𝑈,𝑈)=codim(𝑊,𝑋)

as required. ∎

Proposition 6.2.56 (Irreducible Closed Subsets of Dimension 0)

Let 𝑋 be a variety and 𝑍⊂𝑋 an irreducible closed subset. Then the following are equivalent

  1. dim𝑍=0
  2. 𝑍={𝑧}― for some 𝑧∈𝑍
  3. 𝑍 is finite

In particular for every 𝑥∈𝑋 we have dim𝒪︀𝑋,𝑥=dim𝑋.

Proof. Follows from (6.2.12). The dimension formula follows from (6.2.55). ∎
Proposition 6.2.57

Let 𝑋 be a variety. Then there is a bijective correspondence

𝑋0⟷{𝑊⊂𝑍|𝑊closed and irreducible anddim𝑊=0}
Proof. By (6.2.11) and (4.1.94). ∎
