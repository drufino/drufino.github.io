3.4.3 Extensions of Scalars

We observe that for a ring homomorphism 𝜑:𝐴→𝐵 we have on 𝐵 a natural (𝐴,𝐵)-bimodule and (𝐵,𝐴)-bimodule structure. Using the tensor product then we may use this to extend the coefficients from 𝐴 to 𝐵. We first prove some elementary lemmas

Lemma 3.4.14 (Unit Hom)

Let 𝜑:𝐵→𝐶 be a ring homomorphism and 𝑍 a (𝐴,𝐶)-bimodule. Then there is a natural isomorphism of (𝐴,𝐵)-bimodules

RHom(𝐶𝐶𝐵,𝑍𝐶𝐴)≅𝑍𝐵𝐴𝜃→𝜃(1)𝑧(−)←𝑧

and similarly let 𝜑:𝐴→𝐵 be a ring homomorphism and 𝑌 a (𝐵,𝐶)-bimodule then there is a natural isomorphism of (𝐴,𝐶)-bimodules

LHom(𝐵𝐴𝐵,𝑌𝐶𝐵)≅𝑌𝐶𝐴𝜃→𝜃(1)

Proof. Observe

(𝑎𝜃𝑏)(1)=𝑎𝜃(𝜑(𝑏))=𝑎𝜃(1)𝜑(𝑏)

so the left to right map is an (𝐴,𝐵)-bimodule homomorphism. Further 𝜃(𝑐)=𝜃(1)𝑐 so the maps are mutual inverses. ∎

Proposition 3.4.16 (Extension of Scalars)

Let 𝑀 be an (𝐴,𝐶)-bimodule and 𝜑:𝐴→𝐵 a ring homomorphism. We may define a (𝐵,𝐶)-module

𝑀(𝐵)≔𝐵𝐴𝐵⊗𝐴𝑀

For 𝑍 a (𝐵,𝐶)-bimodule there is a natural isomorphism of abelian groups

Hom(𝑀(𝐵),𝑍)→∼Hom(𝑀,𝑍𝐶𝐴)𝜓→𝑚→𝜓(1⊗𝑚)𝑏⊗𝑚→𝑏⋅𝜙(𝑚)←𝜙

In otherwords we have an adjunction (2.6.69)

𝐌𝐨𝐝𝐶𝐴⟷𝐌𝐨𝐝𝐶𝐵

Setting 𝐶=ℤ yields an adjunction for left modules

𝐌𝐨𝐝𝐴⟷𝐌𝐨𝐝𝐵

In particular for 𝑀 a left 𝐴-module we have an isomorphism of abelian groups

Hom(𝑀(𝐵),𝐵)⟶∼Hom𝐴(𝑀,𝐵)

which is 𝐵-linear when 𝐵 is commutative.

Proof. By the tensor-hom adjunction (3.4.12) and (3.4.14) there is a natural isomorphism

Hom(𝐵⊗𝐴𝑀,𝑍)≅Hom(𝑀,LHom(𝐵𝐴𝐵,𝑍𝐶𝐵))≅Hom(𝑀,𝑍𝐶𝐴)

 ∎

Proposition 3.4.17

Let 𝜑:𝐴→𝐵 be a homomormorphism of commutative rings and 𝜑:𝑀→𝑁 a homomorphism of 𝐴-modules. Then there is a unique 𝐵-module homomorphism 𝜑(𝐵):𝑀(𝐵)→𝑁(𝐵) such that

𝜑(𝐵)(1⊗𝑚)=1⊗𝜑(𝑚)
Proof. The homomorphism 𝜑(𝐵)=1𝐵⊗𝜓 exists by functoriality of the tensor product (3.4.4). It clearly satisfies the given property, and as the tensor product is generated by elementary tensors it is evidentally unique. ∎
Proposition 3.4.18 (Tensor Unit)

Let 𝑀 be an (𝐴,𝐶)-bimodule then there is a natural isomorphism of (𝐴,𝐶)-bimodules

𝐴⊗𝐴𝑀≅𝑀𝑎⊗𝑚→𝑎⋅𝑚1⊗𝑚←𝑚

Similarly there is a natural isomorphism of (𝐴,𝐶)-bimodules

𝑀⊗𝐶𝐶≅𝑀

Proof. By (3.4.16) with 𝜙=1𝐴 there is a natural isomorphism

Hom(𝐴⊗𝐴𝑀,−)≅Hom(𝑀,−)

and so natural isomorphism follows from (2.6.68).

We may deduce that there is a natural isomorphism of (𝐶op,𝐴op)-bimodules

𝐶op⊗𝐶op𝑀op≅𝑀op

which amounts to a natural isomorphism of (𝐴,𝐶)-bimodules

𝑀⊗𝐶𝐶≅𝑀

 ∎

Proposition 3.4.19 (Transitivity of Extension of Scalars)

Let 𝑀 be a (𝐴,𝐷)-bimodule and 𝜑:𝐴→𝐵, 𝜓:𝐵→𝐶 homomorphisms of commutative rings. Then there is an isomorphism of (𝐶,𝐷)-bimodules

𝐶⊗𝐵(𝐵⊗𝐴𝑀)→∼𝐶𝐴𝐶⊗𝐴𝑀𝑐⊗(𝑏⊗𝑚)→(𝑐𝜓(𝑏))⊗𝑚

Proof. By associativity (3.4.13) and (3.4.18) there is a natural isomorphism

𝐶⊗𝐵(𝐵⊗𝐴𝑀)≅(𝐶⊗𝐵𝐵)⊗𝐴𝑀≅𝐶⊗𝐴𝑀

 ∎

Proposition 3.4.20 (Transitivity of Extension of Scalars (Commutative Case))

Let 𝑀 be an 𝐴-module and 𝜙:𝐴→𝐵 and 𝜓:𝐵→𝐶 homomorphisms of commutative rings. Then there is an isomorphism of 𝐶-modules

𝐶⊗𝐵(𝐵⊗𝐴𝑀)≅𝐶𝐴𝐶⊗𝐴𝑀
Proof. Regarding 𝑀 as an (𝐴,𝐴)-bimodule we have an isomorphism of (𝐶,𝐴)-bimodules by (3.4.19) which is a-fortiori a 𝐶-module isomorphism. ∎
