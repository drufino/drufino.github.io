5.2 Complex Numbers

Proposition 5.2.1 (Existence of Complex Numbers)

Let 𝐾 be a field for which −1 has no square root (and in particular char(𝐾)≠2). Then

𝐾[𝑖]≔𝐾[𝑋]/(𝑋2+1)

is a field with a 𝐾-basis {1,𝑖} such that 𝑖2=−1. Multiplication in 𝐾[𝑖] is defined as follows

(𝑎+𝑏𝑖)(𝑐+𝑑𝑖)=(𝑎𝑐−𝑏𝑑)+(𝑏𝑐+𝑎𝑑)𝑖

and the inverse is given by

(𝑎+𝑏𝑖)−1=𝑎−𝑏𝑖𝑎2+𝑏2

The field extension 𝐾(𝑖)/𝐾 is Galois of degree two with

Gal(𝐾(𝑖)/𝐾)={1,⋅―}

where ⋅― is the complex conjugation automorphism given by

𝑎+𝑏𝑖―=𝑎−𝑏𝑖

In particular we see that ⋅― satisfies the following properties

𝛼+𝛽―=𝛼―+𝛽―𝛼𝛽―=𝛼―𝛽―

Proof. The polynomial 𝑋2+1 is of degree 2 so it is irreducible if and only if has no root. Therefore the quotient ring is a field by (3.19.40). ℝ satisfies this property by (5.1.5), so ℂ is a field.

Observe that as polynomials in 𝐾[𝑋]

(𝑎+𝑏𝑋)(𝑐+𝑑𝑋)=𝑎𝑐+(𝑏𝑐+𝑎𝑑)𝑋+𝑏𝑑𝑋2=(𝑎𝑐−𝑏𝑑)+(𝑏𝑐+𝑎𝑑)𝑋+𝑏𝑑(𝑋2+1)

from which the multiplication identity follows immediately.

For the inverse we claim that 𝑎≠0or𝑏≠0⟹𝑎2+𝑏2≠0. For otherwise either 𝑎/𝑏 or 𝑏/𝑎 would be a square root of −1. Therefore the expression for the inverse is well-defined and it may be verified directly to be an inverse.

The complex conjugation operator exists by (3.19.49). Therefore Aut(𝐾(𝑖)/𝐾) has order at least [𝐾(𝑖):𝐾]=2, and we deduce 𝐾(𝑖)/𝐾 is Galois (3.19.107). ∎

Proposition 5.2.4 (Complex Numbers are Complete)
ℂ is complete as a normed vector space.

Proof. Let (𝛼𝑛) be a cauchy sequence in ℂ, and consider the decomposition into real and imaginary parts

𝛼𝑛=𝑎𝑛+𝑏𝑛𝑖

Then

|𝑎𝑛|≤|𝛼𝑛||𝑏𝑛|≤|𝛼𝑛|

Therefore we see both (𝑎𝑛) and (𝑏𝑛) are cauchy sequences of reals, which therefore have limits 𝑎 and 𝑏 respectively. We may verify directly that 𝛼𝑛→𝛼 where 𝛼≔𝑎+𝑏𝑖. ∎
