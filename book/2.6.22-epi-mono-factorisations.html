2.6.22 Epi-Mono Factorisations

We saw that under mild conditions image and coimage yield an epi-mono factorisation. Strengthening this factorisation can ensure functorial properties; concretely, in an algebraic category, upgrading an epimorphism to a regular epimorphism is exactly upgrading from the (possibly too weak) categorical notion of epimorphism to the familiar notion of a surjective homomorphism (2.6.195).

Definition 2.6.173

Let 𝒞︀ be a category and 𝑓:𝑋→𝑌 a morphism. We say that 𝑓=𝑚∘𝑒 is

  • a regular epi-mono factorisation if 𝑚 is a monomorphism and 𝑒 is a regular epimorphism.
  • an epi-regular mono factorisation if 𝑚 is a regular monomorphism and 𝑒 is an epimorphism

We note that 𝑓=𝑚∘𝑒 is a regular epi-mono (resp. epi-regular mono) factorisation if and only if 𝑓op=𝑒op∘𝑚op is an epi-regular mono (resp. regular epi-mono) factorisation.

Proposition 2.6.174 (Regular Epi-Mono Factorisation)

Let 𝑓:𝑋→𝑌 be a morphism with regular epi-mono factorisation 𝑓=𝑚∘𝑒. Then (𝐼,𝑒,𝑚) is an image and every image is a regular epi-mono factorisation.

Dually, let 𝑓:𝑋→𝑌 be a morphism with epi-regular mono factorisation 𝑓=𝑚∘𝑒. Then (𝐼,𝑒,𝑚) is a coimage and every coimage is an epi-regular mono factorisation.

Proposition 2.6.175 (Regular Epi-Mono)
𝐒𝐞𝐭 has regular epi-mono factorisations.

Proof. Drafted by Claude Sonnet 5, reviewed by David Rufino.

Let 𝑓:𝑋→𝑌 be a morphism in 𝐒𝐞𝐭. Set Im(𝑓)≔{𝑓(𝑥)|𝑥∈𝑋} and let 𝑚:Im(𝑓)↪︎𝑌 denote the inclusion, which is injective and therefore monic by (2.6.38). Let 𝑒:𝑋→Im(𝑓) be the corestriction of 𝑓, so that 𝑓=𝑚∘𝑒.

Define 𝑅={(𝑥,𝑥′)∈𝑋×𝑋|𝑓(𝑥)=𝑓(𝑥′)} with projections 𝛼,𝛽:𝑅⇉𝑋, 𝛼(𝑥,𝑥′)=𝑥, 𝛽(𝑥,𝑥′)=𝑥′. We claim 𝑒 is the coequaliser of (𝛼,𝛽).

First 𝑒∘𝛼=𝑒∘𝛽: for (𝑥,𝑥′)∈𝑅 we have 𝑓(𝑥)=𝑓(𝑥′) by definition of 𝑅, that is 𝑒(𝑥)=𝑒(𝑥′).

Now let 𝜋′:𝑋→𝑍 satisfy 𝜋′∘𝛼=𝜋′∘𝛽, that is 𝑓(𝑥)=𝑓(𝑥′)⟹𝜋′(𝑥)=𝜋′(𝑥′) for all 𝑥,𝑥′∈𝑋. Since every 𝑦∈Im(𝑓) is of the form 𝑓(𝑥) for some 𝑥∈𝑋, we may define 𝜓:Im(𝑓)→𝑍 by 𝜓(𝑦)=𝜋′(𝑥) for any such 𝑥; this is well-defined precisely because 𝑓(𝑥)=𝑓(𝑥′)=𝑦 implies 𝜋′(𝑥)=𝜋′(𝑥′). By construction 𝜓∘𝑒=𝜋′, and since 𝑒 is surjective onto Im(𝑓) by definition, any 𝜓′ with 𝜓′∘𝑒=𝜋′ agrees with 𝜓 at every point of Im(𝑓), so 𝜓 is the unique such factorisation.

Hence 𝑒 is a coequaliser of (𝛼,𝛽), so is a regular epimorphism (2.6.158). ∎

Lemma 2.6.176 (Diagonal Fill-In Lemma)

Let 𝑒:𝑋→𝐼 be a regular epimorphism and 𝑚′:𝐼′→𝑌 a monomorphism. Then for every commutative square

there is a unique 𝜃 such that 𝜃∘𝑒=𝑎. Furthermore it satisfies 𝑚′∘𝜃=𝑚.

Proof. Drafted by Claude Sonnet 5, reviewed by David Rufino.

Since 𝑒 is a regular epimorphism, by (2.6.158) there are morphisms 𝛼,𝛽:𝑊⇉𝑋 such that 𝑒 is the coequaliser of (𝛼,𝛽).

We have 𝑒∘𝛼=𝑒∘𝛽, so 𝑚∘𝑒∘𝛼=𝑚∘𝑒∘𝛽, whence by commutativity of the square 𝑚′∘𝑎∘𝛼=𝑚′∘𝑎∘𝛽. As 𝑚′ is monic we may cancel it to get 𝑎∘𝛼=𝑎∘𝛽.

By the universal property of the coequaliser 𝑒 there is therefore a unique morphism 𝜃:𝐼→𝐼′ such that

𝜃∘𝑒=𝑎

We check that 𝜃 also satisfies the remaining identity: 𝑚′∘𝜃∘𝑒=𝑚′∘𝑎=𝑚∘𝑒, and since 𝑒 is an epimorphism (2.6.135) we may cancel it to get

𝑚′∘𝜃=𝑚

 ∎

Proposition 2.6.177 (Regular Epi-Mono Factorisation is Functorial)

Suppose 𝒞︀ has regular epi-mono factorisations 𝑓=𝑚∘𝑒:𝑋→𝑌, 𝑓′=𝑚′∘𝑒′:𝑋′→𝑌′ and morphisms 𝑎:𝑋→𝑋′, 𝑏:𝑌→𝑌′ such that 𝑏∘𝑓=𝑓′∘𝑎. Then there is a unique morphism 𝜓𝑎,𝑏:𝐼→𝐼′ which makes the top square commute

Furthermore the whole diagram commutes and the construction is functorial: 𝜓1𝑋,1𝑌=1𝐼 (taking 𝑓′=𝑓, 𝑎=1𝑋, 𝑏=1𝑌), and given a further regular epi-mono factorisation 𝑓″=𝑚″∘𝑒″:𝑋″→𝑌″ and morphisms 𝑐:𝑋′→𝑋″, 𝑑:𝑌′→𝑌″ such that 𝑑∘𝑓′=𝑓″∘𝑐 (with induced comparison morphism 𝜓𝑐,𝑑:𝐼′→𝐼″), we have 𝜓𝑐,𝑑∘𝜓𝑎,𝑏=𝜓𝑐∘𝑎,𝑑∘𝑏.

Proof. Drafted by Claude Sonnet 5, reviewed by David Rufino.

Since 𝑒 is a regular epimorphism, by (2.6.158) there are morphisms 𝛼,𝛽:𝑊⇉𝑋 such that 𝑒 is the coequaliser of (𝛼,𝛽).

We first show 𝑒′∘𝑎 coequalises (𝛼,𝛽). Since 𝑒∘𝛼=𝑒∘𝛽 we have 𝑓∘𝛼=𝑚∘𝑒∘𝛼=𝑚∘𝑒∘𝛽=𝑓∘𝛽, and so by hypothesis 𝑓′∘𝑎∘𝛼=𝑏∘𝑓∘𝛼=𝑏∘𝑓∘𝛽=𝑓′∘𝑎∘𝛽, that is 𝑚′∘𝑒′∘𝑎∘𝛼=𝑚′∘𝑒′∘𝑎∘𝛽. As 𝑚′ is monic we may cancel it to get (𝑒′∘𝑎)∘𝛼=(𝑒′∘𝑎)∘𝛽.

By the universal property of the coequaliser 𝑒 there is therefore a unique morphism 𝜓𝑎,𝑏:𝐼→𝐼′ such that

𝜓𝑎,𝑏∘𝑒=𝑒′∘𝑎

We check that 𝜓𝑎,𝑏 also satisfies the second required identity: 𝑚′∘𝜓𝑎,𝑏∘𝑒=𝑚′∘𝑒′∘𝑎=𝑓′∘𝑎=𝑏∘𝑓=𝑏∘𝑚∘𝑒, and since 𝑒 is an epimorphism (2.6.135) we may cancel it to get

𝑚′∘𝜓𝑎,𝑏=𝑏∘𝑚

so the diagram commutes.

Taking 𝑓′=𝑓, 𝑎=1𝑋, 𝑏=1𝑌 (a valid compatible pair since 1𝑌∘𝑓=𝑓=𝑓∘1𝑋), 1𝐼 satisfies 1𝐼∘𝑒=𝑒=𝑒∘1𝑋 and 𝑚∘1𝐼=𝑚=1𝑌∘𝑚, so by the uniqueness established above 𝜓1𝑋,1𝑌=1𝐼.

For the composition law, let 𝑐:𝑋′→𝑋″, 𝑑:𝑌′→𝑌″ satisfy 𝑑∘𝑓′=𝑓″∘𝑐, with induced comparison morphism 𝜓𝑐,𝑑:𝐼′→𝐼″. Then

(𝜓𝑐,𝑑∘𝜓𝑎,𝑏)∘𝑒=𝜓𝑐,𝑑∘(𝑒′∘𝑎)=(𝜓𝑐,𝑑∘𝑒′)∘𝑎=𝑒″∘𝑐∘𝑎=𝑒″∘(𝑐∘𝑎)

Note 𝑑∘𝑏∘𝑓=𝑑∘𝑓′∘𝑎=𝑓″∘𝑐∘𝑎=𝑓″∘(𝑐∘𝑎), so (𝑐∘𝑎,𝑑∘𝑏) is again a compatible pair, with induced comparison morphism 𝜓𝑐∘𝑎,𝑑∘𝑏:𝐼→𝐼″ satisfying 𝜓𝑐∘𝑎,𝑑∘𝑏∘𝑒=𝑒″∘(𝑐∘𝑎) by definition. Since both 𝜓𝑐,𝑑∘𝜓𝑎,𝑏 and 𝜓𝑐∘𝑎,𝑑∘𝑏 satisfy this same equation, and 𝜓𝑐∘𝑎,𝑑∘𝑏 is, by the uniqueness established above, the unique morphism 𝐼→𝐼″ doing so, we conclude

𝜓𝑐,𝑑∘𝜓𝑎,𝑏=𝜓𝑐∘𝑎,𝑑∘𝑏

 ∎

Proposition 2.6.178 (Regular Epi-Mono Factorisation is Unique)
Let 𝑓=𝑚∘𝑒=𝑚′∘𝑒′ be two regular epi-mono factorisations, with 𝑒:𝑋→𝐼, 𝑒′:𝑋→𝐼′. Then there is a unique morphism 𝜃:𝐼→𝐼′ such that 𝑒′=𝜃∘𝑒. Furthermore it is an isomorphism and satisfies 𝑚′∘𝜃=𝑚.

Proof. Drafted by Claude Sonnet 5, reviewed by David Rufino.

Applying (2.6.177) to the factorisations (𝐼,𝑒,𝑚) and (𝐼′,𝑒′,𝑚′) of 𝑓, with 𝑓′=𝑓, 𝑎=1𝑋, 𝑏=1𝑌, gives a unique morphism 𝜃:𝐼→𝐼′ such that 𝜃∘𝑒=𝑒′ and 𝑚′∘𝜃=𝑚.

Symmetrically, applying (2.6.177) to the factorisations (𝐼′,𝑒′,𝑚′) and (𝐼,𝑒,𝑚) of 𝑓 (i.e. with the roles of the two factorisations swapped) gives a unique morphism 𝜃′:𝐼′→𝐼 such that 𝜃′∘𝑒′=𝑒 and 𝑚∘𝜃′=𝑚′.

By the composition law of (2.6.177), applied to the chain of factorisations (𝐼,𝑒,𝑚)→(𝐼′,𝑒′,𝑚′)→(𝐼,𝑒,𝑚) (i.e. 𝑓′=𝑓, 𝑓″=𝑓, with 𝑐=1𝑋, 𝑑=1𝑌, and 𝜃, 𝜃′ as the two successive comparison morphisms), 𝜃′∘𝜃 is the comparison morphism from (𝐼,𝑒,𝑚) to itself, and so equals 1𝐼 by the identity law of (2.6.177).

Symmetrically 𝜃∘𝜃′=1𝐼′, so 𝜃 is an isomorphism with inverse 𝜃′. ∎
