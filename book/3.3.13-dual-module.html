3.3.13 Dual Module

Proposition 3.3.108 (Dual Module)

Let 𝑀 be a left (resp. right) 𝐴-module. Then the set

𝑀∨≔Hom𝐴(𝑀,𝐴)

is canonically a right (resp. left) 𝐴-module.

If 𝑀 is a finite free left (resp. right) 𝐴-module with basis {𝑣1,…,𝑣𝑛} then 𝑀∨ is a finite free right (resp. left) 𝐴-module with basis {𝑣1∨,…,𝑣𝑛∨} where these are the unique homomorphisms satisfying

𝑣𝑖∨(𝑣𝑗)=𝛿𝑖𝑗

Moreover every basis of 𝑀∨ is of this form.

Proposition 3.3.109 (Hom-Set is free)

Let 𝑀 be a finite free left 𝐴-module with basis {𝑣1,…,𝑣𝑛} and 𝑁 a (𝐴,𝐵)-bimodule. Then there is an isomorphism of right 𝐵-modules

Hom𝐴(𝑀,𝑁)≅⨁𝑖=1𝑛𝑁𝜃→(𝜃(𝑣𝑖))𝑖∈𝐼

If 𝐴 is commutative and 𝑁 is a finite-free 𝐴-module with basis {𝑤1,…,𝑤𝑚} then there is further an isomorphism of 𝐴-modules

Hom𝐴(𝑀,𝑁)≅⨁𝑖,𝑗=1𝑛,𝑚𝐴𝜃→(𝑤𝑗∨(𝜃(𝑣𝑖)))𝑖,𝑗

where 𝑤1∨,…,𝑤𝑚∨ is the dual basis for 𝑁∨. In particular Hom𝐴(𝑀,𝑁) is a finite-free 𝐴-module with basis

{𝑤𝑗𝑣𝑖∨}𝑖,𝑗
Definition 3.3.110 (Dual Functor)

Let 𝐴 be a commutative ring and 𝜑:𝑀→𝑁 an 𝐴-module homomorphism. Define the dual homomorphism 𝜑∨:𝑁∨→𝑀∨ by

𝜑∨(𝜓)≔𝜓∘𝜑

This determines a contravariant functor

Corollary 3.3.111 (Double Dual Natural Isomorphism)

Let 𝐴 be a commutative ring and 𝑀 a finite free 𝐴-module then the canonical 𝐴-module homomorphism

𝜂:𝑀⟶𝑀∨∨𝑥→(𝜑→𝜑(𝑥))

is an isomorphism, which is natural in 𝑀.
