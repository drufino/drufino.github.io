3.19.7 Normal Extensions

Recall that an algebraic extension 𝐾/𝑘 is normal if all minimal polynomials split completely. They are in some sense “closed”. Furthermore 𝑘―/𝑘 is clearly normal and results about 𝑘― can often be generalized to normal fields 𝐿/𝑘. We also show that an extension is normal iff it is a splitting field.

Lemma 3.19.79

Let 𝐿/𝐾/𝑘 be a tower of algebraic extensions and 𝛼∈𝐿. If 𝑚𝛼,𝑘(𝑋) splits completely in 𝐿 so does 𝑚𝛼,𝐾(𝑋). In particular

𝐿/𝑘normal⟹𝐿/𝐾normal
Proof. Note 𝑚𝛼,𝐾(𝑋)|𝑚𝛼,𝑘𝑖𝑘𝐾(𝑋) as elements of 𝐾[𝑋] by (3.19.50). Apply 𝑖𝐾𝐿 and then we may use (3.19.19). ∎
Proposition 3.19.80 (Conjugate elements in Normal Extensions)

Let 𝐿/𝑘 be a normal extension (e.g. 𝐿=𝑘―) and 𝛼,𝛽∈𝐿 elements with the same minimal polynomial 𝑚𝛼(𝑋)=𝑚𝛽(𝑋). Then there exists 𝜎∈Aut(𝐿/𝑘) such that

𝜎(𝛼)=𝛽
Proof. Apply (3.19.64) with 𝐾=𝐿. ∎
Proposition 3.19.81 (Normal Criteria)

Let 𝐿/𝐾/𝑘 be a tower of extensions such that 𝐿/𝑘 is normal (e.g. 𝐿=𝑘―). Then the following are equivalent

  • NOR1: For any 𝜎∈Mor𝑘(𝐾,𝐿) we have 𝜎(𝐾)=𝑖𝐾𝐿(𝐾).
  • NOR2: 𝐾/𝑘 is the splitting field of some family of polynomials 𝑓𝑖∈𝑘[𝑋].
  • NOR3: 𝐾/𝑘 is normal

Proof. Clearly 3⟹2, for 𝐾 is the splitting field of all the minimal polynomials of elements in 𝐾.

2⟹1. This is (3.19.70).

1⟹3. Consider any 𝛼∈𝐾 with minimal polynomial 𝑚𝛼,𝑘(𝑋). By definition 𝑚𝛼,𝑘(𝑋) splits completely in 𝐿 because it has a root 𝛼1=𝑖𝐾𝐿(𝛼). Denote the roots by 𝛼1,…,𝛼𝑟. By (3.19.80) there is 𝜎𝑗∈Aut(𝐿/𝑘) such that 𝜎𝑗(𝛼1)=𝛼𝑗. By hypothesis we have 𝛼𝑗∈(𝜎𝑗∘𝑖𝐾𝐿)(𝐾)=𝑖𝐾𝐿(𝐾) whence there exists 𝛼𝑗′∈𝐾 such that 𝑖𝐾𝐿(𝛼𝑗′)=𝛼𝑗. By (3.19.69) 𝑚𝛼,𝑘(𝑋) splits completely in 𝐾. Therefore 𝐾/𝑘 is normal as required. ∎

Corollary 3.19.82 (Splitting fields are normal)
An algebraic extension 𝐾/𝑘 is normal if and only if it is a splitting field.

Proof. We may apply the previous Proposition with 𝐿=𝑘―.

We may prove a splitting field is normal more directly (without recourse to 𝑘― or Zorn’s Lemma in the finite case). Suppose 𝐾/𝑘 is a splitting field for 𝑆⊂𝑘[𝑋]. Consider 𝛼∈𝐾 with minimal polynomial 𝑚𝛼,𝑘(𝑋). Let (𝐿/𝐾,𝑖𝐾𝐿) be a splitting field for 𝑚𝛼,𝑘(𝑋) (as a polynomial in 𝐾[𝑋], NB may not be irreducible).

Let 𝛽∈𝐿 be another root of 𝑚𝛼,𝑘(𝑋). Observe that 𝑆 splits in 𝐿, so by (3.19.71) there is a morphism 𝜎:𝐾/𝑘→𝐿/𝑘 with 𝜎(𝛼)=𝛽. By (3.19.70) we have 𝑖𝐾𝐿(𝐾)=𝜎(𝐾) whence 𝛽∈𝑖𝐾𝐿(𝐾). As 𝛽 was an arbitrary root of 𝑚𝛼,𝑘(𝑋) we see it splits completely in 𝑖𝐾𝐿(𝐾). Finally by (3.19.69) 𝑚𝛼,𝑘(𝑋) splits completely in 𝐾. As 𝛼 was arbitrary then 𝐾/𝑘 is normal. ∎

Proposition 3.19.83 (Extension to normal overfield)

Let 𝐿/𝐾/𝑘 be a tower of algebraic field extensions with 𝐿/𝑘 normal (e.g. 𝐿=𝑘―) then there is a canonical surjection

Mor𝑘(𝑖𝐾𝐿,𝐿):Aut(𝐿/𝑘)→Mor𝑘(𝐾,𝐿)𝜎→𝜎∘𝑖𝐾𝐿

When 𝑖𝐾𝐿 is inclusion then this is simply the restriction to 𝐾. The kernel is precisely Aut(𝐿/𝐾).

Proof. Given 𝜎̃∈Mor𝑘(𝐾,𝐿), apply (3.19.64) to construct a morphism 𝜎:(𝐿/𝐾,𝑖𝐾𝐿)→(𝐿/𝐾,𝜎̃). The hypotheses apply because the minimal polynomial 𝑚𝛼,𝐾(𝑋) with respect to either extension divides the minimal polynomial 𝑚𝛼,𝑘𝑖𝑘𝐾(𝑋) which by assumption splits completely in 𝐿. By (3.19.49) it is an automorphism. Furthermore

𝜎∘𝑖𝑘𝐿=𝜎∘𝑖𝐾𝐿∘𝑖𝑘𝐾=𝜎̃∘𝑖𝑘𝐾=𝑖𝑘𝐿

whence 𝜎∈Aut(𝐿/𝑘) as required. ∎

Corollary 3.19.84 (Lifting inside normal overfield)

Let 𝐿/𝐾/𝐹/𝑘 be a tower of field extensions with 𝐿/𝑘 normal, then there is a surjection

Mor𝑘(𝑖𝐹𝐾,𝐿):Mor𝑘(𝐾,𝐿)→Mor𝑘(𝐹,𝐿)
Proof. Note that Mor𝑘(𝑖𝐹𝐾,𝐿)∘Mor𝑘(𝑖𝐾𝐿,𝐿)=Mor𝑘(𝑖𝐹𝐿,𝐿). By (3.19.83) this composition is surjective, whence the result follows. ∎
Corollary 3.19.85 (Quotient of automorphism group)

Let 𝐿/𝐾/𝑘 be a tower of extensions such that 𝐿/𝑘 and 𝐾/𝑘 are normal. Then 𝐿/𝐾 is normal and there is an isomorphism of groups.

Aut(𝐿/𝑘)/Aut(𝐿/𝐾)⟶Aut(𝐾/𝑘)𝜎→𝑖𝐾𝐿−1∘𝜎∘𝑖𝐾𝐿
Proof. The given map is well-defined by (3.19.81) since (𝜎∘𝑖𝐾𝐿)(𝐾)=𝑖𝐾𝐿(𝐾), and 𝜎 fixes 𝐾 precisely when 𝜎∘𝑖𝐾𝐿=𝑖𝐾𝐿, that is 𝜎∈Aut(𝐿/𝐾). Given 𝜏∈Aut(𝐾/𝑘), by (3.19.83) there exists 𝜎∈Aut(𝐿/𝑘) such that 𝜎∘𝑖𝐾𝐿=𝑖𝐾𝐿∘𝜏. This shows the given map is surjective. The result follows from the group isomorphism theorem. ∎
Definition 3.19.86 (Normal Closure)

Let 𝐾/𝑘 be an algebraic extension. Then an algebraic extension 𝐿/𝐾 is a normal closure for 𝐾/𝑘 if

  • 𝐿/𝑘 is normal
  • No proper subfield 𝑖𝐾𝐿(𝐾)⊆𝐿′⊊𝐿 is normal over 𝑘
Proposition 3.19.87 (Existence and Uniqueness of Normal Closure)

Let 𝐾/𝑘 be an algebraic extension. Then a normal closure 𝐿/𝐾 exists and is unique up to isomorphism. Indeed it is the splitting field for all the minimal polynomials {𝑚𝛼,𝑘(𝑋)|𝛼∈𝐾} over 𝑘.

Furthermore if 𝐾/𝑘 is finite then so is 𝐿/𝑘

Proof. Suppose 𝐾=𝑘({𝛼𝑖}𝑖∈𝐼). Let 𝐿/𝑘 be the splitting field for 𝑆={𝑚𝛼𝑖,𝑘(𝑋)}𝑖∈𝐼. By (3.19.82) 𝐿/𝑘 is normal and by (3.19.64) there is a morphism 𝜎:𝐾/𝑘→𝐿/𝑘, so we may consider it as an extension (𝐿/𝐾,𝜎). Suppose 𝑖𝐾𝐿(𝐾)⊂𝐿′⊂𝐿 is normal. As 𝑖𝐾𝐿(𝛼𝑖)∈𝐿′, by definition 𝑆 splits in 𝐿′ and therefore 𝐿′=𝐿 by (3.19.68). Therefore 𝐿/𝐾 is a normal closure as required.

If 𝐾/𝑘 is finite then we may choose 𝐼 to be finite, and therefore 𝐿/𝑘 is finite.

Let 𝐿/𝐾 be an arbitrary normal closure, then we claim 𝐿/𝑘 is a splitting field for 𝑆′≔{𝑚𝛼,𝑘(𝑋)|𝛼∈𝐾}. Clearly 𝑆′ splits in 𝐿/𝑘, because each has a root in 𝐿. Let 𝐿′/𝑘 be the subfield generated by roots of 𝑆′. Then it is a splitting field and therefore normal by (3.19.82). By assumption 𝐿′=𝐿 and therefore 𝐿/𝑘 is the splitting field for 𝑆′. Uniqueness follows from the uniqueness of splitting fields (3.19.71). ∎
