3.33.1 Etale Algebras

Definition 3.33.6

Let 𝐴 be a finite-dimensional 𝑘-algebra. Then define the separable degree as follows

[𝐴:𝑘]𝑠≔#︎AlgHom𝑘(𝐴,𝑘―)

We say that 𝐴 is etale if this equals the dimension of 𝐴 as a 𝑘-vector space

[𝐴:𝑘]𝑠=[𝐴:𝑘]

Recall a finite field extension 𝐾/𝑘 is etale if and only if it is separable (3.17.80).

Lemma 3.33.7

Let 𝐴 be a finite-dimensional 𝑘-algebra and 𝐾/𝑘 a field extension. Then

[Hom𝑘(𝐴,𝐾):𝐾]=[𝐴∨:𝑘]=[𝐴:𝑘]

More precisely if {𝑎1,…,𝑎𝑛} is a 𝑘-basis for 𝐴 then {𝑎1∨,…,𝑎𝑛∨} is a 𝐾-basis for Hom𝑘(𝐴,𝐾).

Proof. This is simply a special case of (3.4.27). ∎
Corollary 3.33.8 (Separable degree is finite)
Let 𝐴 be a finite-dimensional 𝑘-algebra then #︎AlgHom𝑘(𝐴,𝐾)≤[𝐴:𝑘] for every field extension 𝐾/𝑘.
Proposition 3.33.9 (Image of 𝐴 is finite-dimensional)

Let 𝐴 be a finite-dimensional 𝑘-algebra and let 𝑁𝐴⊂𝑘― be the compositum of the images 𝜑(𝐴) of all 𝑘-algebra homomorphisms 𝜑:𝐴→𝑘―. Then 𝑁𝐴/𝑘 is a finite normal extension, and for every field extension Ω/𝑘 containing a copy of 𝑁𝐴, that is with a 𝑘-embedding 𝜄:𝑁𝐴→Ω, composition with 𝜄 is a bijection

AlgHom𝑘(𝐴,𝑁𝐴)→AlgHom𝑘(𝐴,Ω)

In particular if Ω contains 𝑘― then these are both equal to AlgHom𝑘(𝐴,𝑘―).

Proof. Drafted by Claude Sonnet, reviewed by David Rufino.

Let 𝜑:𝐴→𝑘― be a 𝑘-algebra homomorphism. Then 𝜑(𝐴) is a quotient of 𝐴 and so finite-dimensional over 𝑘, and it is a domain, being a subring of a field. A finite-dimensional domain is a field (3.17.47). Therefore 𝜑(𝐴)⊂𝑘― is a finite extension of 𝑘. There are only finitely many such 𝜑 (3.33.8), say 𝜑1,…,𝜑𝑟, so 𝑁𝐴=𝑘(𝜑1(𝐴),…,𝜑𝑟(𝐴)) is finite over 𝑘 (3.17.48).

To see 𝑁𝐴/𝑘 is normal, let 𝜎∈Mor𝑘(𝑁𝐴,𝑘―). For each 𝑗 the composite 𝜎∘𝜑𝑗:𝐴→𝑘― is again a 𝑘-algebra homomorphism so 𝜎(𝜑𝑗(𝐴))⊂𝑁𝐴, whence 𝜎(𝑁𝐴)⊂𝑁𝐴. As 𝜎 is injective and 𝑘-linear and 𝑁𝐴 is finite-dimensional, 𝜎(𝑁𝐴)=𝑁𝐴. So 𝑁𝐴/𝑘 is normal by (3.17.69).

Injectivity of the given map is clear as 𝜄 is injective. For surjectivity let 𝜓:𝐴→Ω be a 𝑘-algebra homomorphism. As above 𝐿≔𝜓(𝐴) is a finite extension of 𝑘, so by (3.17.63) there is a 𝑘-embedding 𝜎:𝐿→𝑘―. Then 𝜎∘𝜓:𝐴→𝑘― has image in 𝑁𝐴 by definition. Let 𝑏∈𝐿 with minimal polynomial 𝑚𝑏,𝑘. Then 𝑚𝑏,𝑘 has the root 𝜎(𝑏) in 𝑁𝐴 and hence splits completely in 𝑁𝐴 as 𝑁𝐴/𝑘 is normal, say 𝑚𝑏,𝑘=∏𝑖(𝑋−𝑐𝑖) with 𝑐𝑖∈𝑁𝐴. Applying 𝜄 gives 𝑚𝑏,𝑘=∏𝑖(𝑋−𝜄(𝑐𝑖)) in Ω[𝑋] and since Ω is a domain the root 𝑏 is one of the 𝜄(𝑐𝑖). Thus 𝜓(𝐴)⊆𝜄(𝑁𝐴) and 𝜓=𝜄∘𝜓′ for some 𝜓′:𝐴→𝑁𝐴.

Finally 𝑁𝐴⊂𝑘―, so applying this to Ω=𝑘― with the inclusion, and then to any Ω⊇𝑘―, shows AlgHom𝑘(𝐴,𝑁𝐴)=AlgHom𝑘(𝐴,𝑘―)=AlgHom𝑘(𝐴,Ω). ∎

Proposition 3.33.10 (Etale-ness is preserved under base extension)

Let 𝐴 be a finite-dimensional 𝑘-algebra and 𝐾/𝑘 a field extension then

[𝐴⊗𝑘𝐾:𝐾]=[𝐴:𝑘][𝐴⊗𝑘𝐾:𝐾]𝑠=[𝐴:𝑘]𝑠

In particular 𝐴 is etale if and only if 𝐴⊗𝑘𝐾 is.

Proof. The first equality follows from (3.4.26) and (3.3.109).

For the second equality we see that by (3.33.9) and (3.4.38)

AlgHom𝑘(𝐴,𝑘―)=AlgHom𝑘(𝐴,𝐾―)≅AlgHom𝐾(𝐴⊗𝑘𝐾,𝐾―)

 ∎

Lemma 3.33.11
Let 𝐴=𝐴1×⋯×𝐴𝑑 be a finite product of finite-dimensional 𝑘-algebras. Then 𝐴 is etale if and only if each 𝐴𝑖 is.

Proof. This follows because

AlgHom𝑘(𝐴1×⋯×𝐴𝑑,𝑘―)≅AlgHom𝑘(𝐴1,𝑘―)×⋯×AlgHom𝑘(𝐴𝑑,𝑘―)

and a similar consideration for the dimension over 𝑘. ∎

Lemma 3.33.12

Let 𝐴 be a finite-dimensional 𝑘-algebra. The following are equivalent

  1. 𝐴 is reduced
  2. 𝐴≅𝐿1×⋯×𝐿𝑛 for some finite extensions 𝐿𝑖/𝑘

Further 𝐴 is etale if and only if each 𝐿𝑖/𝑘 is separable. If 𝑘 is perfect then 𝐴 is automatically etale.

Proof. One direction is obvious. Suppose conversely that 𝐴 is reduced. If dim𝑘𝐴=1 then any proper ideal must have strictly lower dimension and therefore 𝐴 is a field. It is therefore sufficient to show (by induction on dimension) that if 𝐴 is not a field then 𝐴≅𝐴1×𝐴2 for two (non-zero) 𝑘-algebras 𝐴1,𝐴2.

Suppose 𝐴 is not a field and let 𝔞 be a non-zero proper ideal of 𝐴 which has minimal dimension over 𝑘 as a vector space. As 𝐴 is reduced, and by minimality, we have 𝔞2=𝔞. By Nakayama’s Lemma (3.19.5) there is 𝑒∈𝔞 such that 𝑒𝑥=𝑥 for all 𝑥∈𝔞 and in particular 𝑒2=𝑒 and 𝔞=𝐴𝑒. By assumption 𝑒≠0,1. Define 𝑓≔1−𝑒≠0,1 then evidently 𝑓2=𝑓 and 𝑒𝑓=0. The homomorphism of 𝐴-modules

𝐴→𝐴𝑒×𝐴𝑓𝑎→(𝑎𝑒,𝑎𝑓)

is injective because 𝑎𝑒+𝑎𝑓=𝑎 and surjective because the image of 𝑎1𝑒+𝑎2𝑓 is (𝑎1𝑒,𝑎2𝑓). The sub-modules 𝐴𝑒 and 𝐴𝑓 are actually sub-rings by the idempotence property and the given map is an isomorphism of rings.

The last statement follows from (3.33.11) and (3.17.106). ∎

Lemma 3.33.13 (Primes of a Finite Product of Fields)
Let 𝐴≅𝐿1×⋯×𝐿𝑛 be a finite product of fields (in particular, any reduced finite-dimensional 𝑘-algebra, (3.33.12)). Then every prime ideal of 𝐴 is maximal, and there are exactly 𝑛 of them.

Proof. For each 𝑗, 𝔪𝑗≔ker(𝜋𝑗) (the kernel of the 𝑗-th projection 𝜋𝑗:𝐴↠𝐿𝑗) is maximal, being the kernel of a surjection onto a field (3.3.59), and the 𝔪𝑗 are pairwise distinct (the element with 1 in coordinate 𝑗 and 0 elsewhere lies in every 𝔪𝑖, 𝑖≠𝑗, but not 𝔪𝑗), giving 𝑛 maximal ideals.

Let 𝔭 be any prime. As ⋂𝑗𝔪𝑗=ker(𝜋1,…,𝜋𝑛)=0⊆𝔭, (3.3.40) gives 𝔪𝑗⊆𝔭 for some 𝑗; as 𝔪𝑗 is already maximal, 𝔭=𝔪𝑗. ∎

Lemma 3.33.14 (Algebra Homomorphisms out of a Product of Fields)
Let Ω be a field. The Ω-algebra homomorphisms 𝜒:Ω𝑛→Ω are exactly the 𝑛 projections 𝜋𝑖, and these are pairwise distinct. In particular #︎AlgHomΩ(Ω𝑛,Ω)=𝑛.

Proof. Drafted by Claude Sonnet, reviewed by David Rufino.

Indeed let 𝑒𝑖 be the 𝑖-th standard idempotent, so 𝑒𝑖2=𝑒𝑖, 𝑒𝑖𝑒𝑗=0 for 𝑖≠𝑗 and ∑𝑖𝑒𝑖=1. Then each 𝜒(𝑒𝑖) is an idempotent of the field Ω so equals 0 or 1; as 𝜒(𝑒𝑖)𝜒(𝑒𝑗)=0 for 𝑖≠𝑗 at most one of them is 1, and as ∑𝑖𝜒(𝑒𝑖)=1 exactly one, say 𝜒(𝑒𝑗), is. Then by Ω-linearity 𝜒(𝑥)=∑𝑖𝑥𝑖𝜒(𝑒𝑖)=𝑥𝑗, that is 𝜒=𝜋𝑗. The 𝜋𝑖 are pairwise distinct as 𝜋𝑖(𝑒𝑖)=1≠0=𝜋𝑗(𝑒𝑖) for 𝑗≠𝑖. ∎

Lemma 3.33.15

Let 𝐴 be a finite-dimensional 𝑘-algebra and Ω/𝑘 a field extension containing a copy of the extension 𝑁𝐴/𝑘 of (3.33.9) (for instance any Ω containing 𝑘―). Then the following are equivalent

  1. 𝐴 is etale
  2. the map Θ:𝐴⊗𝑘Ω→ΩAlgHom𝑘(𝐴,Ω),𝑎⊗𝑐↦(𝑐𝜑(𝑎))𝜑 is an isomorphism of Ω-algebras
  3. 𝐴⊗𝑘Ω≅Ω𝑛 as an Ω-algebra for some 𝑛

In this case the 𝑛 of c) equals [𝐴:𝑘], and 𝐴⊗𝑘Ω has exactly 𝑛 maximal ideals, namely the kernels of the coordinates of Θ.

Proof. 𝑎)⟹𝑏) By (3.33.9) #︎AlgHom𝑘(𝐴,Ω)=#︎AlgHom𝑘(𝐴,𝑘―)≕[𝐴:𝑘]𝑠=[𝐴:𝑘] with the last equality because 𝐴 is assumed etale. Then defining 𝐵≔𝐴⊗𝑘Ω

#︎AlgHomΩ(𝐵,Ω)=(3.4.38)#︎AlgHom𝑘(𝐴,Ω)=[𝐴:𝑘]=(3.4.16)[𝐵:Ω]=[𝐵∨:Ω]

By (3.17.133) and (3.3.127) we see that AlgHomΩ(𝐵,Ω) is a Ω-basis for 𝐵∨. By (3.4.38) the map sending 𝜑∈AlgHom𝑘(𝐴,Ω) to 𝜑̃:𝐵→Ω, 𝑎⊗𝑐↦𝑐𝜑(𝑎), is a bijection AlgHom𝑘(𝐴,Ω)→AlgHomΩ(𝐵,Ω). Write AlgHom𝑘(𝐴,Ω)={𝜑1,…,𝜑𝑛}, so that 𝜑̃1,…,𝜑̃𝑛 is the basis of 𝐵∨. Then by (3.3.109) this corresponds to a basis 𝑒1,…,𝑒𝑛 of 𝐵 and the algebra isomorphism is exhibited explicitly by

Θ:𝐵≅Ω𝑛𝑎⊗𝑐→(𝑐𝜑1(𝑎),…,𝑐𝜑𝑛(𝑎))

Indeed Θ(𝑏)=(𝜑̃1(𝑏),…,𝜑̃𝑛(𝑏)) is an Ω-algebra homomorphism, and it is injective since a 𝑏 killed by every element of a basis of 𝐵∨ is killed by every functional, so 𝑏=0. It is therefore an isomorphism by dimensions.

𝑏)⟹𝑐) Immediate, with 𝑛≔#︎AlgHom𝑘(𝐴,Ω), which is finite (3.33.8).

𝑐)⟹𝑎) By (3.33.14) the Ω-algebra homomorphisms Ω𝑛→Ω are the 𝑛 projections 𝜋𝑖, so #︎AlgHomΩ(𝐵,Ω)=𝑛=[𝐵:Ω]. As before then [𝐴:𝑘]𝑠=#︎AlgHom𝑘(𝐴,𝑘―)=#︎AlgHom𝑘(𝐴,Ω)=#︎AlgHomΩ(𝐵,Ω)=[𝐵:Ω]=[𝐴:𝑘] which shows that 𝐴 is etale.

Finally Ω𝑛 is a finite product of fields, so by (3.33.13) its primes are the 𝑛 coordinate kernels, and these correspond to the maximal ideals of 𝐴⊗𝑘Ω under Θ. ∎

Corollary 3.33.16 (A Field Splits Completely Over Any Normal Extension it Embeds Into)

Let 𝐾/𝑘 be a finite separable field extension and 𝐿/𝑘 a field extension containing (a copy of) a normal closure of 𝐾/𝑘. Fix a primitive element 𝜃 for 𝐾/𝑘 (3.17.108), with minimal polynomial 𝑚∈𝑘[𝑋] of degree 𝑛≔[𝐾:𝑘], and write 𝑚=∏𝑖=1𝑛(𝑋−𝛽𝑖) for its (pairwise distinct) roots 𝛽𝑖∈𝐿. Then there is a canonical isomorphism of 𝐿-algebras

𝐾⊗𝑘𝐿≅𝐿𝑛,𝑝(𝜃)⊗𝑐↦(𝑐𝑝(𝛽1),…,𝑐𝑝(𝛽𝑛))(𝑝∈𝑘[𝑋],𝑐∈𝐿)

In particular 𝐾⊗𝑘𝐿 has exactly 𝑛 maximal ideals, 𝔪𝑖 the kernel of the 𝑖-th coordinate.

Proof. Drafted by Claude Sonnet, reviewed by David Rufino.

As 𝐾/𝑘 is separable it is etale (3.33.6). By (3.17.85).c) applied with Ω=𝑘―, 𝑁𝐾 is a normal closure of 𝐾/𝑘, so 𝐿 contains a copy of 𝑁𝐾 (3.17.75) and the isomorphism Θ of (3.33.15) is defined. By (3.17.85).b), 𝜑↦𝜑(𝜃) is a bijection Mor𝑘(𝐾,𝐿)→{𝛽1,…,𝛽𝑛}, and under this indexing Θ sends 𝑝(𝜃)⊗𝑐 to (𝑐𝑝(𝛽𝑖))𝑖. The statement about maximal ideals is the last statement of (3.33.15). ∎

Proposition 3.33.17 (Etale Criteria)

Let 𝐴 be a finite-dimensional 𝑘-algebra. The following are equivalent

  1. 𝐴 is etale
  2. 𝐴 is geometrically reduced
  3. 𝐴⊗𝑘𝑘― is reduced
  4. 𝐴⊗𝑘𝐾 is reduced for some perfect field extension 𝐾/𝑘
  5. 𝐴≅𝐿1×⋯×𝐿𝑛 for 𝐿𝑖/𝑘 finite separable extensions

In particular 𝐴 is reduced.

Proof. 𝑎)⟹𝑏) Consider a field extension 𝐿/𝑘 and the tower of extensions 𝐿―/𝐿/𝑘. Then by (3.33.15) 𝐴⊗𝑘𝐿―≅𝐿―𝑛 is evidently reduced. By (3.33.2) 𝐴⊗𝑘𝐿⊂𝐴⊗𝑘𝐿― is a subring and therefore also reduced.

𝑏)⟹𝑐) immediate.

𝑐)⟹𝑑) immediate as 𝑘― is perfect.

𝑑)⟹𝑎) By (3.33.12) 𝐴⊗𝑘𝐾 is an etale 𝐾-algebra so by (3.33.10) 𝐴 is etale.

𝑒)⟹𝑎) Follows immediately from (3.33.11).

𝑎)⟹𝑒) We know that 𝐴 is reduced so the result follows from (3.33.12). ∎

Corollary 3.33.18

Let 𝐴 be a finite-dimensional 𝑘-algebra and 𝑘 perfect. Then the following are equivalent

  1. 𝐴 is etale
  2. 𝐴 is reduced
  3. 𝐴 is geometrically reduced
  4. 𝐴⊗𝑘𝑘― is reduced.

Corollary 3.33.19 (Equivalent definitions of separability)

Let 𝐾/𝑘 be a finite extension. Then the following are equivalent

  2. 𝐾/𝑘 is etale

  4. 𝐾⊗𝑘𝑘― is reduced

Proof. We have already shown 𝑏)⟺𝑐)⟺𝑑). Furthermore 𝑎)⟺𝑏) is (3.17.80). ∎
Corollary 3.33.20 (Equivalent definitions of separability)

Let 𝐾/𝑘 be an algebraic extension. Then the following are equivalent

  1. 𝐾/𝑘 is separable algebraic (3.17.53)
  2. Every finite subextension of 𝐾/𝑘 is etale
  3. 𝐾/𝑘 is geometrically reduced
  4. 𝐾⊗𝑘𝑘― is reduced

In particular an algebraic extension of a perfect field 𝑘 is geometrically reduced.

Proof. This follows directly from (3.33.19) because each property has “finite character”, i.e. holds if and only if it holds for every finite subextension.

The last statement follows from (3.17.106). ∎
