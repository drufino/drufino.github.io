5.1.3 Uniqueness of Reals

Lemma 5.1.26

Let 𝐾 be a complete ordered field. Then every 𝑥∈𝐾 satisfies

𝑥=sup{𝑚𝑛∈ℚ|𝑚𝑛≤𝑥}

Similarly for every 𝑥∈𝐾 there is a sequence 𝑎𝑛∈ℚ such that 𝑎𝑛↑𝑥.

Proof. By assumption the supremum 𝛼 exists. By definition 𝑥 is an upper bound for the set so 𝛼≤𝑥. Suppose 𝛼<𝑥 then by (5.1.7) there exists 𝑚𝑛 such that 𝛼<𝑚𝑛<𝑥. On the other hand by definition 𝑚𝑛≤𝛼, which is a contradiction. Therefore 𝛼=𝑥 as required.

The final statement follows from (5.1.18). ∎

Lemma 5.1.27
Let 𝜏:𝐾1↪︎𝐾2 be an embedding of ordered fields with 𝐾2 Archimedean. Suppose 𝑥=sup𝑋 for some subset 𝑋⊂𝐾1. Then 𝜏(𝑥)=sup𝜏(𝑋).
Proof. Clearly 𝑥′∈𝑋⟹𝑥′≤𝑥⟹𝜏(𝑥′)≤𝜏(𝑥)⟹𝜏(𝑥)∈𝜏(𝑋)↑. Suppose 𝑦∈𝜏(𝑋)↑ and 𝑦<𝜏(𝑥). Then by (5.1.7) there exists 𝑧∈ℚ such that 𝑦<𝑧<𝜏(𝑥). By the order preserving property we have 𝑧∈𝑋↑ and 𝑧<𝑥 which is a contradiction. Therefore 𝑦∈𝜏(𝑋)↑⟹𝑦≥𝜏(𝑥) and 𝜏(𝑥)=sup𝜏(𝑋). ∎
Proposition 5.1.28 (Uniqueness of Reals)
Let 𝐾1,𝐾2 be complete ordered fields. Then there exists a unique ordered field embedding 𝐾1↪︎𝐾2, which is an isomorphism.

Proof. Let 𝜏,𝜏′:𝐾1→𝐾2 be order embeddings. Then they agree on ℚ. By (5.1.26) every 𝑥 is the supremum of a set 𝑋⊂ℚ. Then uniqueness follows from (5.1.27). The same result shows it is surjective.

For existence we claim that

𝜏(𝑥)≔sup{𝑚𝑛⋅1𝐾2|𝑚𝑛⋅1𝐾1≤𝑥}

is a well-defined, order-preserving field isomorphism. Firstly 𝑥 is bounded by some integer 𝑁 so the supremum is well-defined. Similarly suppose 𝑥′>𝑥 then by (5.1.7) we have 𝑥<𝑚𝑛<𝑥′. This shows that 𝜏(𝑥)≤𝑚′𝑛′≤𝜏(𝑥′) as required, whence 𝜏 is order preserving.

By (5.1.26) there exists 𝑎𝑛,𝑏𝑛∈ℚ such that 𝑎𝑛↑𝑥, 𝑏𝑛↑𝑦 and by the triangle inequality 𝑎𝑛+𝑏𝑛↑𝑥+𝑦. We may show using the definition of 𝜏 that 𝑎𝑛↑𝜏(𝑥),𝑏𝑛↑𝜏(𝑦),𝑎𝑛+𝑏𝑛↑𝜏(𝑥+𝑦). Uniqueness of limits shows that 𝜏(𝑥+𝑦)=𝜏(𝑥)+𝜏(𝑦). Similarly we may show that 𝜏(𝑥𝑦)=𝜏(𝑥)𝜏(𝑦). ∎
