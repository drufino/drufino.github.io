6.2.21 Algebraic Curves

Definition 6.2.120
We say an integral, separated algebraic variety of dimension 1 is an algebraic curve or just a curve.
Proposition 6.2.121 (Characterisation Regular Point on a curve)

Let 𝑋 be an algebraic curve and 𝑥∈𝑋. Then the following are equivalent

  1. 𝑥 is regular
  2. 𝒪︀𝑋,𝑥 is a discrete valuation ring
  3. 𝒪︀𝑋,𝑥 is an integrally closed domain

and when 𝑘 is perfect this is equivalent to 𝑥 being non-singular.

In particular 𝑋 is regular if and only if it is normal.

Proof. Follows exactly the same lines as (6.1.100). ∎

Proposition 6.2.125

Let 𝑋 be a regular algebraic curve and 𝑌 a complete variety. Then the rational maps are precisely the regular maps. In otherwords the canonical map

Mor(𝑋,𝑌)⟶∼Rat(𝑋,𝑌)

is a bijection. Further this restricts to dominant maps.

Proof. Clearly the map is well-defined. Suppose that two regular maps 𝜙,𝜓 are equal as rational maps. Then by definition they agree on a dense open subset. By definition 𝑌 is separated so by (6.2.63) the equaliser is a closed subset, and therefore the whole of 𝑌. This means the map is injective.

Consider a regular map 𝜙:𝑈→𝑌 for 𝑈 a (dense) open subset of 𝑋. Then 𝑍≔𝑋∖𝑈 is a proper closed subset of 𝑋 and therefore dim𝑍=0 by (4.1.84). By (6.2.12) we have 𝑍={𝑧1}―∪…∪{𝑧𝑛}―, and we may apply (6.2.90) finitely many times to lift to a regular map on an open set containing 𝑈 and 𝑧1,…,𝑧𝑛. By (4.1.41) this is the whole of 𝑋. Therefore the given map is surjective. ∎

Proposition 6.2.126 (Image of Complete Curve either Constant or Surjective)
Let 𝑋,𝑌 be algebraic curves with 𝑋 complete and 𝜙:𝑋→𝑌 a regular morphism. Then 𝜙 is either surjective or has image of the form {𝑥}― (which we call constant).
Proof. By (6.2.89) 𝜙 is universally closed, so by (6.2.84) 𝜙(𝑋) is an irreducible closed subvariety of 𝑌. Evidently dim𝜙(𝑋)=0 or 1. Then we may use (6.2.56) or (4.1.84). ∎
Corollary 6.2.127 (Equivalence between nice curves and function fields)

Let 𝑋,𝑌 be complete, regular algebraic curves. Then there are bijections

{𝜙:𝑋→𝑌non-constant}⟷{𝜙:𝑋⟶𝑌dominant}⟷{𝜙⋆:𝑘(𝑌)↪︎𝑘(𝑋)}

All such morphisms are surjective and the degree

deg(𝜙)≔[𝑘(𝑋):𝜙⋆(𝑘(𝑌))]

is always finite.

Finally the fibres are finite (i.e. 𝜙 is “quasi-finite”).

Proof. This follows from (6.2.126), (6.2.125) and (6.2.76).

The fibres are finite because 𝜙−1({𝑦})⊂𝜙−1({𝑦}―). By assumption this is a proper closed subset of 𝑋 and therefore has dimension 0. So we are done by (6.2.56). ∎
