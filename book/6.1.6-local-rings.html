6.1.6 Local Rings

Definition 6.1.55 (Local Ring)

Let 𝑋 be an affine variety and 𝑊⊂𝑋(𝑘―) be an irreducible subset. Then we define the local ring at 𝑊 to be

𝒪︀𝑋,𝑊≔lim⟶𝑈∩𝑊≠∅𝒪︀𝑋(𝑈)

It is a local ring with unique maximal ideal

𝔪𝑋,𝑊={(𝑈,𝜎)|𝜎(𝑥)=0∀𝑥∈𝑊}.

In the case 𝑊={(𝑥)} then we write it as (𝒪︀𝑋,𝑥,𝔪𝑋,𝑥).

Proof. First of all the family of open sets such that 𝑈∩𝑊≠∅ is directed by reverse inclusion precisely because 𝑊 is irreducible, see (4.1.60).

Any section 𝜎∈𝒪︀𝑋(𝑈) is continuous with respect to the cofinite topology on 𝑘. Therefore 𝐷(𝜎)≔𝜎−1(𝑘∖{0})⊂𝑈 is an open set and 𝜎∉𝔪𝑋,𝑊⟹𝐷(𝜎)∩𝑊≠∅. Then evidently [(𝐷(𝜎),𝜎−1)] is a multiplicative inverse for (𝑈,𝜎) and by (…) 𝒪︀𝑋,𝑊 is a local ring with unique maximal ideal 𝔪𝑋,𝑊. ∎

dim𝒪︀𝑋,𝑥=dim𝑋

Proposition 6.1.61

Let 𝑋 be an affine variety and 𝑊⊂𝑋 an irreducible closed subset. Then the minimal primes of 𝒪︀𝑋,𝑊 are in bijection with the irreducible components of 𝑋 containing 𝑊.

𝒪︀𝑋,𝑊 is an integral domain if and only if 𝑊 lies in a unique irreducible component.

Proof. By (6.1.57) and (3.6.42) the minimal primes of 𝒪︀𝑋,𝑊 correspond to minimal primes of 𝑘[𝑋] contained in 𝔐𝑋,𝑊. These correspond to irreducible components of 𝑋 containing 𝑊― (and hence 𝑊) by (6.1.19).

As 𝒪︀𝑋,𝑊 is reduced, it is an integral domain iff it has a unique minimal prime ideal (3.3.66). ∎
