6.3.7 Base Extension of the Constant Field

Throughout this section, in addition to the standing hypothesis that 𝐾/𝑘 is geometrically integral (𝑘̃=𝑘), assume also that 𝑘 is perfect. Let 𝑘′/𝑘 be an algebraic field extension, and write 𝐾𝑘′≔𝐾⊗𝑘𝑘′.

Proposition 6.3.43 (The Base-Changed Function Field)
𝐾𝑘′ is a field, and an algebraic function field over 𝑘′, geometrically integral; if 𝑘′/𝑘 is finite, [𝐾𝑘′:𝐾]=[𝑘′:𝑘].

Proof. Drafted by Claude Sonnet, reviewed by David Rufino.

𝐾𝑘′ is a field. As 𝐾/𝑘 is geometrically integral and 𝑘′/𝑘 is algebraic, 𝐾𝑘′=𝐾⊗𝑘𝑘′ is a field (3.35.45).

𝐾𝑘′/𝑘′ is geometrically integral. By associativity of tensor products (3.4.13) and 𝑘′―=𝑘― (𝑘′/𝑘 algebraic), 𝐾𝑘′⊗𝑘′𝑘′―≅𝐾⊗𝑘(𝑘′⊗𝑘′𝑘―)=𝐾⊗𝑘𝑘―, a field, as 𝐾/𝑘 is geometrically integral.

𝐾𝑘′/𝑘′ is an algebraic function field. Fix a transcendental 𝑥∈𝐾 with 𝐾/𝑘(𝑥) finite (exists, as 𝐾/𝑘 is an algebraic function field). As 𝑘[𝑥]⊗𝑘𝑘′≅𝑘′[𝑥] (tensoring a polynomial ring with a field extension), the image of 𝑥 in 𝐾𝑘′ remains transcendental over 𝑘′. The image of 𝑘(𝑥)⊗𝑘𝑘′ in 𝐾𝑘′ (well-defined and injective, as 𝑘(𝑥)⊂𝐾 is a 𝑘-subalgebra, (3.35.2)) is then a subring of the field 𝐾𝑘′, hence a domain; as 𝑘′/𝑘 is algebraic, it is thus a field (3.34.57), and – being the subfield of 𝐾𝑘′ generated by 𝑘′ and the transcendental 𝑥 – equals 𝑘′(𝑥). By associativity of tensor products (3.4.13) (as 𝐾 is a 𝑘(𝑥)-module),

𝐾𝑘′=𝐾⊗𝑘𝑘′≅𝐾⊗𝑘(𝑥)(𝑘(𝑥)⊗𝑘𝑘′)=𝐾⊗𝑘(𝑥)𝑘′(𝑥)

As 𝐾/𝑘(𝑥) is finite, 𝐾 is a finite-dimensional 𝑘(𝑥)-vector space, hence free (3.3.126) with a basis of size 𝑛≔[𝐾:𝑘(𝑥)]; extension of scalars (3.4.26) then makes 𝐾𝑘′≅𝐾⊗𝑘(𝑥)𝑘′(𝑥) a free 𝑘′(𝑥)-module with a basis of the same size 𝑛, in particular finite over 𝑘′(𝑥). So 𝐾𝑘′ is a finitely generated field extension of 𝑘′ (generated by 𝑥 together with a finite 𝑘′(𝑥)-basis) of transcendence degree 1: an algebraic function field.

Degree. If 𝑘′/𝑘 is finite, 𝑘′ is a free 𝑘-module with a basis of size [𝑘′:𝑘], so extension of scalars along 𝑘↪︎𝐾 (3.4.26) makes 𝐾𝑘′=𝐾⊗𝑘𝑘′ a free 𝐾-module with a basis of the same size, giving [𝐾𝑘′:𝐾]=[𝑘′:𝑘]. ∎

Proposition 6.3.44 (Constant Field Extension is Unramified)

Let 𝑃 be a place of 𝐾/𝑘 and 𝑘′/𝑘 algebraic. Every place 𝑃′ of 𝐾𝑘′/𝑘′ lying over 𝑃 is unramified.

There are exactly 𝑟 such places, where 𝑟 is the number of maximal ideals of 𝐴≔𝑘(𝔪𝑃)⊗𝑘𝑘′ (finite, even when 𝑘′/𝑘 is not). Moreover each such 𝑃′ satisfies 𝒪︀𝑃′∩𝐾=𝒪︀𝑃 and 𝔪𝑃′∩𝐾=𝔪𝑃.

More precisely, writing 𝒪︀𝑃,𝑘′≔𝒪︀𝑃⊗𝑘𝑘′, 𝐴≅𝒪︀𝑃,𝑘′/𝔪𝑃𝒪︀𝑃,𝑘′ as 𝑘′-algebras (3.4.42) there is a bijection

Specm(𝑘(𝔪𝑃)⊗𝑘𝑘′)⟷{places of 𝐾𝑘′/𝑘′ lying over 𝑃}𝔭′/𝔪𝑃𝒪︀𝑃,𝑘′⟶(𝒪︀𝑃,𝑘′)𝔭′(𝔪𝑃′∩𝒪︀𝑃,𝑘′)/𝔪𝑃𝒪︀𝑃,𝑘′⟵𝒪︀𝑃′

Proof. Drafted by Claude Sonnet, reviewed by David Rufino.

We claim that we are in the situation of (3.35.47), applied with 𝒪︀≔𝒪︀𝑃, 𝐹≔𝐾:

  • 𝒪︀𝑃 is a discrete valuation ring containing 𝑘 (a place of 𝐾/𝑘 is by definition a valuation ring 𝑘⊊𝒪︀𝑃⊊𝐾 (6.2.112), automatically discrete (6.2.114))
  • with fraction field 𝐾 which is geometrically integral (the standing hypothesis of this section)
  • 𝑘(𝔪𝑃) is finite over 𝑘 (6.2.114)
  • every valuation ring 𝑅 of 𝐾𝑘′/𝑘′ with 𝑘′⊂𝑅 and 𝒪︀𝑃⊂𝑅 satisfies 𝑅∩𝐾=𝒪︀𝑃: such an 𝑅 is exactly a place of 𝐾𝑘′/𝑘′, and this is (6.2.119) (applied with 𝐿≔𝐾𝑘′, 𝑘″≔𝑘′)

Therefore the result follows from there: writing 𝒪︀𝑃,𝑘′≔𝒪︀𝑃⊗𝑘𝑘′, its 𝔟≔𝔪𝑃𝒪︀𝑃,𝑘′ has quotient 𝒪︀𝑃,𝑘′/𝔟≅𝐴 (above), so a) gives exactly 𝑟 maximal ideals 𝔭1,…,𝔭𝑟 of 𝒪︀𝑃,𝑘′; by b), each (𝒪︀𝑃,𝑘′)𝔭𝑗 is a place 𝑃𝑗′ of 𝐾𝑘′/𝑘′ (with 𝒪︀𝑃𝑗′≔(𝒪︀𝑃,𝑘′)𝔭𝑗), unramified over 𝒪︀𝑃, and, as in the statement, corresponds to 𝔭𝑗/𝔟∈Specm(𝐴); and, by c) together with the last point above, every place 𝑃′ of 𝐾𝑘′/𝑘′ over 𝑃 has 𝒪︀𝑃′=(𝒪︀𝑃,𝑘′)𝔭𝑗 for some 𝑗, that is 𝑃′=𝑃𝑗′, giving the stated bijection. As 𝐾𝑘′/𝑘′ is itself an algebraic function field (6.3.43), every place of 𝐾𝑘′/𝑘′ – in particular 𝑃𝑗′ – is automatically a discrete valuation ring (6.2.114), so 𝑒(𝑃𝑗′/𝑃)=𝑣𝑃𝑗′(𝜋𝑃)=1: unramified, with 𝜋𝑃 a uniformiser of 𝒪︀𝑃 (3.30.2). Finally 𝒪︀𝑃𝑗′∩𝐾=𝒪︀𝑃 and 𝔪𝑃𝑗′∩𝐾=𝔪𝑃 by e). ∎

Lemma 6.3.45 (A Rational Place Extends Uniquely Under Any Algebraic Base Change)
Let 𝑃 be a rational place of 𝐾/𝑘. Then for any algebraic 𝑘′/𝑘, there is a unique place 𝑄 of 𝐾𝑘′ over 𝑃, and 𝑒(𝑄/𝑃)=1, with 𝑄 again rational over 𝑘′.

Proof. Drafted by Claude Sonnet, reviewed by David Rufino.

This is the case 𝑘(𝔪𝑃)=𝑘 of (6.3.44). ∎

Proposition 6.3.46 (Complete Splitting)

Let 𝐾/𝑘 be an algebraic function field, 𝑃 a place of 𝐾/𝑘, and 𝑘′/𝑘 an algebraic extension. Suppose further that 𝑘′ contains a normal closure of 𝑘(𝔪𝑃).

Then 𝑃 splits completely in 𝐾𝑘′/𝑘′: that is there are exactly 𝑛=[𝑘(𝔪𝑃):𝑘] places above 𝑃, each rational over 𝑘′.

This holds in particular when 𝑘′=𝑘―.

Proof. Drafted by Claude Sonnet, reviewed by David Rufino.

Write 𝑘𝑃≔𝑘(𝔪𝑃); 𝑘𝑃/𝑘 is finite (6.2.114) and separable, as 𝑘 is perfect (3.18.103). As 𝑘′ contains a normal closure of 𝑘𝑃/𝑘, (3.35.15) gives 𝑘𝑃⊗𝑘𝑘′≅(𝑘′)𝑛 as 𝑘′-algebras, with exactly 𝑛 maximal ideals, each with residue field 𝑘′ (the coordinate kernels). By (6.3.44) (with 𝐴≔𝑘𝑃⊗𝑘𝑘′) this implies that 𝑃 splits completely into 𝑛 places, each rational (residue field 𝑘′). ∎

Proposition 6.3.47 (Automorphisms Act Transitively on Places over 𝑘―)

Let 𝑃 be a place of 𝐾/𝑘, 𝑑≔[𝑘(𝔪𝑃):𝑘], and 𝑃1′,…,𝑃𝑑′ the places of 𝐾𝑘―/𝑘― over 𝑃 (6.3.46) (with 𝑘′≔𝑘―). For 𝜎∈Aut(𝑘―/𝑘), the map

𝜎∗:𝐾𝑘―→𝐾𝑘―,𝑥⊗𝑐↦𝑥⊗𝜎(𝑐)

is a well-defined ring automorphism of 𝐾𝑘― fixing 𝐾 pointwise, and 𝜎↦𝜎∗ is a group action of Aut(𝑘―/𝑘) on 𝐾𝑘―.

For any place 𝑃′ of 𝐾𝑘―/𝑘― lying over 𝑃 then 𝜎∗(𝒪︀𝑃′) is a place lying over 𝑃 and

𝑣𝜎(𝑃′)(𝜎∗(𝑧))=𝑣𝑃′(𝑧)

for all 𝑧∈𝐾𝑘―⋆; this defines a transitive group action of Aut(𝑘―/𝑘) on {𝑃1′,…,𝑃𝑑′}.

Moreover, for 𝑃′ among 𝑃1′,…,𝑃𝑑′ (rational over 𝑘―), write 𝜄𝑃′:𝑘(𝔪𝑃)↪︎𝑘(𝔪𝑃′)=𝑘― for the residue embedding induced by 𝒪︀𝑃⊂𝒪︀𝑃′ (well-defined as 𝔪𝑃⊂𝔪𝑃′∩𝒪︀𝑃). Then 𝜄𝜎(𝑃′)=𝜎∘𝜄𝑃′.

This yields a bijection

{places of 𝐾/𝑘}⟷{Aut(𝑘―/𝑘)-orbits of places of 𝐾𝑘―/𝑘―}𝑃⟶{𝑃1′,…,𝑃𝑑′}

Proof. As 𝜎:𝑘―→𝑘― is a 𝑘-algebra homomorphism, 1𝐾⊗𝜎 is a well-defined 𝑘-algebra endomorphism of 𝐾⊗𝑘𝑘―=𝐾𝑘― (3.4.36).

As (1𝐾⊗𝜎)∘(1𝐾⊗𝜏)=1𝐾⊗(𝜎𝜏) then 𝜎∗ is an automorphism of 𝐾𝑘―/𝐾 and 𝜎↦𝜎∗ is a group homomorphism.

As 𝜎∗ is a field automorphism of 𝐾𝑘― then 𝜎∗(𝒪︀𝑃′) is a place of 𝐾𝑘― which lies over the same place as 𝒪︀𝑃′, as 𝜎∗ fixes 𝐾⊃𝒪︀𝑃.

The valuation identity follows because 𝜎∗ restricts to an isomorphism on the valuation rings.

As 𝑘 is perfect, 𝑘(𝔪𝑃)/𝑘 is separable (3.18.103), so (3.35.15) gives a canonical isomorphism of 𝑘―-algebras

𝑘(𝔪𝑃)⊗𝑘𝑘―≅𝑘―𝑑,𝑝(𝜃)⊗𝑐↦(𝑐𝑝(𝜃1),…,𝑐𝑝(𝜃𝑑))(𝑝∈𝑘[𝑋],𝑐∈𝑘―)

identifying the 𝑑 maximal ideals of this fibre with 𝔭𝑗, the kernel of the 𝑗-th coordinate.

Let 𝔭1′,…,𝔭𝑑′ denote the corresponding maximal ideals of 𝒪︀𝑃,𝑘―=𝒪︀𝑃⊗𝑘𝑘―. Define 𝒪︀𝑃𝑖′≔(𝒪︀𝑃,𝑘―)𝔭𝑖′ then by (6.3.44) these are precisely the places 𝑃1′,…,𝑃𝑑′ above 𝑃, and 𝒪︀𝑃𝑖′∩𝐾=𝒪︀𝑃, which shows that the given map is injective.

By (3.18.66) Aut(𝑘―/𝑘) permutes the roots 𝜃𝑖 transitively. We may show that if 𝜎(𝜃𝑗)=𝜃𝑗′ then 𝜎∗(𝒪︀𝑃𝑗′)=𝒪︀𝑃𝑗′′ and the action on places is transitive and {𝑃1′,…,𝑃𝑑′} is precisely an Aut(𝑘―/𝑘)-orbit.

Given a place 𝑃― of 𝐾𝑘―/𝑘―, 𝒪︀≔𝒪︀𝑃―∩𝐾 is a (proper) valuation ring of 𝐾 by (6.2.118), giving a place 𝑃 of 𝐾/𝑘 with 𝒪︀𝑃=𝒪︀⊂𝒪︀𝑃―, that is 𝑃― lies over 𝑃. As 𝑃1′,…,𝑃𝑑′ are all the places of 𝐾𝑘―/𝑘― above 𝑃 (above), 𝑃― is one of them. So the given map is a surjection. ∎

Lemma 6.3.48 (Residue Embeddings are Exactly the Embeddings into 𝑘―)

Let 𝑃 be a place of 𝐾/𝑘, 𝑑≔[𝑘(𝔪𝑃):𝑘], and 𝑃1′,…,𝑃𝑑′ the places of 𝐾𝑘―/𝑘― over 𝑃 (6.3.46) (with 𝑘′≔𝑘―).

We show that 𝑗↦𝜄𝑗 is injective, i.e. that 𝜄𝑗=𝜄𝑗′ implies 𝒪︀𝑃𝑗′=𝒪︀𝑃𝑗′′ (and so 𝑗=𝑗′). Consider the composite 𝜌𝑗:𝒪︀𝑃,𝑘―→(𝒪︀𝑃,𝑘―)𝔭𝑗′=𝒪︀𝑃𝑗′→𝒪︀𝑃𝑗′/𝔪𝑃𝑗′=𝑘―. It is 𝑘―-linear and is uniquely determined by

so 𝜄𝑗=𝜄𝑗′⟹𝜌𝑗=𝜌𝑗′, and their kernels agree, that is 𝔭𝑗′=𝔭𝑗′′ (as ker𝜌𝑗=𝔪𝑃𝑗′∩𝒪︀𝑃,𝑘―=(𝔭𝑗′(𝒪︀𝑃,𝑘―)𝔭𝑗′)𝑐=𝔭𝑗′ (3.5.42)). By (6.3.44) 𝑃𝑗′=𝑃𝑗′′ and we are done by counting. ∎
