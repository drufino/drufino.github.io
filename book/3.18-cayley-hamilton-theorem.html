3.18 Cayley-Hamilton Theorem

Definition 3.18.1 (Characteristic Polynomial of a Matrix)

For a matrix 𝐸∈Mat𝑛(𝐴) define the characteristic polynomial by

𝑃𝐸(𝑋)≔det(𝑋⋅𝐼𝑛−𝐸)

working in Mat𝑛(𝐴[𝑋]). This is a monic polynomial in 𝐴[𝑋] and satisfies 𝑃𝐸(𝑋)=𝑃𝐸𝑡(𝑋) (3.5.20).

Definition 3.18.2 (Characteristic Polynomial of an endomorphism of a free module)

Let 𝑀 be a finite free 𝐴-module. Let ℬ︀ be a basis for 𝑀, 𝜙:𝑀→𝑀 an 𝐴-module endomorphism and [𝜙] the matrix representation of 𝜙 with respect to ℬ︀. Define the characteristic polynomial of 𝜙 by

𝑃𝜙(𝑋)≔𝑃[𝜙](𝑋)

This is independent of the basis ℬ︀ by (3.5.20).

Lemma 3.18.3

Suppose 𝑀=⟨𝑚1,…,𝑚𝑛⟩ is a finitely generated 𝐴-module and 𝔞◃𝐴 an ideal, then

𝔞𝑀=𝔞𝑚1+…+𝔞𝑚𝑛

That is every 𝑚∈𝔞𝑀 may be written as

𝑚=∑𝑖=1𝑛𝑎𝑖𝑚𝑖for some𝑎𝑖∈𝔞

Proof. By hypothesis

𝑚=∑𝑗=1𝑛𝑏𝑗𝑚𝑗′for some𝑚𝑗′∈𝑀,𝑏𝑗∈𝔞

Furthermore by the finite-generation hypothesis for 𝑗=1…𝑛

𝑚𝑗′=∑𝑖=1𝑛𝑐𝑗𝑖𝑚𝑖for some𝑐𝑗𝑖∈𝐴.

Therefore

𝑚=∑𝑖=1𝑛(∑𝑗=1𝑛𝑏𝑗𝑐𝑗𝑖)𝑚𝑖

whence 𝑎𝑖≔∑𝑗=1𝑛𝑏𝑗𝑐𝑗𝑖 lie in 𝔞 and give the required coefficients. ∎

Theorem 3.18.4 (Cayley-Hamilton)

Let 𝑀 be a finitely generated 𝐴-module and 𝜙∈End𝐴(𝑀). Then there exists a monic polynomial 𝑃(𝑋)∈𝐴[𝑋] such that 𝑃(𝜙)=0.

This may be strengthened in the following ways

  1. If 𝑀 is a finite free 𝐴-module then 𝑃 may be taken to be the characteristic polynomial 𝑃𝜙(𝑋).
  2. If 𝜙(𝑀)⊆𝔞𝑀 for some ideal 𝔞◃𝐴, then the non-leading coefficients of 𝑃(𝑋) may be chosen to be in 𝔞.

Proof. First since End𝐴(𝑀) is an 𝐴-algebra there is a canonical evaluation morphism

ev𝜙:𝐴[𝑋]→End𝐴(𝑀)

and the meaning of 𝑃(𝜙) is simply ev𝜙(𝑃).

Let {𝑚1,…,𝑚𝑛} be a generating set, then by definition

𝜙(𝑚𝑖)=∑𝑗=1𝑛𝐸𝑖𝑗𝑚𝑗𝑖=1…𝑛

for some 𝐸∈Mat𝑛(𝐴). Consider the matrix

𝐵(𝑋)=𝑋𝐼𝑛−𝐸∈Mat𝑛(𝐴[𝑋])

Then we may define 𝐵(𝜙)≔𝐵(𝑋)ev𝜙∈Mat𝑛(End𝐴(𝑀)) pointwise, so given by

𝐵(𝜙)𝑖𝑗=𝛿𝑖𝑗𝜙−𝐸𝑖𝑗1𝑀𝑖,𝑗=1…𝑛

By definition

∑𝑗𝐵(𝜙)𝑖𝑗𝑚𝑗=𝜙(𝑚𝑖)−∑𝑗𝐸𝑖𝑗𝑚𝑗=0

Formally we have a group action

Mat𝑛(End𝐴(𝑀))×𝑀𝑛→𝑀𝑛𝐹⋅(𝑥1⋮𝑥𝑛)→(∑𝑗=1𝑛𝐹1𝑗(𝑥𝑗)⋮∑𝑗=1𝑛𝐹𝑛𝑗(𝑥𝑗))

such that (𝐸𝐹)𝑣=𝐸(𝐹𝑣) (check). And we have shown that

𝐵(𝜙)(𝑚1⋮𝑚𝑛)=(0⋮0)

Using (3.5.21), premultiply by the adjugate matrix to show that

det(𝐵(𝜙))𝐼𝑛(𝑚1⋮𝑚𝑛)=(0⋮0)

and det(𝐵(𝜙))∈End𝐴(𝑀) annihilates 𝑚1,…,𝑚𝑛 and therefore 𝑀.

Finally we claim that 𝑃(𝑋)≔det(𝐵(𝑋))∈𝐴[𝑋] is a suitable monic polynomial. We see that

𝑃(𝜙)≔ev𝜙(det(𝐵(𝑋)))=det(𝐵(𝑋)ev𝜙)=det(𝐵(𝜙))=0

When 𝑀 is a finite free 𝐴-module then we may choose {𝑚1,…,𝑚𝑛} to be a basis, and then the matrix 𝐸 equals [𝜙]𝑇 as required.

Finally when 𝜙(𝑀)⊆𝔞𝑀 then (3.18.3) shows we may choose the coefficients 𝐸𝑖𝑗 to be in 𝔞. It’s clear that 𝑃(𝑋) then has non-leading coefficients in 𝔞. ∎
