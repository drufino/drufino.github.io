3.4.5 Tensor Product Exact Sequences

Proposition 3.4.28 (Tensor Product is Right Exact)

Consider an exact sequence of 𝐴-modules (i.e. such that 𝛽 is surjective and ker(𝛽)=Im(𝛼))

𝑀⟶𝛼𝑁⟶𝛽𝑃→0

Then the corresponding sequence of 𝐴-modules

𝑀⊗𝐴𝑍⟶𝛼⊗1𝑍𝑁⊗𝐴𝑍⟶𝛽⊗1𝑍𝑃⊗𝐴𝑍→0

is exact. Similarly the sequence

𝑍⊗𝐴𝑀⟶1𝑍⊗𝛼𝑍⊗𝐴𝑁⟶1𝑍⊗𝛽𝑍⊗𝐴𝑃→0

is exact.

Proof. Trivially the submodule Im(𝛽⊗1𝑍) contains elementary tensors and therefore equals 𝑃⊗𝐴𝑍. In otherwords 𝛽⊗1𝑍 is surjective. where 𝜓 is yet to be defined. For a given 𝑝∈𝑃 and 𝑧∈𝑍 consider the subset

ℐ︀𝑝,𝑧≔{𝑛⊗𝑧|𝑛∈𝑁,𝛽(𝑛)=𝑝}⊂𝑁⊗𝐴𝑍

Proposition 3.4.29 (Quotient of a Tensor Product)

Consider exact sequences

0→𝑀′⟶𝑖𝑀⟶𝜋1𝑀/𝑀′→00→𝑁′⟶𝑗𝑁⟶𝜋2𝑁/𝑁′→0

Then there is an exact sequence

(𝑀′⊗𝑁)⊕(𝑀⊗𝑁′)⟶𝛼𝑀⊗𝑁⟶𝜋1⊗𝜋2𝑀/𝑀′⊗𝑁/𝑁′→0
(𝑚′⊗𝑛,𝑚⊗𝑛′)→𝑖(𝑚′)⊗𝑛+𝑚⊗𝑗(𝑛′)→𝑚―⊗𝑛―→0

In particular there is a canonical isomorphism

(𝑀⊗𝑁)/(𝑀′⊗𝑁+𝑀⊗𝑁′)≅𝑀/𝑀′⊗𝑁/𝑁′
Proof. Observe 𝜋1⊗𝜋2=(𝜋1⊗1𝑁/𝑁′)∘(1𝑀⊗𝜋2) by comparison on elementary tensors. By (3.4.28) each of these maps is surjective, and so the composite is surjective. Evidently (𝜋1⊗𝜋2)∘(𝑖⊗1𝑁)=0 and (𝜋1⊗𝜋2)∘(1𝑀⊗𝑗)=0 by checking elementary tensors. This in particular means that (𝜋1⊗𝜋2)∘𝛼=0 where 𝛼 is the first map. Therefore Im(𝛼)⊆ker(𝜋1⊗𝜋2) and it suffices to demonstrate the reverse inclusion. Suppose 𝑧∈ker(𝜋1⊗𝜋2). This means precisely that (1𝑀⊗𝜋2)(𝑧)∈ker(𝜋1⊗1𝑁/𝑁′)=Im(𝑖⊗1𝑁/𝑁′) by (3.4.28). Therefore (1𝑀⊗𝜋2)(𝑧)=(𝑖⊗1𝑁/𝑁′)(𝑦) for 𝑦∈𝑀′⊗𝑁/𝑁′. By (3.4.28) again 𝑦=(1𝑀′⊗𝜋2)(𝑥) for 𝑥∈𝑀′⊗𝑁 and (1𝑀⊗𝜋2)(𝑧)=(𝑖⊗𝜋2)(𝑥). Therefore (1𝑀⊗𝜋2)(𝑧−(𝑖⊗1𝑁)(𝑥))=0 and 𝑧−(𝑖⊗1𝑁)(𝑥)=(1𝑀⊗𝑗)(𝑤) for some 𝑤∈𝑀⊗𝑁′. Therefore we conclude 𝑧∈Im(𝛼) as required. ∎
