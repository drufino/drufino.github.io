3.5.7 Localisation at a prime ideal

Definition 3.5.39 (Localisation at a prime ideal)

Let 𝐴 be a ring and 𝔭◃𝐴 a prime ideal. Then 𝑆≔𝐴∖𝔭 is a saturated multiplicatively closed subset, and we define

𝐴𝔭≔(𝐴∖𝔭)−1𝐴

For an ideal 𝔞◃𝐴 write the extended ideal

𝔞𝐴𝔭≔𝑆−1𝔞=𝔞𝑒.
Definition 3.5.40 (Relative localisation at a prime ideal)

Let 𝜙:𝐴→𝐵 be a ring homomorphism and 𝔭◃𝐴 a prime ideal. Define

𝐵𝔭≔𝜙(𝐴∖𝔭)−1𝐵

For an ideal 𝔞◃𝐴 write

𝔞𝐵𝔭≔(𝜙(𝔞)𝐵)𝐵𝔭

Observe 𝐵𝔭=0⟺𝔭⊊ker(𝜙), so we would typically assume ker(𝜙)⊆𝔭.

Proposition 3.5.41 (Relative Localisation as a Tensor Product)

Let 𝜙:𝐴→𝐵 be a ring homomorphism (so 𝐵 is an 𝐴-algebra) and 𝔭◃𝐴 a prime ideal, 𝑆≔𝐴∖𝔭. Then 𝐵𝔭 is an 𝐴𝔭-algebra via 𝑎𝑠↦𝜙(𝑎)/𝜙(𝑠) (well-defined as 𝜙(𝑆)⊆𝐵𝔭⋆ already), and there is an isomorphism of 𝐴𝔭-algebras

Ψ:𝐵⊗𝐴𝐴𝔭⟶∼𝐵𝔭,𝑏⊗𝑎𝑠↦𝜙(𝑎)𝑏𝜙(𝑠),

with inverse Θ(𝑏/𝜙(𝑠))≔𝑏⊗(1𝑠).

Proof. Drafted by Claude Sonnet, reviewed by David Rufino.

Ψ is well-defined. For fixed 𝑎𝑠∈𝐴𝔭, 𝑏↦𝜙(𝑎)𝑏/𝜙(𝑠) is 𝐴-linear in 𝑏 (𝐵𝔭 being an 𝐴-module via 𝜙); for fixed 𝑏∈𝐵, if 𝑎𝑠=𝑎′𝑠′ in 𝐴𝔭 then 𝑠″(𝑎𝑠′−𝑎′𝑠)=0 for some 𝑠″∈𝑆, so applying 𝜙 and multiplying by 𝑏, 𝜙(𝑠″)(𝜙(𝑎)𝜙(𝑠′)−𝜙(𝑎′)𝜙(𝑠))𝑏=0 in 𝐵, giving 𝜙(𝑎)𝑏/𝜙(𝑠)=𝜙(𝑎′)𝑏/𝜙(𝑠′) in 𝐵𝔭; and 𝑎𝑠↦𝜙(𝑎)𝑏/𝜙(𝑠) is 𝐴-linear in 𝑎𝑠 (direct check, as in (3.5.5)). So the map 𝐵×𝐴𝔭→𝐵𝔭, (𝑏,𝑎𝑠)↦𝜙(𝑎)𝑏/𝜙(𝑠), is 𝐴-bilinear, and induces Ψ by the universal property of the tensor product (3.4.4).

Θ is well-defined. Write 𝑎⋅𝑏≔𝜙(𝑎)𝑏 for the 𝐴-module structure on 𝐵. If 𝑏/𝜙(𝑠)=𝑏′/𝜙(𝑠′) in 𝐵𝔭 then 𝑠″⋅(𝑠′⋅𝑏)=𝑠″⋅(𝑠⋅𝑏′) in 𝐵 for some 𝑠″∈𝑆; using (𝑎⋅𝑏)⊗𝑥=𝑏⊗(𝑎𝑥) (the defining relation of ⊗𝐴) twice,

𝑏⊗1𝑠=(𝑠″𝑠′)⋅𝑏⊗1𝑠″𝑠′𝑠=(𝑠″⋅(𝑠′⋅𝑏))⊗1𝑠″𝑠′𝑠=(𝑠″⋅(𝑠⋅𝑏′))⊗1𝑠″𝑠𝑠′=𝑏′⊗1𝑠′,

so Θ is well-defined on 𝐵𝔭.

Θ and Ψ are mutually inverse. ΨΘ(𝑏/𝜙(𝑠))=Ψ(𝑏⊗1𝑠)=𝜙(1)𝑏/𝜙(𝑠)=𝑏/𝜙(𝑠). And ΘΨ(𝑏⊗𝑎𝑠)=Θ(𝜙(𝑎)𝑏/𝜙(𝑠))=(𝜙(𝑎)𝑏)⊗(1𝑠)=𝑏⊗(𝑎𝑠) (the same tensor relation as above), so ΘΨ is the identity on simple tensors, hence (by linearity) on all of 𝐵⊗𝐴𝐴𝔭.

Ψ is 𝐴𝔭-linear. For 𝑎′𝑠′∈𝐴𝔭,

Ψ(𝑎′𝑠′⋅(𝑏⊗𝑎𝑠))=Ψ(𝑏⊗𝑎𝑎′𝑠𝑠′)=𝜙(𝑎𝑎′)𝑏𝜙(𝑠𝑠′)=𝜙(𝑎′)𝜙(𝑠′)⋅𝜙(𝑎)𝑏𝜙(𝑠)=𝑎′𝑠′⋅Ψ(𝑏⊗𝑎𝑠),

using the 𝐴𝔭-algebra structure on 𝐵𝔭 displayed above; so Ψ is 𝐴𝔭-linear on simple tensors, hence (by linearity) on all of 𝐵⊗𝐴𝐴𝔭.

Multiplicativity. By the algebra tensor product structure on 𝐵⊗𝐴𝐴𝔭, (𝑏⊗𝑎𝑠)(𝑏′⊗𝑎′𝑠′)=𝑏𝑏′⊗𝑎𝑎′𝑠𝑠′, and

Ψ((𝑏⊗𝑎𝑠)(𝑏′⊗𝑎′𝑠′))=𝜙(𝑎𝑎′)𝑏𝑏′𝜙(𝑠𝑠′)=𝜙(𝑎)𝑏𝜙(𝑠)⋅𝜙(𝑎′)𝑏′𝜙(𝑠′)=Ψ(𝑏⊗𝑎𝑠)Ψ(𝑏′⊗𝑎′𝑠′),

so Ψ is multiplicative on simple tensors, hence (by linearity) a ring homomorphism – and so, with 𝐴𝔭-linearity above, an isomorphism of 𝐴𝔭-algebras. ∎

Proposition 3.5.42

Let 𝐴 be a ring and 𝔭 a prime ideal. Consider the localisation 𝐴→𝐴𝔭. Then there is an order-preserving bijection between (prime) ideals contained in 𝔭 and (prime) ideals of 𝐴𝔭

{𝔮◃𝐴|𝔮⊆𝔭}↔{𝔮◃𝐴𝔭}𝔮→𝔮𝐴𝔭

In particular 𝐴𝔭 is a local ring with unique maximal ideal 𝔭𝐴𝔭.

Proof. Clearly 𝔮∩(𝐴∖𝔭)=∅⟺𝔮⊆𝔭, so the result follows from (3.5.19). ∎
Proposition 3.5.43

Let 𝐴 be a ring and 𝔭 a prime ideal. Then the map

Frac(𝐴/𝔭)→∼𝐴𝔭/𝔭𝐴𝔭𝑎―𝑏―→𝑎/1―𝑏/1―=(𝑎𝑏)―

is a ring isomorphism

Proof. Consider the ring map 𝐴→𝐴𝔭→𝐴𝔭/𝔭𝐴𝔭. Then this has kernel 𝔭ec=𝔭 (3.5.18), so may be factored as an injective map 𝐴/𝔭↪︎𝐴𝔭/𝔭𝐴𝔭. By (3.5.42) 𝐴𝔭/𝔭𝐴𝔭 is a field so by universal property of localisation (3.5.5) this extends to an injective map Frac(𝐴/𝔭)↪︎𝐴𝔭/𝔭𝐴𝔭 of the given form. We may verify directly that it is surjective. ∎
Proposition 3.5.44 (Localisation at prime is direct limit of localisation at an element)

Let 𝐴 be a ring and 𝔭 a prime ideal then

𝑆𝔭≔𝐴∖𝔭=⋃𝑓∈𝐴∖𝔭𝑆𝑓―

Therefore there are canonical morphisms (for 𝑓∉𝔭)

𝑖𝑆𝑓𝑆𝔭:𝐴𝑓→𝐴𝔭

Furthermore the family of multiplicatively closed sets {𝑆𝑓}𝑓∉𝔭 (resp. rings {𝐴𝑓}𝑓∉𝔭) form a directed system under the relation 𝑆≺𝑇⟺𝑆⊆𝑇―. Therefore we have a canonical ring homomorphism

lim⟶𝑓∉𝔭𝐴𝑓→𝐴𝔭

which is an isomorphism.

Proof. As 𝐴∖𝔭 is a saturated multiplicatively closed set we have 𝑓∈𝐴∖𝔭⟺𝑆𝑓⊆𝐴∖𝔭⟺𝑆𝑓―⊆𝐴∖𝔭. Therefore the expression for 𝑆𝔭 follows.

The family of multiplicatively closed subsets is a directed system because 𝑆𝑓⊆𝑆𝑓𝑔―. To see this note 𝑓𝑔∈𝑆𝑓𝑔―⟹𝑓∈𝑆𝑓𝑔―⟹𝑆𝑓⊆𝑆𝑓𝑔―.

The final isomorphism follows because we can decompose it into two maps

lim⟶𝑓∉𝔭𝐴𝑓⟶∼lim⟶𝑓∉𝔭𝑆𝑓―−1𝐴⟶∼𝐴𝔭

The first is an isomorphism by (3.5.24) and the second by (3.5.8), in light of the first statement. ∎

Proposition 3.5.45 (Quotient commutes with Localisation at Prime ideal)

Let 𝐴 be a ring, 𝔞 an ideal and 𝔭⊃𝔞 a prime ideal. Then there is an isomorphism of non-zero rings

𝐴𝔭/𝔞𝔭≅(𝐴/𝔞)𝔭/𝔞𝑎𝑠+𝔞𝔭→𝑎+𝔞𝑠+𝔞
Proof. Let 𝜋:𝐴→𝐴/𝔞 be the quotient map then we claim 𝜋(𝐴∖𝔭)=𝜋(𝐴)∖𝜋(𝔭). As 𝜋 is surjective we only need to show that 𝜋−1(𝜋(𝔭))=𝔭. That is if 𝜋(𝑎)=𝜋(𝑏) and 𝑏∈𝔭 then 𝑎∈𝔭. However this follows by assumption because 𝔞⊂𝔭. Therefore we may apply (3.5.25). ∎
Proposition 3.5.46 (Minimal Primes consist of Zero Divisors)

Let 𝔭 be a proper prime ideal minimal over 𝔞. Then for every 𝑥∈𝔭 there exists 𝑎∈𝐴∖𝔞 such that 𝑥𝑎∈𝔞.

In particular a minimal prime ideal consists of zero-divisors.

Proof. Observe that 𝔭/𝔞 is a proper minimal prime ideal of 𝐴/𝔞 by (3.3.56). Then the first statement is equivalent to showing every 𝑥∈𝔭/𝔞 is a zero divisor and we may reduce to the case 𝔞=(0).

Consider the ring 𝐴𝔭, it has a unique maximal ideal 𝔭𝐴𝔭 which is also minimal by (3.5.42). Every other prime ideal is contained in 𝔭𝐴𝔭 by (3.3.37), and therefore equal by minimality. Therefore (0)𝐴𝔭=𝔭𝐴𝔭 by (3.3.46). Suppose 𝑥∈𝔭 then 𝑥𝑛1=0 and 𝑡𝑥𝑛∈𝔞 for 𝑡∉𝔭. Choose 𝑛 minimal subject to this condition. Then 𝑛≥1 because 𝑡∉𝔞, and so 𝑎=𝑡𝑥𝑛−1 has the required properties. ∎
