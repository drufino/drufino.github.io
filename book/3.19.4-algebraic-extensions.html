3.19.4 Algebraic Extensions

Proposition 3.19.36 (Minimal Polynomial)

If 𝛼∈𝐾/𝑘 is algebraic then there is a unique monic, irreducible polynomial 𝑚𝛼,𝑘(𝑋)∈𝑘[𝑋] such that 𝑚𝛼,𝑘(𝛼)=0. This is called the minimal polynomial of 𝛼 over 𝑘 and (𝑚𝛼,𝑘)=ker(ev𝛼).

In particular any polynomial 𝑓(𝑋)∈𝑘[𝑋] which has 𝛼 as a root, satisfies 𝑚𝛼,𝑘(𝑋)|𝑓(𝑋).

Proof. Let 𝔞=ker(ev𝛼). Since 𝑘[𝑋] is a PID it is of the form (𝑚𝛼,𝑘). As 𝛼 is algebraic it is non-zero. 𝑚𝛼,𝑘(𝑋) cannot be a constant, and therefore is not a unit.

We claim 𝑚𝛼,𝑘 is irreducible. If 𝑚𝛼,𝑘(𝑋)=𝑝(𝑋)𝑞(𝑋) then 𝑝,𝑞 are non-zero and either 𝑝(𝛼)=0 or 𝑞(𝛼)=0. If 𝑝(𝛼)=0 then 𝑚𝛼,𝑘|𝑝. As 𝑝|𝑚𝛼,𝑘 by (3.17.30) 𝑚𝛼,𝑘=𝜆𝑝. In particular deg(𝑚𝛼,𝑘(𝑋))=deg(𝑝(𝑋)) so deg(𝑞(𝑋))=0 and 𝑞(𝑋) is a unit (3.8.15). Therefore by definition 𝑚𝛼,𝑘(𝑋) is irreducible.

Dividing by the leading coefficient we may assume that this polynomial is monic. Suppose 𝑚′(𝑋) is another such irreducible monic polynomial. Then 𝑚𝛼|𝑚′. Since 𝑚𝛼 is not a unit, by definition of irreducible 𝑚′∼𝑚𝛼 whence 𝑚′=𝜆𝑚𝛼. Compare leading coefficients to find 𝜆=1 and 𝑚′=𝑚𝛼. ∎

Lemma 3.19.37
Let 𝐾/𝐸/𝑘 be extensions and 𝛼∈𝐾 algebraic over 𝑘. Then 𝛼 is algebraic over 𝐸.
Definition 3.19.38 (Conjugate elements)

Two elements 𝛼,𝛽∈𝐾 are said to be conjugate elements if they have the same minimal polynomial.

NB it’s necessary and sufficient that 𝑚𝛼,𝑘(𝛽)=0.

Proposition 3.19.39
Let 𝜎:𝐾/𝑘→𝐿/𝑘 be a field morphism and 𝛼∈𝐾. Then 𝑚𝛼,𝑘(𝑋)=𝑚𝜎(𝛼),𝑘(𝑋).
Proof. This follows from (3.19.20). ∎

Given an irreducible polynomial 𝑓∈𝑘[𝑋] it’s possible to construct an extension field 𝐾/𝑘 which has at least one root, as follows.

Proposition 3.19.40 (Construct simple extension)

Let 𝑓∈𝑘[𝑋] be an irreducible polynomial. Then (𝑓) is maximal and 𝐾≔𝑘[𝑋]/(𝑓) is a field extension with canonical structural morphism. Define 𝛼≔𝑋+(𝑓)

  • 𝑓(𝛼)=0
  • 𝐾=𝑘(𝛼) is a simple field extension and 𝑘(𝛼)=𝑘[𝛼]
  • 𝑚𝛼=𝑓/ℓ(𝑓) and deg(𝑚𝛼)=deg(𝑓)≕𝑛

  • 𝐾 is a finite-dimensional 𝑘-vector space with basis

    {1,𝛼,…,𝛼𝑛−1}

    .

Example 3.19.41
Take 𝑘=ℝ, 𝑓(𝑋)=𝑋2+1, then ℂ/ℝ=ℝ[𝑖]=ℝ[𝑋]/(𝑋2+1).

Proof. Consider the structural morphism 𝑖:𝑘→𝑘[𝑋] and canonical surjective homomorphism

𝜋:𝑘[𝑋]→𝑘[𝑋]/(𝑓)

and 𝛼=𝑋+(𝑓)=𝜋(𝑋). As 𝑘[𝑋] is a PID, 𝑓 irreducible implies (𝑓) maximal by (3.17.22) so 𝐾 is a field by (3.3.58). The composition 𝜋∘𝑖 makes 𝐾 into a 𝑘-algebra and hence a field extension. Furthermore 𝜋 is then by definition a 𝑘-algebra homomorphism.

Since 𝜋 is surjective every 𝛽∈𝐾 is represented as 𝜋(𝑝(𝑋))=(3.19.20)𝑝(𝜋(𝑋))=𝑝(𝛼). By (3.19.23) we see 𝐾=𝑘[𝛼]. Since 𝐾 is a field then 𝐾=𝑘[𝛼]=𝑘(𝛼) is simple by (3.19.29).

Similarly 𝑓(𝛼)=𝑓(𝜋(𝑋))=(3.19.20)𝜋(𝑓(𝑋))=0, so 𝛼 is a root of 𝑓. By (3.17.30) 𝑓/ℓ(𝑓) is irreducible and by uniqueness in (3.19.36) we have 𝑚𝛼=𝑓/ℓ(𝑓).

Given 𝛽=𝑝(𝛼), the division algorithm (…) yields

𝑝(𝑋)=𝑞(𝑋)𝑓(𝑋)+𝑟(𝑋)

with deg(𝑟)<deg(𝑓)=𝑛. Therefore 𝛽=𝑟(𝛼) and the given set is spanning. A non-trivial linear dependence yields a non-zero polynomial 𝑔(𝑋) such that 𝑔(𝛼)=0 and deg(𝑔)<deg(𝑓). But by definition of the minimal polynomial 𝑚𝛼|𝑔, which is a contradiction by comparing degrees. Therefore the given set is linearly independent and hence a basis. ∎

Conversely any simple algebraic extension is obtained in this way, as follows

Proposition 3.19.42 (Simple extension)

Let 𝑘(𝛼)/𝑘 be a simple extension. Then there is a canonical isomorphism of 𝑘-algebras

𝑘[𝑋]/(𝑚𝛼)⟶𝑘(𝛼)

under which 𝑋+(𝑚𝛼)→𝛼. Further 𝑘(𝛼) is a finite-dimensional vector space with basis

{1,𝛼,…,𝛼𝑛−1}

where 𝑛=deg(𝑚𝛼)=[𝑘(𝛼):𝑘] and 𝑘(𝛼)=𝑘[𝛼].

Proof. By (3.19.36), Definition (3.19.23) and (3.3.56) there is a canonical isomorphism 𝑘[𝑋]/(𝑚𝛼)→𝑘[𝛼] of 𝑘-algebras induced by the evaluation homomorphism ev𝛼:𝑘[𝑋]→𝐾. (3.19.40) shows that the image of this isomorphism, 𝑘[𝛼], is a field, whence 𝑘[𝛼]=𝑘(𝛼) by (3.19.29). Since a 𝑘-algebra isomorphism is a-fortiori a 𝑘-vector space isomorphism it maps a basis to a basis. The result follows from (3.19.40) as the basis thus defined is the image of the basis in the proposition under the specified isomorphism. ∎
Definition 3.19.43 (Degree of an algebraic element)

Let 𝐾/𝑘 be an algebraic extension and 𝛼∈𝐾. Then define

deg𝑘(𝛼)≔deg𝑚𝛼,𝑘=[𝑘(𝛼):𝑘]

We may show the following

Proposition 3.19.44 (Finitely generated by algebraic ⟹ finite and algebraic)

Let 𝐾=𝑘(𝛼1,…,𝛼𝑛)/𝑘 be a field extension such that 𝛼𝑖 is algebraic. Then 𝐾/𝑘 is a finite algebraic extension. Furthermore

𝑘[𝛼1,…,𝛼𝑛]=𝑘(𝛼1,…,𝛼𝑛)

In particular a finitely-generated algebraic extension is finite.

Proof. We write 𝐾𝑖=𝑘(𝛼1,…,𝛼𝑖). Then we have a tower

𝐾=𝐾𝑛/…/𝐾0=𝑘

such that 𝐾𝑖=𝐾𝑖−1(𝛼𝑖) is a simple algebraic extension. By (3.19.42) 𝐾𝑖/𝐾𝑖−1 is finite. Therefore by (3.19.17) 𝐾/𝑘 is finite. By (3.19.34) it’s also algebraic. For the second statement we may proceed inductively. Note we have

𝑘[𝛼1,…,𝛼𝑖+1]=(3.19.24)𝑘[𝛼1,…,𝛼𝑖][𝛼𝑖+1]=𝑘(𝛼1,…,𝛼𝑖)[𝛼𝑖+1]=(3.19.42)𝑘(𝛼1,…,𝛼𝑖)(𝛼𝑖+1)=(3.19.28)𝑘(𝛼1,…,𝛼𝑖+1)

The second equality is simply the inductive hypothesis. ∎

Corollary 3.19.45
Let 𝐾/𝑘 be a field extension then the algebraic elements form a subfield.
Proof. For any two algebraic elements 𝛼,𝛽∈𝐾 we have 𝑘(𝛼,𝛽) is an algebraic extension. ∎

The following is useful for reducing to cases of finite extensions where counting arguments work.

Lemma 3.19.46 (Reduce to finite extensions)

Let 𝐾/𝐸/𝑘 be a tower with 𝐸 algebraic over 𝑘. For every 𝛼∈𝐾 algebraic over 𝐸, there is some subfield 𝐸0⊂𝐸 such that

  • 𝐸0/𝑘 is finite
  • 𝛼 is algebraic over 𝐸0
  • 𝑚𝛼,𝐸=𝑚𝛼,𝐸0

Therefore 𝛼 is algebraic over 𝑘 iff it is algebraic over 𝐸 and 𝑚𝛼,𝐸0|𝑚𝛼,𝑘𝑖𝑘𝐸.

Proof. Suppose

𝑚𝛼,𝐸(𝑋)=𝑎0+𝑎1𝑋+…𝑎𝑛𝑋𝑛

Then define 𝐸0=𝑖𝑘𝐸(𝑘)(𝑎0,…,𝑎𝑛). By (3.19.44) 𝐸0/𝑘 is finite. Clearly 𝛼 is algebraic over 𝐸0 as it is a root of 𝑚𝛼,𝐸. By (3.19.36) 𝑚𝛼,𝐸0|𝑚𝛼,𝐸 as elements of 𝐸0[𝑋] and 𝑚𝛼,𝐸|𝑚𝛼,𝐸0. Therefore 𝑚𝛼,𝐸0=𝑚𝛼,𝐸.

By (3.19.42) 𝐸0(𝛼)/𝐸 is finite, therefore 𝐸0(𝛼)/𝑘 is finite. By (3.19.34) 𝐸0(𝛼)/𝑘 is algebraic, whence 𝛼 is algebraic over 𝑘. The last statement follows from (3.19.36) again. ∎

Corollary 3.19.47 (Algebraic Extensions are Directed Unions of Finite Subextensions)
Let 𝐸/𝑘 be algebraic. Then the finite subextensions of 𝐸/𝑘 are directed under inclusion, and 𝐸 is their union.
Proof. Given 𝛼∈𝐸, taking 𝐾=𝐸 in (3.19.46) (with 𝛼 trivially algebraic over 𝐸) gives a finite subextension 𝐸0⊂𝐸 over which 𝛼 is algebraic; then 𝐸0(𝛼)/𝐸0 is finite (3.19.42), hence 𝐸0(𝛼)/𝑘 is finite too, a finite subextension of 𝐸 containing 𝛼. Hence 𝐸 is the union of its finite subextensions. Given two finite subextensions 𝐸1,𝐸2⊂𝐸, their compositum 𝐸1𝐸2⊂𝐸 is finitely-generated and algebraic over 𝑘, hence finite (3.19.44), and contains both, so the finite subextensions are directed under inclusion. ∎
Corollary 3.19.48
𝐾/𝐸 and 𝐸/𝑘 are both algebraic if and only if 𝐾/𝑘 is.
Proof. One direction is (3.19.37). The converse follows from the previous result. ∎

We may prove the first lifting theorem

Proposition 3.19.49 (Lifting to simple extensions)

Let 𝑘(𝛼)/𝑘 be a simple algebraic extension and 𝐿/𝑘 a field extension such that 𝑚𝛼,𝑘 has a root in 𝐿. Then there exists a morphism 𝜎:𝑘(𝛼)/𝑘→𝐿/𝑘.

More precisely there is a bijective mapping

Mor𝑘(𝑘(𝛼),𝐿)⟶{𝛽∈𝐿|𝑚𝛼,𝑘(𝛽)=0}𝜎→𝜎(𝛼)

and 𝜎(𝑘(𝛼))=𝑘(𝜎(𝛼)). In particular if 𝑚𝛼,𝑘 is separable and splits completely in 𝐿 then there are precisely deg(𝑚𝛼)=(3.19.42)[𝑘(𝛼):𝑘] such extensions.

Proof. Observe 𝑚𝛼,𝑘(𝜎(𝛼))=(3.19.20)𝜎(𝑚𝛼,𝑘(𝛼))=0. Therefore the mapping is well-defined. By (3.19.31) it is injective. We claim it is also surjective. By (3.19.42) there is a 𝑘-algebra isomorphism

𝑘[𝑋]/(𝑚𝛼,𝑘)⟶𝑘(𝛼)

Similarly for 𝛽∈𝑇 there is a 𝑘-algebra isomorphism

𝑘[𝑋]/(𝑚𝛽,𝑘)⟶𝑘(𝛽)

We are done if 𝑚𝛼,𝑘=𝑚𝛽,𝑘. But 𝑚𝛼,𝑘 is monic, irreducible and has 𝛽 as a root. So this follows from uniqueness of the minimal polynomial in (3.19.36). The final statement follows from (3.19.11) ∎

We may use this to generalize to arbitrary extensions, but we require that the minimal polynomials split completely in order for the inductive step to work.

Proposition 3.19.50 (Generic Lifting Theorem)

Let 𝐾/𝑘 be an algebraic field extension such that 𝐾=𝑘({𝛼𝑖}𝑖∈𝐼) and 𝐿/𝑘 a field extension such that 𝑚𝛼𝑖,𝑘(𝑋) splits completely in 𝐿 for all 𝑖∈𝐼.

Then there exists a morphism 𝜎:𝐾/𝑘→𝐿/𝑘.

Furthermore given 𝛼∈𝐾 and 𝛽∈𝐿 any root of 𝑚𝛼,𝑘(𝑋) we may choose 𝜎 such that 𝜎(𝛼)=𝛽.

Proof. If 𝐾/𝑘 is finite then we may proceed by induction on [𝐾:𝑘], using (3.19.49) and applying a similar argument to below.

For the general case we may consider the poset of morphisms 𝜎:𝐾′/𝑘→𝐿/𝑘 for subfields 𝐾′/𝑘⊂𝐾/𝑘 ordered by consistency. It is non-empty by considering 𝐾′=𝑖𝑘𝐾(𝑘). By Zorn’s Lemma it has a maximal element, (𝐾′,𝜎′). It’s enough to show that 𝐾′=𝐾.

If 𝛼𝑖∈𝐾′ for all 𝑖∈𝐼 then 𝐾′=𝐾 and we are done. Otherwise choose 𝛼=𝛼𝑖∉𝐾′. By (…) 𝑚𝛼,𝐾′(𝑋)|𝑚𝛼,𝑘(𝑋). By (3.19.9) 𝑚𝛼,𝐾′(𝑋) splits in 𝐿 (because 𝑚𝛼,𝑘(𝑋) does). Therefore by (3.19.49) there is a morphism 𝜎:𝐾′(𝛼)/𝐾′→(𝐿/𝐾′,𝜎′). Note that by definition 𝜎|𝐾′=𝜎′ and 𝐾′⊊𝐾′(𝛼), contradicting maximality.

For the final part we may consider the poset consisting only of morphisms such that 𝜎(𝛼)=𝛽. By (3.19.49) the poset is non-empty, and the same argument works. ∎
