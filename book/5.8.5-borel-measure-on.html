5.8.5 Borel Measure on ℝ

Proposition 5.8.29

Let ℛ︀ be the semi-ring of half-open intervals over ℝ. Then the set function

𝜇:ℛ︀→[0,∞]

given by

𝜇([𝑎,𝑏))=𝑏−𝑎,

is countably additive and 𝜎-finite.

Proof. Evidently 𝜇 is finitely additive, therefore by (5.8.25) it is sufficient to show that it is countably subadditive. Suppose that

⋃𝑖=1∞[𝑎𝑖,𝑏𝑖)=[𝑎,𝑏)

Let 𝜀<𝑏−𝑎. Then [𝑎,𝑏−𝜀] is compact by (5.7.7) and 𝑈𝑖≔(𝑎𝑖−𝛿2𝑖,𝑏𝑖) is open for every 𝛿>0. By definition of compactness there is some finite integer 𝑛 such that

[𝑎,𝑏̃]≔[𝑎,𝑏−𝜀]⊂⋃𝑖=1𝑛(𝑎𝑖−𝛿2𝑖,𝑏𝑖)≕(𝑎̃𝑖,𝑏𝑖)

By reordering we assume that 𝑎̃1<…<𝑎̃𝑛. As [𝑎,𝑏̃] is an interval we must have 𝑎̃𝑖+1<𝑏𝑖. Further we may assume that 𝑏𝑖<𝑏𝑖+1 (otherwise we may omit the interval (𝑎̃𝑖,𝑏𝑖)). By construction we must also have 𝑎̃1<𝑎 and 𝑏̃<𝑏𝑛.

Consequently

𝑏−𝑎−𝜀=𝑏̃−𝑎≤𝑏𝑛−𝑎̃1=𝑏1−𝑎̃1+∑𝑖=1𝑛−1(𝑏𝑖+1−𝑏𝑖)≤∑𝑖=1𝑛(𝑏𝑖−𝑎̃𝑖)≤∑𝑖=1𝑛(𝑏𝑖−𝑎𝑖)+𝛿≤∑𝑖=1∞(𝑏𝑖−𝑎𝑖)+𝛿

This shows

𝜇([𝑎,𝑏))=𝜇(⋃𝑖=1∞[𝑎𝑖,𝑏𝑖))≤∑𝑖=1∞𝜇([𝑎𝑖,𝑏𝑖))

as required. ∎

Proposition 5.8.30

Let 𝐷⊂ℝ be an additive subgroup containing ℚ. Then ℬ︀(ℝ) is generated by any of the following families of intervals

  1. (𝑎,𝑏]𝑎<𝑏∈𝐷
  2. (𝑎,𝑏)𝑎<𝑏∈𝐷
  3. [𝑎,𝑏)𝑎<𝑏∈𝐷
  4. (𝑎,∞)𝑎∈𝐷
  5. [𝑎,∞)𝑎∈𝐷
  6. (−∞,𝑎]𝑎∈𝐷
  7. (−∞,𝑎)𝑎∈𝐷

Further there is a unique measure

𝜇:ℬ︀(ℝ)→[0,∞]

such that

𝜇([𝑎,𝑏))=𝑏−𝑎

Proof. Denote the corresponding 𝜎-algebras by ℱ︀1,…,ℱ︀7. Observe that by the Archimedean property

(𝑎,𝑏)=⋃𝑛=1∞(𝑎,𝑏−1𝑛]=⋃𝑛=1∞[𝑎+1𝑛,𝑏)[𝑎,𝑏)=⋂𝑛=1∞(𝑎−1𝑛,𝑏)(𝑎,𝑏]=⋂𝑛=1∞(𝑎,𝑏+1𝑛)

so that ℱ︀1=ℱ︀2=ℱ︀3. We may argue similarly ℱ︀4=ℱ︀5=ℱ︀6=ℱ︀7. Finally

(𝑎,𝑏)=(𝑎,∞)∩(−∞,𝑏)

and

(𝑎,∞)=⋃𝑛=1∞(𝑎,𝑛)

to show these are equal. The measure 𝜇 exists by (5.8.29) and (5.8.26). ∎

Proposition 5.8.31 (Borel Measure on the Extended Real Line)

Let ℬ︀(ℝ♯) be the Borel Measure on the extended real line. Then ℬ︀(ℝ♯) consists of sets of the form

{𝐴⊂ℝ♯|𝐴∩ℝ∈ℬ︀(ℝ)}

or equivalently

{𝐴∪𝐵|𝐴∈ℬ︀(ℝ),𝐵∈𝒫︀({−∞,∞})}

Furthermore it is generated by any of the following families

  1. (𝑎,∞]
  2. [𝑎,∞]
  3. [−∞,𝑎)
  4. [−∞,𝑎]

for 𝑎∈𝐷 where 𝐷⊂ℝ is an additive subgroup containing ℚ.

Proof. If 𝑈⊂ℝ is open, then it is also open in ℝ♯. Therefore ℬ︀(ℝ)⊂ℬ︀(ℝ♯). Evidently ℝ, {∞}, {−∞}∈ℬ︀(ℝ♯) as they are open and closed respectively. This shows that the given family equals ℬ︀(ℝ♯).

Denote by ℱ︀1,…,ℱ︀4 the 𝜎-algebras generated by the respective families of rays. Note that ℱ︀1=ℱ︀4 and ℱ︀2=ℱ︀3 by taking complements. Further {∞}∈ℱ︀1∩ℱ︀2 by taking intersections as 𝑎→∞ and similarly {−∞}∈ℱ︀4∩ℱ︀3. This shows that they also contain either the open rays (𝑎,∞) or the semi-open rays [𝑎,∞). Therefore they contain ℬ︀(ℝ) by (5.8.30). Then by the characterisation in the first part we see that this equals ℬ︀(ℝ♯). ∎
