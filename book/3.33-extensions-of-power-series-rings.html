3.33 Extensions of Power Series Rings

We record the Weierstrass division lemma for formal power series, used in the analysis of finite extensions of 𝑘((𝑡)) below.

Remark 3.33.1
[Che51] (Ch. II, §6) treats the case of (3.33.4) where the ramification index 𝑒 is prime to char(𝑘), obtaining the sharper conclusion that 𝑡 itself is an 𝑒-th power in 𝑘((𝑢)) (a literal Puiseux expansion 𝑘((𝑡1/𝑒))), via Hensel’s lemma applied to 𝑊𝑒−𝑣 for the unit 𝑣≔𝑡/𝑢𝑒. That argument is unavailable when char(𝑘)=𝑝∣𝑒: the derivative 𝑒𝑊𝑒−1 vanishes identically, so 𝑊𝑒−𝑣 has no simple root to lift, and 𝑡 need not be an 𝑒-th power at all (an Artin-Schreier extension gives a counterexample). (3.33.4) instead only produces 𝑡=𝑢𝑒𝑐 for some unit 𝑐, obtained by solving 𝜏𝐺(𝜏)=𝑢𝑒 by direct coefficient-stabilisation rather than root extraction; this sidesteps the characteristic obstruction entirely, at the cost of the sharper radical form, which is in any case not needed below.
Lemma 3.33.2 (Free Basis for a Distinguished Power Series)

Let 𝑅 be a commutative ring, 𝑚≥1 an integer, 𝑤∈𝑅[[𝑋]]⋆, and 𝑇≔𝑋𝑚𝑤∈𝑅[[𝑋]] (so the coefficients of 𝑇 below degree 𝑚 vanish, and its coefficient of 𝑋𝑚 is 𝑤(0)∈𝑅⋆). Then every 𝑝∈𝑅[[𝑋]] is uniquely

𝑝=∑𝑖=0𝑚−1𝑓𝑖(𝑇)𝑋𝑖

for power series 𝑓0,…,𝑓𝑚−1∈𝑅[[𝑌]] (substituted at 𝑌=𝑇, well-defined as 𝑇 has zero constant term (3.10.1)).

Proof. Drafted by Claude Sonnet 5, reviewed by David Rufino.

Existence. Define 𝑝(0)≔𝑝 and, recursively for 𝑗≥0: writing 𝑝(𝑗)=∑𝑛𝑝𝑛(𝑗)𝑋𝑛, set 𝑟𝑗≔∑𝑖=0𝑚−1𝑝𝑖(𝑗)𝑋𝑖 (the degree-{<}𝑚 truncation) and 𝑞̃𝑗≔∑𝑛≥0𝑝𝑛+𝑚(𝑗)𝑋𝑛 (the shift), so 𝑝(𝑗)=𝑞̃𝑗𝑋𝑚+𝑟𝑗; put 𝑝(𝑗+1)≔𝑞̃𝑗𝑤−1∈𝑅[[𝑋]] (using 𝑤∈𝑅[[𝑋]]⋆ (3.10.2)), so that 𝑝(𝑗)=𝑝(𝑗+1)𝑇+𝑟𝑗. Iterating,

𝑝=𝑝(𝐽+1)𝑇𝐽+1+∑𝑗=0𝐽𝑟𝑗𝑇𝑗for every𝐽≥0.

As 𝑤(0)∈𝑅⋆, 𝑇𝐽+1=𝑋𝑚(𝐽+1)𝑤𝐽+1 has zero coefficients below degree 𝑚(𝐽+1); so for each fixed 𝑛, the coefficient of 𝑋𝑛 in 𝑝 equals the coefficient of 𝑋𝑛 in ∑𝑗=0𝐽𝑟𝑗𝑇𝑗 once 𝑚(𝐽+1)>𝑛, and this common value no longer depends on 𝐽 thereafter. Writing 𝑟𝑗=∑𝑖=0𝑚−1𝛾𝑗,𝑖𝑋𝑖, set 𝑓𝑖(𝑌)≔∑𝑗≥0𝛾𝑗,𝑖𝑌𝑗∈𝑅[[𝑌]]; the above shows 𝑝=∑𝑖=0𝑚−1𝑓𝑖(𝑇)𝑋𝑖, matching the coefficient of every 𝑋𝑛.

Uniqueness. Suppose ∑𝑖=0𝑚−1𝑓𝑖(𝑇)𝑋𝑖=0 with not every 𝑓𝑖 zero. Let 𝑗0≥0 be minimal such that some 𝑓𝑖 has a nonzero coefficient of 𝑌𝑗0, and let 𝑖⋆ be the least such 𝑖, so 𝑓𝑖⋆(𝑌)=𝛾𝑌𝑗0+𝑂(𝑌𝑗0+1) for some 0≠𝛾∈𝑅, while 𝑓𝑖(𝑌)=𝑂(𝑌𝑗0+1) for 𝑖<𝑖⋆. Then 𝑓𝑖(𝑇)=𝑂(𝑋𝑚(𝑗0+1)) for 𝑖<𝑖⋆, and 𝑓𝑖⋆(𝑇)=𝛾𝑇𝑗0+𝑂(𝑋𝑚(𝑗0+1))=𝛾𝑤(0)𝑗0𝑋𝑚𝑗0+𝑂(𝑋𝑚𝑗0+1); as 𝑚𝑗0+𝑖⋆<𝑚(𝑗0+1), the coefficient of 𝑋𝑚𝑗0+𝑖⋆ in ∑𝑖𝑓𝑖(𝑇)𝑋𝑖 is exactly 𝛾𝑤(0)𝑗0, nonzero (a nonzero element of 𝑅 times a unit is nonzero) – contradicting the sum being 0. ∎

Corollary 3.33.3 (Specialisation Compatibility)
In the setting of (3.33.2), let 𝜙:𝑅→𝑆 be a homomorphism of commutative rings, extended coefficientwise to 𝜙:𝑅[[𝑋]]→𝑆[[𝑋]]. Then 𝜙(𝑤)∈𝑆[[𝑋]]⋆, and the decomposition of 𝜙(𝑝) relative to 𝜙(𝑇)=𝑋𝑚𝜙(𝑤) is 𝜙(𝑝)=∑𝑖=0𝑚−1𝜙(𝑓𝑖)(𝜙(𝑇))𝑋𝑖, where 𝜙(𝑓𝑖)∈𝑆[[𝑌]] denotes 𝜙 applied coefficientwise to 𝑓𝑖.
Proof. If 𝑤𝑣=1 then 𝜙(𝑤)𝜙(𝑣)=1, so 𝜙(𝑤)∈𝑆[[𝑋]]⋆. Substitution of a zero-constant-term series into another is computed, coefficient by coefficient, by a fixed finite combination of additions and multiplications of the two series’ coefficients; as 𝜙 is additive and multiplicative, it commutes with this computation, so applying 𝜙 to 𝑝=∑𝑖𝑓𝑖(𝑇)𝑋𝑖 gives 𝜙(𝑝)=∑𝑖𝜙(𝑓𝑖)(𝜙(𝑇))𝑋𝑖. This exhibits 𝜙(𝑝) in the form guaranteed unique by (3.33.2) over 𝑆, so it is the decomposition of 𝜙(𝑝). ∎
Lemma 3.33.4 (Finite Separable Extensions of 𝑘((𝑡)) are Power Series Fields)
Let 𝑘 be algebraically closed, 𝐹≔𝑘((𝑡)), and 𝐸/𝐹 a finite separable extension of degree 𝑛. Then there are 𝑢∈𝐸, an element 𝜏∈𝑢𝑘[[𝑢]] with 𝜏=𝑢𝑛𝑐 for some 𝑐∈𝑘[[𝑢]]⋆, and a field isomorphism Ψ:𝐸→∼𝑘((𝑢)) with Ψ|𝐹 the substitution 𝑡↦𝜏 and Ψ(𝑢)=𝑢. In particular 1,𝑢,…,𝑢𝑛−1 is an 𝐹-basis of 𝐸.

Proof. Drafted by Claude Sonnet 5, reviewed by David Rufino.

Step 1: a DVR. Let 𝒪︀𝐹≔𝑘[[𝑡]] and let 𝐶 be the integral closure of 𝒪︀𝐹 in 𝐸. By (3.24.26) 𝐶 is a finite 𝒪︀𝐹-module, and by (3.32.9) it is Dedekind. By lying-over (3.24.17) there is a prime 𝔭⊂𝐶 with 𝔭∩𝒪︀𝐹=(𝑡). Put 𝐵≔𝐶𝔭, a DVR with fraction field 𝐸, and let 𝑒≔𝑣𝐵(𝑡)≥1.

Step 2: residue field and valuation. 𝐶/𝔭 is a domain, finite over 𝒪︀𝐹/(𝑡)=𝑘, so 𝑘↪︎𝐶/𝔭 is injective and integral, hence 𝐶/𝔭 is a field (3.24.14), finite over 𝑘. As 𝐵=𝐶𝔭, (3.6.43) gives 𝐵/𝔪𝐵≅Frac(𝐶/𝔭)=𝐶/𝔭 (𝐶/𝔭 already a field); as 𝑘 is algebraically closed, 𝑘→𝐵/𝔪𝐵 is an isomorphism. Every 𝑎∈𝒪︀𝐹× is a unit of 𝐵, so 𝑣𝐵(𝑥)=𝑒𝑣𝐹(𝑥) for 𝑥∈𝐹×. Let 𝑢 be a uniformiser of 𝐵, so 𝑡=𝑢𝑒𝑤 with 𝑤∈𝐵⋆.

Step 3: expansions. Every 𝑏∈𝐵 can be written 𝑏=∑𝑖<𝑒𝑓𝑖𝑢𝑖 with 𝑓𝑖∈𝑘[[𝑡]]. We show by induction on 𝑁≥0 that 𝑏≡∑𝑖<𝑒(∑𝑗<𝑁𝑐𝑖𝑗𝑡𝑗)𝑢𝑖(mod𝑡𝑁𝐵) for some 𝑐𝑖𝑗∈𝑘. The case 𝑁=0 is trivial. For the inductive step, write the current remainder as 𝑡𝑁𝑦 with 𝑦∈𝐵 (inductive hypothesis); since 𝐵/𝑢𝐵=𝑘, repeated division by 𝑢 gives 𝑦=∑𝑖<𝑒𝑑𝑖𝑢𝑖+𝑢𝑒𝑦′ with 𝑑𝑖∈𝑘, 𝑦′∈𝐵 (there is no need to iterate past degree 𝑒−1: stop as soon as 𝑒 terms are extracted). Using 𝑢𝑒=𝑡𝑤−1,

𝑡𝑁𝑦=∑𝑖<𝑒(𝑡𝑁𝑑𝑖)𝑢𝑖+𝑡𝑁+1(𝑤−1𝑦′),

with 𝑤−1𝑦′∈𝐵; so taking 𝑐𝑖,𝑁≔𝑑𝑖 advances the induction, with new remainder 𝑡𝑁+1(𝑤−1𝑦′)∈𝑡𝑁+1𝐵. Put 𝑓𝑖≔∑𝑗𝑐𝑖𝑗𝑡𝑗∈𝑘[[𝑡]]. Then 𝑧≔𝑏−∑𝑖𝑓𝑖𝑢𝑖 satisfies 𝑧∈𝑡𝑁𝐵 for every 𝑁, so 𝑣𝐵(𝑧)≥𝑒𝑁 for all 𝑁 and 𝑧=0.

Step 4: basis and degree. Every element of 𝐸 has the form 𝑡−𝑁𝑏 with 𝑏∈𝐵, so Step 3 gives 𝐸=∑𝑖<𝑒𝐹𝑢𝑖. These elements are independent: if ∑𝑖<𝑒𝑓𝑖𝑢𝑖=0 with 𝑓𝑖∈𝐹 not all zero, the valuations 𝑣𝐵(𝑓𝑖𝑢𝑖)=𝑒𝑣𝐹(𝑓𝑖)+𝑖 are distinct modulo 𝑒 for the nonzero 𝑓𝑖, so the minimum is attained exactly once, a contradiction. Hence 𝑛=𝑒, and 1,…,𝑢𝑒−1 is a basis. Moreover 𝐵=⨁𝑖<𝑒𝑘[[𝑡]]𝑢𝑖, by the valuation estimate min𝑖(𝑒𝑣𝐹(𝑓𝑖)+𝑖)=𝑣𝐵(𝑏)≥0 applied to 𝑏=∑𝑖𝑓𝑖𝑢𝑖 with 𝑓𝑖∈𝐹.

Step 5: the minimal polynomial. Write 𝑤−1=∑𝑖<𝑒𝑔𝑖𝑢𝑖 with 𝑔𝑖∈𝑘[[𝑡]] (Step 3). The constant 𝑔0(0) is the residue of 𝑤−1, so 𝑔0(0)∈𝑘⋆. From 𝑢𝑒=𝑡𝑤−1, 𝑢 is a root of

𝑃(𝑌)≔𝑌𝑒−𝑡∑𝑖<𝑒𝑔𝑖(𝑡)𝑌𝑖∈𝐹[𝑌].

Since 𝐹[𝑢]=𝐸 has degree 𝑒 over 𝐹, 𝑃 is the minimal polynomial of 𝑢, and 𝐸≅𝐹[𝑌]/(𝑃) with 𝑌↦𝑢.

Step 6: solving for 𝜏. For 𝑠∈𝑢𝑘[[𝑢]] put 𝐺(𝑠)≔∑𝑖<𝑒𝑔𝑖(𝑠)𝑢𝑖∈𝑘[[𝑢]], where 𝑔𝑖(𝑠) is the substitution (well defined as 𝑠 has zero constant term). Since 𝐺(𝑠)≡𝑔0(0)(mod𝑢), 𝐺(𝑠) is a unit. Define 𝑇(𝑠)≔𝑢𝑒𝐺(𝑠)−1∈𝑢𝑒𝑘[[𝑢]]. For 𝑠≡𝑠′(mod𝑢𝑁) we have 𝑔𝑖(𝑠)−𝑔𝑖(𝑠′)∈(𝑠−𝑠′)𝑘[[𝑢]]⊆𝑢𝑁𝑘[[𝑢]], hence 𝐺(𝑠)≡𝐺(𝑠′)(mod𝑢𝑁) and 𝑇(𝑠)≡𝑇(𝑠′)(mod𝑢𝑁+𝑒). Starting from 𝑠0≔0 and setting 𝑠𝑚+1≔𝑇(𝑠𝑚), the sequence satisfies 𝑠𝑚+1≡𝑠𝑚(mod𝑢𝑚+𝑒), so each coefficient stabilises. Let 𝜏∈𝑢𝑘[[𝑢]] be the coefficientwise limit. Then 𝜏≡𝑇(𝜏)(mod𝑢𝑁) for every 𝑁, so 𝜏=𝑇(𝜏), i.e. 𝜏𝐺(𝜏)=𝑢𝑒. Also 𝜏=𝑢𝑒𝑐 with 𝑐∈𝑘[[𝑢]]⋆, since 𝜏𝐺(𝜏)=𝑢𝑒 and 𝐺(𝜏) is a unit.

Step 7: the homomorphism. The substitution 𝑡↦𝜏 defines a ring homomorphism 𝜎:𝑘[[𝑡]]→𝑘[[𝑢]] ((3.10.11), as 𝜏 has zero constant term), and since 𝜏 is a unit times 𝑢𝑒 it extends to a field homomorphism 𝜎:𝐹→𝑘((𝑢)). The homomorphism 𝐹[𝑌]→𝑘((𝑢)), 𝑌↦𝑢, extending 𝜎, kills 𝑃:

𝜎(𝑃)(𝑢)=𝑢𝑒−𝜏∑𝑖<𝑒𝑔𝑖(𝜏)𝑢𝑖=𝑢𝑒−𝜏𝐺(𝜏)=0.

So it factors through 𝐸≅𝐹[𝑌]/(𝑃), giving a field homomorphism Ψ:𝐸→𝑘((𝑢)) with Ψ|𝐹=𝜎 and Ψ(𝑢)=𝑢. It is injective, as 𝐸 is a field.

Step 8: surjectivity. Since 𝜏=𝑢𝑒𝑐 with 𝑐∈𝑘[[𝑢]]⋆, the Weierstrass division lemma (3.33.2) (with 𝑅≔𝑘, 𝑋≔𝑢, 𝑚≔𝑒, 𝑤≔𝑐, so 𝑇=𝜏) gives 𝑘[[𝑢]]=⨁𝑖<𝑒𝜎(𝑘[[𝑡]])𝑢𝑖. Multiplying by powers of 𝜏 gives 𝑘((𝑢))=∑𝑖<𝑒𝜎(𝐹)𝑢𝑖=𝜎(𝐹)[𝑢]. But imΨ=Ψ(𝐹[𝑢])=𝜎(𝐹)[𝑢], so Ψ is surjective.

Hence Ψ:𝐸→∼𝑘((𝑢)) is an isomorphism of fields over 𝐹, with Ψ(𝑢)=𝑢 and Ψ(𝑡)=𝜏=𝑢𝑛𝑐. ∎

Corollary 3.33.5 (Uniqueness of the Extended Valuation)
In the setting of (3.33.4), let 𝐶 be the integral closure of 𝑘[[𝑡]] in 𝐸. Then 𝐶 has exactly one prime 𝔭 lying over (𝑡), the DVR 𝐶𝔭 is the unique valuation ring of 𝐸 containing 𝑘[[𝑡]] whose maximal ideal contracts to (𝑡), and its ramification index is [𝐸:𝐹].

Proof. Drafted by Claude Sonnet 5, reviewed by David Rufino.

Let 𝔮 be any prime of 𝐶 over (𝑡), which exists by lying-over. Steps 2–4 of the proof of (3.33.4) apply verbatim to the DVR 𝐵≔𝐶𝔮 (which has residue field 𝑘), and give 𝑒𝔮=𝑣𝐵(𝑡)=[𝐸:𝐹]=𝑛.

Now apply the Ramification Formula (3.32.16) to the AKLB situation 𝑘[[𝑡]]⊂𝐶 over 𝐸/𝐹 (both Dedekind by (3.32.9), and 𝐸/𝐹 separable), at the prime (𝑡):

∑𝔮∣(𝑡)𝑒𝔮𝑓𝔮=[𝐶/𝑡𝐶:𝑘]=𝑛.

Each 𝑓𝔮=[𝐶/𝔮:𝑘] is finite, and 𝐶/𝔮 is a field algebraic over 𝑘, hence 𝑓𝔮=1. So ∑𝔮𝑒𝔮=𝑛. By the previous paragraph each term equals 𝑛, so there is exactly one prime 𝔭 over (𝑡) and 𝑒𝔭=𝑛.

For uniqueness of the valuation ring, let 𝑉⊆𝐸 be a valuation ring with 𝑘[[𝑡]]⊆𝑉 and 𝔪𝑉∩𝑘[[𝑡]]=(𝑡). By (3.25.14), 𝐶⊆𝑉. Then 𝔪𝑉∩𝐶 is a prime of 𝐶 over (𝑡), hence equals 𝔭. Every element of 𝐶∖𝔭 lies outside 𝔪𝑉, so is a unit of 𝑉; hence 𝐶𝔭⊆𝑉. Since 𝐶𝔭 is a valuation ring of 𝐸 and valuation rings are maximal for domination among proper subrings of 𝐸 (3.25.2), 𝑉=𝐶𝔭. ∎
