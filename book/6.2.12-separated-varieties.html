6.2.12 Separated Varieties

Proposition 6.2.63

Let 𝜙1,𝜙2:𝑋⇉𝑌 be regular morphisms of varieties with 𝑌 separated. Then the equaliser

{𝑥∈𝑋|𝜙1(𝑥)=𝜙2(𝑥)}

is a closed subset of 𝑋.

Proof. Let 𝑋=⋃𝑖=1𝑛𝑈𝑖 where 𝑈𝑖 are affine. Then the equaliser is the finite union of

{𝑥∈𝑈𝑖|𝜙1|𝑈𝑖(𝑥)=𝜙2|𝑈𝑖(𝑥)}

which is by definition closed. ∎

The following shows that the separated property is analogous to the Hausdorff property in the classical case (4.1.98).

Proposition 6.2.64

Let 𝑋 be a variety. Then 𝑋 is separated if and only if the diagonal

Δ𝑋≔{(𝑥,𝑥)|𝑥∈𝑋}

is a closed subvariety of 𝑋×𝑋.

Proof. Suppose that Δ𝑋 is closed. Then the equaliser of 𝜙1,𝜙2:𝑍⇉𝑋 is equal to (𝜙1×𝜙2)−1(Δ𝑋). By (6.2.61) 𝜙1×𝜙2 is regular and in particular continuous. We conclude the equaliser is closed.

Conversely Δ𝑋 is the equaliser of 𝜋1,𝜋2:𝑋×𝑋⇉𝑋 and so by (6.2.14) is closed. ∎

Proposition 6.2.65
Let 𝑋 be a separated variety and 𝑍⊂𝑋 a closed (resp. open) subvariety. Then 𝑍 is separated.
Proof. Then Δ𝑍=Δ𝑋∩(𝑍×𝑍). By (6.2.61) 𝑍×𝑍 is a closed (resp. open) subvariety of 𝑋×𝑋. Then the result follows by (6.2.64). ∎
Lemma 6.2.66

Let 𝜙:𝑋→𝑌 be a regular map of varieties, with 𝑌 separated. Then the graph

Γ𝜙={(𝑥,𝜙(𝑥))|𝑥∈𝑋}

is a closed subvariety of 𝑋×𝑌. Further there is a canonical isomorphism of varieties

𝑋→∼Γ𝜙𝑥→(𝑥,𝜙(𝑥))

Proof. Consider the regular map

(𝜙∘𝜋𝑋)×𝜋𝑌:𝑋×𝑌→𝑌×𝑌(𝑥,𝑦)→(𝜙(𝑥),𝑦)

Then

Γ𝜙=((𝜙∘𝜋𝑋)×𝜋𝑌)−1(Δ𝑌)

which is closed. The map 𝑋→Γ𝜙 is evidentally regular. Further Γ𝜙↪︎𝑋×𝑌→𝑋 is regular by (6.2.42) and (4.3.2). These are mutual inverses and so the last statement follows immediately. ∎

Proposition 6.2.67 (Intersection of Affines is Affine)

Let 𝑋 be a separated variety and 𝑈,𝑉 open subsets. Then there is a regular isomorphism followed by a closed immersion

𝑈∩𝑉⟶∼(𝑈×𝑉)∩Δ𝑋↪︎𝑈×𝑉𝑥→(𝑥,𝑥)

In particular when 𝑈,𝑉 are affine then so is 𝑈∩𝑉.

Proof. By (6.2.61) 𝑈×𝑉 is an open subvariety of 𝑋×𝑋.

(𝑈×𝑉)∩Δ𝑋=((𝑈∩𝑉)×𝑉)∩Δ𝑋 is the graph of the inclusion map 𝑈∩𝑉↪︎𝑉. By (6.2.66) it is a closed subvariety of (𝑈∩𝑉)×𝑉 and therefore of 𝑈×𝑉. The same result shows that the first map is a regular isomorphism.

When 𝑈,𝑉 are affine, so is 𝑈×𝑉. By (6.2.42) so is (𝑈×𝑉)∩Δ𝑋, and therefore 𝑈∩𝑉. ∎

Proposition 6.2.68 (Criteria for Separated Variety)
Let 𝑋 be a variety with an open cover ⋃𝑖=1𝑛𝑈𝑖. Then 𝑋 is separated if and only if (𝑈𝑖×𝑈𝑗)∩Δ𝑋 is a closed subvariety of 𝑈𝑖×𝑈𝑗 for all 𝑖,𝑗=1…𝑛.

Proof. Suppose 𝑋 is separated then (𝑈𝑖×𝑈𝑗)∩Δ𝑋 is a closed subvariety by (6.2.67).

Conversely if (𝑈𝑖×𝑈𝑗)∩Δ𝑋 is closed for all 𝑖,𝑗 then by (4.1.22)

Δ𝑋=⋃𝑖,𝑗(𝑈𝑖×𝑈𝑗)∩Δ𝑋

is closed, and therefore 𝑋 is separated by (6.2.64). ∎

Proposition 6.2.69 (Criteria for Separated Variety II)

Let 𝑋 be a variety with an affine open cover ⋃𝑖=1𝑛𝑈𝑖. Then 𝑋 is separated if and only if 𝑈𝑖∩𝑈𝑗 is affine and the 𝑘-algebra homomorphism

𝒪︀𝑋(𝑈𝑖)⊗𝑘𝒪︀𝑋(𝑈𝑗)⟶𝒪︀𝑋(𝑈𝑖∩𝑈𝑗)𝑓⊗𝑔⟶𝑓|𝑈𝑖∩𝑈𝑗·𝑔|𝑈𝑖∩𝑈𝑗

is surjective for all 𝑖,𝑗=1…𝑛

Proof. Suppose 𝑋 is separated, then by (6.2.67) 𝑈𝑖∩𝑈𝑗 is affine and there is a closed immersion

𝑈𝑖∩𝑈𝑗⟶∼(𝑈𝑖×𝑈𝑗)∩Δ𝑋↪︎𝑈𝑖×𝑈𝑗

By (6.2.46) the corresponding map of global sections

𝒪︀𝑈𝑖×𝑈𝑗(𝑈𝑖×𝑈𝑗)→𝒪︀𝑈𝑖∩𝑈𝑗(𝑈𝑖∩𝑈𝑗)(*)𝑓→𝑢→𝑓(𝑢,𝑢)

is surjective. Recall from (6.2.61) there is a surjective 𝑘-algebra homomorphism

𝒪︀𝑈𝑖(𝑈𝑖)⊗𝑘𝒪︀𝑈𝑖(𝑈𝑗)→𝒪︀𝑈𝑖×𝑈𝑗(𝑈𝑖×𝑈𝑗)𝑓⊗𝑔→𝑢→𝑓(𝑢)·𝑔(𝑢)

which composes to give the required map.

Conversely suppose that 𝑈𝑖∩𝑈𝑗 are affine and the given map is surjective then evidentally the map in (∗) is surjective. By (6.2.46) this shows that the map 𝑈𝑖∩𝑈𝑗↪︎𝑈𝑖×𝑈𝑗 is a closed immersion and in particular (𝑈𝑖×𝑈𝑗)∩Δ𝑋 is a closed subvariety of (𝑈𝑖×𝑈𝑗). Then we are done by (6.2.68). ∎
