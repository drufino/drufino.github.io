5.4.5 Convergent Series

Definition 5.4.24 (Series)

Let 𝑋 be a normed vector space and (𝑎𝑛) a sequence in 𝑋. The formal expression

∑𝑛=1∞𝑎𝑛

is called the series associated to this sequence. For such a sequence define the partial sum sequence as follows

𝑠𝑛≔∑𝑘=1𝑛𝑎𝑘

If the partial sum sequence converges then we say that the series converges and write

∑𝑛=1∞𝑎𝑛≔lim𝑛→∞𝑠𝑛

If the series

∑𝑛=1∞‖𝑎𝑛‖

converges then we say the original series converges absolutely.

The choice of terminology is justified in the case that 𝑋 is complete.

Proposition 5.4.25 (Absolute Convergence ⟹ Convergence)
Let 𝑋 be a complete normed vector space and ∑𝑛=0∞𝑎𝑛 a series which converges absolutely. Then the series converges in the usual sense.
Proof. The partial sums of the series ∑𝑛=1∞‖𝑎𝑛‖ are cauchy by (5.4.18). By the triangle inequality we may demonstrate that the partial sums of the series ∑𝑛=1∞𝑎𝑛 are cauchy. Then by completeness of 𝑋 the partial sums must also converge. ∎
Proposition 5.4.26

Let 𝑋 be a normed vector space and ∑𝑛=1∞𝑎𝑛 and ∑𝑛=1∞𝑏𝑛 two convergent sequences. Then the following series are convergent with limits

∑𝑛=1∞(𝑎𝑛+𝑏𝑛)=∑𝑛=1∞𝑎𝑛+∑𝑛=1∞𝑏𝑛∑𝑛=1∞𝜆𝑎𝑛=𝜆∑𝑛=1∞𝑎𝑛

where 𝜆∈𝐾.

Proposition 5.4.27
Let 𝑋 be a normed vector space and ∑𝑛=1∞𝑎𝑛 a convergent series. Then ‖𝑎𝑛‖→0.
Proof. Let 𝛼≔∑𝑛=1∞𝑎𝑛. Then by definition there exists 𝑁 such that 𝑛≥𝑁⟹‖∑𝑘=1𝑛𝑎𝑘−𝛼‖<𝜀. As this holds for 𝑛+1 we may use the triangle inequality to find that 𝑛≥𝑁+1⟹‖𝑎𝑛‖<2𝜀. As 𝜀 was arbitrary we find ‖𝑎𝑛‖→0 as required. ∎
Proposition 5.4.28 (Comparison Test)

Let 𝑋 be a complete normed vector space. Suppose (𝑎𝑛) is a sequence in 𝑋 and (𝑐𝑛) is a sequence in ℝ such that

  1. The series ∑𝑛=0∞𝑐𝑛 converges
  2. ‖𝑎𝑛‖≤𝑐𝑛 for 𝑛≥𝑁0

then the series ∑𝑛=0∞𝑎𝑛 converges absolutely.

Proof. By assumption 𝑏) (𝑐𝑛) is non-negative, and by (5.4.29) the sequence of partial sums ∑𝑘=0𝑛𝑐𝑘 is bounded. therefore so is the sequence ∑𝑘=0𝑛‖𝑎𝑘‖, and by the same result the series ∑𝑛=0∞‖𝑎𝑛‖ converges, in other words ∑𝑛=0∞𝑎𝑛 converges absolutely. ∎
Proposition 5.4.29 (Series of Positive Reals)

Let (𝑎𝑛) be a sequence of non-negative real numbers. Then the following are equivalent

  1. The sequence of partial sums 𝐴𝑛≔∑𝑘=0𝑛𝑎𝑘 is bounded
  2. The series ∑𝑛=0∞𝑎𝑛 converges (absolutely)
Proof. By (5.1.23) we always have 𝐴𝑛→sup𝐴𝑛∈ℝ♯, and the limit is finite iff 𝐴𝑛 is bounded. ∎
Proposition 5.4.30 (Geometric Series)

Suppose 0≤𝑥<1 is a real number, then

∑𝑛=0∞𝑥𝑛=11−𝑥

and in particular the partial sums are bounded by 11−𝑥.

Proof. It’s straightforward to show by induction that the partial sums are given explicitly by

∑𝑛=0𝑁𝑥𝑛=1−𝑥𝑁1−𝑥.

Then the result follows from (5.4.17). ∎

Proposition 5.4.31 (Root Test)

Let 𝑋 be a normed vector space. Given a series ∑𝑛=0∞𝑎𝑛, define 𝛼≔lim sup𝑛‖𝑎𝑛‖𝑛 Then

  1. if 𝛼<1 then the series converges absolutely
  2. if 𝛼>1 then the series diverges

Proof.

  1. Define 𝛼≔lim sup𝑛‖𝑎𝑛‖𝑛 and 𝛽≔1+𝛼2<1. Then by definition there is an integer 𝑁 such that

    𝑛≥𝑁⟹‖𝑎𝑛‖𝑛≤𝛼+1−𝛼2=𝛽⟹‖𝑎𝑛‖𝑛≤𝛽𝑛

    In particular for 𝑛≥𝑁

    ∑𝑘=0𝑛‖𝑎𝑘‖≤∑𝑛=0𝑁−1‖𝑎𝑘‖+∑𝑘=𝑁𝑛‖𝑎𝑘‖≤∑𝑘=0𝑁−1‖𝑎𝑘‖+∑𝑘=𝑁𝑛𝛽𝑘≤∑𝑘=0𝑁−1‖𝑎𝑘‖+∑𝑘=0∞𝛽𝑘

    where the last step follows from by (5.4.30). We conclude from (5.4.29) that the series converges absolutely.

  2. By (5.1.35) there exists a subsequence 𝑎𝑛𝑘 such that ‖𝑎𝑛𝑘‖𝑛𝑘→𝛼>1. Therefore we may find a subsequence such that ‖𝑎𝑛𝑘‖𝑛𝑘≥1+𝛼2>1⟹‖𝑎𝑛𝑘‖>1. This shows that ‖𝑎𝑛𝑘‖↛0, whence ‖𝑎𝑛‖↛0 by (…), and the series diverges by (5.4.27).

Proposition 5.4.32 (Ratio Test)

Let 𝐾 be a valued field and consider a series ∑𝑛=1∞𝑎𝑛. If

lim sup𝑛|𝑎𝑛+1𝑎𝑛|<1

then the series is convergent.

Proof. Let 𝛼≔lim sup|𝑎𝑛+1𝑎𝑛| and 𝛽=1+𝛼2. Then by assumption 𝛽<1, and there exists 𝑁 such that 𝑛≥𝑁⟹|𝑎𝑛+1𝑎𝑛|≤𝛽⟹|𝑎𝑁+𝑘|≤|𝑎𝑁|𝛽𝑘. We then see by Comparison Test (5.4.28) that the series is convergent. ∎
Proposition 5.4.33 (Cauchy Product of Series)

Let 𝑋 be a complete valued field and ∑𝑛=1∞𝑎𝑛, ∑𝑛=1∞𝑏𝑛, ∑𝑛=1∞𝑐𝑛 series such that

  1. one of ∑𝑛=1∞𝑎𝑛 and ∑𝑛=1∞𝑏𝑛 are absolutely convergent
  2. 𝑐𝑛≔∑𝑘=0𝑛𝑎𝑘𝑏𝑛−𝑘=∑𝑘=0𝑛𝑎𝑛−𝑘𝑏𝑘

then ∑𝑛=0∞𝑐𝑛 is absolutely convergent and

∑𝑛=0∞𝑐𝑛=(∑𝑛=1∞𝑎𝑛)(∑𝑛=1∞𝑏𝑛)

Proof. Denote the partial sums by 𝐴𝑛,𝐵𝑛 and 𝐶𝑛, and the limits by 𝐴,𝐵 respectively. Then evidently

𝐶𝑛=∑𝑖=0𝑛𝑎𝑛−𝑖𝐵𝑖

therefore

𝐶𝑛=∑𝑖=0𝑛𝑎𝑛−𝑖(𝐵𝑖−𝐵)+𝐴𝑛𝐵
