2.6.19 Kernels and Cokernels

Proposition 2.6.150 (Kernel)

Let 𝒞︀ be a pointed category (2.6.54). Suppose we have a sequence

ker(𝛼)⟶𝑖𝑋⟶𝛼𝑌

then the following are equivalent

  1. 𝑖 is the equaliser of (𝛼,0)
  2. 𝛼∘𝑖=0 and 𝛼∘𝑖′=0⟹∃!𝑒s.t.𝑖∘𝑒=𝑖′

In this case we say that (𝑖,ker(𝛼)) is the kernel of 𝛼

Lemma 2.6.151 (𝑓 monic ⟹ker(𝑓)=0)

Let 𝒞︀ be a pointed category and 𝑓:𝑋→𝑌 monic. Then ker(𝑓)=0.

Dually 𝑓 epic ⟹coker(𝑓)=0.

Proof. Evidentally 𝑓∘0=00,𝑋 and for any object 𝑋′ we have a unique morphism 0𝑋′,0:𝑋′→0, so satisfies the uniqueness property. ∎
Remark 2.6.152
The converse does not hold, for we may define a monoid homomorphism 𝑓:ℕ→ℕ given by 𝑓(𝑛)≔min(𝑛,1). Then 𝑓 is a homomorphism, ker(𝑓)={0} but 𝑓 is not injective.
Proposition 2.6.153 (Cokernel)

Let 𝒞︀ be a pointed category (2.6.54). Suppose we have a sequence

𝑋⟶𝛼𝑌⟶𝜋coker(𝛼)

  2. 𝜋∘𝛼=0 and 𝜋′∘𝛼=0⟹∃!ℎs.t.ℎ∘𝜋=𝜋′,

In this case we say that (𝜋,coker(𝛼)) is the cokernel of 𝑓

Lemma 2.6.154 (Kernels and Cokernels swap under 𝒞︀op)

Let 𝒞︀ be a pointed category and 𝛼:𝑋→𝑌 a morphism, with kernel ker(𝛼)⟶𝑖𝑋 and cokernel 𝑌⟶𝜋coker(𝛼). Regarding 𝑖 and 𝜋 as morphisms of 𝒞︀op,

(ker(𝛼),𝑖)is a cokernel of𝛼opin𝒞︀op,(coker(𝛼),𝜋)is a kernel of𝛼opin𝒞︀op.

Proof. Drafted by Claude Sonnet 5, reviewed by David Rufino.

The defining property of (ker(𝛼),𝑖) (2.6.150) – namely 𝛼∘𝑖=0, and for every 𝑖′:𝑋′→𝑋 with 𝛼∘𝑖′=0 there is a unique 𝑒:𝑋′→ker(𝛼) with 𝑖∘𝑒=𝑖′ – is, upon reversing every arrow, exactly the defining property (2.6.153) of (ker(𝛼),𝑖) as a cokernel of 𝛼op in 𝒞︀op. The dual statement follows similarly. ∎

Proposition 2.6.155 (Left Adjoints Preserve Cokernels)

Let 𝒞︀,𝒟︀ be pointed categories and 𝒞︀⇌𝐺𝐹𝒟︀ a pair of adjoint functors.

Then 𝐹 preserves cokernels and 𝐺 preserves kernels.

Proof. By (2.6.75) we see that 𝐹 and 𝐺 preserve zero morphisms. Therefore we are done by (2.6.142) since a cokernel of 𝑓 is just the coequaliser of (𝑓,0). ∎
