Proposition 5.10.1 (Isometry between $CC$ and $RR^2$)

The canonical map 𝜒:ℂ→ℝ2 is a Banach space isometry (using the square norm ‖⋅‖2). Furthermore define

Re(𝑧)≔𝜋1𝜒(𝑧)Im(𝑧)≔𝜋2𝜒(𝑧)

then

𝑧=Re(𝑧)⋅1+Im(𝑧)⋅𝑖

where ℂ is viewed as an ℝ vector space. One may also verify that complex multiplication may be represented in matrix form

𝜒(𝑧⋅𝑤)=(Re(𝑧)−Im(𝑧)Im(𝑧)Re(𝑧))𝜒(𝑤)𝑇
Proposition 5.10.2 (Cauchy-Riemann Equations)

Let 𝑈⊂ℂ and 𝑓:𝑈→ℂ a function. There exists unique functions 𝑢,𝑣:𝜒(𝑈)→ℝ such that

𝑓(𝑥+𝑦𝑖)=𝑢(𝑥,𝑦)+𝑣(𝑥,𝑦)𝑖

Further the following are equivalent

  1. 𝑓(𝑧) is continuously differentiable (as a complex function)

  2. 𝑢,𝑣 are 𝐶1 and satisfy the relations

    𝜕𝑢𝜕𝑥(𝑥,𝑦)=𝜕𝑣𝜕𝑦(𝑥,𝑦)𝜕𝑢𝜕𝑦(𝑥,𝑦)=−𝜕𝑣𝜕𝑥(𝑥,𝑦)

Proof. We may define 𝐹:𝜒(𝑈)→ℝ2 by 𝐹=𝜒∘𝑓∘𝜒−1 and 𝑢=𝜋1∘𝐹 and 𝑣=𝜋2∘𝐹.

a) ⟹ b) Fix 𝑧=𝑥+𝑦𝑖∈𝑈 and 𝑓′(𝑧)=𝑎+𝑏𝑖. Then for sufficiently small 𝑤=ℎ+𝑘𝑖

𝑓(𝑧+𝑤)−𝑓(𝑧)−𝑓′(𝑧)𝑤

is sublinear in 𝑤. By assumption 𝐹 is 𝐶1 and has Jacobian matrix (…) at (𝑥,𝑦) equal to

𝐽𝐹(𝑥,𝑦)≔(𝜕𝑢𝜕𝑥(𝑥,𝑦)𝜕𝑢𝜕𝑦(𝑥,𝑦)𝜕𝑣𝜕𝑥(𝑥,𝑦)𝜕𝑣𝜕𝑦(𝑥,𝑦))

and 𝐷𝑓(𝑥,𝑦)(ℎ,𝑘)=𝐽𝐹(𝑥,𝑦)(ℎ𝑘)𝑇. However by conjugating the above equation by 𝜒 we find that

𝐹(𝑥+ℎ,𝑦+𝑘)−𝐹(𝑥,𝑦)−(𝑎−𝑏𝑏𝑎)(ℎ𝑘)𝑇

(where 𝑓′(𝑧)=𝑎+𝑏𝑖) is sublinear (and using the fact 𝜒 is an isometry). Therefore the result then follows from the uniqueness of the total derivative 𝐷𝑓(𝑥,𝑦), and hence Jacobian 𝐽𝐹.

b) ⟹ a) Conversely if the conditions hold then we may define 𝑓′(𝑧)≔𝜕𝑢𝜕𝑥(𝜒(𝑧))+𝜕𝑣𝜕𝑥(𝜒(𝑧))𝑖 which is evidently continuous. Then by using matrix representation of complex multiplication (5.10.1) we find

𝜒[𝑓(𝑧+𝑤)−𝑓(𝑧)−𝑓′(𝑧)𝑤]=𝐹(𝜒(𝑧+𝑤))−𝐹(𝜒(𝑧))−𝐽′(𝜒(𝑧))𝜒(𝑤)𝑇

where

𝐽′(𝑥,𝑦)=(𝜕𝑢𝜕𝑥(𝑥,𝑦)−𝜕𝑣𝜕𝑥(𝑥,𝑦)𝜕𝑣𝜕𝑥(𝑥,𝑦)𝜕𝑢𝜕𝑥(𝑥,𝑦))

The conditions mean precisely that 𝐽′(𝑥,𝑦)=𝐽(𝑥,𝑦). As 𝜒 is an isometry we find that 𝑓(𝑧+𝑤)−𝑓(𝑧)−𝑓′(𝑧)𝑤 is sublinear in 𝑤 as required. ∎
