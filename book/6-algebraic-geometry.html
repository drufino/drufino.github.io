Chapter 6Algebraic Geometry

I develop theory of varieties over a non-algebraically closed field 𝑘 which more closely resembles “Faisceaux algébriques cohérents” [Ser55] than say EGA, and therefore is a bit more elementary. The main differences from the scheme-theoretic treatment, to be kept in mind below, are

  • Points and functions. The structure sheaf consists of functions into a single ambient field 𝑘―, and the underlying set consists of solutions to equations rather than Galois orbits of solutions. The closed points of the corresponding scheme are those of the Kolmogorov quotient 𝑋0, in which Galois conjugate points are identified, so correspondences with maximal ideals, valuation rings and places are statements about 𝑋0.
  • Irreducible subsets. By dealing with irreducible subsets rather than just points we avoid the use of generic points or a large ambient field of infinite transcendence degree.
  • Reducedness and base change. Varieties are reduced, and the base change of an affine variety is (𝑘[𝑋]⊗𝑘𝐾)red. Consequently a variety is geometrically integral as soon as it is geometrically irreducible, which for an integral variety means that 𝑘 is relatively separably closed in 𝑘(𝑋) (6.2.9). When 𝑘 is imperfect this is weaker than the scheme-theoretic notion, that 𝑘(𝑋)/𝑘 be a regular extension: over 𝔽𝑝(𝑡) the affine line over 𝔽𝑝(𝑡1/𝑝) is a variety here and is geometrically integral, but it is not geometrically reduced. For perfect 𝑘 the two notions agree.
  • Completeness. A variety 𝑋 is complete if for every irreducible closed subvariety 𝑍 and every valuation ring 𝑘⊂𝑅⊂𝑘(𝑍) there is a point 𝑧∈𝑍 with 𝒪︀𝑍,𝑧⊂𝑅. This implies that 𝑋 is universally closed (6.2.92) and is the form used throughout; projective varieties are complete (6.4.35).
