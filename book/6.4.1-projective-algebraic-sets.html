Proposition 6.4.1 (Projective Space is Functorial)

Let 𝜙:𝐾→𝐿 be an injective ring homomorphism Then there is a well-defined injective map

Proof. Evidently the map is well-defined. Suppose that

(𝜙(𝑥0):…:𝜙(𝑥𝑛))∼(𝜙(𝑥0′):…:𝜙(𝑥𝑛′))

Proposition 6.4.5 (Projective Subvariety)

Proof. There is a canonical graded surjective 𝑘-algebra homomorphism 𝜋:𝑘[𝑋0,…,𝑋𝑛]→𝑘[𝑋]hom by (3.11.12). Then 𝜋−1(𝔟) is graded (3.11.10) and radical (3.3.50) g). Proposition 6.4.8 (Irreducible Subsets)

Let 𝑋=𝑉+(𝔞)⊂ℙ𝑘𝑛 be a projective variety and 𝔟◃𝑘[𝑋] a homogenous radical ideal. Then a closed subvariety 𝑉+(𝔟)⊂𝑋 is integral iff 𝔟 is prime.

In particular ℙ𝑘𝑛(𝑘―) is integral.

Proof. Let 𝑌≔𝑉+(𝔟). Suppose that 𝑌 is not irreducible then 𝑌⊆𝑉+(𝔠)∪𝑉+(𝔡) is a non-trivial decomposition into closed subsets for 𝔠,𝔡 essential homogenous radical ideals for which 𝑌⊄𝑉+(𝔠)⟹𝔠⊄𝔟 and similarly 𝔡⊄𝔟 (see (4.1.61)). Choose 𝑓∈𝔠∖𝔟 and 𝑔∈𝔡∖𝔟. Then 𝑓𝑔 is zero on 𝑌 whence 𝑓𝑔∈𝔟. This shows that 𝔟 is not prime.

Conversely suppose 𝑌⊂𝑉+(𝔠)∪𝑉+(𝔡)=𝑉+(𝔠∩𝔡) then by (6.4.2) 𝔠𝔡⊂𝔠∩𝔡⊂𝔟. By (3.3.39) we have 𝔠⊂𝔟 or 𝔡⊂𝔟, whence 𝑌⊂𝑉+(𝔠) or 𝑌⊂𝑉+(𝔡). Therefore we conclude that 𝑌 is irreducible by (4.1.61).

Therefore ℙ𝑘𝑛(𝑘―) is irreducible by the special case both 𝔞 and 𝔟 being the zero ideals. ∎

  • irreducible components (of 𝑉+(𝔟)) correspond to minimal prime ideals (of 𝔟)
