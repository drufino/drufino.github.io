Proposition 6.3.20 (A Distinguished Basis for Der𝑘(𝐾,𝐾))
Let 𝑥∈𝐾 be transcendental over 𝑘 and assume 𝑘 is perfect. Then there is a unique 𝜕𝑥∈Der𝑘(𝐾,𝐾) with 𝜕𝑥(𝑥)=1, and 𝐷=𝐷(𝑥)⋅𝜕𝑥 for every 𝐷∈Der𝑘(𝐾,𝐾).

Proof. Drafted by Claude Sonnet 5, reviewed by David Rufino.

By (3.35.44), dim𝐾Der𝑘(𝐾)=1. So there is at least one non-zero 𝐷0. If 𝐷0(𝑥)=0 then it vanishes on 𝑘(𝑥) by (3.26.9), (3.26.10) and therefore on 𝐾 as 𝐾/𝑘(𝑥) is separable (3.19.103) (3.35.42), a contradiction. Therefore 𝐷0(𝑥)≠0 for any non-zero derivation 𝐷0. Therefore define 𝜕𝑥≔𝐷0(𝑥)−1𝐷 and by monogenicity we are done. ∎
