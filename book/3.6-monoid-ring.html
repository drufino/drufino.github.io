3.6 Monoid Ring

Definition 3.6.1 (Monoid Ring)

Let 𝐴 be a commutative ring and 𝐺 be a commutative monoid. We define the monoid ring to be the free abelian group

𝐴[𝐺]≔⨁𝑔∈𝐺𝐴

We may write a general element as a formal sum

∑𝑔∈𝐺𝑎𝑔𝑔

where all but finitely many 𝑎𝑔 are zero. Multiplication is defined extending the group operation. More precisely

(∑𝑔∈𝐺𝑎𝑔𝑔)⋅(∑𝑔∈𝐺𝑏𝑔𝑔)=∑𝑔∈𝐺(∑(ℎ,𝑘)|ℎ+𝑘=𝑔𝑎ℎ𝑏𝑘𝑔)

We may verify that the identity is simply 𝑒 and the multiplication is both associative and distributive.

When 𝐺 is an abelian group we say that 𝐴[𝐺] is the group ring. There is a canonical map 𝐴→𝐴[𝐺] making 𝐴[𝐺] into an 𝐴-algebra.

Proposition 3.6.2

Let 𝐴,𝐵 be commutative rings and 𝐺 a commutative monoid. Let 𝜙:𝐴→𝐵 be a ring homomorphism and 𝛼:𝐺→(𝐵,×) a monoid homomorphism. Then there is a unique homomorphism

𝜙⋉𝛼:𝐴[𝐺]→𝐵

such that (𝜙⋉𝛼)(𝑎𝑒)=𝜙(𝑎) and (𝜙⋉𝛼)(1𝐴𝑔)=𝛼(𝑔).

Proof. Define

(𝜙⋉𝛼)(∑𝑔∈𝐺𝑎𝑔𝑔)=∑𝑔∈𝐺𝑎𝑔≠0𝜙(𝑎𝑔)𝛼(𝑔)
