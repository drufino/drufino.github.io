3.13 Graded Rings

Definition 3.13.1 (Graded Ring over ℤ)

A ring 𝐴 (resp. 𝐴-module 𝑀) is said to be ℤ-graded (or simply graded) if it is graded in the sense of (3.12.2) with gradation group Δ=ℤ.

If 𝑖<0⟹𝐴𝑖=0 (resp. 𝑀𝑖=0) then we say 𝐴 (resp. 𝑀) is positively graded.

Note in this case that we have the multiplication formula

(𝑎⋅𝑏)𝑖=∑𝑗+𝑘=𝑖𝑎𝑗⋅𝑏𝑘
Definition 3.13.2

For an element 𝑎∈𝐴 we define the degree to be

deg(𝑎)≔max{𝑖|𝑎𝑖≠0}
Definition 3.13.3 (Essential and Irrelevant Ideals)

Let 𝐴 be a positively graded ring. Then we say a homogenous ideal 𝔞 is irrelevant if the following condition holds

∃𝑛0s.t.𝑛≥𝑛0⟹𝔞𝑛=𝐴𝑛

In particular 𝐴+ is irrelevant. Otherwise we say that it is essential.

Proposition 3.13.4 (Radical of Homogenous Ideal is Homogenous)
Let 𝐴 be a graded ring and 𝔞 a homogenous ideal. Then 𝔞 is also a homogenous ideal.

Proof. Define 𝔯≔𝔞 and fix 𝑥∈𝔯. Suppose

𝑥=∑𝑖=−∞∞𝑥𝑖

where 𝛿(𝑥𝑖)=𝑖 and 𝑥𝑘∈𝔯. Then

𝑥𝑘=∑𝑖=−∞∞𝑥𝑖𝑘

where 𝛿(𝑥𝑖𝑘)=𝑘𝑖. Without loss of generality we may assume 𝑘>0 so by assumption 𝑥𝑖𝑘∈𝔯 for all 𝑖 and therefore 𝑥𝑖∈𝔯. ∎

Proposition 3.13.5

Let 𝐴 be a graded ring and 𝔭 a homogenous ideal satisfying the following property

𝑎∈𝐴𝑛,𝑏∈𝐴𝑚,𝑎𝑏∈𝔭⟹𝑎∈𝔭or𝑏∈𝔭

𝑎𝑏=∑𝑖=−∞∞(∑(𝑗,𝑘)|𝑗+𝑘=𝑖𝑎𝑗𝑏𝑘)

(𝑎𝑏)𝑖≔∑(𝑗,𝑘)|𝑗+𝑘=𝑖𝑎𝑗𝑏𝑘∈𝔭𝑖

Let 𝑗′ (resp. 𝑘′) be the largest integer such that 𝑎𝑗′∉𝔭 (resp. 𝑏𝑘′∉𝔭). For all other pairs 𝑗″, 𝑘″ such that 𝑗′+𝑘′=𝑗″+𝑘″ we have by maximality 𝑎𝑗″𝑏𝑘″∈𝔭𝑖. Therefore we conclude 𝑎𝑗′𝑏𝑘′∈𝔭, which contradicts the original assumption. ∎

Proposition 3.13.6

Let 𝐴 be a positively graded ring. Then the following are equivalent

  1. 𝐴+ is a finitely-generated 𝐴-module
  2. 𝐴 is a finitely-generated 𝐴0-algebra

More precisely for homogenous elements 𝑥0,…,𝑥𝑛∈𝐴+ we have

𝐴+=∑𝑖=0𝑛𝐴𝑥𝑖⟺𝐴=𝐴0[𝑥0,…,𝑥𝑛]

In this case we say that 𝐴 is finitely generated. Further

𝐴+𝑚=(𝑥0,…,𝑥𝑛)𝑚

for all integers 𝑚>0.
