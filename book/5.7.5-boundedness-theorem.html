5.7.5 Boundedness Theorem

Proposition 5.7.8
Let 𝑋 be a compact topological space and 𝑓:𝑋→ℝ𝑘 be a continuous function. Then 𝑓 is bounded and attains its bounds.

Proof 1.. We may prove directly the case 𝑋=[𝑎,𝑏] and 𝑘=1.

Suppose 𝑓 is not bounded, then there is some sequence (𝑥𝑛) in 𝑋 such that |𝑓(𝑥𝑛)|≥𝑛. By choosing a convergent subsequence (5.7.5) we may assume that 𝑥𝑛→𝑥. Further 𝑋 is closed so 𝑥∈𝑋 (4.1.19). By continuity (5.4.13) 𝑓(𝑥𝑛)→𝑓(𝑥). Choose 𝑁1>|𝑓(𝑥)| and 𝑁2 such that 𝑛≥𝑁2⟹|𝑓(𝑥𝑛)−𝑓(𝑥)|<𝑁1−|𝑓(𝑥)|⟹|𝑓(𝑥𝑛)|<𝑁1. Then 𝑛≔max(𝑁1,𝑁2)⟹|𝑓(𝑥𝑛)|<𝑁1 and |𝑓(𝑥𝑛)|>𝑁1 a contradiction. ∎

Proof 2.. We may reduce to the case 𝑘=1 by considering the composition with the norm ‖⋅‖.

By (4.1.109) (or (4.1.112)) 𝑓(𝑋) is (sequentially) compact. Then by (5.7.7) 𝑓(𝑋) is closed and bounded.

Let 𝑦=sup𝑓(𝑋). By (5.1.18) there exists 𝑦𝑛→𝑦 with 𝑦𝑛∈𝑓(𝑋). Since 𝑓(𝑋) is closed we see 𝑦∈𝑓(𝑋) by (4.1.19) as required. ∎
