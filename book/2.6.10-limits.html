2.6.10 Limits

Definition 2.6.76
A functor 𝐹:𝐼→𝒞︀ where 𝐼 is a small category is referred to as a diagram
Definition 2.6.77 (Cones)

Let 𝐹:𝐼→𝒞︀ be a diagram. We say that the pair (𝐿,{𝜆𝑖:𝐿→𝐹(𝑖)}𝑖∈𝐼) is a cone over 𝐹 if

𝐹(𝛼)∘𝜆𝑖=𝜆𝑗for all morphisms𝛼:𝑖→𝑗

The cones for 𝐹 constitute a cone category (Δ↓𝐹) where a morphism 𝛼:(𝐿,{𝜆𝑖}𝑖∈𝐼)→(𝐿′,{𝜆𝑖′}𝑖∈𝐼) is just a morphism 𝛼:𝐿→𝐿′ such that 𝜆𝑖′∘𝛼=𝜆𝑖 for all 𝑖∈𝐼

Definition 2.6.78 (Small Limit)

Let 𝐹:𝐼→𝒞︀ be a diagram. A cone (𝐿,{𝜆𝑖}𝑖∈𝐼) over 𝐹 is a limit for 𝐹 if it is a terminal object (2.6.50) in the cone category.

Explicitly for every cone (𝐿′,{𝜆𝑖′}𝑖∈𝐼) there is a unique morphism 𝛼:𝐿′→𝐿 such that 𝜆𝑖∘𝛼=𝜆𝑖′.

We may denote this as lim𝐹.

Proposition 2.6.79

Let 𝐹:𝐼→𝒞︀ be a diagram, (𝐿,{𝜆𝑖}𝑖∈𝐼) a limit of 𝐹 and (𝐿′,{𝜆𝑖′}𝑖∈𝐼) a cone. Then the following are equivalent

  1. 𝐿′ is a limit for 𝐹
  2. there is an isomorphism of cones 𝐿′→∼𝐿
  3. there is a morphism of cones 𝐿′→𝐿 and precisely one (cone) endomorphism of 𝐿′.

In this case the (cone) isomorphism is the unique cone morphism.

Proof. By definition (𝐿,{𝜆𝑖}𝑖∈𝐼) is a terminal object of the cone category (Δ↓𝐹), so this is precisely (2.6.51) applied with ⋆≔(𝐿,{𝜆𝑖}𝑖∈𝐼) and 𝑋≔(𝐿′,{𝜆𝑖′}𝑖∈𝐼). ∎
Proposition 2.6.80 (Uniqueness of Limits)
Let 𝐹:𝐼→𝒞︀ be a diagram and (𝐿,{𝜆𝑖}𝑖∈𝐼) and (𝐿′,{𝜆𝑖′}𝑖∈𝐼) limits of 𝐹. Then there is a unique morphism 𝜓:𝐿→𝐿′ satisfying 𝜆𝑖′∘𝜓=𝜆𝑖, which is an isomorphism.
Proof. Since (𝐿′,{𝜆𝑖′}𝑖∈𝐼) is a limit for 𝐹, (2.6.79) gives a unique isomorphism of cones 𝜓:𝐿→𝐿′, that is, a unique isomorphism 𝜓 with 𝜆𝑖′∘𝜓=𝜆𝑖 for all 𝑖∈𝐼. ∎
Proposition 2.6.81 (Product)

Let 𝐼 be a discrete small category and {𝑋𝑖}𝑖∈𝐼 be a family of objects in 𝒞︀. Then there is a unique functor 𝐹:𝐼→𝒞︀ such that 𝐹(𝑖)=𝑋𝑖.

Consider an object 𝑋∈𝒞︀ and family of morphisms 𝜋𝑖:𝑋→𝑋𝑖, then the following are equivalent

  1. The cone (𝑋,{𝜋𝑖}𝑖∈𝐼) is a limit for 𝐹
  2. For any family of morphisms 𝜆𝑖:𝑋′→𝑋𝑖 there exists a unique 𝜆:𝑋′→𝑋 such that 𝜋𝑖∘𝜆=𝜆𝑖

  3. For all objects 𝑋′ the map

    Φ:Mor(𝑋′,𝑋)⟶∏𝑖∈𝐼Mor(𝑋′,𝑋𝑖)𝑓→𝜋𝑖∘𝑓

    is a bijection, i.e. (𝑋,Φ) represents the contravariant functor ∏𝑖∈𝐼Mor(−,𝑋𝑗).

In this case we say that (𝑋,{𝜋𝑖}𝑖∈𝐼) is a product for {𝑋𝑖}𝑖∈𝐼 and denote this by ∏𝑖∈𝐼𝑋𝑖.

For any family of morphisms 𝜆𝑖:𝑋′→𝑋𝑖 we write the induced morphism 𝜆:𝑋′→∏𝑖∈𝐼𝑋𝑖 as (𝜆𝑖)𝑖∈𝐼.

Proposition 2.6.82 (Functoriality of Product)

Let 𝐼 be a discrete small category and suppose that 𝒞︀ has 𝐼-shaped limits. Then we may define a map on objects

∏𝑖∈𝐼(−):𝒞︀𝐼→𝒞︀

Recall that a morphism ∏𝑖∈𝐼𝑋𝑖→∏𝑖∈𝐼𝑌𝑖 in 𝒞︀𝐼 is simply a family of morphisms (𝑓𝑖:𝑋𝑖→𝑌𝑖)𝑖∈𝐼 in 𝒞︀. Then we define

∏𝑖∈𝐼𝑓𝑖≔(𝑓𝑖∘𝜋𝑖)𝑖∈𝐼

which is the unique morphism satisfying

𝜋𝑖∘∏𝑖∈𝐼𝑓𝑖=𝑓𝑖∘𝜋𝑖

This makes ∏𝑖∈𝐼(−) into a functor.

Proposition 2.6.83 (Products of Monomorphisms)
Let 𝐼 be a discrete small category and {𝑓𝑖:𝑋𝑖→𝑌𝑖}𝑖∈𝐼 a family of monomorphisms such that ∏𝑖∈𝐼𝑋𝑖 and ∏𝑖∈𝐼𝑌𝑖 exist. Then ∏𝑖∈𝐼𝑓𝑖:∏𝑖∈𝐼𝑋𝑖→∏𝑖∈𝐼𝑌𝑖 is a monomorphism.

Proof. Drafted by Claude Sonnet 5, reviewed by David Rufino.

Suppose 𝑎,𝑏:𝑍→∏𝑖∈𝐼𝑋𝑖 satisfy (∏𝑖∈𝐼𝑓𝑖)∘𝑎=(∏𝑖∈𝐼𝑓𝑖)∘𝑏. For each 𝑖∈𝐼, composing with the projection 𝜋𝑖:∏𝑖∈𝐼𝑌𝑖→𝑌𝑖 and using 𝜋𝑖∘∏𝑖∈𝐼𝑓𝑖=𝑓𝑖∘𝜋𝑖 gives

𝑓𝑖∘(𝜋𝑖∘𝑎)=𝑓𝑖∘(𝜋𝑖∘𝑏)

and since 𝑓𝑖 is monic, 𝜋𝑖∘𝑎=𝜋𝑖∘𝑏. As this holds for every 𝑖∈𝐼, by the uniqueness clause of the universal property of the product ∏𝑖∈𝐼𝑋𝑖 (2.6.81) we conclude 𝑎=𝑏. ∎

Proposition 2.6.84 (Functoriality of Binary Product)

Let 𝑋,𝑋′,𝑌,𝑌′∈𝒞︀ be such that 𝑋×𝑌 and 𝑋′×𝑌′ exists. For 𝑓:𝑋→𝑋′ and 𝑔:𝑌→𝑌′ there exists a unique morphism

𝑓×𝑔:𝑋×𝑌→𝑋′×𝑌′

such that

𝜋𝑋′∘(𝑓×𝑔)=𝑓∘𝜋𝑋

and

𝜋𝑌′∘(𝑓×𝑔)=𝑔∘𝜋𝑌

If 𝒞︀ has all binary products then this determines a bifunctor

×:𝒞︀×𝒞︀→𝒞︀
Proposition 2.6.85 (Limits exist in Set)

Limits exist in 𝐒𝐞𝐭 more precisely for a diagram 𝐹:𝐼→𝐒𝐞𝐭

lim𝐹={(𝑥𝑖)𝑖∈𝐼∈∏𝑖∈𝐼𝐹(𝑖)|𝐹(𝛼)(𝑥𝑖)=𝑥𝑗for all𝛼:𝑖→𝑗}
Definition 2.6.86

Let 𝑈:𝒞︀→𝒟︀ be a functor and 𝐼 a small category.

We say that 𝑈 creates limits of shape 𝐼 if for all diagrams 𝐹:𝐼→𝒞︀ such that lim𝑈∘𝐹 exists then lim𝐹 exists and is the unique cone such that 𝑈(lim𝐹)=lim(𝑈∘𝐹).

We say that 𝑈 preserves limits of shape 𝐼 if whenever lim𝐹 exists in 𝒞︀ we have lim𝑈∘𝐹 exists in 𝒟︀ and is equal to 𝑈(lim𝐹).

If we restrict to the case 𝐼 is discrete (resp. 𝐼=𝟐) then we say that 𝑈 creates/preserves products (resp. equalisers).

Proposition 2.6.87 (Creates⇒Preserves)

Let 𝑈:𝒞︀→𝒟︀ which creates limits of shape 𝐼 and 𝒟︀ has all limits of shape 𝐼. Then 𝑈 preserves limits of shape 𝐼.

In particular this holds unconditionally for 𝒟︀=𝐒𝐞𝐭 since this has all limits.
