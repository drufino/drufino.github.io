3.3.3 Operations on Ideals

For this section we assume 𝐴 is a commutative ring.

Definition 3.3.26 (Product of ideal and module)

Let 𝑀 be an 𝐴-module and 𝔞◃𝐴 an ideal. Define

𝔞𝑀=⟨𝔞⋅𝑀⟩={∑𝑖=1𝑛𝑎𝑖𝑚𝑖|𝑎𝑖∈𝔞𝑚𝑖∈𝑀}
Proposition 3.3.27 (Lattice of Ideals)

Let 𝐴 be a ring and ℐ︀(𝐴) the set of ideals. Then ℐ︀(𝐴) forms a complete lattice ordered by inclusion with join and meets given by

⋀𝑖∈𝐼𝔞𝑖=⋂𝑖∈𝐼𝔞𝑖

and

⋁𝑖∈𝐼𝔞𝑖=⋂𝔞𝑖⊆𝔞𝔞≕∑𝑖𝔞𝑖≔{∑𝑖𝑎𝑖|𝑎𝑖∈𝔞𝑖}

This induces a corresponding closure operator

⟨−⟩:𝒫︀(𝐴)→ℐ︀(𝐴)

given by

⟨𝑋⟩≔⋂𝑋⊆𝔞𝔞={∑𝑗𝑎𝑗𝑥𝑗|𝑎𝑗∈𝐴𝑥𝑗∈𝑋}
Proposition 3.3.28

Let 𝐴 be a ring and 𝔞𝑖 a family of ideals. Then

⟨⋃𝑖∈𝐼𝔞𝑖⟩=∑𝑖∈𝐼𝔞𝑖
Definition 3.3.29 (Product of ideals)

The product of two ideals 𝔞𝔟 is

𝔞𝔟={∑𝑖=1𝑛𝑎𝑖𝑏𝑖|𝑎𝑖∈𝔞𝑏𝑖∈𝔟}

and is itself an ideal.

Definition 3.3.30 (Coprime)

We say two elements 𝑥,𝑦 of a commutative ring 𝐴 are co-prime if (𝑥,𝑦)=(1)⟺𝑎𝑥+𝑏𝑦=1 for some 𝑎,𝑏∈𝐴

We say a family of ideals {𝔞𝑖}𝑖∈𝐼 are co-prime if ∑𝑖∈𝐼𝔞𝑖=𝐴.

Definition 3.3.31 (Principal Ideal)

A principal ideal is an ideal generated by a single element

(𝑎)≔⟨{𝑎}⟩=𝐴𝑎
Lemma 3.3.32
A principal ideal (𝑎) is proper if and only if 𝑎∉𝐴⋆
Definition 3.3.33 (Maximal Ideal)
An ideal 𝔪◃𝐴 is maximal if it is both proper and not contained in another proper ideal.
Definition 3.3.34 (Prime Ideal)

An ideal 𝔭◃𝐴 is prime if it is both proper and satisfies the following property

𝑥𝑦∈𝔭⟹𝑥∈𝔭∨𝑦∈𝔭
Definition 3.3.35 (Radical Ideal)

An ideal 𝔞◃𝐴 is radical if it satisfies the following property

𝑥𝑛∈𝔞⟹𝑥∈𝔞
Proposition 3.3.36 (Maximal ideals exist)

Let 𝐴 be a ring and 𝔞◃𝐴 a proper ideal. Then it is contained in some maximal ideal 𝔪.

In particular there always exists a maximal ideal by considering 𝔞=(0).

Proof. Simple application of Zorn’s Lemma. ∎
Proposition 3.3.37 (Properties of prime ideals)

Let 𝔭 be a prime ideal and 𝔞, 𝔟 be ideals then the following are equivalent

  1. 𝔞⊆𝔭or𝔟⊆𝔭
  2. 𝔞∩𝔟⊆𝔭
  3. 𝔞𝔟⊆𝔭

Proof. a) ⟹ b) Follows because 𝔞∩𝔟⊆𝔞

b) ⟹ c) Follows because 𝔞𝔟⊆𝔞∩𝔟

c) ⟹ a) If 𝔞⊈𝔭, then choose 𝑎∈𝔞∖𝔭. By hypothesis 𝑎𝔟⊆𝔭 and since 𝔭 is prime 𝔟⊆𝔭. ∎

Corollary 3.3.38 (Ideal version of primality)

Let 𝔭 be a proper ideal. Then 𝔭 is prime if and only if the following condition holds for all ideals 𝔞, 𝔟

𝔞𝔟⊆𝔭⟹𝔞⊆𝔭or𝔟⊆𝔭

In particular for all 𝑘>0 we have

𝔞⊆𝔭⟺𝔞𝑘⊆𝔭
Proof. One direction has been shown in (3.3.37). Conversely suppose 𝑓𝑔∈𝔭 then apply the condition to the ideals (𝑓) and (𝑔) we find 𝑓∈𝔭 or 𝑔∈𝔭. ∎
Lemma 3.3.39 (Prime ideals are meet-prime)

Let 𝔭 be a prime ideal. Then

⋂𝑖=1𝑛𝔞𝑖⊆𝔭⟹𝔞𝑖⊆𝔭some𝑖=1…𝑛

in other words 𝔭 is meet-prime in the lattice of ideals.

Proof. Suppose 𝔞𝑖⊈𝔭 for all 𝑖 then there exists 𝑥𝑖∈𝔞𝑖∖𝔭. Then 𝑥1…𝑥𝑛∈⋂𝑖=1𝑛𝔞𝑖⊆𝔭 by hypothesis, so by primality 𝑥𝑖∈𝔭 for some 𝑖, a contradiction. ∎
Lemma 3.3.40 (Generate prime ideals)

Let 𝐴 be a ring, 𝑆 a multiplicative set and 𝔟◃𝐴 such that 𝔟∩𝑆=∅ then

ℐ︀={𝔞|𝔟⊆𝔞𝔞∩𝑆=∅}

has a maximal element, which is prime.

Proof. Since 𝔟∈ℐ︀ it is non-empty. By Zorn’s Lemma it has a maximal element, 𝔭. We claim it is prime, for suppose 𝑥𝑦∈𝔭 and 𝑥,𝑦∉𝔭. Then by maximality 𝔭+(𝑥) and 𝔭+(𝑦) intersect 𝑆. Therefore 𝑆 intersects (𝔭+(𝑥))(𝔭+(𝑦))⊆𝔭, a contradiction. ∎
Definition 3.3.41 (Minimal prime)

Let 𝐴 be a ring and 𝔞◃𝐴 a proper ideal. A prime ideal 𝔭 is a minimal prime over 𝔞 if it contains 𝔞, and every other such prime ideal contains 𝔭.

We say it is simply a minimal prime if it is minimal over (0).

Proposition 3.3.42 (Prime ideals are chain complete)
Let {𝔭𝑖}𝑖∈𝐼 be a chain of prime ideals, then ⋂𝑖𝔭𝑖 and ⋃𝑖𝔭𝑖 are prime ideals.
Proof. By (3.3.36) ⋃𝑖𝔭𝑖 is an ideal, and it’s easily verified to be prime. Clearly ⋂𝑖𝔭𝑖 is an ideal. Suppose 𝑎,𝑏∉⋂𝑖𝔭𝑖 then 𝑎∉𝔭𝑗 and 𝑏∉𝔭𝑘 with 𝑗≤𝑘. Then 𝑏∉𝔭𝑗, and 𝑎𝑏∉𝔭𝑗 by primality, whence 𝑎𝑏∉⋂𝑖𝔭𝑖. ∎
Corollary 3.3.43 (Minimal primes exist)

Let 𝐴 be a ring and 𝔞◃𝐴 be a proper ideal contained in a prime ideal 𝔭. Then there exists a minimal prime over 𝔞 contained in 𝔭.

In particular there always exists a minimal prime over 𝔞 and every prime ideal contains a minimal prime ideal.

Proof. We may use (3.3.42) together with Zorn’s Lemma. ∎
Proposition 3.3.44

Let 𝐴 be a ring. Then the set Rad(𝐴) of radical ideals forms a complete sub-lattice of the lattice of ideals ℐ︀(𝐴). This induces a closure operator

−:ℐ︀(𝐴)→Rad(𝐴)

given by

𝔞≔⋂𝔞⊆𝔯𝔯={𝑥|𝑥𝑛∈𝔞𝑛>0}

The “join” is given by

⋁𝑖∈𝐼𝔞𝑖=∑𝑖𝔞𝑖

In particular

  1. 𝔞⊆𝔞
  2. 𝔞⊆𝔟⟹𝔞⊆𝔟
  3. 𝔞=𝔞

Proof. The set of radical ideals is closed under arbitrary intersections (which are meets in the lattice ℐ︀(𝐴)). Therefore by (2.1.41) it forms a complete sub-lattice with meet given by intersection of ideals.

It also shows that − as defined is a closure operator with image Rad(𝐴), which demonstrates the required properties.

Finally we just need to show that 𝐼′≔{𝑥|𝑥𝑛∈𝔞𝑛>0} is equal to 𝔞. Firstly it’s an ideal for if 𝑥,𝑦∈𝐼′ then 𝑥𝑛∈𝔞 and 𝑦𝑚∈𝔞, so we may show that (𝑥+𝑦)𝑛+𝑚∈𝔞 whence 𝑥+𝑦∈𝐼′. Similarly 𝑎∈𝐴 and 𝑥∈𝐼′ implies (𝑎𝑥)𝑛=𝑎𝑛𝑥𝑛∈𝐼′. It’s radical for suppose 𝑥𝑚∈𝐼′ then 𝑥𝑚𝑛=(𝑥𝑚)𝑛∈𝔞 by definition whence 𝑥∈𝐼′. As it contains 𝔞 we find that 𝔞⊆𝐼′. Let 𝔯 be another radical ideal containing 𝔞 then 𝑥∈𝐼′⟹𝑥𝑛∈𝔞⟹𝑥𝑛∈𝔯⟹𝑥∈𝔯. Therefore the reverse inclusion follows. ∎

Proposition 3.3.45 (Prime Nullstellensatz)

The radical of an ideal satisfies

𝔞=⋂𝔞⊆𝔭:𝔭prime𝔭

Further the intersection may be taken over all minimal primes over 𝔞.

Proof. Suppose 𝑥∈𝔞 and 𝔭⊇𝔞. Then 𝑥𝑛∈𝔭⟹𝑥∈𝔭. Therefore 𝔞⊆⋂𝔞⊆𝔭𝔭. Conversely suppose 𝑥∉𝔞 then 𝑆≔{1,𝑥,𝑥2,…} is a proper multiplicative set such that 𝑆∩𝔞=∅. By (3.3.40) there is a prime ideal 𝔭 containing 𝔞 which does not intersect 𝑆. Therefore 𝑥∉RHS as required. ∎
Proposition 3.3.46 (Properties of Radical Ideals)

Let 𝔞, 𝔟 be ideals then

  1. 𝔞𝑘=𝔞 for 𝑘>0
  2. ∑𝑖𝔞𝑖=∑𝑖𝔞𝑖

  4. ∑𝑖𝔞𝑖=𝐴⟺∑𝑖𝔞𝑖=𝐴

  2. This follows by applying (2.1.44) to the closure operator −.
  3. 𝔞=𝐴⟺1∈𝔞⟺1∈𝔞⟺𝔞=𝐴
  4. This follows from combining c) and b)
  5. This follows from d), b) and a)

 ∎

Lemma 3.3.47 (Ideal Finitely Generated by Nilpotents is Nilpotent)
Let 𝐴 be a ring with ideals 𝔞,𝔟 such that 𝔞⊆𝔟 and 𝔟 finitely generated. Then there exists an integer 𝑛>0 such that 𝔟𝑛⊆𝔞.
Definition 3.3.48 (Extended and contracted ideals)

Let 𝜑:𝐴→𝐵 be a homomorphism and 𝔞 (resp. 𝔟) be an ideal of 𝐴 (resp. 𝐵). Define the contraction (resp. extension) ideals as follows

𝔟𝑐≔𝜑−1(𝔟)𝔞𝑒≔𝜑(𝔞)𝐵≔⟨𝜑(𝔞)⟩={∑𝑖𝑏𝑖𝜑(𝑎𝑖)|𝑎𝑖∈𝔞}

An ideal is said to be contracted (resp. extended) if it is of the form 𝔟𝑐 (resp. 𝔞𝑒)

Proposition 3.3.49 (Operations on ideals)

Let 𝜑:𝐴→𝐵 a ring homomorphism and 𝔞◃𝐴, 𝔟◃𝐵 ideals then

  1. 𝔟𝑐◃𝐴 and 𝔞𝑒◃𝐵
  2. 𝔟𝑐 proper if and only if 𝔟 is proper
  3. 𝔟𝑐𝑒⊆𝔟 and 𝔞⊆𝔞𝑒𝑐
  4. 𝔞𝑒𝑐𝑒=𝔞𝑒 and 𝔟𝑐𝑒𝑐=𝔟𝑐
  5. 𝔟𝑐𝑒=𝔟⟺𝔟 is an extended ideal ⟺𝔟⊆𝔟𝑐𝑒
  6. 𝔞𝑒𝑐=𝔞⟺𝔞 is a contracted ideal ⟺𝔞𝑒𝑐⊆𝔞
  7. 𝔟𝑐=(𝔟)𝑐
  8. (𝔟𝑐)𝑒⊆𝔟 with equality when 𝜑 is surjective

When 𝜑 is surjective every ideal 𝔟◃𝐵 is extended, and the contracted ideals are precisely the ideals containing ker(𝜑).

Proof. We prove each in turn

a)-c) Straightforward

d) By the previous step 𝔟𝑐𝑒⊆𝔟⟹(𝔟𝑐𝑒)𝑐⊆𝔟𝑐, similarly 𝔟𝑐⊆(𝔟𝑐)𝑒𝑐. The other relation is similar.

e)-f) These follow from c) and d)

g) 𝑥∈(𝔟)𝑐⟺𝜑(𝑥)∈𝔟⟺𝜑(𝑥)𝑛∈𝔟⟺𝜑(𝑥𝑛)∈𝔟⟺𝑥𝑛∈𝔟𝑐⟺𝑥∈𝔟𝑐

h) By c) and g) we find (𝔟𝑐)𝑒=(𝔟)𝑐𝑒⊆𝔟. We will show that when 𝜑 is surjective every ideal is extended, in which case the equality follows from e).

Suppose that 𝜑 is surjective. Then by e) we only need to show that 𝔟⊆𝔟𝑐𝑒 for every ideal 𝔟. Let 𝑦∈𝔟 then 𝑦=𝜑(𝑥), whence 𝑥∈𝔟𝑐 and 𝑦∈𝔟𝑐𝑒. ∎

Proposition 3.3.50

Let 𝜑:𝐴→𝐵 be a ring homomorphism and 𝔞1,…,𝔞𝑛 ideals of 𝐴. Then

(∑𝑖=1𝑛𝔞𝑖)𝑒=∑𝑖=1𝑛𝔞𝑖𝑒

Similarly

(∏𝑖=1𝑛𝔞𝑖)𝑒=∏𝑖=1𝑛𝔞𝑖𝑒

Proof. Evidently 𝜑(𝔞𝑖)⊆𝜑(∑𝑖=1𝑛𝔞𝑖)⊆(∑𝑖=1𝑛𝔞𝑖)𝑒 and therefore 𝔞𝑖𝑒⊆(∑𝑖=1𝑛𝔞𝑖)𝑒⟹∑𝑖=1𝑛𝔞𝑖𝑒⊆(∑𝑖=1𝑛𝔞𝑖)𝑒.

Similarly 𝜑(∑𝑖=1𝑛𝔞𝑖)⊆∑𝑖=1𝑛𝜑(𝔞𝑖)⊆∑𝑖=1𝑛𝔞𝑖𝑒 whence the reverse inclusion follows.

The second statement follows similarly. ∎

Corollary 3.3.51

Let 𝜑:𝐴→𝐵 be a ring homomorphism then extension and contraction constitute a monotone Galois connection

{𝔞◃𝐴}⟷{𝔟◃𝐵}

and therefore is order-preserving and satisfies the adjoint property

𝔞⊆𝔟𝑐⟺𝔞𝑒⊆𝔟

is satisfied.

Proof. Extension and contraction satisfy conditions of (2.1.50) by (3.3.49) c) and d) ∎
Corollary 3.3.52

Let 𝜑:𝐴→𝐵 be a ring homomorphism then there is a order-preserving bijection between “contracted” and “extended ideals”

{𝔞◃𝐴|𝔞contracted}⟷{𝔟◃𝐵|𝔟extended}

which restricts to proper ideals.

Proof. We’ve shown that 𝔞 (resp. 𝔟) is contracted (resp. extended) if and only if the given maps are mutually inverse. Note that 𝔟 is proper implies 𝔟𝑐 is proper. Furthermore 𝔟𝑐 proper implies 𝔟𝑐𝑒⊆𝔟 is proper. Therefore it restricts to proper ideals. ∎

  • 𝔫◃𝐵 maximal and 𝜑 surjective ⟹𝜑−1(𝔫) is maximal
