6.1.1 Topological Properties

Proposition 6.1.15 (Principal Open Sets)

Let 𝑋=𝑉(𝔞) be an affine variety over 𝑘. For every 𝑓∈𝑘[𝑋] the sets

𝐷(𝑓)≔𝑋(𝑘―)∖𝑉𝑘―((𝑓))={(𝑥)∈𝑋(𝑘―)|𝑓(𝑥)≠0}

Proof. Let 𝑈=𝑋(𝑘―)∖𝑉(𝔟)(𝑘―) be a non-empty open set. For every 𝑥∈𝑈 there by definition exists 𝑓∈𝔟 such that 𝑓(𝑥)≠0. Therefore 𝐷(𝑓) is a neighbourhood of 𝑥 in the Zariski topology. ∎

The topological notion of irreducibility is important, and may be reduced to a purely algebraic statement on the coordinate ring.

Proposition 6.1.16 (Criterion for Irreducibility)

Let 𝑋 be an affine variety (e.g. 𝔸𝑘𝑛) and 𝑌=𝑉(𝔟) an affine subvariety corresponding to a radical ideal 𝔟◃𝑘[𝑋]. Then the following are equivalent

  1. 𝑌(𝑘―) is an irreducible subset of 𝑋(𝑘―)
  2. 𝑌0(𝑘―) is an irreducible subset of 𝑋0(𝑘―)
  3. 𝔟 is prime
  4. 𝑘[𝑌] is an integral domain.

Proof. Suppose 𝑋 is not irreducible. Then we have 𝑋⊆𝑉𝑘―(𝔟)∪𝑉𝑘―(𝔠) a non-trivial decomposition into closed subsets (and associated ideals). Then by the dual isomorphism we have also 𝔞⊊𝔟 and we may choose 𝑓∈𝔟∖𝔞 and similarly 𝑔∈𝔠∖𝔞. However 𝑓𝑔 vanishes on 𝑋(𝑘―) ands so we have 𝑓𝑔∈𝔞. Therefore 𝔞 is not prime.

Conversely suppose 𝑋 is irreducible and 𝔟𝔠⊆𝔞. Then 𝑋⊆𝑉𝑘―(𝔟)∪𝑉𝑘―(𝔠). By irreducibility we have 𝑋⊆𝑉𝑘―(𝔟), whence applying 𝐼𝑘―(−) we see 𝔟⊆𝐼𝑘―𝑉𝑘―(𝔟)⊆𝔞 (since 𝔞 is radical). Therefore 𝔞 is prime. ∎

When 𝑊⊂𝑋 is an irreducible subset then write 𝔐𝑋,𝑊≔𝐼(𝑊), by the previous result this equals 𝐼(𝑊―) and 𝔐𝑋,𝑊 is a prime ideal.

Proposition 6.1.18 (Points are Closed)

Let 𝑋=𝑉(𝔞) be an affine variety and 𝑥∈𝑋(𝑘―). Then

{𝑥}―=𝑉(𝔐𝑋,𝑥)={𝜎(𝑥)|𝜎∈Aut(𝑘―/𝑘)}

is irreducible and finite of order [𝑘(𝑥):𝑘]𝑠. Further 𝑋 is a symmetric space.

Proof. The equalities are proven in (6.1.9). Further {𝑥}― is irreducible by (4.1.67). We may replace Aut(𝑘―/𝑘) with Mor𝑘(𝑘(𝑥),𝑘―) to determine the order.

Clearly 𝑋 is symmetric because it satisfies criteria (4.1.41) b). ∎

Proposition 6.1.19 (Closed sets ⟷ radical ideals)

Let 𝑋=𝑉(𝔞) be an affine variety. Recall (6.1.13) there is a dual lattice isomorphism between radical ideals and “Zariski”-closed subsets of 𝑋(𝑘―) (and indeed 𝑋0(𝑘―)).

Rad(𝑘[𝑋])⇌𝐼𝑘(−)𝑉𝑘―(−)Zar𝑘(𝑋(𝑘―))

Under this isomorphism we have

  • maximal ideals correspond to Aut(𝑘―/𝑘)-orbits of 𝑋(𝑘―) (or elements of 𝑋0(𝑘―))
  • prime ideals of 𝑘[𝑋] correspond to irreducible subsets of 𝑋(𝑘―) (and therefore of 𝑋0(𝑘―))
  • minimal prime ideals of 𝑘[𝑋] correspond to irreducible components of 𝑋(𝑘―) (and therefore of 𝑋0(𝑘―))

Recall that prime ideals are precisely the meet-prime radical ideals and irreducible subsets are precisely the join-prime closed subsets (see (4.1.61)). Therefore we have a dual isomorphism between the Krull Lattice of radical ideals of 𝑘[𝑋] and the Krull Latice of closed subsets of 𝑋(𝑘―).

Proof. The content of the Strong Nullstellensatz is precisely that 𝐼𝑘(−)∘𝑉𝑘―(−)=𝟏. The other direction was already proven so we have a dual order isomorphism. The statement about maximal ideals was already shown in (6.1.11) and prime ideals in (6.1.16). Then as irreducible components are precisely maximal irreducible subsets the final statement follows from the dual order isomorphism. ∎
Corollary 6.1.20
Let 𝑋=𝑉(𝔞) be an affine variety. There is a bijective correspondence between affine subvarieties of 𝑋 and closed subsets of 𝑋(𝑘―).
Definition 6.1.21 (Irreducible Affine Variety)

We say an affine variety 𝑋=𝑉(𝔞) is integral or irreducible if the topological space 𝑋(𝑘―) is irreducible.

This is the case precisely when 𝔞 is prime, or when 𝑘[𝑋] is an integral domain by (6.1.19), or equivalently irreducible (3.3.66), since it is assumed to be reduced.

Note trivially that 𝔸𝑘𝑛 is irreducible.

Proposition 6.1.22 (Decomposition into Irreducible Components)

Let 𝑋=𝑉(𝔞) be an affine variety then the topological space 𝑋(𝑘―) is Noetherian. Furthermore it has finitely many irreducible components 𝑋𝑖 and the decomposition

𝑋(𝑘―)=𝑋1∪…∪𝑋𝑛

is the unique incomparable decomposition into irreducible closed subsets.

Proof. By Hilbert’s Basis Theorem (3.15.8) 𝑘[𝑋] is a Noetherian ring, so by (6.1.19) 𝑋(𝑘―) is Noetherian. The result then follows from (4.1.76) ∎

Proof. For the first correspondence consider the localisation map 𝑖:𝑘[𝑋]→𝑘[𝑋]𝑓. By (3.6.27) the extension of a maximal ideal not containing 𝑓 is maximal.
