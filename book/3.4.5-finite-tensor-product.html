3.4.5 Finite Tensor Product

We generalise the tensor product of two modules to any finite number of modules, characterised by multilinear maps.

Definition 3.4.28 (Multilinear Map)

Let 𝑀1,…,𝑀𝑛,𝑍 be 𝐴-modules then a map

𝜓:𝑀1×…×𝑀𝑛→𝑍

is 𝐴-multilinear if it is 𝐴-linear in each variable, whilst fixing the other variables at any value. Denote the set of such maps by Mult(𝑀1,…,𝑀𝑛;𝑍). It is an 𝐴-module under pointwise addition and scalar multiplication. It is also a functor, contravariant in each 𝑀𝑖 and covariant in 𝑍: given 𝐴-linear maps 𝑓𝑖:𝑀𝑖′→𝑀𝑖 and 𝑔:𝑍→𝑍′ we define

Mult(𝑓1,…,𝑓𝑛;𝑔):Mult(𝑀1,…,𝑀𝑛;𝑍)→Mult(𝑀1′,…,𝑀𝑛′;𝑍′)𝜓↦𝑔∘𝜓∘(𝑓1×⋯×𝑓𝑛)

which is again multilinear and compatible with identities and composition. For 𝑛=2 this is Bilin(𝑀1,𝑀2;𝑍).

Lemma 3.4.29 (Currying for Multilinear Maps)

Let 0≤𝑘≤𝑛 and 𝑀1,…,𝑀𝑛,𝑍 be 𝐴-modules, where we set Mult(;𝑊)≔𝑊 for the empty family. Then

Mult(𝑀1,…,𝑀𝑛;𝑍)≅Mult(𝑀1,…,𝑀𝑘;Mult(𝑀𝑘+1,…,𝑀𝑛;𝑍))𝜓→(𝑥1,…,𝑥𝑘)↦((𝑥𝑘+1,…,𝑥𝑛)↦𝜓(𝑥1,…,𝑥𝑛))

is an isomorphism of 𝐴-modules, natural in all arguments. For 𝑘=𝑛−1 this is Mult(𝑀1,…,𝑀𝑛;𝑍)≅Mult(𝑀1,…,𝑀𝑛−1;Hom(𝑀𝑛,𝑍)).

Proof. Drafted by Claude Sonnet, reviewed by David Rufino.

Given 𝜓 multilinear and 𝑥1,…,𝑥𝑘 fixed, (𝑥𝑘+1,…,𝑥𝑛)↦𝜓(𝑥1,…,𝑥𝑛) is multilinear in the last 𝑛−𝑘 variables, so lies in Mult(𝑀𝑘+1,…,𝑀𝑛;𝑍). As the module structure is pointwise, the assignment (𝑥1,…,𝑥𝑘)↦𝜓(𝑥1,…,𝑥𝑘,−) is linear in each 𝑥𝑖 with the others fixed, so the map is well defined, and it is 𝐴-linear in 𝜓. Conversely given Ψ∈Mult(𝑀1,…,𝑀𝑘;Mult(𝑀𝑘+1,…,𝑀𝑛;𝑍)) the map (𝑥1,…,𝑥𝑛)↦Ψ(𝑥1,…,𝑥𝑘)(𝑥𝑘+1,…,𝑥𝑛) is linear in each variable, hence multilinear, and the two constructions are mutually inverse. Naturality in the 𝑀𝑖 and 𝑍 is immediate from the formulas, as precomposition with 𝑓1×⋯×𝑓𝑛 and postcomposition with 𝑔 commute with this rewriting of the arguments. ∎

Proposition 3.4.30 (Existence of Finite Tensor Product)

Let 𝑀1,…,𝑀𝑛 be 𝐴-modules. The functor Mult(𝑀1,…,𝑀𝑛;−) is representable by an 𝐴-module 𝑀1⊗⋯⊗𝑀𝑛, that is there is a universal multilinear map

𝜄:𝑀1×⋯×𝑀𝑛→𝑀1⊗⋯⊗𝑀𝑛(𝑚1,…,𝑚𝑛)↦𝑚1⊗⋯⊗𝑚𝑛

and a bijection

Φ:Hom(𝑀1⊗⋯⊗𝑀𝑛,𝑍)≅Mult(𝑀1,…,𝑀𝑛;𝑍)𝑓↦𝑓∘𝜄

natural in 𝑍 and in the 𝑀𝑖. Clearly 𝜄=Φ(1). For 𝑛=0 it is 𝐴, for 𝑛=1 it is 𝑀1, for 𝑛=2 it is the tensor product of (3.4.4), and for 𝑛≥2 the canonical map (𝑀1⊗⋯⊗𝑀𝑛−1)⊗𝑀𝑛→𝑀1⊗⋯⊗𝑀𝑛 is an isomorphism.

Proposition 3.4.31 (Permutation and Grouping of Tensor Factors)

Let 𝑀1,…,𝑀𝑛 be 𝐴-modules. For every 𝜎∈𝑆𝑛 there is an isomorphism

𝑀1⊗⋯⊗𝑀𝑛≅𝑀𝜎(1)⊗⋯⊗𝑀𝜎(𝑛)𝑚1⊗⋯⊗𝑚𝑛↦𝑚𝜎(1)⊗⋯⊗𝑚𝜎(𝑛)

and for 0≤𝑘≤𝑛 there is an isomorphism

(𝑀1⊗⋯⊗𝑀𝑘)⊗(𝑀𝑘+1⊗⋯⊗𝑀𝑛)≅𝑀1⊗⋯⊗𝑀𝑛(𝑚1⊗⋯⊗𝑚𝑘)⊗(𝑚𝑘+1⊗⋯⊗𝑚𝑛)↦𝑚1⊗⋯⊗𝑚𝑛

Proof. Drafted by Claude Sonnet, reviewed by David Rufino.

In each case we exhibit a natural isomorphism of the represented functors Hom(−,𝑍), and conclude by the Yoneda Lemma (2.6.58): a natural isomorphism Hom(𝑋,−)→Hom(𝑋′,−) corresponds to an isomorphism 𝑋′→𝑋, namely the image of 1𝑋, and so is determined by where it sends the universal element.

Permutation. Write 𝑁𝑖≔𝑀𝜎(𝑖) and let 𝑠:𝑀1×⋯×𝑀𝑛→𝑁1×⋯×𝑁𝑛, (𝑚𝑖)↦(𝑚𝜎(𝑖)). This is a bijection of sets, and 𝜓↦𝜓∘𝑠 is an isomorphism Mult(𝑁1,…,𝑁𝑛;𝑍)≅Mult(𝑀1,…,𝑀𝑛;𝑍) natural in 𝑍 (with inverse 𝜓′↦𝜓′∘𝑠−1). Composing with the bijections Φ𝑁, Φ𝑀 of (3.4.30) gives an isomorphism, natural in 𝑍,

Θ𝑍:Hom(𝑁1⊗⋯⊗𝑁𝑛,𝑍)≅Hom(𝑀1⊗⋯⊗𝑀𝑛,𝑍),Θ𝑍(𝑔)=Φ𝑀−1(Φ𝑁(𝑔)∘𝑠)

that is Θ𝑍(𝑔) is the unique ℎ with ℎ∘𝜄𝑀=𝑔∘𝜄𝑁∘𝑠. By the Yoneda Lemma it corresponds to the isomorphism 𝜃:𝑀1⊗⋯⊗𝑀𝑛→𝑁1⊗⋯⊗𝑁𝑛 obtained from 𝑔=1, which is the unique map with 𝜃∘𝜄𝑀=𝜄𝑁∘𝑠, that is 𝜃(𝑚1⊗⋯⊗𝑚𝑛)=𝑚𝜎(1)⊗⋯⊗𝑚𝜎(𝑛).

Grouping. Let 𝑃≔𝑀1⊗⋯⊗𝑀𝑘 and 𝑄≔𝑀𝑘+1⊗⋯⊗𝑀𝑛. By (3.4.29) there is an isomorphism natural in 𝑍

Mult(𝑀1,…,𝑀𝑛;𝑍)≅Mult(𝑀1,…,𝑀𝑘;Mult(𝑀𝑘+1,…,𝑀𝑛;𝑍))

and by (3.4.30) applied to 𝑄 and to 𝑃 and the tensor-hom adjunction for 𝑃⊗𝑄 (3.4.6) we have isomorphisms, natural in 𝑍,

Hom(𝑃⊗𝑄,𝑍)≅Hom(𝑃,Hom(𝑄,𝑍))≅Mult(𝑀1,…,𝑀𝑘;Hom(𝑄,𝑍))≅Mult(𝑀1,…,𝑀𝑘;Mult(𝑀𝑘+1,…,𝑀𝑛;𝑍))

Composing with the first isomorphism and with Φ𝑀 gives a natural isomorphism Hom(𝑃⊗𝑄,𝑍)≅Hom(𝑀1⊗⋯⊗𝑀𝑛,𝑍). By the Yoneda Lemma it corresponds to an isomorphism 𝑀1⊗⋯⊗𝑀𝑛→𝑃⊗𝑄, which is the image of 1𝑃⊗𝑄, and following 1 through the chain above (it becomes the universal bilinear map, then 𝑝↦(𝑞↦𝑝⊗𝑞), then the multilinear map (𝑚𝑖)↦𝜄𝑃(𝑚1,…,𝑚𝑘)⊗𝜄𝑄(𝑚𝑘+1,…,𝑚𝑛)) shows it is 𝑚1⊗⋯⊗𝑚𝑛↦(𝑚1⊗⋯⊗𝑚𝑘)⊗(𝑚𝑘+1⊗⋯⊗𝑚𝑛). Its inverse is the required isomorphism. ∎

Corollary 3.4.32 (Finite Tensor Product of Free Modules)
If each 𝑀𝑖 is free with basis {𝑚𝑖,𝑗}𝑗∈𝐽𝑖 then 𝑀1⊗⋯⊗𝑀𝑛 is free with basis {𝑚1,𝑗1⊗⋯⊗𝑚𝑛,𝑗𝑛}(𝑗1,…,𝑗𝑛)∈𝐽1×⋯×𝐽𝑛.
