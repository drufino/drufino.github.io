6.2.15 Completeness

Completeness is analogous to compactness in the Hausdorff case, and many results remain true when complete is replaced with compact and the standard product topology is used (in place of the topology induced by the product variety structure). We show in the next section that every projective variety is complete, so it is quite a natural condition.

Definition 6.2.83

Let 𝑋 be a separated variety. Then it is

  • universally closed if for every variety 𝑌 the projection map

    𝜋𝑌:𝑋×𝑌→𝑌

    is closed (i.e. the image of a closed subset is closed).

  • complete if for every irreducible closed subvariety 𝑍⊂𝑋 and valuation ring 𝑘⊂𝑅⊂𝑘(𝑍) there is a point 𝑧∈𝑍 such that 𝒪︀𝑍,𝑧⊂𝑅
Proposition 6.2.84 (Image of Complete Variety is Closed)
Let 𝜙:𝑋→𝑌 be a morphism of varieties where 𝑋 is universally closed and 𝑌 is separated. Then 𝜙(𝑋) is a closed subvariety of 𝑌.
Proof. By (6.2.66) the graph Γ𝜙 is a closed subset of 𝑋×𝑌. Then 𝜙(𝑋) is the projection of Γ𝜙 and therefore closed by definition. ∎
Proposition 6.2.85 (Closed Subvariety of Complete Variety is also Complete)
A closed subvariety of a universally closed (resp. complete) variety is universally closed (resp. complete)

Proof. Suppose 𝑍⊂𝑋 is closed then by (6.2.61) 𝑍×𝑌 is a closed subset of 𝑋×𝑌. Further 𝑇 closed in 𝑍×𝑌 implies 𝑇 closed in 𝑋×𝑌 and the result follows.

The complete case is obvious. ∎

Proposition 6.2.86
A separated variety 𝑋 is universally closed (resp. complete) iff all its irreducible components are universally closed (resp. complete).

Proof. By (4.1.66) every irreducible component is closed, and therefore complete by (6.2.85).

Conversely suppose 𝑋=𝑋1∪…∪𝑋𝑛 is the irreducible decomposition (6.2.8). If 𝑇⊂𝑋×𝑌 is closed then 𝑇∩(𝑋𝑖×𝑌) is closed in 𝑋𝑖×𝑌 and therefore by assumption 𝜋𝑌(𝑇∩(𝑋𝑖×𝑌))=𝜋𝑌(𝑇)∩𝑋𝑖 is closed. Therefore 𝜋𝑌(𝑇)=⋃𝑖=1𝑛𝜋𝑌(𝑇)∩𝑋𝑖 is also closed.

The complete case is straightforward. ∎

Proposition 6.2.87

Let 𝑋 be a separated variety. To demonstrate universal closedness it is sufficient to show that

𝜋𝑌:𝑋×𝑌→𝑌

is closed for 𝑌 affine (or even 𝑌≔𝔸𝑘𝑛).

Further it is sufficient to show that 𝜋𝑌(𝑇) is closed for all irreducible closed subsets 𝑇⊂𝑋×𝑌.

Proof. Suppose 𝑌=⋃𝑖=1𝑛𝑈𝑖 for 𝑈𝑖 open affines. Then by (6.2.61) 𝑋×𝑈𝑖 is an open cover of 𝑋×𝑌. Let 𝑇⊂𝑋×𝑌 closed then 𝑇∩(𝑋×𝑈𝑖) is closed in 𝑋×𝑈𝑖. By assumption 𝜋𝑌(𝑇∩(𝑋×𝑈𝑖))=𝜋𝑌(𝑇)∩𝑈𝑖 is closed in 𝑈𝑖. Therefore by (4.1.22) 𝜋𝑌(𝑇) is closed in 𝑌 as required.

Clearly the closed property is preserved under isomorphism of 𝑌, therefore we may assume that 𝑌=𝑉(𝔞)⊂𝔸𝑘𝑛 is a closed subset for some 𝑛 and ideal 𝔞◁𝑘[𝑋1,…,𝑋𝑛].

By (6.2.61) 𝑋×𝑌 is a closed subset of 𝑋×𝔸𝑘𝑛. There 𝑇⊂𝑋×𝑌 is also closed in 𝑋×𝔸𝑘𝑛 and the result follows.

Finally if 𝑇 is closed then by (…) 𝑇=𝑇1∪…∪𝑇𝑚 for 𝑇𝑗 irreducible closed subsets of 𝑇 (and therefore of 𝑋×𝑌). Therefore

𝜋𝑌(𝑇)=𝜋𝑌(⋃𝑗=1𝑚𝑇𝑗)=⋃𝑗=1𝑚𝜋𝑌(𝑇𝑗)

and it suffices to show that 𝜋𝑌(𝑇𝑗) is closed. ∎

Proof. We claim it’s enough to find an open neighbourhood 𝑉 of 𝑥 such that 𝑈∩𝑉≠∅ and a regular function 𝜓:𝑉→𝑌 such that 𝜓|𝑈∩𝑉=𝜙|𝑈∩𝑉. For then this extends to a regular function 𝑈∪𝑉 as required.
