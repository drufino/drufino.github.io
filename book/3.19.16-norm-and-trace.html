3.19.16 Norm and Trace

In what follows we assume that 𝐾 is a commutative ring and 𝐴 is a 𝐾-algebra which is finite free as a 𝐾-module (free is automatic when 𝐾 is a field).

Definition 3.19.136 (Norm, Trace and Characteristic Polynomial)

For 𝛼∈𝐴 consider the 𝐾-linear multiplication map

mult𝛼,𝐾:𝐴→𝐴

and define

  1. Tr𝐴/𝐾(𝛼)≔Tr(mult𝛼)∈𝐾
  2. Norm𝐴/𝐾(𝛼)≔det(mult𝛼)∈𝐾
  3. Pc𝐴/𝐾(𝛼;𝑋)≔𝜒mult𝛼=det(mult𝑋−𝛼,𝐾[𝑋])=det(𝑋⋅𝐼𝑛−[mult𝛼])∈𝐾[𝑋] the characteristic polynomial.
Proposition 3.19.137

With the notation as in (3.19.136) we have the following properties

  1. Tr(𝛼+𝛽)=Tr(𝛼)+Tr(𝛽)
  2. Tr(𝛼𝛽)=Tr(𝛽𝛼)
  3. Norm(𝛼𝛽)=Norm(𝛼)Norm(𝛽)

and further that

Pc(𝛼;𝑋)=𝑋𝑛−Tr(𝛼)𝑋𝑛−1+⋯+(−1)𝑛Norm(𝛼)

Finally for 𝜆∈𝐾 we have

Tr(𝜆)=dim𝐾𝐴⋅𝜆Norm(𝜆)=𝜆dim𝐾𝐴

Proof. We prove each in turn

  1. We have mult𝛼+𝛽=mult𝛼+mult𝛽 and ordinary trace is additive (3.5.24).
  2. When 𝐴 is commutative this is obvious, however even in general we have mult𝛼𝛽=mult𝛼∘mult𝛽 and so the result follows from symmetry of the ordinary trace (3.5.25)
  3. This follows similarly because the determinant is multiplicative (3.5.17).

The identity for the characteristic polynomial follows directly from (3.18.7). Finally the matrix representation of mult𝜆 is simply 𝜆⋅𝐼𝑛 with respect to any basis. ∎

Proposition 3.19.138 (Characteristic Polynomial is a Norm)

For 𝛼∈𝐴 we have the relationship

Pc𝐴/𝐾(𝛼;𝑋)=Norm𝐴[𝑋]/𝐾[𝑋](𝑋−𝛼)
Proof. This follows from the definitions and (3.18.7). ∎
Proposition 3.19.139

Let 𝑀 be a finite free 𝐴-module. Then 𝑀 is a finite free 𝐾-module. Explicitly given a basis (𝑚𝑗) of 𝑀 over 𝐴 and (𝑎𝑖) of 𝐴 over 𝐾, then (𝑎𝑖⋅𝑚𝑗) is a basis for 𝑀 over 𝐾.

If 𝑢:𝑀→𝑀 is an endomorphism then we denote by 𝑢𝐾 the endomorphism of 𝑀 regarded as a (finite free) 𝐾-module.

Proposition 3.19.140 (Transitivity of Trace)

Suppose 𝐴 is commutative, 𝑀 is a finite free 𝐴-module and 𝑢:𝑀→𝑀 an endomorphism. Then

Tr(𝑢𝐾)=Tr𝐴/𝐾(Tr(𝑢))

and

det(𝑢𝐾)=Norm𝐴/𝐾(det(𝑢))

Proof. Let (𝑒1,…,𝑒𝑚) be an 𝐴-basis for 𝑀 and (𝑎1,…,𝑎𝑛) be a 𝐾-basis for 𝐴. Then by (3.19.139) (𝑎𝑖⋅𝑒𝑗) is a 𝐾-basis for 𝑀. For 𝛼∈𝐴 let 𝑀(𝛼)∈Mat𝑛(𝐾) denote the matrix corresponding mult𝛼 and let (𝑐𝑙𝑗)∈Mat𝑚(𝐴) be the matrix of 𝑢 with respect to (𝑒𝑗). By definition this means

𝑢(𝑒𝑗)=∑𝑙=1𝑚𝑐𝑙𝑗⋅𝑒𝑙

and

𝑎𝑖𝛼=𝛼𝑎𝑖=∑𝑘=1𝑛𝑀(𝛼)𝑘𝑖𝑎𝑘.

Therefore

𝑢𝐾(𝑎𝑖𝑒𝑗)=𝑎𝑖𝑢(𝑒𝑗)=𝑎𝑖⋅(∑𝑙=1𝑚𝑐𝑙𝑗⋅𝑒𝑙)=∑𝑙=1𝑚(𝑎𝑖𝑐𝑙𝑗)⋅𝑒𝑙=∑𝑙=1𝑚(∑𝑘=1𝑛𝑀(𝑐𝑙𝑗)𝑘𝑖𝑎𝑘)⋅𝑒𝑙=∑𝑙=1𝑚∑𝑘=1𝑛𝑀(𝑐𝑙𝑗)𝑘𝑖⋅(𝑎𝑘⋅𝑒𝑙)

Consequentally the matrix for 𝑢𝐾 with respect to the basis (𝑎𝑖⋅𝑒𝑗) is given in block form by

(𝑀(𝑐11)…𝑀(𝑐1𝑚)⋮⋮𝑀(𝑐𝑚1)…𝑀(𝑐𝑚𝑚))

Therefore we see that

Tr(𝑢𝐾)=∑𝑗=1𝑚Tr(𝑀(𝑐𝑗𝑗))=∑𝑗=1𝑚Tr𝐴/𝐾(𝑐𝑗𝑗)=Tr𝐴/𝐾(∑𝑗=1𝑚𝑐𝑗𝑗)=Tr𝐴/𝐾(Tr(𝑢))

as required. Similarly by (3.5.33)

det(𝑢𝐾)=det(𝐷𝑚(𝑀(𝑐11),…,𝑀(𝑐𝑚𝑚)))

and by the Laplace Expansion

𝐷𝑚(𝑀(𝑐11),…,𝑀(𝑐𝑚𝑚))=∑𝜎∈𝑆𝑚𝜀(𝜎)∏𝑖=1𝑚𝑀(𝑐𝑖𝜎(𝑖))=∑𝜎∈𝑆𝑚𝜀(𝜎)𝑀(∏𝑖=1𝑚𝑐𝑖𝜎(𝑖))=𝑀(∑𝜎∈𝑆𝑚𝜀(𝜎)∏𝑖=1𝑚𝑐𝑖𝜎(𝑖))=𝑀(det(𝑢))

where we implicitly use that 𝛼→mult𝛼→𝑀(𝛼) is a ring homomorphism. Therefore by definition

det(𝑢𝐾)=det(𝑀(det(𝑢)))=Norm𝐴/𝐾(det(𝑢))

 ∎

Corollary 3.19.141 (Transitivity)

Suppose 𝐵 is a commutative 𝐴-algebra which is finite free as an 𝐴-module. Then

Tr𝐵/𝐾(𝑏)=Tr𝐴/𝐾(Tr𝐵/𝐴(𝑏))

and

Norm𝐵/𝐾(𝑏)=Norm𝐴/𝐾(Norm𝐵/𝐴(𝑏))

for all 𝑏∈𝐵. Furthermore

Pc𝐵/𝐾(𝑏;𝑋)=Norm𝐴[𝑋]/𝐾[𝑋](Pc𝐵/𝐴(𝑏;𝑋))
Proposition 3.19.142

Let 𝐾/𝑘 be a finite field extension and 𝛼∈𝐾. Then

Pc𝐾/𝑘(𝛼;𝑋)=𝑚𝛼,𝑘(𝑋)[𝐾:𝑘(𝛼)]

Proof. Consider first the case 𝐾=𝑘(𝛼). Then by (3.18.4) we have Pc𝐾/𝑘(𝛼;𝑋)(mult𝛼)=0 and in particular Pc𝐾/𝑘(𝛼;𝑋)(𝛼)=0 (since 𝛼𝑛=𝛼𝑛⋅1). By (…) 𝑚𝛼,𝑘 divides Pc𝐾/𝑘(𝛼;𝑋). Further by (…) deg(𝑚𝛼,𝑘)=[𝑘(𝛼):𝑘]=[𝐾:𝑘]=deg(Pc𝐾/𝑘(𝛼;𝑋)). Since they are both monic then we find they are equal.

For the general case we may write the matrix for mult𝛼 in block form by considering the tower basis for 𝐾/𝑘(𝛼)/𝑘 (see (3.19.139)). This is block diagonal, so we may use the Leibniz formula to compute this directly as product of determinants of the blocks. ∎

We may pull together these results to give a more classical expression for norm and trace.

Proposition 3.19.143

Let 𝐿/𝐾/𝑘 be a tower of extensions such that 𝐿/𝑘 is normal (e.g. 𝐿=𝑘―). Then for any 𝛼∈𝐾 we have the identities

𝑖𝑘𝐿(Norm𝐾/𝑘(𝛼))=(∏𝜎∈Mor𝑘(𝐾,𝐿)𝜎(𝛼))[𝐾:𝑘]𝑖

and

𝑖𝑘𝐿(Tr𝐾/𝑘(𝛼))=[𝐾:𝑘]𝑖∑𝜎∈Mor𝑘(𝐾,𝐿)𝜎(𝛼)

Proof. Recall from (3.19.92) that the restriction map

Mor𝑘(𝐾,𝐿)→Mor𝑘(𝑘(𝛼),𝐿)

is surjective with finite fibers of order [𝐾:𝑘(𝛼)]𝑠. Therefore we may write the right hand side as

(∏𝜎∈Mor𝑘(𝐾,𝐿)𝜎(𝛼))[𝐾:𝑘]𝑖=(∏𝜎∈Mor𝑘(𝑘(𝛼),𝐿)𝜎(𝛼))[𝐾:𝑘(𝛼)]𝑠[𝐾:𝑘]𝑖=(∏𝜎∈Mor𝑘(𝑘(𝛼),𝐿)𝜎(𝛼))[𝐾:𝑘(𝛼)][𝑘(𝛼):𝑘]𝑖

Similarly by (3.19.141) and (3.19.137) the left hand side may be written as

Norm𝐾/𝑘(𝛼)=Norm𝑘(𝛼)/𝑘(Norm𝐾/𝑘(𝛼)(𝛼))=Norm𝑘(𝛼)/𝑘(𝛼[𝐾:𝑘(𝛼)])=Norm𝑘(𝛼)/𝑘(𝛼)[𝐾:𝑘(𝛼)]

Comparing these expressions shows we may reduce to the case 𝐾=𝑘(𝛼).

From (3.19.63) and (3.19.108) the right hand side is the product of all the roots of 𝑚𝛼,𝑘 including multiplicity, which is (−1)[𝐾:𝑘]𝑖𝑘𝐿(𝑐0) where 𝑐0 is the constant term of 𝑚𝛼,𝑘(𝑋). Then using (3.19.142) and (3.19.137) we see that this also equals 𝑖𝑘𝐿(Norm𝑘(𝛼)/𝑘(𝛼)) as required.

The case of trace is similar. ∎

Lemma 3.19.144 (Dedekind's Lemma)

Let 𝐾/𝑘 be a field extension and 𝐴 a 𝑘-algebra. The set of 𝑘-algebra homomorphisms

AlgHom𝑘(𝐴,𝐾)

is 𝐾-linearly independent as a subset of Hom𝑘(𝐴,𝐾).

Proof. Suppose that the 𝑘-algebra homomorphisms are not linearly independent. Then choose a minimal subset of distinct 𝑘-algebra homomorphisms 𝜑1,…,𝜑𝑛 with a non-trivial relationship over 𝐾

∑𝑖=1𝑛𝜆𝑖𝜑𝑖=0𝜆𝑖∈𝐾⋆

If 𝑛=1 then 𝜑1=0 which is a contradiction. Suppose without loss of generality that 𝑛>1. For a given 𝑥∈𝐾 we may define

𝜑̂𝑖≔(𝜑𝑖(𝑥)−𝜑𝑛(𝑥))𝜑𝑖𝑖=1…𝑛−1

We may choose 𝑥 such that at least one is non-zero. Then for all 𝑦∈𝐴 we have

∑𝑖=1𝑛−1𝜆𝑖𝜑̂𝑖(𝑦)=∑𝑖=1𝑛𝜆𝑖(𝜑𝑖(𝑥)−𝜑𝑛(𝑥))𝜑𝑖(𝑦)=∑𝑖=1𝑛𝜆𝑖𝜑𝑖(𝑥𝑦)−𝜑𝑛(𝑥)∑𝑖=1𝑛𝜆𝑖𝜑𝑖(𝑦)=0

This contradicts minimality of the subset. ∎

Proposition 3.19.145

Let 𝐾/𝑘 be a finite extension. Then the following are equivalent

  1. 𝐾/𝑘 is separable
  2. Tr𝐾/𝑘 is not identically zero

  3. The bilinear form

    (𝑥,𝑦)→Tr𝐾/𝑘(𝑥𝑦)

    is a perfect pairing (see (3.3.147)).

Proof. 𝑐)⟹𝑏) By (3.3.147) for all 0≠𝑥∈𝐾 we have 𝑦→Tr𝐾/𝑘(𝑥𝑦) is non-zero, so there exists 𝑦 such that Tr𝐾/𝑘(𝑥𝑦)≠0 as required.

𝑏)⟹𝑐) By (3.3.148) it’s enough to show that for all 0≠𝑥∈𝐾 the map 𝑦→Tr𝐾/𝑘(𝑥𝑦) is non-zero. By assumption there exists some 𝑧 such that Tr𝐾/𝑘(𝑧)≠0 then we may consider 𝑦=𝑧/𝑥.

𝑎)⟹𝑏) By (3.19.107) [𝐾:𝑘]𝑖=1, and so from (3.19.143) the trace equals ∑𝜎𝜎, which is non-zero by (3.19.144).

𝑏)⟹𝑎) Suppose 𝐾/𝑘 is not separable then [𝐾:𝑘]𝑖 is a power of 𝑝 (3.19.109), and so the trace is zero by (3.19.143). ∎
