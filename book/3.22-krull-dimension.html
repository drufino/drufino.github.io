3.22 Krull Dimension

Definition 3.22.1 (Krull Dimension)

Let 𝐴 be a commutative ring. We say that a chain of distinct prime ideals

𝔭0⊊𝔭1⊊…⊊𝔭𝑛

has length 𝑛.

  1. The Krull dimension dim𝐴 of 𝑆 is the maximum length of all chains of prime ideals.
  2. The height of a prime ideal 𝔭, denoted ht(𝔭), is the maximum length of chains of prime ideals contained in 𝔭. More generally define ht(𝔞)=inf{ht(𝔭)|𝔞⊆𝔭}. By (3.3.44) we may take the infimum over only minimal prime ideals.
  3. The dimension of an ideal 𝔞◃𝐴, denoted dim𝔞, is the maximum length of chains of prime ideals containing 𝔞.

We say 𝐴 is finite-dimensional if dim𝐴<∞. Observe that 𝔞⊆𝔭⟺𝔞⊆𝔭 for any prime ideal 𝔭 so

dim𝔞=dim𝔞ht(𝔞)=ht(𝔞)

and we may, without loss of generality, consider only radical ideals.

Definition 3.22.2

Let 𝐴 be a commutative ring. We say a chain of prime ideals is

  • maximal if it’s not properly contained in any chain
  • saturated if 𝔭𝑖⊆𝔭⊆𝔭𝑖+1⟹𝔭=𝔭𝑖 or 𝔭=𝔭𝑖+1.

We say that 𝐴 is biequidimensional if every maximal chain has the same length (equal to dim𝐴).

We say 𝐴 is quasi-biequidimensional if 𝐴/𝔭 is biequidimensional for every minimal prime 𝔭. Note that equidimensional + quasi-biequidimensional ⟺ biequidimensional.

We say that 𝐴 is catenary if the length of a saturated chain

𝔭0⊊…⊊𝔭𝑛

depends only on 𝔭0 and 𝔭𝑛 and is equal to ht(𝔭𝑛/𝔭0)

We say that 𝐴 is equidimensional if every minimal prime ideal has the same dimension (equal to dim𝐴). Note an irreducible ring has only one minimal prime ideal so is trivially equidimensional.

We say that 𝐴 is equicodimensional if every maximal ideal has the same height (equal to dim𝐴).

In order to connect this to the lattice-theoretic notion of Krull Dimension (see the Krull Dimension section of Foundations) we prove the following result (provided we consider the lattice of radical ideals ordered by reverse inclusion).

Proposition 3.22.3

Let 𝐴 be a ring then the lattice of radical ideals Rad(𝐴) is distributive, that is we have equality

𝔯1∩𝔯2+𝔯3=𝔯1∩𝔯2+𝔯1∩𝔯3

Furthermore the meet-prime radical ideals are precisely the prime ideals. Therefore the lattice of radical ideals of a finite-dimensional Noetherian ring, ordered by reverse inclusion, is a Krull Lattice.

Proof. Clearly it’s enough to show that LHS ⊆ RHS. Suppose 𝑥∈LHS then 𝑥∈𝔯1 and 𝑥𝑛=𝑎+𝑏 where 𝑎∈𝔯2 and 𝑏∈𝔯3. Then 𝑥𝑛+1=𝑎𝑥+𝑏𝑥∈𝔯1∩𝔯2+𝔯1∩𝔯3 whence 𝑥∈RHS.

We’ve shown that prime ideals are meet-prime (3.3.40). Suppose 𝔯 is a meet-prime radical ideal, and 𝑓𝑔∈𝔯. Then we claim that

𝔯+(𝑓)∩𝔯+(𝑔)⊆𝔯

For 𝑥∈LHS⟹𝑥𝑛∈𝔯+(𝑓) and 𝑥𝑚∈𝔯+(𝑔)⟹𝑥𝑛+𝑚∈𝔯⟹𝑥∈𝔯. As 𝔯 is meet-prime then for example 𝔯+(𝑓)⊆𝔯 and in particular 𝑓∈𝔯. Therefore 𝔯 is also prime. ∎

Proposition 3.22.4 (Simple properties)

The following properties of Krull dimension hold

  1. dim𝐴=dim𝐴/𝑁(𝐴)
  2. ht(𝔭)=dim𝐴𝔭
  3. dim𝔞=dim𝐴/𝔞
  4. dim𝔞=dim𝔞/𝔟 for any ideal 𝔟⊆𝔞
  5. dim𝐴=sup𝔭dim𝐴𝔭
  6. codimension inequality ht(𝔭)≥ht(𝔭/𝔮)+ht(𝔮)
  7. dim𝑘=0 for any field 𝑘
  8. A principal ideal domain 𝐴 which is not a field has dimension 1

Proof. a) By (3.3.56) there is an order-isomorphism between prime ideals of 𝐴 containing 𝑁(𝐴) and prime ideals of 𝐴/𝑁(𝐴). However by (3.3.46) all prime ideals of 𝐴 contain 𝑁(𝐴), so there is a bijection between chains of 𝐴 and chains of 𝐴/𝑁(𝐴), and the result follows.

b) This follows similarly from (3.6.42).

c) This follows similarly from (3.3.56).

d) dim𝔞=dim𝐴/𝔞=dim(𝐴/𝔞)/(𝔞/𝔟)=dim𝔞/𝔟

e) This follows from (2.5.6)

f) This follows from (2.5.6)

g) The only (prime) ideal is (0)

h) By (…) every prime ideal (besides (0)) is maximal so every chain has length at most 1. ∎

Lemma 3.22.5

Let 𝐴 be a UFD and 𝔭◃𝐴 a non-zero prime ideal. Then it contains a non-zero principal prime ideal (𝑝).

In particular ht(𝔭)=1 if and only if it is principal.

Proof. Choose 0≠𝑓∈𝔭. Then by definition it has a factorization into primes, and at least one must be in 𝔭, say 𝑝. Then (𝑝) is prime by (3.17.9). Therefore if ht(𝔭)=1 then it is equal to (𝑝).

Conversely if 𝔮⊆(𝑝) then (𝑞)⊆𝔮⊆(𝑝) for 𝑞 prime, which implies 𝑝∣𝑞. As 𝑞 is irreducible (…) then (𝑝)=(𝑞)=𝔮. Therefore ht((𝑝))=1. ∎

Proposition 3.22.6

Let 𝐴 be a Noetherian ring and 𝔞 a proper ideal. Then there are only finitely many minimal primes containing 𝔞, say 𝔭1,…,𝔭𝑛. Further we have a decomposition

𝔞=⋂𝑖=1𝑛𝔭𝑖

which is irredundant (and the only such decomposition). Furthermore

ht(𝔞)=min𝑖ht(𝔭𝑖)dim(𝔞)=max𝑖dim(𝔭𝑖)
Proof. This follows from (2.4.7) and (3.22.3) applied to the radical ideal 𝑎. ∎

We’ve noted in general (3.22.4) that the so-called codimension formula does not hold. However it holds in the following case, for essentially trivial reasons.

Proposition 3.22.8 (Codimension 1 formula)
Let 𝐴 be an irreducible Noetherian ring of finite Krull Dimension, and 𝔞 such that dim(𝔞)=dim(𝐴)−1 then ht(𝔞)=1.
Proof. Apply (2.5.7). ∎

In practice most rings of geometric interest are catenary or quasi-biequidimensional. We recall some properties and equivalent criteria for these cases.

Proposition 3.22.9 (Catenary Criteria)

Let 𝐴 be a ring. Then the following are equivalent

  1. 𝐴 is catenary

  2. For all prime ideals 𝔯⊆𝔮⊆𝔭 the following holds

    ht(𝔭/𝔯)=ht(𝔭/𝔮)+ht(𝔮/𝔯)

When 𝐴 is irreducible this is equivalent to the following condition

ht(𝔭)=ht(𝔮)+ht(𝔭/𝔮)
Proof. This is restatement of (2.5.9). ∎
Proposition 3.22.10 (Biequidimensional Criteria)

Let 𝐴 be a ring. Then the following are equivalent

  1. 𝐴 is quasi-biequidimensional
  2. 𝐴 is catenary and 𝐴/𝔭 is equicodimensional for every minimal prime 𝔭

  3. 𝐴 satisfies the formula

    dim𝔮=dim𝔭+ht(𝔭/𝔮)∀𝔮⊆𝔭
  4. 𝐴 satisfies c) whenever ht(𝔭/𝔮)=1 (i.e. 𝔮⊊𝔭 is saturated)

Furthermore

ht(𝔭)=ht(𝔭/𝔮)+ht(𝔮)
Proof. This is a restatement of (2.5.11). ∎
Proposition 3.22.11 (Irreducible Biequidimensional Criteria)

When 𝐴 is irreducible the following are equivalent

  1. 𝐴 is biequidimensional
  2. 𝐴 is quasi-biequidimensional
  3. 𝐴 is catenary and equicodimensional

  4. 𝐴 satisfies the formula

    dim𝔮=dim𝔭+ht(𝔭/𝔮)∀𝔮⊆𝔭
  5. 𝐴 satisfies d) whenever ht(𝔭/𝔮)=1 (i.e. 𝔮⊊𝔭 is saturated)
Proof. This is a restatement of (2.5.14). ∎
Proposition 3.22.12 (Codimension Formula)

Let 𝐴 be a quasi-biequidimensional ring, 𝔞 an ideal and 𝔭⊂𝔞 a prime ideal. Then the following properties hold

ht(𝔞)=ht(𝔞/𝔭)+ht(𝔭)dim𝔭=dim𝔞+ht(𝔞/𝔭)

When 𝐴 is biequidimensional (e.g. quasi-biequidimensional and integral), then for all ideals 𝔞 we have the relation

dim𝐴=dim𝔞+ht(𝔞)
Proof. This is a restatement of (2.5.13). ∎
