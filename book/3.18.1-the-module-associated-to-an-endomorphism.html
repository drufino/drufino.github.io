Proposition 3.18.5

Let 𝜙:𝑀→𝑀 be an 𝐴-module endomorphism. Then there is a well-defined 𝐴[𝑋]-module structure defined by

𝐴[𝑋]×𝑀→𝑀(𝑃,𝑚)→𝑃(𝜙)(𝑚)

With this notation then 𝜙 is itself an 𝐴[𝑋]-module endomorphism of 𝑀𝜙.

Proof. Let 𝑃,𝑄∈𝐴[𝑋] be two polynomials. Then we need to show that

(𝑃𝑄)(𝜙)=𝑃(𝜙)∘𝑄(𝜙)

which amounts to saying that 𝐴[𝑋]→End𝐴(𝑀) is an 𝐴-algebra homomorphism.

The final statement follows because 𝑋 commutes with any polynomial in 𝐴[𝑋]. More explicitly

𝜙(𝑃⋅𝑚)=𝜙(𝑃(𝜙)(𝑚))=(𝑋𝑃)(𝜙)(𝑚)=(𝑃𝑋)(𝜙)(𝑚)=𝑃(𝜙)(𝜙(𝑚))=𝑃⋅(𝜙(𝑚))

 ∎

Proposition 3.18.6

Let 𝑀 be an 𝐴-module and 𝜙∈End𝐴(𝑀). Then is an 𝐴[𝑋]-linear map

Proof. By the universal property 𝜙̃ exists and is 𝐴-linear. To show it is 𝐴[𝑋]-linear observe that

𝜙̃(𝑄(𝑃⊗𝑚))=𝜙̃((𝑄𝑃)⊗𝑚)=(𝑄𝑃)(𝜙)(𝑚)=𝑄(𝜙)(𝑃(𝜙)(𝑚))=𝑄⋅𝑃(𝜙)(𝑚)=𝑄⋅𝜙̃(𝑃⊗𝑚)

The second exists by (3.4.17). ∎
