5.8.4 Measure Spaces

To create a robust theory of integration it is necessary to replace finite additivity with countably additivity, and further show that such set functions can be defined over 𝜎-algebras and not just algebras.

Definition 5.8.22 (Countably Additive Set Function)

Let ℱ︀ be a family of subsets of Ω containing ∅. A function

𝜇:ℱ︀→[0,∞]

is a countably additive set function (or measure) if it satisfies

  1. 𝜇(∅)=0
  2. 𝜇(⨆𝑛=1∞𝐴𝑛)=∑𝑖=1∞𝜇(𝐴𝑖) whenever this is well-defined

We say it is countably subadditive if it satisfies

𝜇(⋃𝑖=1∞𝐴𝑖)≤∑𝑖=1∞𝜇(𝐴𝑖)

whenever this is well-defined. We show in (5.8.25) that these conditions are almost equivalent.

Definition 5.8.23 (Measurable Space)
Let (Ω,ℱ︀) be a measurable space and 𝜇:ℱ︀→[0,∞] a measure. Then the triplet (Ω,ℱ︀,𝜇) is a measurable space.

The following are standard results which are somewhat more awkward to prove in the case of semi-rings. However the proofs follow precisely the same lines and semi-ring extension theorem may be more directly applicable.

Proposition 5.8.24 (Continuity of Measures)

Let ℛ︀ be a semi-ring of subsets of Ω and 𝜇:ℛ︀→[0,∞] a countably additive measure. Then for any sequence of sets 𝐴1,𝐴2,…∈ℛ︀ we have

lim𝑛→∞𝜇(⋃𝑖=1𝑛𝐴𝑖)=𝜇(⋃𝑛=1∞𝐴𝑛)

whenever this is well-defined.

Proof. Define the increasing sequence

𝐵𝑛≔⋃𝑖=1𝑛𝐴𝑖

then wish to show

lim𝑛→∞𝜇(𝐵𝑛)=𝜇(⋃𝑛=1∞𝐵𝑛)

By definition we have (with the convention 𝐵0=∅)

𝐵𝑖∖⋃𝑗=1𝑖−1𝐵𝑗=𝐵𝑖∖𝐵𝑖−1=⨆𝑘=1𝑁𝑖𝐶𝑖𝑘𝑖=1,2,…

for 𝐶𝑖𝑘∈ℛ︀ whence

𝐵𝑛=⨆𝑖=1𝑛𝐵𝑖∖𝐵𝑖−1=⨆𝑖=1𝑛⨆𝑘=1𝑁𝑛𝐶𝑖𝑘

for 𝑛=1,2,…,∞. Then by additivity

lim𝑛→∞𝜇(𝐵𝑛)=lim𝑛→∞∑𝑖=1𝑛∑𝑘=1𝑁𝑖𝜇(𝐶𝑖𝑘)=∑𝑖=1∞∑𝑘=1𝑁𝑖𝜇(𝐶𝑖𝑘)

and

𝜇(⋃𝑛=1∞𝐵𝑛)=𝜇(⨆𝑖=1∞⨆𝑘=1𝑁𝑖𝐶𝑖𝑘)=∑𝑖=1∞∑𝑘=1𝑁𝑖𝜇(𝐶𝑖𝑘)

 ∎

Proposition 5.8.25 (Finitely Additive + Countably Subadditive ⟺ Countably Additive)

Let ℛ︀ be a semi-ring of subsets of Ω and 𝜇:ℛ︀→[0,∞] a finitely additive set function. Then

𝜇(⨆𝑖=1∞𝐴𝑖)≥∑𝑖=1∞𝜇(𝐴𝑖)

whenever this is well-defined. In particular 𝜇 is a measure iff it is countably subadditive.

Proof. By (5.8.9).b) we have

∑𝑖=1𝑛𝜇(𝐴𝑖)≤𝜇(⨆𝑖=1∞𝐴𝑖)

and the result follows by taking 𝑛→∞ (5.1.21).

Therefore if 𝜇 is countably subadditive it must also be countably additive. Conversely if 𝜇 is countably additive then we aim to show

𝜇(⋃𝑖=1∞𝐴𝑖)≤∑𝑖=1∞𝜇(𝐴𝑖)

for a not necessarily disjoint sequence 𝐴𝑖∈ℛ︀. By assumption 𝜇 is finitely additive, so by (5.8.9).c)

𝜇(⋃𝑖=1𝑛𝐴𝑖)≤∑𝑖=1𝑛𝜇(𝐴𝑖)≤∑𝑖=1∞𝜇(𝐴𝑖)

Using continuity of measures (5.8.24) we deduce that

𝜇(⋃𝑖=1∞𝐴𝑖)=lim𝑛→∞𝜇(⋃𝑖=1𝑛𝐴𝑖)≤∑𝑖=1∞𝜇(𝐴𝑖)

as required. ∎

Proposition 5.8.26 (Extension Theorem II)

Let ℛ︀ be a semi-ring of sets over Ω and 𝜇:ℛ︀→[0,∞] a measure. Suppose that Ω is denumerable union of sets in ℛ︀. Then there exists an extension

𝜇̃:𝜎(ℛ︀)→[0,∞]

to the 𝜎-algebra generated by ℛ︀. When 𝜇 is 𝜎-finite then this extension is unique. More precisely it is given by

𝜇̃(𝐴)≔inf{∑𝑖=1∞𝜇(𝐴𝑖)|𝐴⊂⋃𝑖=1∞𝐴𝑖,𝐴𝑖∈ℛ︀}

Proof. Define first the “outer measure”

𝜇⋆:𝒫︀(Ω)→[0,∞]𝜇⋆(𝐴)≔inf{∑𝑖=1∞𝜇(𝐸𝑖)|𝐴⊂⋃𝑖=1∞𝐸𝑖𝐸𝑖∈ℱ︀}

Note that the infimum is well-defined precisely by the hypothesis. Further define the family of subsets

ℛ︀̂≔{𝐴⊂Ω|𝜇⋆(𝐵)=𝜇⋆(𝐴∩𝐵)+𝜇⋆(𝐴𝑐∩𝐵)∀𝐵⊂Ω}

We make a number of claims

  1. 𝜇⋆(𝐴)=𝜇(𝐴) for all 𝐴∈ℛ︀

  2. 𝜇⋆ is countably subadditive and monotone

    𝜇⋆(⋃𝑖=1∞𝐴𝑖)≤∑𝑖=1∞𝜇⋆(𝐴𝑖)𝐴𝑖⊂Ω
  3. ℛ︀⊂ℛ︀̂
  4. ℛ︀̂ is a 𝜎-algebra containing 𝜎(ℛ︀) and 𝜇⋆ is a measure on ℛ︀̂

and prove each in turn

  1. Evidently 𝜇⋆(𝐴)≤𝜇(𝐴) as 𝐴⊂𝐴∪∅…∪∅…. Conversely suppose that 𝐴⊂⋃𝑖=1∞𝐴𝑖 for 𝐴𝑖∈ℛ︀ and 𝜇⋆(𝐴)<∞ (otherwise the reverse inequality is clear). Then evidently

    𝐴=⋃𝑖=1∞(𝐴𝑖∩𝐴)

    As 𝜇 is countably subadditive (5.8.25) and monotone (5.8.9) we see

    𝜇(𝐴)≤∑𝑖=1∞𝜇(𝐴𝑖∩𝐴)≤∑𝑖=1∞𝜇(𝐴𝑖)

    By definition of the infimum we find 𝜇(𝐴)≤𝜇⋆(𝐴).

  2. Let 𝐴1,…,𝐴𝑛,… be subsets of Ω such that 𝐴⊂⋃𝑖=1∞𝐴𝑖. For a given 𝜀>0, choose 𝐴𝑖𝑗∈ℛ︀ such that 𝐴𝑖⊂⋃𝑗=1∞𝐴𝑖𝑗 and

    ∑𝑗=1∞𝜇(𝐴𝑖𝑗)≤𝜇⋆(𝐴𝑖)+𝜀2𝑖

    Then evidently 𝐴⊂⋃𝑖,𝑗=1∞𝐴𝑖𝑗 and so by definition

    𝜇⋆(𝐴)≤∑𝑖,𝑗=1∞𝜇(𝐴𝑖𝑗)≤∑𝑖=1∞𝜇⋆(𝐴𝑖)+𝜀

    As 𝜀 was arbitrary we deduce that 𝜇⋆ is countably subadditive. Monotonicity is straightforward from the definition.

  3. Suppose 𝐴∈ℛ︀ and 𝐵⊂Ω then we require to prove that

    𝜇⋆(𝐵)=𝜇⋆(𝐴∩𝐵)+𝜇⋆(𝐴𝑐∩𝐵)

    By subadditivity it is sufficient so show that

    𝜇⋆(𝐵)≥𝜇⋆(𝐴∩𝐵)+𝜇⋆(𝐴𝑐∩𝐵)

    Let 𝐵⊂⋃𝑖=1∞𝐵𝑖 be an arbitrary cover with 𝐵𝑖∈ℛ︀, then by definition of 𝜇⋆ it is sufficient to show that

    𝜇⋆(𝐴∩𝐵)+𝜇⋆(𝐴𝑐∩𝐵)≤∑𝑖=1∞𝜇(𝐵𝑖)

    Recall by definition of semi-ring that

    𝐴𝑐∩𝐵𝑖=⨆𝑘=1𝑛𝑖𝐶𝑖𝑘

    for some disjoint sets 𝐶𝑖𝑘∈ℛ︀ and evidently

    𝐴∩𝐵⊂⋃𝑖=1∞(𝐴∩𝐵𝑖)𝐴𝑐∩𝐵⊂⋃𝑖=1∞⨆𝑘=1𝑛𝑖𝐶𝑖𝑘

    Therefore by monotonicity and countable subadditivity of 𝜇⋆

    𝜇⋆(𝐴∩𝐵)+𝜇⋆(𝐴𝑐∩𝐵)≤𝜇⋆(⋃𝑖=1∞(𝐴∩𝐵𝑖))+𝜇⋆(⋃𝑖=1∞⨆𝑘=1𝑛𝑖𝐶𝑖𝑘)≤∑𝑖=1∞𝜇⋆(𝐴∩𝐵𝑖)+∑𝑖=1∞∑𝑘=1𝑛𝑖𝜇(𝐶𝑖𝑘)=∑𝑖=1∞𝜇(𝐴∩𝐵𝑖)+∑𝑖=1∞∑𝑘=1𝑛𝑖𝜇(𝐶𝑖𝑘)=∑𝑖=1∞(𝜇(𝐴∩𝐵𝑖)+∑𝑘=1𝑛𝑖𝜇(𝐶𝑖𝑘))=∑𝑖=1∞𝜇(𝐵𝑖)

    where the last step follows from finite additivity of 𝜇.

  4. We prove the various properties in turn

    1. Evidently 𝜇⋆(∅)=0 and therefore ∅,Ω∈ℛ︀̂

    2. Given 𝐴1,𝐴2∈ℛ︀̂ and 𝐵⊂Ω. Observe that we may apply the measurability condition to 𝐵 and 𝐴1, and then to 𝐵∩𝐴1 and 𝐴2, and 𝐵∩𝐴1𝑐 and 𝐴2 to find

      𝜇⋆(𝐵)=𝜇⋆(𝐵∩𝐴1)+𝜇⋆(𝐵∩𝐴1𝑐)=𝜇⋆(𝐵∩𝐴1∩𝐴2)+𝜇⋆(𝐵∩𝐴1∩𝐴2𝑐)+𝜇⋆(𝐵∩𝐴1𝑐∩𝐴2)+𝜇⋆(𝐵∩𝐴1𝑐∩𝐴2𝑐)

      We may also consider the relationship with 𝐵 replaced with 𝐵∩(𝐴1∪𝐴2) to find

      Combining these two relations we deduce

      𝜇⋆(𝐵)=𝜇⋆(𝐵∩(𝐴1∪𝐴2))+𝜇⋆(𝐵∩(𝐴1∪𝐴2)𝑐)

      As 𝐵 was arbitrary then we deduce 𝐴1∪𝐴2∈ℛ︀̂. Evidently by definition 𝐴𝑐∈ℛ︀̂ and therefore by de-morgan 𝐴1∩𝐴2∈ℛ︀̂. Therefore ℛ︀̂ is closed under finite unions and intersections.

    3. Let 𝐴1,…,𝐴𝑛∈ℛ︀̂ be disjoint then we claim that

      𝜇⋆(𝐵∩(⨆𝑖=1𝑛𝐴𝑖))=∑𝑖=1𝑛𝜇⋆(𝐵∩𝐴𝑖)

      From (*) we have

      𝜇⋆(𝐵∩(𝐴1⊔𝐴2))=𝜇⋆(𝐵∩𝐴1)+𝜇⋆(𝐵∩𝐴2)

      and the result follows by induction.

    4. Let 𝐴1,𝐴2,…∈ℛ︀̂ be a countable family and 𝐵⊂Ω. We require to prove that

      𝜇⋆(𝐵)=𝜇⋆(𝐵∩⋃𝑖=1∞𝐴𝑖)+𝜇⋆(𝐵∩(⋃𝑖=1∞𝐴𝑖)𝑐)

      By (5.8.6) we may consider the decomposition

      𝐴𝑖∖⋃𝑗=1𝑖−1𝐴𝑗=⨆𝑘=1𝑛𝑖𝐶𝑖𝑘

      where 𝐶𝑖𝑘∈ℛ︀ are disjoint. Observe

      ⋃𝑖=1𝑛𝐴𝑖=⨆𝑖=1𝑛⨆𝑘=1𝑛𝑖𝐶𝑖𝑘for𝑛=1,2,…,∞

      We have shown in ii) that ⋃𝑖=1𝑛𝐴𝑖∈ℛ︀̂, therefore

      𝜇⋆(𝐵)=𝜇⋆(𝐵∩(⋃𝑖=1𝑛𝐴𝑖))+𝜇⋆(𝐵∩(⋃𝑖=1𝑛𝐴𝑖)𝑐)=𝜇⋆(𝐵∩(⨆𝑖=1𝑛⨆𝑘=1𝑛𝑖𝐶𝑖𝑘))+𝜇⋆(𝐵∩(⋃𝑖=1𝑛𝐴𝑖)𝑐)

      Recall from iii) that 𝜇⋆(𝐵∩−) is finitely additive on ℛ︀̂. Additionally by monotonicity we may deduce that

      𝜇⋆(𝐵)=∑𝑖=1𝑛∑𝑘=1𝑛𝑖𝜇⋆(𝐵∩𝐶𝑖𝑘)+𝜇⋆(𝐵∩(⋃𝑖=1𝑛𝐴𝑖)𝑐)≥∑𝑖=1𝑛∑𝑘=1𝑛𝑖𝜇⋆(𝐵∩𝐶𝑖𝑘)+𝜇⋆(𝐵∩(⋃𝑖=1∞𝐴𝑖)𝑐)

      Letting 𝑛→∞ we find by subadditivity

      𝜇⋆(𝐵)≥∑𝑖=1∞∑𝑘=1𝑛𝑖𝜇⋆(𝐵∩𝐶𝑖𝑘)+𝜇⋆(𝐵∩(⋃𝑖=1∞𝐴𝑖)𝑐)≥𝜇⋆(𝐵∩(⋃𝑖=1∞𝐴𝑖))+𝜇⋆(𝐵∩(⋃𝑖=1∞𝐴𝑖)𝑐)

      However by subadditivity the reverse inequality holds and we deduce that these are all equal. This shows that ⋃𝑖=1∞𝐴𝑖∈ℛ︀̂ and ℛ︀̂ is closed under countable unions. Consider further the case 𝐴𝑖 are disjoint and 𝐵≔⨆𝑗=1∞𝐴𝑗. Then we may assume that 𝑛𝑖=1 and 𝐶𝑖1=𝐴𝑖. Using the above relationship

      𝜇⋆(⨆𝑗=1∞𝐴𝑗)=∑𝑖=1∞𝜇⋆((⨆𝑗=1∞𝐴𝑗)∩𝐴𝑖)=∑𝑖=1∞𝜇⋆(𝐴𝑖)

      This shows that 𝜇⋆ is countably additive.

 ∎

Definition 5.8.27 (Complete Set Function)
Let (Ω,ℱ︀,𝜇) be a measure space. We say it is complete if for every null set 𝑁 (such that 𝜇(𝑁)=0) and 𝑁′⊆𝑁 we have 𝑁′∈ℱ︀.
Proposition 5.8.28 (Completion of a Measure Space)

Let (Ω,ℱ︀,𝜇) be a measure space and define the completion by

ℱ︀―≔{𝐴∪𝑍|𝐴∈ℱ︀,𝑍⊆𝑁,𝜇(𝑁)=0}𝜇―(𝐴∪𝑍)≔𝜇(𝐴)

Then (Ω,ℱ︀―,𝜇―) is a well-defined complete measure space. Furthermore ℱ︀― is the smallest complete 𝜎-algebra containing ℱ︀, and 𝜇― is the unique extension.
