6.3.11 Tate’s Approach to Residues: A Sketch

The route taken above defines Res𝑃 through a uniformiser and the formal derivative, and proves the Residue Theorem (6.3.54) by reducing to 𝐾=𝑘(𝑥) along a finite separable extension. Tate [Tat68] gives a different, intrinsic route, which we only sketch here (nothing later depends on it): the residue is defined from linear algebra alone, and the Residue Theorem is deduced from the finiteness results of the Riemann-Roch theory.

  1. Traces of finite-rank operators. The trace of a finite-rank endomorphism 𝑇 of a 𝑘-vector space 𝑉 is its trace on any finite-dimensional subspace containing 𝑇(𝑉). It is additive, Tr(𝐴𝐵)=Tr(𝐵𝐴) whenever 𝐴:𝑉→𝑊, 𝐵:𝑊→𝑉 and one of them has finite rank, and Tr(𝑇)=Tr(𝑇|𝐻)+Tr(𝑇―) if 𝑇 preserves a finite-dimensional 𝐻, with 𝑇― the induced operator on 𝑉/𝐻. In particular Tr([𝑇,𝐹])=0 for 𝐹 of finite rank and 𝑇 arbitrary.
  2. The local residue. Let 𝜋 be a 𝑘-linear retraction of the local space onto the lattice 𝒪︀𝑃 (in Tate’s setting the completion 𝐾𝑃, with 𝜋 the projection killing the principal parts). For 𝑓 in the local space let 𝑇𝑓≔𝜋∘(𝑓⋅−)|𝒪︀𝑃 be the Toeplitz operator on 𝒪︀𝑃. Then [𝑇𝑓,𝑇𝑔] has finite rank, because (1−𝜋)(𝑓𝒪︀𝑃) lies in the finite-dimensional image of 𝑡−𝑛𝒪︀𝑃/𝒪︀𝑃, and Res𝑃(𝑓d𝑔)≔Tr([𝑇𝑓,𝑇𝑔]). Changing 𝜋 changes each 𝑇𝑓 by a finite-rank operator, which does not change the trace of the commutator. So no uniformiser, derivation or chain rule enters.
  3. Local properties. It is 𝑘-linear, vanishes on differentials regular at 𝑃, has Res𝑃(d𝑡/𝑡)=deg𝑃 (the trace is taken over 𝑘) and is invariant under automorphisms of 𝐾𝑃; it should agree with the trace of the coefficient of 𝑡−1 used above.
  4. Global input. The finiteness underlying Riemann-Roch: 𝐻≔𝐾∩𝒜︀𝐾(0)=𝐿(0) and 𝒜︀𝐾/(𝐾+𝒜︀𝐾(0)) are finite dimensional (6.3.15) and 𝒜︀𝐾(𝐷)/𝒜︀𝐾(0)=⨁𝑃𝑡𝑃−𝑛𝑃𝒪︀𝑃/𝒪︀𝑃 is finite dimensional for every divisor 𝐷=∑𝑃𝑛𝑃𝑃.

  5. The Residue Theorem as a trace identity. Write 𝒜︀≔𝒜︀𝐾 and 𝐿≔𝒜︀(0)=∏𝑃𝒪︀𝑃, and for 𝑓,𝑔∈𝐾 let 𝐷 be a divisor with 𝑓𝒜︀(0),𝑔𝒜︀(0)⊂𝒜︀(𝐷).

    1. For the retraction 𝜋𝐿≔∏𝑃𝜋𝑃:𝒜︀→𝐿 and 𝑇𝑓≔𝜋𝐿∘(𝑓⋅−)|𝐿, the commutator [𝑇𝑓,𝑇𝑔] is the direct sum over 𝑃 of the local commutators, of which only finitely many are nonzero (where 𝑓,𝑔 are regular the operators are multiplication by 𝑓,𝑔 and commute). So Tr([𝑇𝑓,𝑇𝑔])=∑𝑃Res𝑃(𝑓d𝑔).
    2. As in b), Tr([𝑇𝑓,𝑇𝑔]) does not depend on the retraction 𝜋:𝒜︀→𝐿: another retraction changes 𝑇𝑓 by 𝜓∘(1−𝜋)𝑓|𝐿, which has finite rank since (1−𝜋)(𝒜︀(𝐷)) is finite dimensional.
    3. Choose 𝜋 adapted to 𝐾: write 𝐾=𝐻⊕𝑊𝐾 and choose a finite-dimensional 𝐹 with 𝒜︀=𝐿⊕𝑊𝐾⊕𝐹 (possible by d)), and let 𝜋 be the projection onto 𝐿 along 𝑊𝐾⊕𝐹. For ℎ∈𝐻, 𝑓ℎ∈𝐾=𝐻⊕𝑊𝐾 so 𝑇𝑓ℎ∈𝐻: both 𝑇𝑓,𝑇𝑔 preserve 𝐻 and act there as operators on a finite-dimensional space, whose commutator has trace 0.
    4. On 𝐿/𝐻≅(𝐿+𝐾)/𝐾≕Λ⊂𝒜︀/𝐾 the induced operator 𝑇―𝑓 is the Toeplitz operator of multiplication by 𝑓 on 𝒜︀/𝐾 (where 𝑓,𝑔 commute exactly), with respect to the retraction 𝒜︀/𝐾=Λ⊕𝐹―→Λ. Writing 𝜋―=1−𝑝 with 𝑝 the projection onto 𝐹―, and using 𝑓𝑔=𝑔𝑓 on 𝒜︀/𝐾, one finds [𝑇―𝑓,𝑇―𝑔]=−𝜋―𝑓𝑝𝑔+𝜋―𝑔𝑝𝑓 on Λ, whose trace equals Tr𝐹―([𝑝𝑔|𝐹―,𝑝𝑓|𝐹―])=0, a commutator of operators on the finite-dimensional space 𝐹―.

    By (c), (d) and additivity of the trace, Tr([𝑇𝑓,𝑇𝑔])=0, so ∑𝑃Res𝑃(𝑓d𝑔)=0 by (a) and (b).

Nothing here uses the topology of 𝐾𝑃. The construction of b) uses only the subspace 𝒪︀𝑃⊂𝐾, a retraction 𝜋:𝐾→𝒪︀𝑃, and dim𝑘𝑡−𝑛𝒪︀𝑃/𝒪︀𝑃<∞ (as 𝑘(𝔪𝑃)/𝑘 is finite): “lattices” and “continuity” reduce to commensurability of subspaces and to the pole order of 𝑓, so the local residue may be defined on 𝐾 itself, and the global step on the repartitions 𝒜︀𝐾 of this book (components in 𝐾, not in the 𝐾𝑃) together with the finiteness of d). Compared with the proof above, this avoids the reduction to 𝑘(𝑥), the trace formula and separability, and works in any characteristic. What is left to check is that this Res𝑃 agrees with the Laurent-coefficient residue used above (for a place of degree 𝑑, Res𝑃(d𝑡/𝑡)=𝑑 with the trace taken over 𝑘); the global argument of e) is then complete.
