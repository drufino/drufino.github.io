2.7.1 Images, Coimages and the First Isomorphism Theorem

In case of modules we have coker(𝑓)=𝑌/Im(𝑓) and ker(coker(𝑓))=Im(𝑓) so we might expect this relation to hold in the abstract setting.

Proposition 2.7.5 (Im(𝑓)=ker(coker(𝑓)) and Coim(𝑓)=coker(ker(𝑓)))

Let 𝒞︀ be a pointed category with kernels and cokernels and 𝑓:𝑋→𝑌 a morphism. Then there is a unique morphism 𝑒:𝑋→ker(coker(𝑓)) making the following diagram commute

When 𝒞︀ is Abelian 𝑒 is an epimorphism and Im(𝑓)=(ker(coker(𝑓)),𝑒,𝑚).

Dually, there is a unique morphism 𝑚′:coker(ker(𝑓))→𝑌 such that the following diagram commutes

When 𝒞︀ is Abelian 𝑚′ is a monomorphism and Coim(𝑓)=(coker(ker(𝑓)),𝑒′,𝑚′).

Proof. Let 𝑝:𝑌→coker(𝑓) be the cokernel of 𝑓, so by (2.6.153) 𝑝∘𝑓=0. Then by (2.6.150), applied to the kernel 𝑚=ker(𝑝), 𝑒 exists and is unique.

Dually, let 𝑖:ker(𝑓)→𝑋 be the kernel of 𝑓, so by (2.6.150) 𝑓∘𝑖=0. Then by (2.6.153), applied to the cokernel 𝑒′=coker(𝑖), 𝑚′ exists and is unique.

Now suppose additionally that 𝒞︀ is Abelian. We first show that (ker(coker(𝑓)),𝑒,𝑚)=Im(𝑓). By (2.6.135) 𝑚 is a monomorphism. Consider another factorisation 𝑓=𝑚″∘𝑒″ with 𝑚″ a monomorphism

Let 𝑟≔coker(𝑚″). Then 𝑟∘𝑓=𝑟∘𝑚″∘𝑒″=0. Therefore, by the universal property of coker(𝑓) (2.6.153), there is a unique morphism ℎ:coker(𝑓)→coker(𝑚″) such that ℎ∘𝑝=𝑟, and we deduce that 𝑟∘𝑚=ℎ∘𝑝∘𝑚=ℎ∘0=0. Therefore by (2.7.3) 𝑚 factors through 𝑚″ as required. Uniqueness follows because 𝑚″ is monic and therefore (ker(coker(𝑓)),𝑒,𝑚) is an image for 𝑓

Next we show 𝑒 is an epimorphism. By (2.7.4) applied to 𝑒 (with codomain 𝐼≔ker(coker(𝑓))), it suffices to show Im(𝑒)=𝐼, i.e. that (𝐼,𝑒,1𝐼) is the image of 𝑒. Suppose 𝑒=𝑚1∘𝑒1 for some monomorphism 𝑚1:𝐼1→𝐼. Then 𝑓=𝑚∘𝑒=(𝑚∘𝑚1)∘𝑒1 is a factorisation of 𝑓 through the monomorphism 𝑚∘𝑚1, so by the universal property of the image (𝐼,𝑒,𝑚) of 𝑓 (2.6.167) (established above) there is a unique morphism 𝑔:𝐼→𝐼1 such that (𝑚∘𝑚1)∘𝑔=𝑚; as 𝑚 is monic this gives 𝑚1∘𝑔=1𝐼. This is precisely the factorisation required for (𝐼,𝑒,1𝐼) to be the image of 𝑒, so Im(𝑒)=𝐼, and therefore 𝑒 is an epimorphism.

The remaining claims for the dual statement now follow by duality, applying the above to 𝑓op in 𝒞︀op, which is again Abelian (2.7.2): by (2.6.154), ker𝒞︀op(coker𝒞︀op(𝑓op)) is identified with coker(ker(𝑓)), matching (by uniqueness of the first paragraph, dualized) our 𝑚′ and 𝑒′, and by (2.6.33) and (2.6.169) the epic/image conclusions in 𝒞︀op translate to 𝑚′ monic and (coker(ker(𝑓)),𝑒′,𝑚′)=Coim(𝑓) in 𝒞︀. ∎

In the category of modules we have coker(ker(𝑓))=𝑋/ker(𝑓) and a canonical isomorphism 𝑋/ker(𝑓)→∼Im(𝑓)=ker(coker(𝑓)), so we may expect something similar to hold true in the Abelian setting.

Proposition 2.7.6 (First Isomorphism Theorem)

Let 𝒞︀ be a pointed category with kernels and cokernels and 𝑓:𝑋→𝑌 a morphism. Then there is a unique morphism 𝜃:coker(ker(𝑓))→ker(coker(𝑓)) such that the following diagram commutes

When 𝒞︀ is an Abelian category then 𝜃 is an isomorphism. Furthermore both (coker(ker(𝑓)),𝑒′,𝑚∘𝜃) and (ker(coker(𝑓)),𝜃∘𝑒′,𝑚) are epi-mono factorisations of 𝑓 and both an image and coimage.

Proof. Drafted by Claude Sonnet 5, reviewed by David Rufino.

By (2.7.5) there is a unique morphism 𝑚′:coker(ker(𝑓))→𝑌 such that 𝑓=𝑚′∘𝑒′.

Since 𝑞∘𝑓=0, where 𝑞:𝑌→coker(𝑓) is the cokernel of 𝑓 (2.6.153), we have 𝑞∘𝑚′∘𝑒′=𝑞∘𝑓=0; as 𝑒′ is epic (2.6.135), 𝑞∘𝑚′=0. So by the universal property of 𝑚=ker(𝑞) (2.6.150), there is a unique morphism 𝜃:coker(ker(𝑓))→ker(coker(𝑓)) such that 𝑚∘𝜃=𝑚′. Combining, 𝑚∘𝜃∘𝑒′=𝑚′∘𝑒′=𝑓, as required.

For uniqueness, suppose 𝜃′ also satisfies 𝑚∘𝜃′∘𝑒′=𝑓. Then 𝑚∘(𝜃∘𝑒′)=𝑓=𝑚∘(𝜃′∘𝑒′), and since 𝑚 is monic (2.6.135), 𝜃∘𝑒′=𝜃′∘𝑒′; as 𝑒′ is epic (2.6.135), 𝜃=𝜃′.

Suppose now 𝒞︀ is Abelian, so that 𝑓=𝑚∘𝑒 is the image factorisation and 𝑓=𝑚′∘𝑒′ is the coimage factorisation (2.7.5). Since 𝑚∘𝜃∘𝑒′=𝑓=𝑚∘𝑒 and 𝑚 is monic (2.6.135), we may conclude that 𝜃∘𝑒′=𝑒.

Since 𝑚∘𝜃=𝑚′ is monic, 𝜃 is monic, and since 𝜃∘𝑒′=𝑒 is epic, 𝜃 is epic. As 𝒞︀ is Abelian it is binormal (2.7.1), hence balanced (2.6.164), so 𝜃, being both monic and epic, is an isomorphism.

Finally, applying (2.6.172) to the coimage (coker(ker(𝑓)),𝑒′,𝑚′), and dually to the image (ker(coker(𝑓)),𝑒,𝑚), via the isomorphism 𝜃 shows each factorisation is both an image and a coimage of 𝑓. ∎

Proposition 2.7.7 (Epi-Mono Factorisation in an Abelian Category)

Let 𝒞︀ be an Abelian category and 𝑓:𝑋→𝑌 a morphism. Then 𝑓 factors as 𝑓=𝑚∘𝑒 for some epimorphism 𝑒 and monomorphism 𝑚

and this factorisation is unique up to isomorphism: if 𝑓=𝑚1∘𝑒1 is another such factorisation, with 𝑒1:𝑋→𝐼1 epic and 𝑚1 monic, then there is a unique morphism 𝜃:𝐼→𝐼1 such that the following diagram commutes, and 𝜃 is (moreover) an isomorphism

Moreover, every epimorphism and every monomorphism of 𝒞︀ is regular, so any such factorisation 𝑓=𝑚1∘𝑒1 is simultaneously a regular epi-mono and an epi-regular-mono factorisation of 𝑓, and hence (𝐼1,𝑒1,𝑚1) is both an image and a coimage of 𝑓 (2.6.174).

Proof. Drafted by Claude Sonnet 5, reviewed by David Rufino.

Regularity. Since 𝒞︀ is Abelian it is binormal (2.7.1), so every monomorphism is normal and every epimorphism is conormal (2.6.163), hence every monomorphism and every epimorphism of 𝒞︀ is regular (2.6.160). In particular, for any epi-mono factorisation 𝑓=𝑚1∘𝑒1, 𝑒1 is a regular epimorphism and 𝑚1 is a regular monomorphism, so 𝑓=𝑚1∘𝑒1 is simultaneously a regular epi-mono and an epi-regular-mono factorisation of 𝑓, and by (2.6.174) (both halves), (𝐼1,𝑒1,𝑚1) is both an image and a coimage of 𝑓.

Existence. By (2.7.5), taking 𝐼≔ker(coker(𝑓)) with structure maps 𝑒,𝑚, we obtain a factorisation 𝑓=𝑚∘𝑒 with 𝑚 monic and, since 𝒞︀ is Abelian, 𝑒 epic.

Uniqueness up to isomorphism. Let 𝑓=𝑚1∘𝑒1 be any other such factorisation, 𝑒1:𝑋→𝐼1. By the Regularity paragraph, 𝑒 and 𝑒1 are regular epimorphisms, so 𝑓=𝑚∘𝑒 and 𝑓=𝑚1∘𝑒1 are regular epi-mono factorisations of 𝑓 (2.6.173), and (2.6.178) gives a unique isomorphism 𝜃:𝐼→𝐼1 with 𝜃∘𝑒=𝑒1 and 𝑚1∘𝜃=𝑚.

Alternatively, uniqueness may be shown directly from the universal property of the image (2.6.167), without invoking regular epi-mono factorisations at all.

Uniqueness up to isomorphism. Let 𝑓=𝑚1∘𝑒1 be any other epi-mono factorisation, 𝑒1:𝑋→𝐼1. Since (𝐼,𝑒,𝑚)=Im(𝑓) (2.7.5) and 𝑚1 is monic, the universal property of the image (2.6.167) gives a unique morphism 𝜃:𝐼→𝐼1 such that 𝑚1∘𝜃=𝑚, and (as noted there) 𝜃∘𝑒=𝑒1.

We show 𝜃 is an isomorphism. If 𝜃𝑘1=𝜃𝑘2 then 𝑚1𝜃𝑘1=𝑚1𝜃𝑘2, i.e. 𝑚𝑘1=𝑚𝑘2, and since 𝑚 is monic, 𝑘1=𝑘2; so 𝜃 is monic. Since 𝜃∘𝑒=𝑒1 is epic, 𝜃 is epic. As 𝒞︀ is Abelian it is binormal (2.7.1), hence balanced (2.6.164), so 𝜃, being both monic and epic, is an isomorphism. ∎
