5.4.2 Product Space

Definition 5.4.8 (Product Norm)

Let 𝑋1,⋯,𝑋𝑛 be normed vector spaces over the same valued field 𝐾. Then we may define the product norm on the vector space 𝑋1×⋯×𝑋𝑛 by

‖(𝑥1,⋯,𝑥𝑛)‖≔max(‖𝑥1‖,⋯,‖𝑥𝑛‖)
Proposition 5.4.9
Let 𝑋1,⋯,𝑋𝑛 be normed vector spaces over the same valued field 𝐾. Then the metric (resp. topology) induced by product norm is the same as product metric (resp. product topology).

Proof. Consider a point 𝑥=(𝑥1,⋯,𝑥𝑛) then

𝐵(𝑥;𝜀)={(𝑥1,⋯,𝑥𝑛)|‖𝑥𝑖‖≤𝜀}=𝐵(𝑥1;𝜀)×⋯×𝐵(𝑥𝑛;𝜀)

Therefore the open balls are open in the product topology, and being a base for the norm topology, this is a subset of the product topology (…). To show that the product topology is a subset of the norm topology it is sufficient to show that open sets of the form

𝑈≔𝑈1×⋯×𝑈𝑛

are open in the product norm topology. For given 𝑦∈𝑈 then by definition 𝐵(𝑦𝑖;𝜀𝑖)⊂𝑈𝑖. Consider 𝜀≔min(𝜀1,⋯,𝜀𝑛) and 𝑧∈𝐵(𝑦;𝜀). Then

‖𝑧−𝑦‖<𝜀⟹max𝑖(‖𝑧𝑖−𝑦𝑖‖)<𝜀≤𝜀𝑖⟹𝑧𝑖∈𝐵(𝑦𝑖;𝜀𝑖)⟹𝑧∈𝑈

in other words 𝐵(𝑦;𝜀)⊂𝑈 as required. ∎

Proposition 5.4.10 (Arithmetic Operations are Continuous)

Let 𝑋 be a normed vector space over a valued field 𝐾. The following maps are continuous

  1. addition +:𝑋×𝑋→𝑋
  2. scalar multiplication ⋅:𝐾×𝑋→𝑋
  3. inverse scalar (−)−1:𝐾∖{0}→𝐾

Similarly if 𝑋 is a Banach algebra then the multiplication operator

⋅:𝑋×𝑋→𝐾

is also continuous.

Proof. By the triangle inequality

‖(𝑥1,𝑥2)−(𝑦1,𝑦2)‖<𝜀⟹‖𝑥1−𝑦1‖<𝜀∧‖𝑥2−𝑦2‖<𝜀⟹‖(𝑥1+𝑥2)−(𝑦1+𝑦2)‖<2𝜀

Therefore we may apply the criterion given by (5.4.6), and using the product norm.

For scalar multiplication observe

‖(𝜆1,𝑥1)−(𝜆2,𝑥2)‖<𝛿⟹|𝜆1−𝜆2|<𝛿∧‖𝑥1−𝑥2‖<𝛿⟹‖𝜆1𝑥1−𝜆2𝑥2‖≤|𝜆1|‖𝑥1−𝑥2‖+‖𝑥2‖|𝜆1−𝜆2|⟹‖𝜆1𝑥1−𝜆2𝑥2‖≤|𝜆1|‖𝑥1−𝑥2‖+(‖𝑥1‖+𝛿)|𝜆1−𝜆2|⟹‖𝜆1𝑥1−𝜆2𝑥2‖≤𝛿2+(‖𝑥1‖+𝛿)𝛿

Therefore choose 𝛿<min(1,𝜀‖𝑥1‖+1) to find ‖𝜆1𝑥1−𝜆2𝑥2‖<3𝜀. ∎
