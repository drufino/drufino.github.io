2.6.4 Equivalence of categories

Definition 2.6.25 (Equivalence of categories)

Let 𝒞︀, 𝒟︀ be categories. An equivalence of categories consists of a pair of functors (either both covariant or both contravariant)

𝒞︀⇌𝐺𝐹𝒟︀

together with natural isomorphisms

𝜂:id𝒞︀⇒𝐺𝐹𝜖:𝐹𝐺⇒id𝒟︀

We say 𝐹 is an equivalence of categories if there exists some 𝐺 satisfying these conditions.

Definition 2.6.26
We say a functor 𝐹:𝒞︀→𝒟︀ is essentially surjective if for all 𝐷∈𝒟︀ there exists 𝐶∈𝒞︀ such that 𝐹(𝐶) is isomorphic to 𝐷.
Lemma 2.6.27

Let 𝐹:𝒞︀→𝒟︀, 𝐺:𝒟︀→𝒞︀ be functors.

If there exists a natural isomorphism 𝜂:id𝒞︀⇒𝐺𝐹 then 𝐹 is faithful. Explicitly 𝐹(−) has a left-inverse given by

𝑔→𝜂𝐶′−1∘𝐺(𝑔)∘𝜂𝐶

Furthermore 𝐺𝐹(𝜂𝐶)=𝜂𝐺𝐹(𝐶).

Proof. Consider the sequence of maps

Mor(𝐶,𝐶′)⟶𝐹(−)Mor(𝐹(𝐶),𝐹(𝐶′))⟶𝐺(−)Mor(𝐺𝐹(𝐶),𝐺𝐹(𝐶′))⟶Mor(𝜂𝐶,𝜂𝐶′−1)Mor(𝐶,𝐶′)

Note that the composite of this map is given by

𝑓→𝜂𝐶′−1∘𝐺𝐹(𝑓)∘𝜂𝐶=𝜂𝐶′−1∘𝜂𝐶′∘𝑓=𝑓

in other words 𝐹(−) has a left inverse and therefore 𝐹 is faithful.

Note by naturality we have 𝐺𝐹(𝜂𝐶)∘𝜂𝐶=𝜂𝐺𝐹(𝐶)∘𝜂𝐶. Since 𝜂𝐶 is an isomorphism we may cancel to find 𝐺𝐹(𝜂𝐶)=𝜂𝐺𝐹(𝐶). ∎

Proposition 2.6.28 (Equivalence is full and faithful)

Let 𝐹:𝒞︀→𝒟︀ be a functor then the following are equivalent

  • 𝐹 is full, faithful and essentially surjective (2.6.26)
  • 𝐹 is an equivalence of categories (2.6.25)

In other words 𝐹(−) is bijective and hence has a two-sided inverse. Explicitly it is given by

Mor(𝐶,𝐶′)⟷Mor(𝐹(𝐶),𝐹(𝐶′))𝑓→𝐹(𝑓)𝜂𝐶′−1∘𝐺(𝑔)∘𝜂𝐶←𝑔

Proof. We prove only the second implies the first. By assumption there is an equivalence with 𝐺 and by the previous Lemma both 𝐹 and 𝐺 are faithful by considering 𝜂 and 𝜖−1 in turn. Further the given map is already shown to be a left inverse. We claim it’s also a right inverse, for consider

𝑔′≔𝐹(𝜂𝐶′−1)∘𝐹𝐺(𝑔)∘𝐹(𝜂𝐶).

We claim that 𝐺(𝑔′)=𝐺(𝑔). As 𝐺 is faithful this would imply 𝑔′=𝑔 and the given map is a right inverse as required. Observe

𝐺(𝑔′)=𝐺𝐹(𝜂𝐶′−1)∘𝐺𝐹𝐺(𝑔)∘𝐺𝐹(𝜂𝐶)=𝜂𝐺𝐹(𝐶′)−1∘𝐺𝐹𝐺(𝑔)∘𝜂𝐺𝐹(𝐶)=𝜂𝐺𝐹(𝐶′)−1∘𝜂𝐺𝐹(𝐶′)∘𝐺(𝑔)=𝐺(𝑔)

where we have used the result that 𝐺𝐹(𝜂𝐶)=𝜂𝐺𝐹(𝐶). Since the maps are mutual inverses we see that 𝐹 is full and faithful as required.

Given 𝐷∈𝒟︀ then 𝐹(𝐺(𝐷)) is isomorphic to 𝐷 via 𝜖 so 𝐹 is essentially surjective. ∎

Proposition 2.6.29 (Duality)

Let (−)⋆:𝒞︀→𝒞︀ be a contravariant functor such that there is a natural isomorphism

𝜂:id𝒞︀⇒(−)⋆⋆

then (−)⋆ is an equivalence of categories and in particular full and faithful.

Proof. Define 𝜖=𝜂−1 to determine the equivalence of categories. By the previous result then (−)⋆ is full and faithful. ∎
