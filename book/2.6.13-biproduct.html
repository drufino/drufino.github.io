2.6.13 Biproduct

In 𝐀𝐛𝐆𝐫𝐩 and 𝐴-𝐌𝐨𝐝 coproducts and products coincide, so we formalise this property.

Definition 2.6.103 (Finite Biproduct)

Let 𝒞︀ be a pointed category, 𝐼 a finite set and {𝑋𝑖}𝑖∈𝐼 be a family of objects. Suppose both the product ∏𝑖∈𝐼𝑋𝑖 and coproduct ∐𝑖∈𝐼𝑋𝑖 exists. Then there is a unique comparison morphism

  1. 𝜓 is an isomorphism

Proposition 2.6.105 (Binary biproducts are enough)
Let 𝒞︀ be a pointed category. Then it has all (finite) biproducts iff it has all binary biproducts (i.e. the case #𝐼=2).

Proof. We prove that ⨁𝑖∈𝐼𝑋𝑖 exists for 𝐼={1,…,𝑛} by induction on 𝑛, where 𝑛=1 is immediate and 𝑛=2 is the base case. Consider a biproduct (⨁𝑖=1𝑛𝑋𝑖,{𝑠𝑖},{𝜋𝑖}) which we denote by 𝑌, and the binary biproduct (𝑌⊕𝑋𝑛+1,{𝑓,𝑔},{𝑝,𝑞}). Then we claim that

𝑍≔(𝑌⊕𝑋𝑛+1,{𝑓∘𝑠1,…,𝑓∘𝑠𝑛,𝑔},{𝜋1∘𝑝,…,𝜋𝑛∘𝑝,𝑞})

is a biproduct. There is a natural isomorphism

Mor(𝑍,−)→∼Mor(𝑌,−)×Mor(𝑋𝑛+1,−)→∼∏𝑖=1𝑛+1Mor(𝑋𝑖,−)

which shows that 𝑍 is a product with the given projection maps. Similarly we may show that 𝑍 is a coproduct. Finally

𝜋𝑖∘𝑝∘𝑓∘𝑠𝑗=𝜋𝑖∘1𝑌∘𝑠𝑗=𝛿𝑖𝑗𝜋𝑖∘𝑝∘𝑔=𝜋𝑖∘0=0𝑞∘𝑓∘𝑠𝑗=0∘𝑠𝑗=0

so that the cone and co-cone morphisms satisfy the required orthogonality conditions. ∎

In 𝐀𝐛𝐆𝐫𝐩 and 𝐴-𝐌𝐨𝐝, a homomorphism between direct sums ⨁𝑗∈𝐽𝑋𝑗→⨁𝑖∈𝐼𝑌𝑖 is determined by, and can be recovered from, a matrix of homomorphisms 𝑋𝑗→𝑌𝑖; we record this categorically.

Definition 2.6.106 (Matrix of a Morphism)

Let 𝒞︀ be a pointed category with biproducts ⨁𝑗∈𝐽𝑋𝑗 (injections 𝑠𝑗, projections 𝜋𝑗𝑋) and ⨁𝑖∈𝐼𝑌𝑖 (injections 𝑠𝑖𝑌, projections 𝜋𝑖𝑌), and 𝑓:⨁𝑗∈𝐽𝑋𝑗→⨁𝑖∈𝐼𝑌𝑖 a morphism. The matrix of 𝑓 is the family (𝑓𝑖𝑗)𝑖∈𝐼,𝑗∈𝐽 of morphisms

𝑓𝑖𝑗≔𝜋𝑖𝑌∘𝑓∘𝑠𝑗:𝑋𝑗→𝑌𝑖

When 𝐼={1,…,𝑚} and 𝐽={1,…,𝑛} we display this as an 𝑚×𝑛 array (𝑓𝑖𝑗), as for ordinary matrices.

Remark 2.6.107
Calling (𝑓𝑖𝑗) a matrix is not yet fully justified: for this notation to behave like ordinary matrix notation, composition should correspond to matrix multiplication, (𝑓∘𝑔)𝑖𝑘=∑𝑗𝑓𝑖𝑗∘𝑔𝑗𝑘, but this formula presupposes an addition on Mor(𝑋𝑘,𝑌𝑖), which is no part of the data of a pointed category with biproducts. We can nonetheless justify composition whenever it requires no addition, namely when each row of 𝑓 (or column of 𝑔) has at most one nonzero entry (2.6.114); addition on hom-sets is only introduced once biproducts are used to construct such an addition (2.6.118), after which the general composition formula above is recovered in full (2.6.122).
Proposition 2.6.108 (Matrix Notation for Biproducts)

In the setting of (2.6.106), the assignment 𝑓↦(𝑓𝑖𝑗)𝑖∈𝐼,𝑗∈𝐽 is a bijection

Mor(⨁𝑗∈𝐽𝑋𝑗,⨁𝑖∈𝐼𝑌𝑖)⟶∼∏𝑖∈𝐼,𝑗∈𝐽Mor(𝑋𝑗,𝑌𝑖)

Proof. Drafted by Claude Sonnet 5, reviewed by David Rufino.

Given a family (𝑔𝑖𝑗)𝑖∈𝐼,𝑗∈𝐽 of morphisms 𝑔𝑖𝑗:𝑋𝑗→𝑌𝑖, for each 𝑗∈𝐽 the family (𝑔𝑖𝑗)𝑖∈𝐼 determines, by the universal property of the product ⨁𝑖∈𝐼𝑌𝑖 (2.6.81), a unique morphism 𝑐𝑗:𝑋𝑗→⨁𝑖∈𝐼𝑌𝑖 with 𝜋𝑖𝑌∘𝑐𝑗=𝑔𝑖𝑗 for every 𝑖∈𝐼. In turn, the family (𝑐𝑗)𝑗∈𝐽 determines, by the universal property of the coproduct ⨁𝑗∈𝐽𝑋𝑗 (2.6.93), a unique morphism 𝑔:⨁𝑗∈𝐽𝑋𝑗→⨁𝑖∈𝐼𝑌𝑖 with 𝑔∘𝑠𝑗=𝑐𝑗 for every 𝑗∈𝐽. We show 𝑓↦(𝑓𝑖𝑗) and (𝑔𝑖𝑗)↦𝑔 are mutually inverse.

Starting from 𝑓, form its matrix (𝑓𝑖𝑗) and then 𝑐𝑗,𝑔 as above. Since 𝜋𝑖𝑌∘(𝑓∘𝑠𝑗)=𝑓𝑖𝑗 for every 𝑖, 𝑓∘𝑠𝑗 satisfies the defining property of 𝑐𝑗, so 𝑐𝑗=𝑓∘𝑠𝑗 by uniqueness; then 𝑓 itself satisfies 𝑓∘𝑠𝑗=𝑐𝑗 for every 𝑗, so 𝑔=𝑓 by uniqueness.

Starting from a family (𝑔𝑖𝑗), form 𝑐𝑗,𝑔 as above and then the matrix of 𝑔: for every 𝑖,𝑗, the (𝑖,𝑗) entry of the matrix of 𝑔 is 𝜋𝑖𝑌∘𝑔∘𝑠𝑗=𝜋𝑖𝑌∘𝑐𝑗=𝑔𝑖𝑗, using 𝑔∘𝑠𝑗=𝑐𝑗 and then the defining property of 𝑐𝑗. So the matrix of 𝑔 is again (𝑔𝑖𝑗). ∎

Remark 2.6.109
The matrix of 1⨁𝑖∈𝐼𝑋𝑖 is the identity matrix (𝛿𝑖𝑗1𝑋𝑖)𝑖,𝑗∈𝐼, immediate from the orthogonality conditions 𝜋𝑖∘𝑠𝑗=𝛿𝑖𝑗 (2.6.103).
Remark 2.6.110
Any object 𝑋 is trivially a biproduct of the one-element family {𝑋}, via (𝑋,{1𝑋},{1𝑋}) (the orthogonality condition reduces to 1𝑋∘1𝑋=1𝑋). This lets us apply (2.6.108) whenever one side of a morphism is a single object rather than a genuine biproduct, by regarding it as such.
Remark 2.6.111 (Injections and Projections as Matrices)

For the binary biproduct 𝑋1⊕𝑋2, the injections and projections are themselves matrices in the sense of (2.6.106), namely the columns and rows of the identity matrix

𝑠1=(1𝑋10)𝑠2=(01𝑋2)𝜋1=(1𝑋10)𝜋2=(01𝑋2)

immediate from 𝜋𝑖∘𝑠𝑗=𝛿𝑖𝑗 (2.6.103). More generally, for ⨁𝑖∈𝐼𝑋𝑖, regarding 𝑋𝑖0 as the trivial biproduct of {𝑋𝑖0}, 𝑠𝑖0 is the 𝑖0-th column and 𝜋𝑖0 the 𝑖0-th row of the identity matrix (𝛿𝑖𝑗1𝑋𝑖)𝑖,𝑗∈𝐼.

Lemma 2.6.112 (A Matrix is a Row of Columns and a Column of Rows)

Let 𝑀:𝑋1⊕𝑋2→𝑌1⊕𝑌2 have matrix (𝑀11𝑀12𝑀21𝑀22). Then

𝑀=((𝑀11𝑀21)(𝑀12𝑀22))=((𝑀11𝑀12)(𝑀21𝑀22))

Proof. Drafted by Claude Sonnet 5, reviewed by David Rufino.

Write 𝑐1≔𝑀∘𝑠1, 𝑐2≔𝑀∘𝑠2 for the columns of 𝑀. The morphism (𝑐1𝑐2):𝑋1⊕𝑋2→𝑌1⊕𝑌2 is, by definition of the coproduct-induced notation (2.6.93), the unique morphism satisfying (𝑐1𝑐2)∘𝑠1=𝑐1 and (𝑐1𝑐2)∘𝑠2=𝑐2. Since 𝑀 itself satisfies 𝑀∘𝑠1=𝑐1 and 𝑀∘𝑠2=𝑐2 by definition of 𝑐1,𝑐2, uniqueness gives 𝑀=(𝑐1𝑐2).

Dually, writing 𝑟1≔𝜋1∘𝑀, 𝑟2≔𝜋2∘𝑀 for the rows of 𝑀, (𝑟1𝑟2) is the unique morphism with 𝜋1∘(𝑟1𝑟2)=𝑟1 and 𝜋2∘(𝑟1𝑟2)=𝑟2 (2.6.81), and 𝑀 satisfies these by definition of 𝑟1,𝑟2, so 𝑀=(𝑟1𝑟2). ∎

Lemma 2.6.113 (Composing a Column or Row on the Outside)

This is a general fact about products and coproducts, not requiring biproducts: let 𝑝:𝑍→𝑌1, 𝑞:𝑍→𝑌2 and 𝑁:𝑊→𝑍. Then

(𝑝𝑞)∘𝑁=(𝑝∘𝑁𝑞∘𝑁)

Dually, for 𝑎:𝑋1→𝑍, 𝑏:𝑋2→𝑍 and 𝑁:𝑍→𝑊

𝑁∘(𝑎𝑏)=(𝑁∘𝑎𝑁∘𝑏)

Proof. Drafted by Claude Sonnet 5, reviewed by David Rufino.

Write 𝜋1,𝜋2 for the projections of 𝑌1⊕𝑌2. Since 𝜋1∘(𝑝𝑞)=𝑝 and 𝜋2∘(𝑝𝑞)=𝑞 (2.6.106), for any 𝑁:𝑊→𝑍, by associativity of composition

𝜋1∘((𝑝𝑞)∘𝑁)=𝑝∘𝑁𝜋2∘((𝑝𝑞)∘𝑁)=𝑞∘𝑁

so (𝑝𝑞)∘𝑁 satisfies the defining property of (𝑝∘𝑁𝑞∘𝑁), and the two are equal by the uniqueness clause of (2.6.81).

Dually, writing 𝑠1,𝑠2 for the injections of 𝑋1⊕𝑋2, since (𝑎𝑏)∘𝑠1=𝑎 and (𝑎𝑏)∘𝑠2=𝑏, for any 𝑁:𝑍→𝑊

(𝑁∘(𝑎𝑏))∘𝑠1=𝑁∘𝑎(𝑁∘(𝑎𝑏))∘𝑠2=𝑁∘𝑏

so 𝑁∘(𝑎𝑏) satisfies the defining property of (𝑁∘𝑎𝑁∘𝑏), and the two are equal by the uniqueness clause of (2.6.93). ∎
