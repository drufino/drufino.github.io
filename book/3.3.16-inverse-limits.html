3.3.16 Inverse Limits

We record the basic construction of an inverse limit of a tower of rings, modules, or algebras, as a concrete model for the categorical inverse limit (2.6.129), together with its functoriality and its compatibility with products; these are used repeatedly to build completions later on.

Definition 3.3.153 (Inverse Limit of a Tower)

Let 𝒞︀ be one of 𝐑𝐢𝐧𝐠, 𝐴-𝐌𝐨𝐝 (for a fixed ring 𝐴), or Alg𝐴 (for a fixed ring 𝐴, so in particular 𝐀𝐥𝐠𝑘) – each an algebraic category (2.6.184) (via the usual axioms; (2.6.185), (2.6.186), (2.6.187) respectively). Let ⋯↠𝑀2↠𝑀1↠𝑀0 be a sequence of morphisms 𝜌𝑛:𝑀𝑛→𝑀𝑛−1 of 𝒞︀ (𝑛≥1). Define

lim⟵𝑛𝑀𝑛≔{(𝑚𝑛)𝑛≥0∈∏𝑛≥0𝑀𝑛|𝜌𝑛(𝑚𝑛)=𝑚𝑛−1for every𝑛≥1},

a sub-object of ∏𝑛𝑀𝑛 in 𝒞︀ (a subring, submodule, or subalgebra respectively, the ambient operations acting componentwise), with the evident projections 𝜆𝑛:lim⟵𝑚𝑀𝑚→𝑀𝑛. Writing 𝜌𝑛,𝑛≔1𝑀𝑛 and 𝜌𝑛,𝑚≔𝜌𝑚+1∘⋯∘𝜌𝑛:𝑀𝑛→𝑀𝑚 for 𝑛>𝑚 (composites of consecutive 𝜌𝑘’s), every (𝑚𝑛)𝑛≥0∈lim⟵𝑛𝑀𝑛 satisfies the repeated compatibility

Lemma 3.3.154 (This Realises the Categorical Inverse Limit)
In the setting of (3.3.153), regard 𝐼≔ℕ (usual order) as a directed preorder and the tower as an inverse system 𝐹:𝐼op→𝒞︀, 𝐹(𝑛)≔𝑀𝑛, with restriction maps 𝜌𝑛,𝑚 as in (3.3.153). Then (lim⟵𝑛𝑀𝑛,{𝜆𝑛}) is an inverse limit for 𝐹 in the sense of (2.6.129).

Proof. Drafted by Claude Sonnet 5, reviewed by David Rufino.

𝒞︀ is an algebraic category (2.6.184), in each of the three cases (as recalled in (3.3.153)). By (2.6.191), lim⟵𝑛𝐹(𝑛) exists in 𝒞︀, with underlying set {(𝑚𝑛)𝑛∈∏𝑛𝑀𝑛:𝜌𝑛,𝑚(𝑚𝑛)=𝑚𝑚for all𝑛≥𝑚} and the ambient operations acting coordinatewise; by the repeated compatibility (3.3.153), this defining condition holds for all 𝑛≥𝑚 as soon as it holds for consecutive 𝑛=𝑚+1, so this is exactly the sub-object lim⟵𝑛𝑀𝑛 of (3.3.153), with the same (componentwise) structure and projections. So (lim⟵𝑛𝑀𝑛,{𝜆𝑛}) is this categorical inverse limit. ∎

Corollary 3.3.155 (Functoriality of the Inverse Limit)

Let 𝒞︀ be as in (3.3.153), (𝐴𝑛,𝜌𝑛)𝑛≥0 and (𝐵𝑛,𝜏𝑛)𝑛≥0 towers in 𝒞︀, and for each 𝑛≥0 let 𝜎𝑛:𝐴𝑛→𝐵𝑛 be a morphism of 𝒞︀ such that, for every 𝑛≥1, the square

commutes, i.e. 𝜏𝑛∘𝜎𝑛=𝜎𝑛−1∘𝜌𝑛. Then

lim⟵𝜎:lim⟵𝑛𝐴𝑛⟶lim⟵𝑛𝐵𝑛,(𝑎𝑛)𝑛↦(𝜎𝑛(𝑎𝑛))𝑛

is a well-defined morphism of 𝒞︀; and if every 𝜎𝑛 is an isomorphism, so is lim⟵𝜎.

Proof. By (3.3.154), lim⟵𝑛𝐴𝑛 and lim⟵𝑛𝐵𝑛 (with their projections) are inverse limits, in the sense of (2.6.129), of the inverse systems 𝐹,𝐺:ℕop→𝒞︀ built from the two towers; the commuting squares above are exactly the naturality hypothesis of (2.6.132) for the family 𝜎=(𝜎𝑛)𝑛 (composing squares for consecutive 𝑛 gives it for the composite restriction maps 𝜌𝑛,𝑚, 𝜏𝑛,𝑚, 𝑛>𝑚, as needed there). That corollary gives the induced morphism lim⟵𝑛𝜎𝑛, agreeing on each coordinate with 𝜎𝑛 by construction of the induced map on limits-as-sets (2.6.130) – that is, the map (𝑎𝑛)𝑛→(𝜎𝑛(𝑎𝑛))𝑛 above – and an isomorphism whenever every 𝜎𝑛 is. ∎
Proposition 3.3.156 (Inverse Limits Commute with Products)

Let 𝒞︀ be as in (3.3.153), 𝐾 a set, and for each 𝑘∈𝐾 a tower (𝑀𝑘,𝑛,𝜌𝑘,𝑛)𝑛≥0 in 𝒞︀. Then (∏𝑘∈𝐾𝑀𝑘,𝑛)𝑛≥0, with restriction maps ∏𝑘∈𝐾𝜌𝑘,𝑛:∏𝑘𝑀𝑘,𝑛→∏𝑘𝑀𝑘,𝑛−1, is again a tower in 𝒞︀, and

Φ:∏𝑘∈𝐾lim⟵𝑛𝑀𝑘,𝑛⟶lim⟵𝑛(∏𝑘∈𝐾𝑀𝑘,𝑛),Φ((𝑥𝑘)𝑘∈𝐾)≔((𝑥𝑘,𝑛)𝑘∈𝐾)𝑛≥0(𝑥𝑘=(𝑥𝑘,𝑛)𝑛≥0)

is a well-defined isomorphism of 𝒞︀.

Write 𝐹𝑘:ℕop→𝒞︀ for the inverse system of the tower (𝑀𝑘,𝑛)𝑛, so (lim⟵𝑛𝑀𝑘,𝑛,{𝜆𝑘,𝑛}𝑛) realises lim⟵𝐹𝑘 (3.3.154), and likewise (lim⟵𝑛(∏𝑘𝑀𝑘,𝑛),{Λ𝑛}𝑛) realises lim⟵(∏𝑘𝐹𝑘), where Λ𝑛 is projection onto the 𝑛-th coordinate. Since ∏𝑘∈𝐾lim⟵𝑛𝑀𝑘,𝑛 (with the evident coordinate projections 𝑝𝑘) is the categorical product of the lim⟵𝑛𝑀𝑘,𝑛 in 𝒞︀, (2.6.133) gives that lim⟵𝑛(∏𝑘𝑀𝑘,𝑛) exists (as it already does, here) and that there is a unique morphism Φ′ (of 𝒞︀, not assumed a priori to be an isomorphism) such that

𝜋𝑘,𝑛∘Λ𝑛∘Φ′=𝜆𝑘,𝑛∘𝑝𝑘for all𝑘∈𝐾,𝑛≥0,

where 𝜋𝑘,𝑛:∏𝑘′𝑀𝑘′,𝑛→𝑀𝑘,𝑛 is the product projection; and that this Φ′ is an isomorphism.

The explicit Φ above satisfies exactly this defining property: for (𝑥𝑘)𝑘∈∏𝑘lim⟵𝑛𝑀𝑘,𝑛 (so 𝑥𝑘=(𝑥𝑘,𝑛)𝑛), both 𝜋𝑘,𝑛(Λ𝑛(Φ((𝑥𝑘)𝑘))) and 𝜆𝑘,𝑛(𝑝𝑘((𝑥𝑘)𝑘)) equal 𝑥𝑘,𝑛. By uniqueness of the morphism with this property, Φ=Φ′; as Φ′ is an isomorphism, so is Φ. ∎

Corollary 3.3.157 (Cofinal Reindexing of a Tower)

Let 𝒞︀ be as in (3.3.153), (𝑀𝑛,𝜌𝑛)𝑛≥0 a tower in 𝒞︀, and 𝑛0<𝑛1<𝑛2<⋯ a strictly increasing sequence in ℕ. Then, writing 𝜌̃𝑁≔𝜌𝑛𝑁+1,𝑛𝑁:𝑀𝑛𝑁+1→𝑀𝑛𝑁 for the composite restriction map of (3.3.154), (𝑀𝑛𝑁,𝜌̃𝑁)𝑁≥0 is again a tower in 𝒞︀, and

(𝑚𝑛)𝑛≥0↦(𝑚𝑛𝑁)𝑁≥0

defines an isomorphism lim⟵𝑛𝑀𝑛→∼lim⟵𝑁𝑀𝑛𝑁 of 𝒞︀.

Proof. Drafted by Claude Sonnet 5, reviewed by David Rufino.

Regard 𝐼≔ℕ as a directed preorder and, per (3.3.154), the tower (𝑀𝑛)𝑛 as an inverse system 𝐹:𝐼op→𝒞︀ realising lim⟵𝑛𝑀𝑛. Let 𝐼′≔{𝑛𝑁:𝑁≥0}⊂𝐼, a full subcategory: it is directed (a subset of the totally ordered set 𝐼), and cofinal, as 𝑛𝑁→∞ so every 𝑛∈𝐼 satisfies 𝑛≤𝑛𝑁 for some 𝑁 (2.6.126). The order-preserving bijection 𝜎:ℕ→𝐼′, 𝜎(𝑁)≔𝑛𝑁, is an isomorphism of the directed preorders ℕ and 𝐼′, and 𝐹𝜎:ℕop→𝒞︀ is exactly the tower (𝑀𝑛𝑁,𝜌̃𝑁)𝑁 (its restriction map for 𝑁<𝑁+1 is 𝐹(𝜎(𝑁)→𝜎(𝑁+1))=𝜌𝑛𝑁+1,𝑛𝑁=𝜌̃𝑁, and composites thereof match for 𝑁<𝑁′); so, by (2.6.98) (applied to 𝜎) together with (3.3.154) applied to the tower (𝑀𝑛𝑁,𝜌̃𝑁)𝑁, (lim⟵𝑛𝑀𝑛,{𝜆𝑛𝑁}𝑁≥0) realises lim⟵𝑖∈𝐼′𝐹(𝑖), and (lim⟵𝑁𝑀𝑛𝑁,{𝜆𝑁′}𝑁≥0) (the latter’s own projections) also realises it; identifying these two cones over 𝐹|𝐼′op via the (unique, by terminality) comparison isomorphism between them, which sends (𝑚𝑛)𝑛≥0∈lim⟵𝑛𝑀𝑛 (viewed as a cone-point of the first) to the compatible sequence (𝑚𝑛𝑁)𝑁≥0∈lim⟵𝑁𝑀𝑛𝑁 agreeing with it on projections 𝜆𝑛𝑁=𝜆𝑁′∘(this isomorphism).

By (2.6.131) (as 𝐼′ is cofinal in 𝐼), the canonical morphism of cones lim⟵𝑖∈𝐼𝐹(𝑖)→lim⟵𝑖∈𝐼′𝐹(𝑖) is an isomorphism, and by construction (restriction of the limit cone’s own projections 𝜆𝑛 to 𝑖=𝑛𝑁∈𝐼′) it sends (𝑚𝑛)𝑛≥0→(𝑚𝑛𝑁)𝑁≥0 under the identification of the previous paragraph. Composing the two isomorphisms of this and the previous paragraph gives the stated isomorphism lim⟵𝑛𝑀𝑛→∼lim⟵𝑁𝑀𝑛𝑁, (𝑚𝑛)𝑛→(𝑚𝑛𝑁)𝑁. ∎
