6.2.17 Affine and Finite Morphisms

Definition 6.2.96

Let 𝜙:𝑋→𝑌 be a regular morphism of varieties. We say 𝜙 is affine if for every affine open 𝑉⊂𝑌, 𝜙−1(𝑉) is affine.

We say in addition that 𝜙 is finite if the ring map 𝒪︀𝑌(𝑉)→𝒪︀𝑋(𝜙−1(𝑉)) is finite (and therefore integral …).

Other than the definition we do not require this section in the sequel. But it may be convenient for other applications to show that it’s sufficient to demonstrate a morphism is affine (resp. finite) only over a single affine cover.

Lemma 6.2.97

Let 𝑋 be a variety with 𝑈,𝑉 open affine subsets. Then there exists 𝑔1,…,𝑔𝑟∈𝒪︀𝑋(𝑈) and 𝑔1′,…,𝑔𝑟′∈𝒪︀𝑋(𝑉) such that

  1. 𝑊𝑖≔𝐷(𝑔𝑖)=𝐷(𝑔𝑖′) for 𝑖=1…𝑟
  2. 𝑈∩𝑉=⋃𝑖=1𝑟𝑊𝑖
Proof. Given 𝑥∈𝑈∩𝑉, then as 𝑈∩𝑉 is open in 𝑈 by (6.2.10) we may find 𝑓𝑥∈𝒪︀𝑋(𝑈) such that 𝑥∈𝐷(𝑓𝑥)⊂𝑈∩𝑉. Similarly we may find 𝑓𝑥′∈𝒪︀𝑋(𝑉) with 𝑥∈𝐷(𝑓𝑥′)⊂𝐷(𝑓𝑥). By (6.2.21).c) there is a canonical isomorphism 𝒪︀𝑋(𝑈)𝑓𝑥→∼𝒪︀𝑋(𝐷(𝑓𝑥)). Therefore 𝑓𝑥′|𝐷(𝑓𝑥)=𝑔(𝑓𝑥)𝑟 for some 𝑔∈𝒪︀𝑋(𝑈). Observe that 𝐷(𝑓𝑥′)=𝐷(𝑔)∩𝐷(𝑓𝑥)=𝐷(𝑔𝑓𝑥). This gives the required pair of sections for an arbitrary 𝑥∈𝑈∩𝑉. Clearly these open sets cover 𝑈∩𝑉, and as 𝑈∩𝑉 is quasi-compact (6.2.7) we are done. ∎
Proposition 6.2.98

Let 𝜙:𝑋→𝑌 be a regular morphism of varieties, and suppose there is an open cover 𝑌=⋃𝑖=1𝑛𝑉𝑖 for which 𝑉𝑖 and 𝜙−1(𝑉𝑖) are affine. Then 𝜙 is affine.

If in addition 𝒪︀𝑌(𝑉𝑖)→𝒪︀𝑋(𝜙−1(𝑉𝑖)) is finite for all 𝑖=1…𝑛 then 𝜙 is finite.

Proof. By assumption we have affine open sets 𝑈𝑖≔𝜙−1(𝑉𝑖) for 𝑖=1…𝑛. Let 𝑉⊂𝑌 be an open affine set, then by (6.2.97) for each 𝑖 we have an open cover

𝑉∩𝑉𝑖=⋃𝑗=1𝑛𝑖𝑊𝑖𝑗

with 𝑊𝑖𝑗≔𝐷(𝑓𝑖𝑗)=𝐷(𝑓𝑖𝑗′) for 𝑓𝑖𝑗′∈𝒪︀𝑌(𝑉𝑖) and 𝑓𝑖𝑗∈𝒪︀𝑌(𝑉). Evidently

𝑉=⋃𝑖=1𝑛⋃𝑗=1𝑛𝑖𝐷(𝑓𝑖𝑗)(*)

and so by (6.2.26) the 𝑓𝑖𝑗 generate 𝒪︀𝑌(𝑉), and consequentally 𝑔𝑖𝑗≔𝜙♯(𝑓𝑖𝑗) generate 𝒪︀𝑋(𝑈) (as both ideals contain 1). Further 𝜙−1(𝑊𝑖𝑗)=𝜙−1(𝐷(𝑓𝑖𝑗′))=𝐷(𝜙♯(𝑓𝑖𝑗′))⊂𝑈𝑖 is affine (6.2.21).c). Therefore from (6.2.28) 𝑈 is affine, because we also have the representation 𝜙−1(𝑊𝑖𝑗)=𝜙−1(𝐷(𝑓𝑖𝑗))=𝐷(𝑔𝑖𝑗).

Suppose 𝜙 is finite then by definition this means the ring maps 𝒪︀𝑌(𝑉𝑖)→𝒪︀𝑋(𝑈𝑖) are finite. By (3.6.48).a) this means the localised maps are finite

Using equation (∗) and (6.2.26) we see that 𝑓𝑖𝑗 generate the unit ideal of 𝒪︀𝑌(𝑉). Therefore by (3.6.48).b) 𝜙𝑉♯ is finite. ∎
