4.1.12 Krull Dimension

References:

  • Éléments de géométrie algébrique IV [Gro64] (Chap. 0 §14.4.1)
  • Some remarks on biequidimensionality of topological spaces and Noetherian schemes [Hei17]

For a Noetherian topological space 𝑋 the closed subsets form an Artinian, distributive lattice. Furthermore the irreducible subsets are precisely the join-prime elements by (4.1.61). Therefore we may use the notions of Krull Lattice developed in Foundations.tex’s Krull Dimension section ((2.5.2)).

Definition 4.1.80 (Chain of irreducibles)

Let 𝑋 be a Noetherian topological space. A chain of irreducible closed subsets

𝑍0⊊𝑍1⊊…⊊𝑍𝑛

is said to have length 𝑛. A chain is saturated if there is no proper refinement, that is if 𝑌 is irreducible then

𝑍𝑖⊆𝑌⊆𝑍𝑖+1⟹𝑌=𝑍𝑖or𝑌=𝑍𝑖+1.

If in addition 𝑍𝑛 (resp. 𝑍0) is maximal (resp. minimal) then the chain is maximal.

Definition 4.1.81 (Krull Lattice of Closed Subsets)

Let 𝑋 be a topological space.

  • The Krull dimension dim(𝑋) of 𝑋 is the maximal length of all chains of irreducible closed subsets. Note this may be ∞.
  • The height or codimension of an irreducible closed subset 𝑌⊆𝑋, denoted codim(𝑌,𝑋), is the maximal length of chains of irreducible subsets containing 𝑌.

  • When 𝑌 is not irreducible we write

    codim(𝑌,𝑋)=inf𝛼codim(𝑌𝛼,𝑋)

    where 𝑌𝛼 varies among the irreducible components of 𝑌.

If dim(𝑋)<∞ then we say 𝑋 is finite-dimensional. In this case it’s clear the closed subsets of a topological space form a Krull Lattice where the irreducible closed subsets are precisely the join-prime elements of the lattice.

Note any saturated chain for 𝑌⊂𝑋 must start at 𝑌 and terminate at an irreducible component of 𝑋. In particular if 𝑋 is irreducible then a saturated chain must terminate at 𝑋.

Proposition 4.1.82 (Extending Chains)

Let 𝑋 be a finite-dimensional topological space. Then

  1. Every chain is contained in a saturated chain with the same endpoints
  2. Every chain is contained in a maximal chain
Proposition 4.1.83 (Simple properties of (co-)dimension)

Let 𝑋 be a topological space. Then the following properties hold

  1. dim(𝑋)=sup𝛼dim(𝑋𝛼) where 𝑋𝛼 are the irreducible components of 𝑋
  2. codim(𝑌,𝑋)=sup𝛼codim(𝑌,𝑋𝛼) where 𝑋𝛼 are the irreducible components of 𝑋 containing 𝑌
  3. dim(𝑌)+codim(𝑌,𝑋)≤dim(𝑋) for every closed subset 𝑌⊂𝑋
  4. codim(𝑌,𝑍)+codim(𝑍,𝑇)≤codim(𝑌,𝑇) for 𝑌⊂𝑍⊂𝑇 irreducible closed subsets
  5. 𝑌⊊𝑍 is a saturated chain of irreducible closed subsets if and only if codim(𝑌,𝑍)=1.
  6. Let 𝑌 be a closed subset of 𝑋. Then codim(𝑌,𝑋)=0⟺ every irreducible component of 𝑌 is an irreducible component of 𝑋. In particular if 𝑋 is irreducible then codim(𝑌,𝑋)=0⟺𝑌=𝑋⟺dim(𝑋)=dim(𝑌).

In particular if 𝑋 is finite-dimensional then all codimensions are also finite.

Proposition 4.1.84
Let 𝑋 be an irreducible topological space and 𝑌⊂𝑋 a closed subset. Then dim(𝑌)≤dim(𝑋) with equality iff 𝑌=𝑋.
Proof. The inequality follows from (4.1.83).b). If dim(𝑌)=dim(𝑋) then by c) codim(𝑌,𝑋)=0 and so by f) we have 𝑌=𝑋. ∎
Definition 4.1.85 (Properties)

Let 𝑋 be a topological space of finite dimension. Then we say 𝑋 is

  • Equidimensional if all irreducible components of 𝑋 have the same dimension
  • Equicodimensional if codim(𝑌,𝑋) is constant as 𝑌 varies over minimal irreducible subsets of 𝑋
  • Biequidimensional if all maximal chains of irreducible subsets have the same length.
  • Quasi-Biequidimensional if every irreducible component is biequidimensional
  • Catenary if any two saturated chains with the same endpoints, say 𝑌 and 𝑍, have the same length, namely codim(𝑌,𝑍)

Observe that quasi-biequidimensional + equidimensional ⟺ biequidimensional and irreducible ⟹ equidimensional

Proposition 4.1.86 (Equivalent characterisations of biequidimensional)

Suppose 𝑋 is a topological space of finite dimension. Then the following are equivalent

  1. 𝑋 is quasi-biequidimensional
  2. 𝑋 is catenary and every irreducible component is equicodimensional

  3. 𝑋 satisfies the codimension formula for 𝑍⊂𝑌 irreducible

    dim(𝑌)=dim(𝑍)+codim(𝑍,𝑌)
  4. 𝑋 satisfies c) in the case codim(𝑍,𝑌)=1

Furthermore for irreducible subsets 𝑍⊂𝑌

codim(𝑍)=codim(𝑍,𝑌)+codim(𝑌)
Proof. This is a translation of (2.5.11) to the topological case. ∎
Proposition 4.1.87 (Codimension Formula)

Suppose 𝑋 is a quasi-biequidimensional topological space. Then for every subset 𝑍⊂𝑋 which is irreducible and closed, and every closed subset 𝑌⊂𝑍 the following relations hold.

dim(𝑍)=dim(𝑌)+codim(𝑌,𝑍)codim(𝑌)=codim(𝑌,𝑍)+codim(𝑍)

Further every closed subset is also quasi-biequidimensional.

Proof. This follows from (2.5.13) and (2.5.15). ∎
Proposition 4.1.88
Suppose 𝑋=⋃𝑖∈𝐼𝑈𝑖. Then dim(𝑋)=sup𝑖∈𝐼dim(𝑈𝑖).

Proof. By (4.1.73) a chain of irreducible closed subsets of 𝑈𝑖 lifts to a chain of the same length in 𝑋. Therefore dim(𝑈𝑖)≤dim(𝑋) for all 𝑖∈𝐼 whence sup𝑖∈𝐼dim(𝑈𝑖)≤dim(𝑋). Similarly given a chain of irreducible closed subsets

𝑋0⊊…⊊𝑋𝑛⊆𝑋

choose 𝑖 such that 𝑋0∩𝑈𝑖≠∅. Then (4.1.79) shows that this restricts to a chain of the same length in 𝑈𝑖. Therefore dim(𝑋)≤dim(𝑈𝑖)≤sup𝑖∈𝐼dim(𝑈𝑖). ∎

Proposition 4.1.89
Let 𝑋 be a catenary topological space and 𝑈⊂𝑋 an open subset. Then 𝑈 is catenary.

Proof. Consider 𝑍⊂𝑌⊂𝑈 irreducible closed subsets. Then for any saturated chain

𝑍=𝑍0⊊…⊊𝑍𝑛=𝑌

we have by (4.1.73)

𝑍―=𝑍0―⊊…⊊𝑍𝑛―=𝑌―

a saturated chain in 𝑋. As 𝑋 is catenary we find 𝑛=codim(𝑍―,𝑌―). As the chain was arbitrary we find 𝑈 is catenary. ∎

Lemma 4.1.90

Suppose that 𝑋 is a topological space and 𝑥∈𝑋 is quasi-closed. Then dim({𝑥}―)=0.

Conversely suppose 𝑍 is an irreducible closed set of dimension 0. Then 𝑍={𝑧}― for all 𝑧∈𝑍 and 𝑍 consists of quasi-closed elements.

Proof. By (4.1.67) {𝑥}― is irreducible. Suppose ∅≠𝑍⊂{𝑥}― is closed then 𝑦∈𝑍⟹𝑥∈{𝑦}―⊂𝑍 whence {𝑥}―=𝑍. This shows that dim({𝑥}―)=0.

Conversely if dim(𝑍)=0 then {𝑧}―⊂𝑍 is an irreducible subset for all 𝑧∈𝑍, whence they are equal. This also shows that 𝑧 is quasi-closed. ∎

Proposition 4.1.92 (Curves have co-finite topology)
Let 𝑋 be an irreducible, Noetherian and symmetric topological space such that dim(𝑋)=1. Then 𝑋0 has the cofinite topology.

Proof. We may reduce to the case 𝑋=𝑋0 is Kolmogorov, then by (4.1.45) every point 𝑥∈𝑋 is closed.

Suppose 𝑍⊂𝑋 is finite then 𝑍=⋃𝑧∈𝑍{𝑧} is a finite union of closed subsets and therefore closed. Conversely if 𝑍⊂𝑋 is closed and proper then by (4.1.83).f) dim(𝑍)=0 and therefore 𝑍 is finite by (4.1.91). ∎

Lemma 4.1.93 (Cofinite Topology is Quasi-Compact)
Let 𝑋 be a set equipped with the cofinite topology. Then 𝑋 is quasi-compact.
Proof. Let {𝑈𝑖}𝑖∈𝐼 be an open cover of 𝑋 and choose any nonempty 𝑈𝑖0 from it. Then 𝑋∖𝑈𝑖0 is finite by definition of the cofinite topology, say {𝑥1,…,𝑥𝑛}. For each 𝑘 choose 𝑈𝑖𝑘∋𝑥𝑘 from the cover. Then {𝑈𝑖0,𝑈𝑖1,…,𝑈𝑖𝑛} is a finite subcover. ∎
