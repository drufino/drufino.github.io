6.3 Function Fields and the Riemann-Roch Theorem

We develop the theory of algebraic function fields 𝐾/𝑘, which by (6.3.4) are the same thing as complete regular algebraic curves over 𝑘 which are geometrically integral. Our goal is the Riemann-Roch theorem for such curves over a perfect base field 𝑘, the algebraic counterpart of the classical theorem for compact Riemann surfaces. Via the correspondence it suffices to prove it for function fields, where it was proved by Weil and Chevalley [Che51] using adèles (répartitions). That proof needs the least machinery, but in it the genus 𝑔 and the index of speciality 𝑖(𝐷) appear only as extremal or dimension-count invariants, with no direct geometric meaning. As in the classical case, the remaining work is to relate them to rational differentials: for a non-zero rational differential 𝜔 with divisor (𝜔) we show 𝑖(𝐷)=ℓ((𝜔)−𝐷) and 𝑔=ℓ((𝜔)). This rests on the Residue Theorem ∑𝑃Res𝑃(𝜔)=0 (6.3.66), which we need over a general perfect field and not only for 𝑘=𝑘―. The closed points of a curve over 𝑘 have residue fields 𝑘(𝑃) of arbitrary finite degree over 𝑘. At such a place we expand in 𝑘(𝑃)((𝑋)) and set Res𝑃≔Tr𝑘(𝑃)/𝑘∘Res𝑃𝑘(𝑃) (6.3.38) equivalently Res𝑃 is the trace of the residue at a conjugate rational place over 𝑘― (6.3.65). A rational differential 𝜔 defines a Weil differential by 𝛼↦∑𝑃Res𝑃(𝛼𝑃𝜔), whose vanishing on 𝐾 is exactly the Residue Theorem. We deduce it from the algebraically closed case (6.3.47) by base change to 𝑘―: the places of 𝐾 correspond to Galois orbits of places of 𝐾𝑘―, and the residue at 𝑃 is the sum of the residues over its orbit.

We first fix the basic notions for function fields (an algebraic function field, its field of constants and its places) and then state the correspondence with curves precisely.

Proposition 6.3.1

Let 𝐾/𝑘 be a field extension. Then the following are equivalent

  1. 𝐾/𝑘 is finitely generated with transcendence degree 1.
  2. There exists a transcendental element 𝑥∈𝐾/𝑘 such that 𝐾/𝑘(𝑥) is finite.

In this case we say that 𝐾/𝑘 is an algebraic function field. We typically assume that 𝑘 is perfect (either a number field or a finite field).

Proof. 𝑎)⟹𝑏) By (3.18.139) a transcendence base consists of a single element 𝑥. By assumption 𝐾/𝑘(𝑥) is algebraic and finitely generated, hence also finite (3.18.44).

𝑏)⟹𝑎) Evidently {𝑥} is a transcendence base. By assumption 𝐾/𝑘(𝑥) is finite and therefore certainly finitely generated, whence so is 𝐾/𝑘. ∎

Definition 6.3.2 (Algebraic Function Field)

Let 𝐾/𝑘 be an algebraic function field. Denote by 𝑘̃ the field of constants, the relative algebraic closure of 𝑘 in 𝐾:

𝑘̃≔{𝑥∈𝐾|𝑥algebraic over𝑘}

(a subfield of 𝐾 by (3.18.45)). By (3.18.46), 𝐾/𝑘̃ is relatively algebraically closed. We say that 𝐾/𝑘 is geometrically integral if 𝑘=𝑘̃ (c.f. (3.35.41)).

Definition 6.3.3 (Place of an Algebraic Function Field)

Let 𝐾/𝑘 be an algebraic function field. By a place of 𝐾/𝑘 we refer to a valuation ring 𝒪︀𝑃 such that 𝑘⊊𝒪︀𝑃⊊𝐾. By (3.24.2) this is a local ring with unique maximal ideal 𝔪𝑃.

From (3.3.13) we verify immediately that 𝑘∩𝔪𝑃=(0).

For every 𝑥∈𝐾 we say that 𝑥 is regular at a place 𝑃 if 𝑥∈𝒪︀𝑃.

Theorem 6.3.4 (Curves and Function Fields)

Let 𝑘 be a field. The assignment 𝑋↦𝑘(𝑋) is an anti-equivalence between

  • complete, regular, geometrically integral curves 𝑋 over 𝑘, with non-constant morphisms, and
  • algebraic function fields 𝐾/𝑘 in which 𝑘 is relatively separably closed, with 𝑘-embeddings.

Here 𝑋 is geometrically integral (6.2.7) if and only if 𝑘 is relatively separably closed in 𝑘(𝑋) (6.2.8). When 𝑘 is perfect this says 𝑘̃=𝑘.

Writing 𝑋0 for the Kolmogorov quotient of the points of 𝑋, under this correspondence

  1. the points 𝑥0∈𝑋0 are the places 𝑃 of 𝑘(𝑋)/𝑘, with 𝒪︀𝑋,𝑥=𝒪︀𝑃 and 𝑘(𝑥)=𝑘(𝔪𝑃);
  2. a non-constant morphism 𝜙:𝑋→𝑌 corresponds to the embedding 𝜙⋆:𝑘(𝑌)↪︎𝑘(𝑋), with deg(𝜙)=[𝑘(𝑋):𝜙⋆(𝑘(𝑌))], and 𝜙0(𝑥0)=𝑦0 if and only if 𝜙⋆(𝒪︀𝑌,𝑦)⊂𝒪︀𝑋,𝑥;
  3. a quasi-inverse sends 𝐾/𝑘 to a curve 𝑋𝐾 whose points are the places of 𝐾/𝑘.

Proof. Drafted by Claude Sonnet, reviewed by David Rufino.

A complete regular curve 𝑋 as above has 𝑘(𝑋)/𝑘 finitely generated of transcendence degree dim𝑋=1 (6.1.52), so 𝑘(𝑋) is an algebraic function field in which 𝑘 is relatively separably closed (6.3.1). The bijection between morphisms and embeddings, together with the degree, is (6.2.119). The points of 𝑋0 are the valuation rings 𝑘⊊𝑅⊊𝑘(𝑋), which are exactly the places of 𝑘(𝑋)/𝑘, and 𝑅=𝒪︀𝑋,𝑥 for the corresponding point 𝑥 (6.2.116) in particular points of 𝑋 with the same local ring are identified in 𝑋0 (6.2.22).

For the last statement in b), the morphism induces a local homomorphism 𝒪︀𝑌,𝜙(𝑥)→𝒪︀𝑋,𝑥, so 𝜙⋆(𝒪︀𝑌,𝜙(𝑥))⊂𝒪︀𝑋,𝑥; if [𝑦]=[𝜙(𝑥)] then 𝒪︀𝑌,𝑦=𝒪︀𝑌,𝜙(𝑥) (6.2.22) and the inclusion follows. Conversely, if 𝜙⋆(𝒪︀𝑌,𝑦)⊂𝒪︀𝑋,𝑥 then 𝑅≔𝒪︀𝑋,𝑥∩𝑘(𝑌) is a valuation ring of 𝑘(𝑌) containing the discrete valuation rings 𝒪︀𝑌,𝑦 and 𝒪︀𝑌,𝜙(𝑥). It is proper, as 𝜙 is non-constant: a uniformiser of 𝒪︀𝑌,𝜙(𝑥) lies in 𝔪𝑋,𝑥 and so is not a unit of 𝑅. By (3.30.5) 𝑅=𝒪︀𝑌,𝑦=𝒪︀𝑌,𝜙(𝑥), so [𝑦]=[𝜙(𝑥)] (6.2.22).

It remains to show that every algebraic function field 𝐾/𝑘 in which 𝑘 is relatively separably closed is 𝑘(𝑋) for such a curve 𝑋≕𝑋𝐾. The construction is sketched in the following remark; the details are to be added. ∎

Remark 6.3.5 (Construction of the curve)
Let 𝐾/𝑘 be an algebraic function field in which 𝑘 is relatively separably closed, and choose 𝑓∈𝐾∖𝑘. Then 𝐵≔𝑘[𝑓]―𝐾 is a Dedekind domain and a finite 𝑘[𝑓]-module whose non-zero primes correspond to the places containing 𝑓 (6.3.8), so it is the coordinate ring of a regular affine curve 𝑈𝑓 with function field 𝐾; likewise 𝐵′≔𝑘[𝑓−1]―𝐾 gives 𝑈𝑓−1. As normalisation commutes with localisation, 𝐵[𝑓−1]=𝐵′[𝑓] is the integral closure of 𝑘[𝑓,𝑓−1], and 𝑈𝑓, 𝑈𝑓−1 glue along the open subsets with this coordinate ring. The result 𝑋𝐾 is a regular curve; it is separated as 𝐵⊗𝐵′→𝐵[𝑓−1] is surjective, and complete as every place of 𝐾/𝑘 contains 𝑓 or 𝑓−1 and hence is the local ring of a point of 𝑈𝑓 or 𝑈𝑓−1. Its points are the places of 𝐾/𝑘, and it is geometrically integral as 𝑘 is relatively separably closed in 𝐾 (6.2.8).
