6.3 Function Fields and the Riemann-Roch Theorem

We develop the theory of algebraic function fields 𝐾/𝑘, which by (6.2.128) are the same thing as complete regular algebraic curves over 𝑘. Our goal is the Riemann-Roch theorem for such curves over a perfect base field 𝑘, the algebraic counterpart of the classical theorem for compact Riemann surfaces. Via the correspondence it suffices to prove it for function fields, where it was proved by Weil and Chevalley [Che51] using adèles (répartitions). That proof needs the least machinery, but in it the genus 𝑔 and the index of speciality 𝑖(𝐷) appear only as extremal or dimension-count invariants, with no direct geometric meaning. As in the classical case, the remaining work is to relate them to rational differentials: for a non-zero rational differential 𝜔 with divisor (𝜔) we show 𝑖(𝐷)=ℓ((𝜔)−𝐷) and 𝑔=ℓ((𝜔)). This rests on the Residue Theorem ∑𝑃Res𝑃(𝜔)=0 (6.3.53), which we need over a general perfect field and not only for 𝑘=𝑘―. The closed points of a curve over 𝑘 have residue fields 𝑘(𝑃) of arbitrary finite degree over 𝑘. At such a place we expand in 𝑘(𝑃)((𝑋)) and set Res𝑃≔Tr𝑘(𝑃)/𝑘∘Res𝑃𝑘(𝑃) (6.3.25) equivalently Res𝑃 is the trace of the residue at a conjugate rational place over 𝑘― (6.3.52). A rational differential 𝜔 defines a Weil differential by 𝛼↦∑𝑃Res𝑃(𝛼𝑃𝜔), whose vanishing on 𝐾 is exactly the Residue Theorem. We deduce it from the algebraically closed case (6.3.34) by base change to 𝑘―: the places of 𝐾 correspond to Galois orbits of places of 𝐾𝑘―, and the residue at 𝑃 is the sum of the residues over its orbit.
