3.21 Modules over Local Rings (Nakayama’s Lemma)

The main result of this section (3.21.7) is that every finitely-generated module over a local ring has a minimal spanning set. Recall in the vector space case a minimal spanning set is precisely a basis (2.3.8). Analogously we may also show that (in the local case) every minimal spanning set has the same order. The crucial result is Nakayama’s Lemma, which we develop here.

Definition 3.21.1 (Jacobson Radical)

Let 𝐴 be a commutative ring. Define the Jacobson Radical to be the intersection of all maximal ideals

0𝐽≔⋂𝔪◃𝐴𝔪
Proposition 3.21.2
The Jacobson Radical 0𝐽 is a proper ideal
Example 3.21.3
When (𝐴,𝔪𝐴) is a local ring then 0𝐽=𝔪𝐴.
Lemma 3.21.4 (Characterization of Jacobson Radical)

For an ideal 𝔞 and 𝔪 a maximal ideal

  1. 𝔞⊈𝔪⟺𝔞+𝔪=𝐴⟺(1+𝔞)∩𝔪≠∅
  2. 𝔞⊆0𝐽⟺1+𝔞⊆𝐴⋆
  3. 𝑥∈0𝐽⟺1+(𝑥)⊆𝐴⋆

Proof. We prove each in turn.

a) Clearly 𝔞⊆𝔪⟹𝔞+𝔪=𝔪. Conversely 𝔞⊈𝔪⟹𝔞+𝔪=𝐴 by maximality. Suppose 𝔞+𝔪=𝐴 then 1=𝑎+𝑚 whence (1−𝑎)∈𝔪. The converse is similar.

b) By a) 𝔞⊆0𝐽⟹(1+𝔞)∩𝔪=∅ for all maximal ideals 𝔪. By (3.3.15) this implies (1+𝔞)⊆𝐴⋆.

Conversely if (1+𝔞)⊆𝐴⋆ then (1+𝔞)∩𝔪=∅ for any maximal ideal 𝔪 by (3.3.13). Again by a) 𝔞⊆𝔪 as required.

c) This follows from b) and noting 𝑥∈0𝐽⟺(𝑥)⊆0𝐽. ∎

Proposition 3.21.5 (Nakayama's Lemma)

Let 𝑀 be a finitely generated 𝐴-module and 𝔞◃𝐴 an ideal. Then the following holds

  1. If 𝑀=𝔞𝑀 then there exists 𝑎∈𝔞 such that 𝑚=𝑎𝑚 for all 𝑚∈𝑀

Suppose in addition that 𝔞⊆0𝐽 (e.g. if 𝐴 is local and 𝔞 is proper) then

  1. 𝑀=𝔞𝑀⟹𝑀=0
  2. 𝑁≤𝑀 and 𝑀=𝑁+𝔞𝑀⟹𝑀=𝑁.

Proof. We prove each in turn

a) Apply (3.18.4) with 𝜙≔𝟏𝑀 to find a monic polynomial 𝑃(𝑋)∈𝐴[𝑋] with non-leading coefficients in 𝔞 such that 𝑃(𝜙)(𝑚)=0 for all 𝑚∈𝑀. Then we see that (1+𝑎𝑛−1+…𝑎0)𝑚=0 for all 𝑚∈𝑀 whence 𝑎≔−(𝑎𝑛−1+…+𝑎0) is the required element.

More directly, suppose 𝑚1,…,𝑚𝑛 is a generating set for 𝑀. By (3.18.3) (and 𝑀=𝔞𝑀) there is a matrix 𝐸 with coefficients in 𝔞 such that

(𝐼𝑛−𝐸)𝒎=0

where 𝒎 is the column vector consisting of 𝑚1,…,𝑚𝑛. By (3.5.23) we see det(𝐼𝑛−𝐸)𝑚𝑖=0 for all 𝑖=1…𝑛. It’s enough to show 𝑎≔det(𝐼𝑛−𝐸)∈1+𝔞. Observe

det(𝐼𝑛−𝐸)=∏𝑖(1−𝐸𝑖𝑖)+∑𝜎≠id𝜖(𝜎)∏𝑗𝐸𝑗𝜎(𝑗)

The second term lies in 𝔞 and

∏𝑖(1−𝐸𝑖𝑖)=1−∑𝑖=1𝑛𝐸𝑖𝑖∏𝑗>𝑖(1−𝐸𝑗𝑗)∈1+𝔞

b) Consider any 𝑚∈𝑀. By a) we have (1−𝑎)𝑚=0 for some 𝑎∈𝔞, and by (3.21.4) (1−𝑎) is invertible, whence 𝑚=0 as required.

c) Observe 𝔞(𝑀/𝑁)=(3.3.106)(𝑁+𝔞𝑀)/𝑁=𝑀/𝑁 whence 𝑀/𝑁=0 by b). Therefore 𝑁=𝑀 as required.

We may show b) more directly. Suppose 𝑀≠0, and let

{𝑚1,…,𝑚𝑛}

be a non-zero generating set for 𝑀 of minimal size. Then by (3.18.3)

𝑚1=∑𝑗𝑎𝑗𝑚𝑗𝑎𝑗∈𝔞

whence

(1−𝑎1)𝑚1=∑𝑗≥2𝑎𝑗𝑚𝑗

As 𝑎1∈0𝐽 we have 1−𝑎1∈𝐴⋆ by (3.21.4). Then

{𝑚2,…,𝑚𝑛}

is a smaller generating set, a contradiction. Therefore 𝑀=0.

a) may be deduced from b) as follows. Observe 𝑆≔1+𝔞 is a multiplicatively closed subset, so we may consider 𝑆−1𝑀 as an 𝑆−1𝐴-module. It’s easy to verify that 1+𝑆−1𝔞⊆(𝑆−1𝐴)⋆ so by (3.21.4) 𝑆−1𝔞⊆𝐽(𝑆−1𝐴). Clearly 𝔞𝑀=𝑀⟹(𝑆−1𝔞)𝑆−1𝑀=𝑆−1𝑀 so by the weaker form 𝑆−1𝑀=0. By (3.6.16) there exists 𝑠∈𝑆 such that 𝑠𝑀=0, which is the required result as 𝑠=1+𝑎 for some 𝑎∈𝔞. ∎

Recall (3.3.107) in the case of a local ring (𝐴,𝔪) that 𝑀̃≔𝑀/𝔪𝑀 is a vector space over 𝑘≔𝐴/𝔪. We may use Nakayama’s Lemma to exhibit a correspondence between minimal spanning sets of 𝑀 and bases of 𝑀/𝔪𝑀 as a 𝑘-vector space. First we prove a simpler form

Lemma 3.21.6

Let (𝐴,𝔪) be a local ring with residue field 𝑘=𝐴/𝔪, 𝑀 a finite 𝐴-module and 𝑆⊂𝑀 a subset. Then

𝑆spans𝑀⟺𝑆̃spans𝑀̃

where ̃ denotes reduction modulo 𝔪𝑀.

Proof. One direction is obvious. Conversely suppose 𝑆̃ spans 𝑀̃. Define 𝑁≔⟨𝑆⟩, then this means precisely that 𝑁+𝔪𝑀=𝑀, so 𝑁=𝑀 by (3.21.5) c) as required. ∎

Recall from (2.1.57) that every subset of 𝑇 of 𝑀/𝔪𝑀 may be written in the form 𝑆̃ for 𝑆⊂𝑀 and ̃ injective on 𝑆.

Proposition 3.21.7 (Structure theorem for modules over a local ring)

Let (𝐴,𝔪) be a local ring with residue field 𝑘=𝐴/𝔪, 𝑀 a finite 𝐴-module. Then

  1. 𝑀̃≔𝑀/𝔪𝑀 is a finite-dimensional 𝑘-module (of dimension 𝑛 say)
  2. If 𝑆̃ is a 𝑘-basis for 𝑀̃ and ̃|𝑆 is injective, then 𝑆 is a minimal spanning set for 𝑀 and #︎𝑆=#︎𝑆̃=𝑛
  3. If 𝑆 is a minimal spanning set then 𝑆̃ is a basis for 𝑀̃ (and ̃|𝑆 is injective so #︎𝑆=#︎𝑆̃=𝑛).

Proof. a) By (3.3.107) 𝑀̃ is a 𝑘-module, and it’s clearly finite.

b) By (3.21.6) 𝑆 spans 𝑀. Suppose 𝑆′⊂𝑆 spans 𝑀, then by the same result 𝑆′̃ spans 𝑀̃. Recall (2.3.8) that a vector space basis is precisely a minimal spanning set, so 𝑆′̃=𝑆̃. As ̃ is injective this means 𝑆′=𝑆. Therefore 𝑆 is a minimal spanning set.

c) Let 𝑆 be a minimal spanning set. Then by (3.21.6) 𝑆̃ spans 𝑀̃. Suppose 𝑇̃⊂𝑆̃ also spans 𝑀̃. By (3.21.6) 𝑇 spans 𝑀, and by hypothesis 𝑇=𝑆. Therefore 𝑇̃=𝑆̃. As 𝑇̃ was arbitrary we see that 𝑆̃ is a minimal spanning set for 𝑀̃, which by (2.3.8) is a basis.

Finally suppose ̃ is not injective on 𝑆, that is 𝑠1̃=𝑠2̃. Then 𝑆′≔𝑆∖{𝑠1} satisfies 𝑆′̃=𝑆̃. Therefore by the Lemma 𝑆′ spans 𝑀, contradicting minimality. ∎
