6.1.4 Dominant Morphisms

Definition 6.1.46

Let 𝜙:𝑋→𝑌 be a regular morphism of affine varieties. Then we say it is

  • finite if 𝜙𝑌♯:𝒪︀𝑌(𝑌)→𝒪︀𝑋(𝑋) is module-finite (equivalently integral)
  • dominant if 𝜙(𝑋(𝑘―)) is dense in 𝑌(𝑘―)
Proposition 6.1.47 (Criteria for dominant morphisms)

A regular morphism 𝜙:𝑋→𝑌 is dominant if and only if 𝜙𝑌♯:𝒪︀𝑌(𝑌)→𝒪︀𝑋(𝑋) is injective. In particular when both 𝑋 and 𝑌 are integral then this induces a field extension

𝜙⋆:𝑘(𝑌)↪︎𝑘(𝑋)

Proof. Recall 𝜙(𝑋) is dense precisely when the closure 𝜙(𝑋)―=𝑌. Then

ker(𝜙𝑌♯)=𝐼𝑘(𝜙(𝑋))⟹𝑉𝑘―(ker(𝜙♯))=𝑉𝑘―(𝐼𝑘(𝜙(𝑋)))=𝜙(𝑋)―⟹ker(𝜙♯)=𝐼𝑘(𝜙(𝑋)―)

by (6.1.2) and (6.1.13). If ker(𝜙𝑌♯)=0 then clearly 𝜙(𝑋)―=𝑌. Conversely if this holds then

ker(𝜙𝑌♯)=𝐼𝑘(𝑌(𝑘―))=𝐼𝑘(𝑉𝑘―(0))=(0)=(0)

as 𝒪︀𝑌(𝑌) is reduced, whence ker(𝜙𝑌♯)=0. ∎
