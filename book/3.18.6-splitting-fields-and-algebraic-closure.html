3.18.6 Splitting Fields and Algebraic Closure

In this section we discuss splitting fields, which are the “smallest” extensions in which a given set of polynomials split completely. The fundamental result is that splitting fields are precisely the Normal extensions. Further we discuss the algebraic closure, in which every polynomial splits and in which every algebraic extension (normal or otherwise) may be embedded.

Definition 3.18.52 (Splitting field)

Let 𝑆⊂𝑘[𝑋] a family of polynomials. We say that 𝐾/𝑘 is a splitting field for 𝑆 if

  • Every polynomial 𝑓∈𝑆 splits completely in 𝐾
  • 𝐾 is generated by the roots of all the polynomials in 𝑆

Note that by (3.18.44) 𝐾/𝑘 is necessarily algebraic, and if 𝑆 is finite then so is 𝐾/𝑘.

Definition 3.18.53 (Set of roots)

Let 𝐾/𝑘 be a field extension and 𝑓∈𝑘[𝑋]. Then define

𝑇𝑓,𝐾≔{𝛽∈𝐾|𝑓(𝛽)=0}
Proposition 3.18.54 (Splitting field is minimal)

Let 𝑆⊂𝑘[𝑋] be a family of polynomials which split completely in 𝐾/𝑘. Then the following are equivalent

  • 𝐾 is generated by the roots of every polynomial 𝑓∈𝑆, that is

    𝐾=𝑘(⋃𝑓∈𝑆𝑇𝑓,𝐾)
  • Any subfield 𝐾′⊂𝐾 in which 𝑆 splits completely is equal to 𝐾

Proof. For all 𝑓∈𝑆 there is a factorisation

𝑓=∏𝛼∈𝑇𝑓𝑖,𝐾(𝑋−𝛼)

Suppose 𝐾=𝑘(⋃𝑓∈𝑆𝑇𝑓,𝐾) and let 𝐾′ be a subfield in which all 𝑓∈𝑆 split completely. Then by unique factorization in 𝐾[𝑋] we have 𝑇𝑓,𝐾⊂𝐾′ for all 𝑓∈𝑆 and therefore 𝐾′=𝐾.

Conversely it’s clear that 𝑆 splits completely in 𝑘(⋃𝑓∈𝑆𝑇𝑓,𝐾), therefore by hypothesis this equals 𝐾. ∎

Lemma 3.18.55

Let 𝜎:𝐾/𝑘→𝐿/𝑘 be a morphism and 𝑓(𝑋)∈𝑘[𝑋] a polynomial. Then

  • 𝜎 induces an injective map on the roots 𝑇𝑓,𝐾→𝑇𝑓,𝐿

  • 𝑓 splits completely in 𝐾 iff 𝑓 splits completely in 𝜎(𝐾). In this case the above map is a bijection

Proposition 3.18.56 (Image of a splitting field is fixed)

Let 𝐾/𝑘 be a splitting field for 𝑆 and 𝜎:𝐾/𝑘→𝐿/𝑘 a morphism. Then 𝑆 splits completely in 𝐿. Any such 𝜎 satisfies

𝜎(𝐾)=𝑘(⋃𝑓∈𝑆𝑇𝑓,𝐿)

Proof. Clearly by (3.18.55) 𝑆 splits completely in 𝐿.

By the same result 𝜎 induces a bijection 𝑇𝑓,𝐾⟷𝑇𝑓,𝐿. Therefore 𝜎(𝐾)=𝜎(𝑘(⋃𝑓∈𝑆𝑇𝑓,𝐾))=𝑘(𝜎(⋃𝑓∈𝑆𝑇𝑓,𝐾))=𝑘(⋃𝑓∈𝑆𝑇𝑓,𝐿) by (3.18.30). ∎

Proposition 3.18.57 (Uniqueness of Splitting Fields)

Let 𝑆⊂𝑘[𝑋] be a family of polynomials. Let 𝐾/𝑘 be a splitting field for 𝑆 and 𝐿/𝑘 an extension in which 𝑆 splits completely.

Then there exists a morphism 𝜎:𝐾/𝑘→𝐿/𝑘. Let 𝛼∈𝐾 and 𝛽∈𝐿 be conjugate elements, then we may choose 𝜎 such that 𝜎(𝛼)=𝛽.

Furthermore any two splitting fields are isomorphic.

Proof. By assumption 𝐾 is generated by the roots 𝛼𝑖𝑗 of 𝑓𝑖∈𝑆. For each 𝛼𝑖𝑗 we therefore have 𝑚𝛼𝑖𝑗,𝑘(𝑋)|𝑓𝑖(𝑋) and 𝑚𝛼𝑖𝑗,𝑘(𝑋) splits completely in 𝐿 by (3.18.9). Therefore the morphism 𝜎:𝐾/𝑘→𝐿/𝑘 exists by (3.18.50).

Note by (3.18.56) 𝑆 splits in 𝜎(𝐾)=𝑘(⋃𝑓∈𝑆𝑇𝑓,𝐿). If 𝐿 is also a splitting field for 𝑆 then 𝐿=𝜎(𝐾) by (3.18.54) and therefore 𝜎 is an isomorphism as required. ∎

Proposition 3.18.58 (Algebraically Closed)

A field 𝑀 is algebraically closed if one of the following equivalent conditions holds

  • Every algebraic extension 𝑀′/𝑀 is trivial
  • Every non-constant polynomial in 𝑀[𝑋] has a root in 𝑀
  • Every non-constant polynomial in 𝑀[𝑋] splits in 𝑀

NB in this case 𝑀 is also normal.

Definition 3.18.59 (Algebraic Closure)
An algebraic closure 𝑘― of 𝑘 is a field extension 𝑘―/𝑘 which is algebraic and for which 𝑘― is algebraically closed.
Proposition 3.18.60 (Existence of Algebraic Closure)
Given a field 𝑘 there exists an algebraic closure 𝑘―/𝑘
Proposition 3.18.61 (Algebraic extensions embed into Algebraic Closure)

Let 𝐾/𝑘 be an algebraic extension and 𝑀/𝑘 be field such that every polynomial 𝑓∈𝑘[𝑋] splits completely then there exists a morphism 𝜎:𝐾/𝑘→𝑀/𝑘.

In particular this holds when 𝑀 is algebraically closed.

Proof. A straightforward application of (3.18.50) since every 𝑚𝛼,𝑘(𝑋) splits in 𝑀. ∎
Corollary 3.18.62 (Uniqueness of algebraic closure)
An algebraic closure 𝑘― of 𝑘 is unique up to (non-unique) isomorphism.

More generally we may show the existence of smaller splitting fields

Proposition 3.18.63 (Existence of Splitting Field)

Given a field 𝑘 and family of polynomials 𝑆⊂𝑘[𝑋] then there exists a splitting field 𝐾.

When 𝑆={𝑓} then this can be chosen such that [𝐾:𝑘]≤𝑛! where 𝑛=deg(𝑓).

Proof. We may take the subfield of 𝑘― generated by the roots of polynomials in 𝑆.

In the case 𝑆 is finite it is possible to avoid the use of 𝑘―. First reduce to the case of a single polynomial 𝑆={𝑓} and proceed by induction on deg(𝑓). The inductive step may be demonstrated using (3.18.40). ∎

Remark 3.18.64

Note if 𝐾/𝑘 is an algebraic extension then (3.18.61) shows that we may construct an embedding 𝐾→𝑘― commuting with 𝑘→𝑘―.
