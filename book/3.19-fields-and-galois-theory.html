3.19.5 Galois Theory Summary

Definition 3.19.65 (Separable, Normal and Galois)

Let 𝐾/𝑘 be an algebraic extension. We say that 𝐾/𝑘 is

  • Separable if every minimal polynomial 𝑚𝛼,𝑘∈𝑘[𝑋] is separable.
  • Galois if it is both normal and separable (iff 𝑚𝛼,𝑘 has deg(𝑚𝛼,𝑘) distinct roots in 𝐾, see (3.19.25)).

In the case of a Galois extension we denote the group of automorphisms by Gal(𝐾/𝑘).

To summarize the main results

  1. The group of automorphism of a normal extension 𝐾/𝑘 acts transitively on the roots of a given irreducible polynomial.
  2. For 𝐾/𝑘 finite we have #︎Aut(𝐾/𝑘)≤[𝐾:𝑘] with equality if and only if 𝐾/𝑘 is Galois.
  3. An algebraic extension 𝐾/𝑘 is automatically separable whenever either char(𝑘)=0 or 𝑘 is finite.

  4. When 𝐾/𝑘 is finite and Galois then we have an order-reversing bijection between subfields and subgroups

3.19.6 Splitting Fields and Algebraic Closure

In this section we discuss splitting fields, which are the “smallest” extensions in which a given set of polynomials split completely. The fundamental result is that splitting fields are precisely the Normal extensions. Further we discuss the algebraic closure, in which every polynomial splits and in which every algebraic extension (normal or otherwise) may be embedded.

3.19.7 Normal Extensions

Recall that an algebraic extension 𝐾/𝑘 is normal if all minimal polynomials split completely. They are in some sense “closed”. Furthermore 𝑘―/𝑘 is clearly normal and results about 𝑘― can often be generalized to normal fields 𝐿/𝑘. We also show that an extension is normal iff it is a splitting field.

We follow Lang and not only characterize separability but define a “separability degree” which equals the extension degree if and only if it’s separable. The proofs are somewhat technical, especially in light of the fact most base fields will be perfect.

Proposition 3.19.117 (Perfect field criteria)

  1. Every irreducible polynomial in 𝑘[𝑋] is separable
  2. Every algebraic extension 𝐾/𝑘 is separable
  3. 𝑘―/𝑘 is separable
  4. 𝑘𝑝=𝑘

In particular every field of characteristic zero is perfect, and an algebraic extension of a perfect field is separable.

Proof. We prove each in turn

𝑎)⟹𝑏) Minimal polynomials are irreducible by (3.19.50) and therefore are separable by hypothesis.

𝑏)⟺𝑐) One direction is automatic as 𝑘― is algebraic. On the other hand every algebraic extension is isomorphic to a subfield of 𝑘―, so the implication follows from (3.19.93).

𝑏)⟹𝑑) We need only show 𝑘𝑝=𝑘 in the case 𝑝>1. If there exists 𝑎∈𝑘∖𝑘𝑝 then by (3.19.100) the polynomial 𝑓(𝑋)≔𝑋𝑝−𝑎 is irreducible. Then it’s clear that the field extension 𝐾≔𝑘[𝑋]/(𝑋𝑝−𝑎) is not separable. For the minimal polynomial of 𝑋― is 𝑓 which by (3.19.105) is not separable. This contradicts the assumption that 𝐾/𝑘 is separable.

𝑑)⟹𝑎) Suppose that 𝑓 is irreducible but not separable. Then by (3.19.105) char(𝑘)=𝑝>1 and 𝑓∈𝑘[𝑋𝑝]. By assumption all the coefficients are 𝑝-th powers and therefore 𝑓=ℎ𝑝 by (3.19.6) for some ℎ∈𝑘[𝑋]. This contradicts irreducibility of 𝑓, and so 𝑓 must be separable. ∎

Definition 3.19.118 (Bounds on Aut(𝐾/𝑘))

Proposition 3.19.119 (Primitive Element Theorem)
Let 𝐾/𝑘 be a finite separable extension of 𝑘 then 𝐾=𝑘(𝛼) is simple.

𝑓(𝑋)=∏𝑖≠𝑗(𝑋(𝜎𝑖(𝛽)−𝜎𝑗(𝛽))−(𝜎𝑖(𝛼)−𝜎𝑗(𝛼)))

Then the embeddings are distinct precisely when 𝑓(𝑐)≠0. Since 𝑓(𝑋) has at most finitely many roots and 𝑘 is infinite, there must exist such a 𝑐. ∎

Proposition 3.19.120 (Normal Criteria II)

Let 𝐿/𝐾/𝑘 be a tower of algebraic extensions such that 𝐿/𝑘 is normal (e.g. 𝐿=𝑘―). Then 𝐾/𝑘 is normal if and only if the embedding

#︎Aut(𝐾/𝑘)≤[𝐾:𝑘]𝑠≤[𝐾:𝑘]

3.19.14 Finite Fields

A finite field 𝐾 necessarily has positive characteristic 𝑝, and therefore the prime subfield is isomorphic to the field 𝔽𝑝≔ℤ/𝑝ℤ. We list some necessary properties of a finite field

Proposition 3.19.122 (Properties of finite fields)

Every finite field 𝐾 is a finite-dimensional vector space over its prime subfield 𝔽𝑝. Define 𝑛=[𝐾:𝔽𝑝].

  • #︎𝐾=𝑝𝑛
  • 𝐾 is a splitting field for 𝑋𝑝𝑛−𝑋∈𝔽𝑝[𝑋], and indeed is equal to the set of roots
  • The multiplicative group of units 𝐾⋆ is cyclic.
  • 𝐾/𝔽𝑝 is simple

Proof. Since 𝐾/𝔽𝑝 is a finite-dimensional vector space it must have order 𝑝𝑛.

The group of units has order 𝑝𝑛−1, so by Lagrange’s theorem every non-zero element satisfies 𝑋𝑝𝑛−1−1=0, so therefore every element satisfies 𝑋𝑝𝑛−𝑋=0. Since this polynomial can have at most 𝑝𝑛 roots (3.19.17) the roots are exactly all the elements of 𝐾.

We note again that 𝑋𝑑−1 has at most 𝑑 roots by (3.19.17). Therefore the fact 𝐾⋆ is cyclic follows from (3.2.25). ∎

Proposition 3.19.123 (Frobenius morphism)

Given any field 𝐾/𝔽𝑝 the Frobenius map

𝜙:𝑥→𝑥𝑝

is an injective field homomorphism. In particular when 𝐾 is finite (or even algebraic) it is an automorphism over 𝔽𝑝.

Proof. The only non-trivial step is showing

(𝑥+𝑦)𝑝=𝑥𝑝+𝑦𝑝

which follows from elementary calculations on binomial coefficients.

For the final statement use (3.19.49). ∎

Further we can show existence and uniqueness of finite fields.

Proposition 3.19.124 (Existence and uniqueness of finite fields)

Consider the algebraic closure 𝔽𝑝― and let 𝔽𝑝𝑛 denote the splitting field of 𝑓(𝑋)=𝑋𝑝𝑛−𝑋 in 𝔽𝑝―. Then

  • 𝔽𝑝𝑛 is equal to the set of roots of 𝑋𝑝𝑛−𝑋
  • It is the unique subfield of order 𝑝𝑛 and every finite field of order 𝑝𝑛 is isomorphic to this.
  • 𝔽𝑝𝑚⊂𝔽𝑝𝑛⟺𝑚∣𝑛

Proof. By (3.19.123) the set of roots of 𝑓(𝑋) forms a subfield of 𝔽𝑝―.

Furthermore 𝑓′(𝑋)=−1 so 𝑓(𝑋) is separable because clearly (𝑓,𝑓′)=1. Therefore by (3.19.25) 𝑓(𝑋) has 𝑝𝑛 distinct roots and the splitting field of 𝑓(𝑋) is exactly the set of roots.

Furthermore every subfield of order 𝑝𝑛 must satisfy this polynomial by Lagrange’s Theorem (3.2.12), so it is the unique such subfield.

Since every algebraic extension of 𝔽𝑝 is isomorphic to a subfield of 𝔽𝑝― it’s also the unique algebraic extension of order 𝑝𝑛 up to isomorphism.

Clearly if 𝔽𝑝𝑚⊂𝔽𝑝𝑛 then [𝔽𝑝𝑛:𝔽𝑝]=[𝔽𝑝𝑛:𝔽𝑝𝑚][𝔽𝑝𝑚:𝔽𝑝], so we must have 𝑚∣𝑛. Conversely if 𝛼∈𝔽𝑝𝑚 then 𝛼𝑝𝑚=𝛼⟹𝛼𝑝𝑟𝑚=𝛼 for all 𝑟>0, so 𝛼∈𝔽𝑝𝑛. ∎

It is usually most convenient to work in 𝔽𝑝― and consider the subfields of order 𝑝𝑛. We’ve seen in (3.19.116) that every finite field 𝔽𝑞≔𝔽𝑝𝑛 is perfect and therefore every algebraic extension is separable (3.19.117). In fact we may show that every finite extension is Galois.

Proposition 3.19.125

The field extension 𝔽𝑝𝑛/𝔽𝑝 is Galois with

Gal(𝔽𝑝𝑛/𝔽𝑝)=⟨𝜙⟩

cyclic of order 𝑛 generated by the Frobenius automorphism.

Proof. Let 𝐺=Aut(𝔽𝑝𝑛/𝔽𝑝). We’ve observed that 𝜙∈𝐺. Let 𝑑=𝑜(𝜙), then we wish to prove that 𝑛=𝑑. Certainly Lagrange’s Theorem applied to the multiplicative group 𝔽𝑝𝑛⋆ implies 𝜙𝑛=1. Therefore 𝑑∣𝑛 by (3.2.12) applied to 𝐺. By definition 𝜙𝑑=𝑒, so every 𝛼∈𝔽𝑝𝑛 satisfies the polynomial 𝑋𝑝𝑑−𝑋=0. This polynomial has at most 𝑝𝑑 roots (3.19.17) so we must have 𝑑≥𝑛, and therefore 𝑑=𝑛. Clearly 𝜙 generates a cyclic subgroup of order 𝑛. However by (3.19.121) 𝐺 has at most order 𝑛, whence 𝐺=⟨𝜙⟩ as required. Furthermore by the same result 𝔽𝑝𝑛/𝔽𝑝 is Galois. ∎
Proposition 3.19.126 (Subfields of 𝔽𝑝𝑛)

Consider the field extension 𝔽𝑝𝑛/𝔽𝑝. Then it has a unique subfield of order 𝑝𝑚 if 𝑚∣𝑛 (and no such subfield otherwise). In this case 𝔽𝑝𝑛/𝔽𝑝𝑚 is Galois and

Gal(𝔽𝑝𝑛/𝔽𝑝𝑚)=⟨𝜙𝑚⟩

and in particular has order 𝑛/𝑚.

Proof. We’ve already shown that 𝔽𝑝𝑛 has a unique subfield of order 𝑝𝑚, by assuming an embedding in 𝔽𝑝―. Let 𝐻=Aut(𝔽𝑝𝑛/𝔽𝑝𝑚). Note 𝜙𝑚 has order 𝑛/𝑚. Furthermore from (3.19.124) every element of 𝔽𝑝𝑚 satisfies 𝑋𝑝𝑚−𝑋. In other words 𝜙𝑚 fixes 𝔽𝑝𝑚 and 𝜙𝑚∈𝐻. Therefore ⟨𝜙𝑚⟩≤𝐻 and #︎𝐻≥𝑛/𝑚. By (3.19.121) #︎𝐻≤[𝔽𝑝𝑛:𝔽𝑝𝑚]=𝑛/𝑚, whence we have equality and so the extension is Galois by (3.19.121) and 𝐻=⟨𝜙𝑚⟩. ∎
Lemma 3.19.127

Let 𝑥∈𝔽𝑝𝑛. Then deg(𝑥)=𝑛⟺Fix(𝑥)={1}⊂Gal(𝔽𝑝𝑛/𝔽𝑝).

3.19.15 Galois Theory

We’ve seen that for 𝐾/𝑘 a finite extension

#︎Aut(𝐾/𝑘)≤[𝐾:𝑘]𝑠≤[𝐾:𝑘]

with equality if and only if 𝐾/𝑘 is Galois, by (3.19.121).

Remark 3.19.128
If 𝑘 is perfect then 𝑘―/𝑘 is Galois.

The main result of Galois Theory is

Proposition 3.19.129

Let 𝐾/𝑘 be a finite Galois extension then there is an order-reversing bijection between subgroups and subfields

{𝐻≤Gal(𝐾/𝑘)}⟷{𝐹/𝑘⊆𝐾/𝑘}𝐻⟶𝐾𝐻≔{𝑥∈𝐾|ℎ(𝑥)=𝑥∀ℎ∈𝐻}Gal(𝐾/𝐹)⟵𝐹
Proof. This is proved in a series of Propositions in the rest of this section. Firstly we show it is well-defined in (3.19.130). The maps are mutual inverses by (3.19.131) and (3.19.132). ∎

Such an order reversing map is usually called an (antitone) Galois connection, as the first such type arose from Galois Theory. Note it is well-defined because of the following proposition.

Proposition 3.19.130
If 𝐾/𝑘 is Galois and 𝐹⊂𝐾/𝑘 then 𝐾/𝐹 is Galois.
Proof. This follows from (3.19.79) and (3.19.93). ∎
Proposition 3.19.131 (Fixed field of Galois group)

If 𝐾/𝑘 is Galois and 𝐹⊂𝐾/𝑘 a subfield then

𝐾Gal(𝐾/𝐹)=𝐹
Proof. Clearly 𝐹⊆𝐾Gal(𝐾/𝐹). Conversely given 𝛼∈𝐾∖𝐹, then deg(𝑚𝛼,𝐹)>1. Since 𝛼 is separable it must have another root 𝛽∈𝐾. By (3.19.80) there is an element 𝜎∈Gal(𝐾/𝐹) such that 𝜎(𝛼)=𝛽. In other words 𝛼∉𝐾Gal(𝐾/𝐹), which shows the reverse inclusion. ∎
Proposition 3.19.132

Let 𝐾/𝑘 be a field extension and 𝐻⊆Aut(𝐾/𝑘) a finite subgroup then 𝐾/𝐾𝐻 is a finite Galois extension with

Gal(𝐾/𝐾𝐻)=𝐻

and order equal to [𝐾:𝐾𝐻]. When 𝐾/𝑘 is finite then 𝐻 is automatically finite.

Proof. Firstly observe that trivially 𝐻⊆Aut(𝐾/𝐾𝐻). If we know that [𝐾:𝐾𝐻]<∞, then by (3.19.121) we have

#︎𝐻≤#︎Aut(𝐾/𝐾𝐻)≤[𝐾:𝐾𝐻]𝑠≤[𝐾:𝐾𝐻]

We can prove equality everywhere if we show that [𝐾:𝐾𝐻]≤#︎𝐻, which is shown either by (3.19.133) or (3.19.134). Note equality also shows that 𝐾/𝐾𝐻 is finite Galois by the same result.

Finally when 𝐾/𝑘 is finite, then #︎Aut(𝐾/𝑘)<∞. So in this case 𝐻 is always finite. ∎

We present two approaches to showing the inequality [𝐾:𝐾𝐻]≤#︎𝐻. The first uses independence of characters style argument, and the second which is more straightforward uses the action of 𝐻 to show that every element has degree at most #︎𝐻 (Artin).

Lemma 3.19.133 (Bound degree of fixed field I)

Let 𝐾/𝑘 be a field extension and 𝐻⊂Aut(𝐾/𝑘) a finite subgroup. Then

[𝐾:𝐾𝐻]≤#︎𝐻

Proof. Let 𝐻={𝜎1,…,𝜎𝑛} with 𝜎1=id and {𝛼1,…,𝛼𝑚} a 𝐾𝐻-linearly independent subset of 𝐾.

Consider the vector space 𝐾𝑛 and the elements 𝛼̂𝑗=(𝜎1(𝛼𝑗),…,𝜎𝑛(𝛼𝑗)) for 𝑗=1…𝑚. It’s enough to show that these are linearly independent over 𝐾, as that implies 𝑚≤𝑛 by (3.3.125). Therefore 𝐾/𝐾𝐻 is finite dimensional with dimension at most 𝑛=#︎𝐻.

Let 𝑆(𝐾)≔{𝑣∈𝐾𝑚|∑𝑗=1𝑚𝑣𝑗𝛼̂𝑗=0}, we aim to show that 𝑆(𝐾)={0}. If we also consider 𝑆(𝐾𝐻), any non-zero elements will be a 𝐾𝐻 linear-dependence for 𝛼1,…,𝛼𝑚 by considering the first component (𝜎1=id). Therefore by linear independence of 𝛼𝑖 we see 𝑆(𝐾𝐻)={0}. So it’s enough to show that 𝑆(𝐾)≠{0}⟹𝑆(𝐾𝐻)≠{0}, to prove 𝑆(𝐾)={0} by contradiction.

First observe that 𝐾⋆ and 𝐻 both act on 𝑆(𝐾) component-wise. The first by multiplication and the second by application. This is well-defined because 𝑣∈𝑆(𝐾) if and only if

∑𝑗𝑣𝑗𝜎(𝛼𝑗)=0∀𝜎∈𝐻.

Apply 𝜏 to obtain

∑𝑗𝜏(𝑣𝑗)(𝜏∘𝜎)(𝛼𝑗)=0∀𝜎∈𝐻

and since multiplication by 𝜏 permutes 𝐻 we see 𝜏(𝑣)∈𝑆(𝐾) as required.

If there exists 0≠𝑣∈𝑆(𝐾), consider 𝑣 with a minimal number of non-zero components. By scaling we can assume 𝜆𝑣 has at least one component in 𝐾𝐻. The vector 𝜏(𝜆𝑣)−𝜆𝑣 then has at least one fewer non-zero components, so by minimality must be zero. Since 𝜏 was arbitrary we see 0≠𝜆𝑣∈𝑆(𝐾𝐻) as required. ∎

Lemma 3.19.134 (Bound degree of fixed field II)
Let 𝐾/𝑘 be a field extension and 𝐻 a finite subgroup of Aut(𝐾/𝑘). Then 𝐾/𝐾𝐻 is finite separable, and simple, with [𝐾:𝐾𝐻]≤#︎𝐻

Proof. We show that 𝐾/𝐾𝐻 is separable and every element has degree at most #︎𝐻. For any 𝛼∈𝐾, consider the orbit 𝛼𝐻={𝜎(𝛼)|𝜎∈𝐻}, which is of order at most #︎𝐻. Then the polynomial

𝑓(𝑋)=∏𝛽∈𝛼𝐻(𝑋−𝛽)

has 𝛼 as a root and is separable by (3.19.25). Furthermore 𝑓𝜏=𝑓 because 𝜏 permutes 𝛼𝐻 (it’s injective and hence bijective). Therefore 𝑓∈𝐾𝐻[𝑋] and 𝑚𝛼,𝐾𝐻∣𝑓. We see that 𝛼 has degree at most #︎𝐻 and is separable by (3.19.23).

If 𝐾/𝑘 is finite, then a-fortiori 𝐾/𝐾𝐻 is finite, so we may apply the Primitive Element Theorem (3.19.119) directly to show the result.

More generally let 𝐾𝐻(𝛼) be a simple subfield of 𝐾 of maximal degree. This exists because the degree of 𝛼 is bounded above by #︎𝐻. We claim 𝐾𝐻(𝛼)=𝐾, for if not then 𝐾𝐻⊆𝐾𝐻(𝛼)⊊𝐾𝐻(𝛼,𝛽) is a finite separable extension of 𝐾𝐻, whence it must be simple by the Primitive Element Theorem (3.19.119), contradicting maximality. Finally the degree of [𝐾:𝐾𝐻] is the degree of 𝛼, which we’ve seen is bounded above by #︎𝐻. ∎

Now we may demonstrate straightforward criteria for subfield to be normal

Proposition 3.19.135

Let 𝐾/𝑘 be a finite Galois extension and 𝑘⊂𝐹⊂𝐾 a subfield.

Then 𝐹/𝑘 is Galois if and only if Gal(𝐾/𝐹)⊲Gal(𝐾/𝑘) is normal. In this case we have a canonical isomorphism

Gal(𝐾/𝑘)/Gal(𝐾/𝐹)→Gal(𝐹/𝑘)

Proof. Recall from (3.19.81) we have 𝐹/𝑘 is normal iff 𝜎(𝐹)=𝐹 for all 𝜎∈Gal(𝐾/𝑘). Recall 𝐾/𝐹 is normal for all subfields 𝐹. Furthermore, we observe that

3.19.17 Transcendental Field Extensions

Definition 3.19.146 (Algebraic Independence)

Let 𝐾/𝑘 be a field extension and 𝑆⊂𝐾. We say 𝑆 is algebraically independent over 𝑘 if for every finite subset of distinct elements 𝑥1,…,𝑥𝑛∈𝑆 we have

𝑓(𝑥1,…,𝑥𝑛)=0⟹𝑓=0

for all 𝑓∈𝑘[𝑋1,…,𝑋𝑛].

For a subset 𝑆⊂𝐾 define the closure operator

𝑐(𝑆)≔𝑘(𝑆)―∩𝐾≔{𝑥∈𝐾|𝑥algebraic over𝑘(𝑆)}

We say Γ is algebraically spanning if 𝑐(Γ)=𝐾, equivalently if 𝐾/𝑘(Γ) is algebraic.

We say that ℬ︀ is a transcendence base if it is both algebraically independent and spanning (i.e. 𝐾/𝑘(ℬ︀) is algebraic).

Essentially we show that (𝐾,𝑐) satisfies the properties of a matroid, in analogy with vector spaces, so that we can use the results of the Matroids section to show that transcendence bases exist and they satisfy certain properties.

Proposition 3.19.147

𝐹(𝑋1,…,𝑋𝑛)=∑𝛼∈ℕ𝑛𝜆𝛼𝑋1𝛼1…𝑋𝑛𝛼𝑛

𝜆𝛼=𝐺𝛼(𝑠1,…,𝑠𝑚)

𝐹̂(𝑋1,…,𝑋𝑛,𝑌1,…,𝑌𝑚)≔∑𝛼∈ℕ𝑛𝐺𝛼(𝑌1,…,𝑌𝑚)𝑋1𝛼1…𝑋𝑛𝛼𝑛

Proof. This is (2.3.7). ∎
Proposition 3.19.153 (Transcendence Base)

Let 𝐾/𝑘 be a field extension and 𝑆⊂𝐾 a subset. Then the following are equivalent

  1. 𝑆 is a transcendence base
  2. 𝑆 is a maximal algebraically independent set
  3. 𝑆 is minimal under the condition 𝐾/𝑘(𝑆) is algebraic

When 𝐾/𝑘 admits a finite algebraic spanning set then all bases are finite of the same size (transcendence degree). Write this as trdeg(𝐾/𝑘) or trdeg𝑘(𝐾).

Proof. Follows from (2.3.8) and (2.3.12). ∎
Proposition 3.19.154

  • 𝐾/𝑘(𝑆) is algebraic ⟹|𝑆|≥trdeg(𝐾/𝑘)
Proof. Follows directly from (2.3.13). ∎
Proposition 3.19.155

Let 𝐾/𝑘 be a field extension of finite transcendence degree and 𝑆⊂𝐾 a subset. Then the following are equivalent

  • 𝑆 is a transcendence base
  • 𝑆 is algebraically independent and |𝑆|≥trdeg(𝐾/𝑘)

3.19.18 Separating Transcendence Base

In applications the existence of a separating transcendence base is important, and is guaranteed when 𝑘 is perfect, or a weaker condition.

Definition 3.19.157 (Separating Transcendence Base)

A field extension 𝐾/𝑘 is separably generated if there exists a transcendence base 𝑆 such that 𝐾/𝑘(𝑆) is separable (and algebraic). We call such an 𝑆 a separating transcendence base.

Note when char(𝑘)=0 then every transcendence base is separating.

Definition 3.19.158 (Maclane's Criterion)

We say that an algebra 𝐴/𝑘 of characteristic exponent 𝑝 satisfies Maclane’s Criterion if it is reduced and for all 𝑎1,…,𝑎𝑛∈𝐴 the following property holds

{𝑎1,…,𝑎𝑛}k-linearly independent⟹{𝑎1𝑝,…,𝑎𝑛𝑝}k-linearly independent

When 𝑝=1 this is trivially always satisfied and when 𝑝>1 such an algebra is automatically reduced by the following result

Lemma 3.19.159

Let 𝐴 be a 𝑘-algebra and 𝑚>1 an integer. Then the following are equivalent

  1. 𝐴 is reduced
  2. 𝑥𝑚=0⟹𝑥=0

In particular if 𝐴 has characteristic exponent 𝑝>1 then it is reduced iff the Frobenius homomorphism is injective.

Proof. 𝑎)⟹𝑏) is trivial. Conversely suppose 𝑥𝑛=0. Then there exists 𝑎>0 such that 𝑛≤𝑎𝑚. In particular 𝑥𝑎𝑚=0 and therefore by induction 𝑥=0 as required. ∎

This is a generalization of the case of a perfect base field by the following result.

Proposition 3.19.160 (Perfect ⟹ Maclane)

Let 𝐴/𝑘 be a reduced algebra with 𝑘 perfect. Then it satisfies Maclane’s Criterion.

In particular this holds for every field extension 𝐾/𝑘 of a perfect base field.

Proof. By assumption 𝐴 is reduced so we may consider only the case 𝑝>1.

Suppose 0=∑𝑖𝜆𝑖𝑎𝑖𝑝 then by hypothesis 𝜆𝑖=𝜇𝑖𝑝. By (3.19.6) 0=(∑𝑖𝜇𝑖𝑎𝑖)𝑝. As 𝐴 is reduced we find 0=∑𝑖𝜇𝑖𝑎𝑖 as required. ∎

Lemma 3.19.161

Let 𝑛≥0 an integer and 𝐾=𝑘(𝑥1,…,𝑥𝑛+1) an extension of 𝑘 such that

  • char(𝑘)≠0
  • {𝑥1,…,𝑥𝑛} is a transcendence base
  • 𝐾/𝑘 satisfies Maclane’s Criterion

Then for some 𝑥𝑖 the set {𝑥1,…,𝑥𝑖̂,…,𝑥𝑛+1} is a separating transcendence base for 𝐾/𝑘.

Note when 𝑛=0 this means precisely that a simple algebraic extension satisfying Maclane’s Criterion is separable.

Proof. By assumption 𝑥𝑛+1 is algebraic over 𝑘(𝑥1,…,𝑥𝑛), and therefore there exists a non-zero 𝐹∈𝑘[𝑋1,…,𝑋𝑛+1] such that 𝐹(𝑥1,…,𝑥𝑛+1)=0. Let 𝐹 be such a polynomial of minimal total degree (total degree = the maximum degree of a monomial with non-zero coefficient appearing in 𝐹). We claim it is irreducible. For suppose not, then one of the irreducible factors must vanish at (𝑥1,…,𝑥𝑛+1), and this factor would have smaller total degree.

We wish to show that not all powers of 𝑋𝑖 appearing in 𝐹 are multiples of 𝑝. Suppose this were the case then the monomials

{𝑥1𝛼1…𝑥𝑛+1𝛼𝑛+1|𝜆𝛼≠0}

are 𝑘-linearly dependent where 𝜆𝛼 are the coefficients of 𝐹 and 𝛼∈ℕ𝑛+1. Then by Maclane’s Criterion the set

{𝑥1𝛼1/𝑝…𝑥𝑛+1𝛼𝑛+1/𝑝|𝜆𝛼≠0}

is linearly dependent. This contradicts the minimality of 𝐹.

Choose 𝑋𝑖 for which a non 𝑝-th power appears in F and define

𝐹𝑖(𝑇)≔𝐹(𝑥1,…,𝑥𝑖−1,𝑇,𝑥𝑖+1,…,𝑥𝑛+1)∈𝑘[𝑆𝑖][𝑇]

where 𝑆𝑖≔{𝑥1,…,𝑥𝑖̂,…,𝑥𝑛+1}. By assumption 𝐹𝑖(𝑇) is non-zero and clearly 𝐹𝑖(𝑥𝑖)=0 so that 𝑥𝑖 is algebraic over 𝑘(𝑆𝑖), and therefore by (3.19.58) 𝐾 is also algebraic over 𝑘(𝑆𝑖). By (3.19.155) 𝑆𝑖 is a transcendence base for 𝐾 and in particular algebraically independent. Therefore 𝑘[𝑆𝑖][𝑇] may be identified with 𝑘[𝑋1,…,𝑋𝑛+1] and we may conclude that 𝐹𝑖(𝑇) is irreducible. Further 𝑘[𝑆𝑖] is a UFD so by (3.17.33) 𝐹𝑖(𝑇) is irreducible as a polynomial in 𝑘(𝑆𝑖)[𝑇]. By construction 𝐹𝑖(𝑇)∉𝑘(𝑆𝑖)[𝑇𝑝] so by (3.19.105) 𝐹𝑖 is separable. Therefore 𝑥𝑖 is separable over 𝑘(𝑆𝑖), and 𝐾/𝑘(𝑆𝑖) is separable by (3.19.94) as required. ∎

Proposition 3.19.162

Let 𝐾/𝑘 be a finitely-generated field extension which satisfies Maclane’s Criterion (e.g. 𝑘 is perfect). Then 𝐾/𝑘 is separably generated.

More precisely every generating set contains a separating transcendence base.

Proof. When char(𝑘)=0 the generating set contains a transcendence base by (3.19.153). Every subfield of 𝐾 also has characteristic 0, so then we are done by (3.19.117). So we may only consider the positive characteristic case, 𝑝>1.

Suppose 𝐾=𝑘(𝑥1,…,𝑥𝑛). By (3.19.152) there is a (possibly empty) subset which is a transcendence base, say {𝑥1,…,𝑥𝑑} after renumbering. By (3.19.58) [𝐾:𝐾′]<∞ where 𝐾′=𝑘(𝑥1,…,𝑥𝑑), and we may choose 𝐾′ such that the degree of inseparability [𝐾:𝐾′]𝑖 is minimal. If 𝐾/𝐾′ is separable then we are done. Otherwise we may assume by renumbering that 𝐾′(𝑥𝑑+1)/𝐾′ is not separable (3.19.94) and therefore [𝐾′(𝑥𝑑+1):𝐾′]𝑖>1. Then by (3.19.161) there is an index 𝑖≤𝑑+1 such that [𝐾′(𝑥𝑑+1):𝐾″]𝑖=1 where 𝐾″≔𝑘(𝑥1,…,𝑥̂𝑖,…,𝑥𝑑+1). By multiplicativity

[𝐾:𝐾′]𝑖=[𝐾:𝐾′(𝑥𝑑+1)]𝑖[𝐾′(𝑥𝑑+1):𝐾′]𝑖>[𝐾:𝐾′(𝑥𝑑+1)]𝑖[𝐾′(𝑥𝑑+1):𝐾″]𝑖=[𝐾:𝐾″]𝑖

which contradicts minimality. ∎
