2.6.16 Inverse Limit

Definition 2.6.129 (Inverse Limit / Directed Limit)

Let 𝐼 be a directed preorder and 𝐹:𝐼op→𝒞︀ a diagram – equivalently, an inverse system indexed by 𝐼: for 𝑖≺𝑗 in 𝐼, a restriction morphism 𝜌𝑖𝑗:𝐹(𝑗)→𝐹(𝑖) in 𝒞︀, with 𝜌𝑖𝑖=1𝐹(𝑖) and 𝜌𝑖𝑘=𝜌𝑖𝑗∘𝜌𝑗𝑘 whenever 𝑖≺𝑗≺𝑘.

A limit for 𝐹 in the sense of (2.6.78) (applied to the small category 𝐼op) is called an inverse limit for the system, denoted lim⟵𝑖∈𝐼𝐹(𝑖): explicitly, a cone (𝐿,{𝜆𝑖:𝐿→𝐹(𝑖)}𝑖∈𝐼) with 𝜌𝑖𝑗∘𝜆𝑗=𝜆𝑖 for every 𝑖≺𝑗, terminal among such cones.

Proposition 2.6.130 (Inverse Limits of Sets)

Let 𝐼 be a directed preorder and 𝐹:𝐼op→𝐒𝐞𝐭 an inverse system, with restriction maps 𝜌𝑖𝑗:𝐹(𝑗)→𝐹(𝑖) (𝑖≺𝑗). Then

lim⟵𝑖∈𝐼𝐹(𝑖)={(𝑥𝑖)𝑖∈𝐼∈∏𝑖∈𝐼𝐹(𝑖)|𝜌𝑖𝑗(𝑥𝑗)=𝑥𝑖for all𝑖≺𝑗},

with projections 𝜆𝑖((𝑥𝑗)𝑗)≔𝑥𝑖.

Proof. This is (2.6.85) applied to the diagram 𝐹:𝐼op→𝐒𝐞𝐭 (𝐼op a small category, as 𝐼 is). ∎
Proposition 2.6.131 (Cofinal Inverse Limit)

Let 𝐼 be a directed preorder, 𝐼′⊂𝐼 a full subcategory which is also directed, and 𝐹:𝐼op→𝒞︀ an inverse system, with restriction maps 𝜌𝑖𝑗 (𝑖≺𝑗 in 𝐼). Then, provided both exist, there is a unique morphism of cones

lim⟵𝑖∈𝐼𝐹(𝑖)⟶lim⟵𝑖∈𝐼′𝐹(𝑖)

(restricting the limit cone over 𝐼 to the subcategory 𝐼′).

If 𝐼′ is cofinal in 𝐼 (2.6.126) then this is an isomorphism.

Proof. Drafted by Claude Sonnet, reviewed by David Rufino.

Write 𝐿′≔lim⟵𝑖∈𝐼′𝐹(𝑖), with projections 𝜆𝑖′:𝐿′→𝐹(𝑖) (𝑖∈𝐼′). The cone (𝐿′,{𝜆𝑖′}𝑖∈𝐼′) over 𝐹|𝐼′op is in particular a cone over all of 𝐹 restricted along the inclusion 𝐼′op⊂𝐼op, so composing with the (terminal, by definition of lim⟵𝑖∈𝐼𝐹(𝑖)) limit cone’s universal property in the other direction gives nothing yet – rather, the stated morphism is simply the unique one afforded by terminality of 𝐿′ among cones over 𝐹|𝐼′op, applied to the cone (lim⟵𝑖∈𝐼𝐹(𝑖),{𝜆𝑖}𝑖∈𝐼′) (the limit cone over 𝐼, restricted in its indexing set to 𝐼′ – still a cone over 𝐹|𝐼′op, by the cone condition on 𝐼 restricted to 𝑖≺𝑗 both in 𝐼′): this gives the unique morphism lim⟵𝑖∈𝐼𝐹(𝑖)→𝐿′ of the statement.

Now suppose 𝐼′ is cofinal. For 𝑖∈𝐼, choose (by cofinality) 𝑖′∈𝐼′ with 𝑖≺𝑖′, and define 𝜇𝑖≔𝜌𝑖,𝑖′∘𝜆𝑖′′:𝐿′→𝐹(𝑖).

𝜇𝑖 is independent of the choice of 𝑖′. If 𝑖≺𝑖1′,𝑖2′ with 𝑖1′,𝑖2′∈𝐼′, directedness of 𝐼′ gives 𝑖3′∈𝐼′ with 𝑖1′,𝑖2′≺𝑖3′; then, using the cone condition for 𝜆′ (within 𝐼′, at 𝑖1′≺𝑖3′) and the restriction composition law of (2.6.129) (at 𝑖≺𝑖1′≺𝑖3′),

𝜌𝑖,𝑖1′∘𝜆𝑖1′′=𝜌𝑖,𝑖1′∘𝜌𝑖1′,𝑖3′∘𝜆𝑖3′′=𝜌𝑖,𝑖3′∘𝜆𝑖3′′,

and symmetrically 𝜌𝑖,𝑖2′∘𝜆𝑖2′′=𝜌𝑖,𝑖3′∘𝜆𝑖3′′; so both choices agree.

(𝐿′,{𝜇𝑖}𝑖∈𝐼) is a cone over 𝐹. Let 𝑖≺𝑗 in 𝐼. Choose 𝑗′∈𝐼′ with 𝑗≺𝑗′; then also 𝑖≺𝑗′, so 𝑗′ may be used to compute both 𝜇𝑖 and 𝜇𝑗:

𝜌𝑖𝑗∘𝜇𝑗=𝜌𝑖𝑗∘𝜌𝑗,𝑗′∘𝜆𝑗′′=𝜌𝑖,𝑗′∘𝜆𝑗′′=𝜇𝑖,

using the restriction composition law at 𝑖≺𝑗≺𝑗′. Moreover for 𝑖∈𝐼′ itself, taking 𝑖′≔𝑖 (with 𝜌𝑖𝑖=1𝐹(𝑖)) gives 𝜇𝑖=𝜆𝑖′: the cone (𝐿′,{𝜇𝑖}𝑖∈𝐼) restricts, on 𝐼′, to the original cone (𝐿′,{𝜆𝑖′}𝑖∈𝐼′).

(𝐿′,{𝜇𝑖}𝑖∈𝐼) is terminal. Let (𝑁,{𝜈𝑖}𝑖∈𝐼) be any cone over 𝐹. Restricting to 𝐼′ gives a cone over 𝐹|𝐼′op, so by terminality of 𝐿′ there is a unique morphism 𝛾:𝑁→𝐿′ with 𝜆𝑖′∘𝛾=𝜈𝑖 for all 𝑖∈𝐼′. For general 𝑖∈𝐼, choosing 𝑖′∈𝐼′ with 𝑖≺𝑖′,

𝜇𝑖∘𝛾=𝜌𝑖,𝑖′∘𝜆𝑖′′∘𝛾=𝜌𝑖,𝑖′∘𝜈𝑖′=𝜈𝑖

using the cone condition for 𝜈 (over all of 𝐼) at 𝑖≺𝑖′; so 𝛾 is a morphism of cones (𝑁,{𝜈𝑖}𝑖∈𝐼)→(𝐿′,{𝜇𝑖}𝑖∈𝐼). Any such morphism 𝛿 satisfies, in particular for 𝑖∈𝐼′ (where 𝜇𝑖=𝜆𝑖′), 𝜆𝑖′∘𝛿=𝜈𝑖, so 𝛿=𝛾 by the uniqueness already established for 𝛾. Hence (𝐿′,{𝜇𝑖}𝑖∈𝐼) is terminal among cones over 𝐹 on all of 𝐼, that is, (𝐿′,{𝜇𝑖}𝑖∈𝐼) is lim⟵𝑖∈𝐼𝐹(𝑖).

By uniqueness of limits up to unique isomorphism of cones (2.6.78), there is a (unique) isomorphism lim⟵𝑖∈𝐼𝐹(𝑖)→∼𝐿′ compatible with the projections {𝜇𝑖}𝑖∈𝐼 (on the 𝐿′ side) and {𝜆𝑖}𝑖∈𝐼 (on the lim⟵𝑖∈𝐼𝐹(𝑖) side); restricting this compatibility to 𝑖∈𝐼′ (where 𝜇𝑖=𝜆𝑖′) shows it is compatible with {𝜆𝑖′}𝑖∈𝐼′ too, so by the uniqueness clause established in the first paragraph, this isomorphism is exactly the morphism of cones lim⟵𝑖∈𝐼𝐹(𝑖)→lim⟵𝑖∈𝐼′𝐹(𝑖)=𝐿′ of the statement. ∎

Corollary 2.6.132 (Functoriality of the Inverse Limit)

Let 𝐼 be a directed preorder, and let 𝒞︀lim𝐼op denote the full subcategory of the functor category 𝒞︀𝐼op (2.6.14) spanned by those inverse systems 𝐹:𝐼op→𝒞︀ for which lim⟵𝑖𝐹(𝑖) exists. Then

lim⟵𝑖(−):𝒞︀lim𝐼op⟶𝒞︀,𝐹↦lim⟵𝑖𝐹(𝑖)

is a functor.

More precisely: for a natural transformation 𝜎=(𝜎𝑖:𝐹(𝑖)→𝐺(𝑖))𝑖∈𝐼 there is a unique morphism

lim⟵𝑖𝜎𝑖:lim⟵𝑖𝐹(𝑖)⟶lim⟵𝑖𝐺(𝑖)

compatible with the projections; and

  1. for a further natural transformation 𝜏=(𝜏𝑖:𝐺(𝑖)→𝐾(𝑖))𝑖 (so 𝜏𝜎≔(𝜏𝑖𝜎𝑖)𝑖, itself a natural transformation 𝐹⇒𝐾),

    lim⟵𝑖(𝜏𝑖𝜎𝑖)=lim⟵𝑖𝜏𝑖∘lim⟵𝑖𝜎𝑖;
  2. lim⟵𝑖1𝐹(𝑖)=1lim⟵𝑖𝐹(𝑖).

Consequently, if every 𝜎𝑖 is an isomorphism, so is lim⟵𝑖𝜎𝑖 (2.6.42).

Proof. Drafted by Claude Sonnet, reviewed by David Rufino.

A natural transformation 𝜎=(𝜎𝑖)𝑖 satisfying 𝜎𝑖∘𝜌𝑖𝑗𝐹=𝜌𝑖𝑗𝐺∘𝜎𝑗 for all 𝑖≺𝑗 is exactly a morphism 𝐹⇒𝐺 of the functor category 𝒞︀𝐼op (2.6.14) (the naturality square for 𝜎 at the morphism 𝑗→𝑖 of 𝐼op corresponding to 𝑖≺𝑗), invertible in 𝒞︀𝐼op exactly when every 𝜎𝑖 is an isomorphism (with inverse (𝜎𝑖−1)𝑖). So this is precisely the case 𝐽≔𝐼op of (2.6.97). ∎

Corollary 2.6.133 (Inverse Limits Commute with Products)

Let 𝐼 be a directed preorder, 𝐾 a set, and (𝐹𝑘)𝑘∈𝐾 inverse systems 𝐹𝑘:𝐼op→𝒞︀, with limit-cone projections 𝜆𝑘,𝑖:lim⟵𝑖𝐹𝑘(𝑖)→𝐹𝑘(𝑖), such that lim⟵𝑖𝐹𝑘(𝑖) exists for every 𝑘∈𝐾; and suppose ∏𝑘∈𝐾𝐹𝑘(𝑖) exists for every 𝑖∈𝐼, with product projections 𝜋𝑘,𝑖:∏𝑘′∈𝐾𝐹𝑘′(𝑖)→𝐹𝑘(𝑖). Φ:∏𝑘∈𝐾lim⟵𝑖𝐹𝑘(𝑖)⟶lim⟵𝑖(∏𝑘∈𝐾𝐹𝑘(𝑖))

and Φ is an isomorphism.

Proof. Drafted by Claude Sonnet, reviewed by David Rufino.

Regard 𝐾 as a discrete small category and define a bifunctor 𝐻:𝐾×𝐼op→𝒞︀ by 𝐻(𝑘,𝑖)≔𝐹𝑘(𝑖) (a morphism of 𝐾×𝐼op is a pair (1𝑘,𝛼) for 𝛼 a morphism of 𝐼op, since 𝐾 is discrete, and 𝐻(1𝑘,𝛼)≔𝐹𝑘(𝛼); this is a bifunctor as each 𝐹𝑘 is a functor). By (2.6.81) (limits over a discrete category are products), (∏𝑘∈𝐾𝐹𝑘(𝑖),{𝜋𝑘,𝑖}𝑘) is lim𝑘∈𝐾𝐻(𝑘,𝑖) for every 𝑖∈𝐼op, and by (2.6.129), (lim⟵𝑖𝐹𝑘(𝑖),{𝜆𝑘,𝑖}𝑖) is lim𝑖∈𝐼op𝐻(𝑘,𝑖) for every 𝑘∈𝐾; both exist by hypothesis, so (writing 𝐿(𝑘)≔lim⟵𝑖𝐹𝑘(𝑖), 𝑅(𝑖)≔∏𝑘∈𝐾𝐹𝑘(𝑖)) (2.6.100) applies to 𝐻.

As (∏𝑘∈𝐾lim⟵𝑖𝐹𝑘(𝑖),{𝑝𝑘}𝑘)=(lim𝑘∈𝐾𝐿(𝑘),{𝑝𝑘}𝑘) exists by hypothesis, (2.6.100) gives that lim𝑖∈𝐼op𝑅(𝑖)=lim⟵𝑖(∏𝑘∈𝐾𝐹𝑘(𝑖)) also exists, with limit-cone projections Λ𝑖 say, together with a unique isomorphism Φ making the stated diagram commute for every 𝑘,𝑖 (that proposition’s diagram, with 𝜈𝑘≔𝑝𝑘, 𝜉𝑖≔Λ𝑖, 𝜆𝑘,𝑖 as here, and 𝜇𝑘,𝑖≔𝜋𝑘,𝑖). ∎
