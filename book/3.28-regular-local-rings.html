3.28 Regular Local Rings

The results of the previous section allow a more direct characterization of the dimension of a Noetherian local ring, which naturally leads to the notion of regular local ring.

Lemma 3.28.1 (𝔪-primary)

Let (𝐴,𝔪) be a local ring and 𝔞 a proper ideal. Then the following are equivalent

  1. 𝔞=𝔪
  2. 𝔪 is a minimal prime of 𝔞

Furthermore 𝔞 is primary. In this case we say 𝔞 is 𝔪-primary. A sufficient condition is that for some 𝑛

𝔪𝑛⊂𝔞⊂𝔪

and this is necessary when 𝔪 is finitely generated.

Proof. a) ⟹ b) Suppose 𝔞⊆𝔭 then 𝔪=𝔞⊆𝔭 by (3.3.45). In particular 𝔪 is a minimal prime.

b) ⟹ a) Let 𝔭 be a prime ideal containing 𝔞. By (3.3.36) 𝔭⊆𝔪 and by assumption 𝔭=𝔪. The result then follows from (3.3.45).

Suppose 𝑥𝑦∈𝔞 and 𝑦∉𝔞 then by (…) 𝑦∈𝐴⋆ whence 𝑥∈𝔞. This shows that 𝔞 is primary.

The condition 𝔪𝑛⊂𝔞 is clearly sufficient, and necessary in the finitely generated case by (3.3.47). ∎

An ideal satisfying one of the equivalent conditions above is called 𝔪-primary, though some authors require the stronger condition.
