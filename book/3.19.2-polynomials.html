Proposition 3.19.12 (Division Algorithm over a field)

For 𝑘 a field consider the polynomial ring 𝑘[𝑋]. For every pair of polynomials 𝑓(𝑋), 𝑔(𝑋) there exists unique polynomials 𝑞(𝑋) and 𝑟(𝑋) such that

Then if 𝑓(𝛼)=0 we clearly have 𝑣𝑋−𝛼(𝑓)>0. Finally we may construct

Definition 3.19.21 (Separable Polynomial)
A polynomial 𝑓∈𝑘[𝑋] is separable if 𝑓 and 𝑓′ are co-maximal, that is (𝑓,𝑓′)=(1). Otherwise it is inseparable.

A separable polynomial 𝑓∈𝑘[𝑋] has no multiple roots (and 𝑓 and 𝑓′ have no common roots) in any extension field 𝐾/𝑘.
Proof. Since (𝑓,𝑓′)=(1) we have 𝑎𝑓+𝑏𝑓′=1 for some 𝑎,𝑏∈𝑘[𝑋]. Clearly 𝑓 and 𝑓′ can have no common roots, and therefore 𝑓 has no multiple roots by (3.19.20). ∎
Proposition 3.19.23
Suppose 𝑓,𝑔∈𝑘[𝑋], 𝑔 is separable and 𝑓|𝑔, then 𝑓 is separable.
Proof. Suppose 𝑓 is not separable then by (3.16.5) 𝑓 and 𝑓′ have a common divisor 𝑑 such that deg(𝑑)>0. Since 𝑔=𝑓ℎ, so 𝑔′=𝑓′ℎ+𝑓ℎ′. Therefore 𝑑 is also a non-trivial common divisor of 𝑔 and 𝑔′ contradicting (3.16.5). ∎
Lemma 3.19.24 (Coprime Polynomials have no Common Roots)
Let 𝐾/𝑘 be a field extension and 𝑓,𝑔∈𝑘[𝑋] with 𝑓≠0 splitting completely in 𝐾. Then 𝑓 and 𝑔 are coprime if and only if they have no common root in 𝐾. In particular this holds whenever 𝑓 and 𝑔 both split completely in 𝐾.

Proof. Drafted by Claude Sonnet 5, reviewed by David Rufino.

Suppose 𝑓,𝑔 are coprime, so 𝑎𝑓+𝑏𝑔=1 for some 𝑎,𝑏∈𝑘[𝑋]. If 𝛼∈𝐾 were a common root then evaluating at 𝛼 gives 0=1, a contradiction.

Conversely suppose 𝑓,𝑔 are not coprime. As 𝑘[𝑋] is a PID (3.19.13), by (3.16.5) they have a common non-invertible divisor 𝑑, and deg(𝑑)>0 as the invertible elements of 𝑘[𝑋] are the nonzero constants (3.19.9). Since 𝑑|𝑓 and 𝑓 splits completely in 𝐾, so does 𝑑 (3.19.19); as deg(𝑑)>0 it has a root 𝛼∈𝐾, which is a common root of 𝑓 and 𝑔 as 𝑑 divides both. ∎
