4.2.11 Exactness

Definition 4.2.43 (Exact Sequence of Sheaves)
Let 𝒞︀ be pointed and algebraic; then 𝐒𝐡(𝑋;𝒞︀) is evidently pointed (zero morphisms computed pointwise, the constant zero presheaf being a zero object), with a regular epi-mono factorisation (4.2.40) for every morphism, so (2.6.200) applies with 𝐒𝐡(𝑋;𝒞︀) in place of 𝒞︀: a pair of composable morphisms of sheaves ℱ︀⟶𝛼𝒢︀⟶𝛽ℋ︀ is exact at 𝒢︀ if Im(𝛼)=ker(𝛽), that is (Im(𝛼),𝑚) is (literally) a kernel of 𝛽, for any (equivalently, by the same argument, every) choice of image factorisation 𝛼=𝑚∘𝑒 — in particular we may as well take the canonical construction of (4.2.40).
Proposition 4.2.44 (Concrete Criterion for Exactness of Sheaves)

Let 𝒞︀ be pointed and algebraic and ℱ︀⟶𝛼𝒢︀⟶𝛽ℋ︀ a pair of composable morphisms of sheaves. Regard Im(𝛼) (4.2.40) and ker(𝛽) (4.2.25) as the canonical constructions, both literal subsheaves of 𝒢︀ since 𝒞︀ is algebraic. Then the following are equivalent

  1. the pair is exact at 𝒢︀ (4.2.43) (for any choice of image factorisation of 𝛼)
  2. Im(𝛼)(𝑈)=ker(𝛽)(𝑈), as subsets of 𝒢︀(𝑈), for every open 𝑈⊂𝑋
  3. 𝛽∘𝛼=0, and every section of ker(𝛽)(𝑈) is locally in the image of 𝛼, for every open 𝑈⊂𝑋

Proof. Drafted by Claude Sonnet 5, reviewed by David Rufino.

Write 𝛼=𝑚∘𝑒 for the canonical image factorisation (4.2.40) and 𝑖:ker(𝛽)↪︎𝒢︀ for the canonical kernel (4.2.25).

𝑎)⟺𝑏) Reduction to the canonical representatives. By (4.2.43) (applied with 𝐒𝐡(𝑋;𝒞︀) in place of 𝒞︀), exactness at 𝒢︀ does not depend on the choice of image factorisation of 𝛼, so it suffices to treat the canonical representative 𝑚: the pair is exact at 𝒢︀ if and only if (Im(𝛼),𝑚) is a kernel of 𝛽. Since (ker(𝛽),𝑖) is also a kernel of 𝛽, by (2.6.80) this holds if and only if there is an isomorphism 𝜃:Im(𝛼)→ker(𝛽) with 𝑖∘𝜃=𝑚.

(⟸) If Im(𝛼)(𝑈)=ker(𝛽)(𝑈) for every 𝑈, then 𝑚=𝑖 as morphisms of sheaves, both being the inclusion of this common subsheaf into 𝒢︀, with the (unique, (2.6.193)) induced structure making it a morphism. So 𝜃≔1Im(𝛼) satisfies 𝑖∘𝜃=𝑖=𝑚.

(⟹) Suppose there is an isomorphism 𝜃:Im(𝛼)→ker(𝛽) with 𝑖∘𝜃=𝑚. Fix 𝑈⊂𝑋 and 𝜎∈Im(𝛼)(𝑈); since 𝑚𝑈 is the inclusion of Im(𝛼)(𝑈) into 𝒢︀(𝑈), 𝑖𝑈(𝜃𝑈(𝜎))=𝑚𝑈(𝜎)=𝜎, and 𝑖𝑈 is also an inclusion, so 𝜃𝑈(𝜎)=𝜎; hence 𝜎∈ker(𝛽)(𝑈), giving Im(𝛼)(𝑈)⊆ker(𝛽)(𝑈). As 𝜃 is an isomorphism, 𝜃𝑈 is a bijection onto ker(𝛽)(𝑈), so every element of ker(𝛽)(𝑈) equals 𝜃𝑈(𝜎)=𝜎 for some 𝜎∈Im(𝛼)(𝑈), giving ker(𝛽)(𝑈)⊆Im(𝛼)(𝑈). So Im(𝛼)(𝑈)=ker(𝛽)(𝑈) for every 𝑈.

𝑏)⟺𝑐) Condition 𝑏) is, for every 𝑈, the conjunction of Im(𝛼)(𝑈)⊆ker(𝛽)(𝑈) and ker(𝛽)(𝑈)⊆Im(𝛼)(𝑈); the latter is precisely “every section of ker(𝛽)(𝑈) is locally in the image of 𝛼” (4.2.40), so it remains to show Im(𝛼)(𝑈)⊆ker(𝛽)(𝑈) for every 𝑈 is equivalent to 𝛽∘𝛼=0.

(⟸) Suppose 𝛽∘𝛼=0. For 𝜏∈Im(𝛼)(𝑈), take a witnessing cover 𝑈=⋃𝑗𝑈𝑗 with 𝜏|𝑈𝑗=𝛼𝑈𝑗(𝜎𝑗) for some 𝜎𝑗∈ℱ︀(𝑈𝑗); then 𝛽𝑈(𝜏)|𝑈𝑗=𝛽𝑈𝑗(𝜏|𝑈𝑗)=𝛽𝑈𝑗(𝛼𝑈𝑗(𝜎𝑗))=(𝛽∘𝛼)𝑈𝑗(𝜎𝑗)=0 for every 𝑗, so by separation of ℋ︀ (4.2.4), 𝛽𝑈(𝜏)=0, i.e. 𝜏∈ker(𝛽)(𝑈).

(⟹) Suppose Im(𝛼)(𝑈)⊆ker(𝛽)(𝑈) for every 𝑈. For any 𝑈 and 𝜎∈ℱ︀(𝑈), 𝛼𝑈(𝜎)∈Im(𝛼)(𝑈) (witnessed by the trivial cover {𝑈}), so 𝛼𝑈(𝜎)∈ker(𝛽)(𝑈), that is 𝛽𝑈(𝛼𝑈(𝜎))=0. As 𝑈,𝜎 were arbitrary, 𝛽∘𝛼=0. ∎

Lemma 4.2.45 (Subsheaves are Determined by Stalks)
Let 𝒞︀ be algebraic and 𝒢︀1,𝒢︀2 be subsheaves of a sheaf 𝒢︀ (4.2.30). Then 𝒢︀1(𝑈)=𝒢︀2(𝑈), as subsets of 𝒢︀(𝑈), for every open 𝑈⊂𝑋, if and only if (𝒢︀1)𝑥=(𝒢︀2)𝑥, as subsets of 𝒢︀𝑥 (via the canonical, injective (4.2.28) stalk maps of the inclusions), for every 𝑥∈𝑋.

Proof. Drafted by Claude Sonnet 5, reviewed by David Rufino.

(⟹) Immediate: if 𝒢︀1(𝑈)=𝒢︀2(𝑈) for every 𝑈, then by (4.2.6) every element of (𝒢︀1)𝑥 is 𝜎𝑥 for some 𝜎∈𝒢︀1(𝑈)=𝒢︀2(𝑈), 𝑈∋𝑥, hence lies in (𝒢︀2)𝑥, and symmetrically.

(⟸) Suppose (𝒢︀1)𝑥=(𝒢︀2)𝑥 for every 𝑥∈𝑋. Fix 𝑈⊂𝑋 and 𝜎∈𝒢︀1(𝑈)⊆𝒢︀(𝑈); we show 𝜎∈𝒢︀2(𝑈) (the reverse inclusion, and hence 𝒢︀1(𝑈)=𝒢︀2(𝑈), then follows by symmetry).

For 𝑥∈𝑈, 𝜎𝑥∈(𝒢︀1)𝑥=(𝒢︀2)𝑥, so by (4.2.6) 𝜎𝑥=𝜏𝑥 for some 𝜏∈𝒢︀2(𝑉𝑥)⊆𝒢︀(𝑉𝑥), 𝑥∈𝑉𝑥; by (4.2.8).a) (applied in 𝒢︀, as 𝜎,𝜏 agree at the stalk 𝑥) there is 𝑊𝑥⊆𝑈∩𝑉𝑥, 𝑥∈𝑊𝑥, with 𝜎|𝑊𝑥=𝜏|𝑊𝑥 in 𝒢︀(𝑊𝑥); as 𝒢︀2 is a subpresheaf, 𝜏|𝑊𝑥∈𝒢︀2(𝑊𝑥), so 𝜎|𝑊𝑥∈𝒢︀2(𝑊𝑥).

So {𝑊𝑥}𝑥∈𝑈 is an open cover of 𝑈 with 𝜎|𝑊𝑥∈𝒢︀2(𝑊𝑥) for every 𝑥; these restrictions agree pairwise on overlaps (all restricting from the common 𝜎), so as 𝒢︀2 is a sheaf, gluing gives 𝜌∈𝒢︀2(𝑈) with 𝜌|𝑊𝑥=𝜎|𝑊𝑥 for every 𝑥. Regarding 𝜌 as an element of 𝒢︀(𝑈) (via the inclusion 𝒢︀2↪︎𝒢︀), 𝜌 and 𝜎 agree on the cover {𝑊𝑥} of 𝑈, so by separation of 𝒢︀ (4.2.4), 𝜌=𝜎; hence 𝜎∈𝒢︀2(𝑈). ∎

Lemma 4.2.46 (Kernels and Stalks Commute)
Let 𝒞︀ be pointed and algebraic, 𝛽:𝒢︀→ℋ︀ a morphism of sheaves, and ker(𝛽)(𝑈)={𝜎∈𝒢︀(𝑈)|𝛽𝑈(𝜎)=0} its kernel (4.2.25), regarded as a subsheaf of 𝒢︀. Then for every 𝑥∈𝑋, the canonical stalk inclusion identifies ker(𝛽)𝑥 with the subset ker(𝛽𝑥)≔{𝜉∈𝒢︀𝑥|𝛽𝑥(𝜉)=0} of 𝒢︀𝑥.

Proof. Drafted by Claude Sonnet 5, reviewed by David Rufino.

Write 𝑖:ker(𝛽)↪︎𝒢︀ for the inclusion. By (2.6.150) and (2.6.135), 𝑖 is monic, so by (4.2.28).d) (as 𝒞︀ is algebraic and ker(𝛽),𝒢︀ are sheaves), 𝑖𝑥 is injective for every 𝑥∈𝑋.

Since 𝛽∘𝑖=0 (2.6.150), stalk-functoriality (4.2.7) gives 𝛽𝑥∘𝑖𝑥=(𝛽∘𝑖)𝑥=0𝑥=0 (the stalk of the zero presheaf, valued 0 at every open with identity restrictions, is 0, so the stalk functor sends the zero morphism to the zero morphism). So 𝑖𝑥(𝜂)∈ker(𝛽𝑥) for every 𝜂∈ker(𝛽)𝑥.

Conversely let 𝜉∈ker(𝛽𝑥), i.e. 𝜉∈𝒢︀𝑥 with 𝛽𝑥(𝜉)=0. By (4.2.6), 𝜉=𝜎𝑥 for some 𝜎∈𝒢︀(𝑈), 𝑈∈ℬ︀, 𝑥∈𝑈. Then (𝛽𝑈(𝜎))𝑥=𝛽𝑥(𝜎𝑥)=𝛽𝑥(𝜉)=0=0𝑥, so by (4.2.8).a) there is 𝑊∈ℬ︀, 𝑊⊆𝑈, 𝑥∈𝑊, with 𝛽𝑈(𝜎)|𝑊=0|𝑊, that is 𝛽𝑊(𝜎|𝑊)=0, so 𝜎|𝑊∈ker(𝛽)(𝑊). Then 𝑖𝑥((𝜎|𝑊)𝑥)=(𝑖𝑊(𝜎|𝑊))𝑥=(𝜎|𝑊)𝑥=𝜎𝑥=𝜉, so 𝜉∈Im(𝑖𝑥).

So 𝑖𝑥 is injective with image exactly ker(𝛽𝑥), that is, identifies ker(𝛽)𝑥 with this subset of 𝒢︀𝑥. ∎

Lemma 4.2.47 (Images and Stalks Commute)
Let 𝒞︀ be algebraic, 𝛼:ℱ︀→𝒢︀ a morphism of sheaves, and 𝛼=𝑚∘𝑒 its image factorisation (4.2.40). Then for every 𝑥∈𝑋, the canonical stalk inclusion identifies Im(𝛼)𝑥 with the subset Im(𝛼𝑥)≔𝛼𝑥(ℱ︀𝑥) of 𝒢︀𝑥.

Proof. Drafted by Claude Sonnet 5, reviewed by David Rufino.

Retain the notation from the proof of (4.2.42): writing 𝑒=𝜂∘𝑒𝛼 with (Impsh(𝛼),𝑒𝛼,𝑚𝛼) the image of 𝛼 in 𝐏𝐒𝐡(𝑋;𝒞︀) (4.2.39) and 𝜂 the sheafification unit exhibiting Im(𝛼)=(Impsh(𝛼))+, it is shown there that (𝑒𝛼)𝑥 is surjective (each 𝑒𝑈 being regular, hence surjective, (2.6.195)) and 𝜂𝑥 is an isomorphism (4.2.18), so 𝑒𝑥=𝜂𝑥∘(𝑒𝛼)𝑥 is surjective. Also 𝑚𝑥 is injective by (4.2.28).d), as 𝑚 is a monomorphism of sheaves.

Since 𝑚𝑥∘𝑒𝑥=𝛼𝑥 and 𝑒𝑥 is surjective onto Im(𝛼)𝑥,

𝑚𝑥(Im(𝛼)𝑥)=𝑚𝑥(𝑒𝑥(ℱ︀𝑥))=𝛼𝑥(ℱ︀𝑥)=Im(𝛼𝑥)

so, 𝑚𝑥 being injective, it identifies Im(𝛼)𝑥 with the subset Im(𝛼𝑥) of 𝒢︀𝑥. ∎

Proposition 4.2.48 (Stalk Criterion for Exactness)
Let 𝒞︀ be pointed and algebraic and ℱ︀⟶𝛼𝒢︀⟶𝛽ℋ︀ a pair of composable morphisms of sheaves. Then this pair is exact at 𝒢︀ (4.2.43) if and only if, for every 𝑥∈𝑋, Im(𝛼𝑥)=ker(𝛽𝑥), as subsets of 𝒢︀𝑥, that is, the stalk pair ℱ︀𝑥⟶𝛼𝑥𝒢︀𝑥⟶𝛽𝑥ℋ︀𝑥 is exact at 𝒢︀𝑥 in 𝒞︀ (in the sense of (4.2.43), applied verbatim in 𝒞︀).

Proof. Drafted by Claude Sonnet 5, reviewed by David Rufino.

By (4.2.44), the pair is exact at 𝒢︀ if and only if Im(𝛼)(𝑈)=ker(𝛽)(𝑈) for every 𝑈⊂𝑋 (using the canonical constructions of Im(𝛼),ker(𝛽)). By (4.2.45) applied to these subsheaves of 𝒢︀, this holds if and only if Im(𝛼)𝑥=ker(𝛽)𝑥, as subsets of 𝒢︀𝑥, for every 𝑥∈𝑋. By (4.2.47) and (4.2.46), this is exactly the condition Im(𝛼𝑥)=ker(𝛽𝑥), as subsets of 𝒢︀𝑥, for every 𝑥∈𝑋. ∎
