6.2 Abstract Varieties

In order to deal with affine, quasi-affine, projective and quasi-projective varieties uniformly it is convenient to define an abstract variety. Further some constructions are more natural in this setting (for example product variety). This is analogous to the notion of manifold, and much closer to the notion of scheme. Some differences

  1. Sections of the structure sheaf are concrete 𝑘―-valued functions rather than abstract elements of a ring in the case of a scheme.
  2. When 𝑘 is not algebraically closed the underlying topological space of the corresponding scheme is the Kolmogorov Quotient (sober-ified to include all the possible generic points). When 𝑘 is algebraically closed the only difference is the inclusion of generic points.
  3. Varieties here are always Noetherian (i.e. there is a finite affine cover and the affines are themselves Noetherian).

The differences are primarily psychological and almost all the theory carries over mutatis mutandis to the scheme setting, with additional hypotheses where necessary.

Definition 6.2.1 (Abstract Variety)

A variety over a field 𝑘 is a space with functions (𝑋,𝒪︀𝑋) such that

  • There exists a finite open cover 𝑋=⋃𝑖=1𝑛𝑈𝑖 together with isomorphisms

    𝜃𝑖:(𝑈𝑖,𝒪︀𝑋|𝑈𝑖)≅(𝑋𝑖(𝑘―),𝒪︀𝑋𝑖)

    for some affine varieties 𝑋1,…,𝑋𝑛 in the sense of (6.1.4).

The sheaf 𝒪︀𝑋 is called the structure sheaf or sheaf of regular functions. A regular morphism is then a morphism of space with functions.

It is immediate that every affine variety is an abstract variety.

Proposition 6.2.2

A variety (𝑋,𝒪︀𝑋) is a local space with functions. That is for all 𝑓∈𝒪︀𝑋(𝑈), the set

𝐷(𝑓)≔{𝑥∈𝑈|𝑓(𝑥)≠0}

is open and 𝑓|𝑈 is invertible.

Proof. We have an open affine cover 𝑋=⋃𝑖=1𝑛𝑈𝑖. Then we have

𝐷(𝑓)=⋃𝑖=1𝑛𝐷(𝑓|𝑈𝑖)

By (6.1.41) 𝐷(𝑓|𝑈𝑖) is open and 𝑓|𝑈𝑖 is invertible. We deduce that 𝐷(𝑓) is open, and using the sheaf conditions that 𝑓|𝐷(𝑓) is invertible. ∎

Definition 6.2.3 (Affine (Abstract) Variety)
Let 𝑋 be a space of functions. We say it is an affine variety if it is isomorphic to (𝑌(𝑘―),𝒪︀𝑌), where 𝑌 is an affine variety in the sense of (6.1.4) and 𝒪︀𝑌 is the corresponding sheaf of regular functions (6.1.25).
Proposition 6.2.4
Let 𝑋 be a variety and 𝑓:𝑋→𝑘― be a function. Then it is regular iff the corresponding map 𝑓:𝑋→𝔸𝑘1(𝑘―) is a regular morphism of abstract varieties.

Proof. Suppose that 𝑓:𝑋→𝔸𝑘1(𝑘―) is a regular morphism. Evidently the identity map 1:𝔸𝑘1(𝑘―)→𝑘― is a regular function. Therefore by definition 𝑓:𝑋→𝑘― is a regular function.

Conversely if 𝑓:𝑋→𝑘― is regular then so is 𝑓|𝑈 for every affine open subset 𝑈⊂𝑋. By (6.1.29) and (6.1.39) this a regular morphism of abstract varieties 𝑈→𝔸𝑘1(𝑘―). By (4.3.27) we have 𝑓 is a regular morphism. ∎
