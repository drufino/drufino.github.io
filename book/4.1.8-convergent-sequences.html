4.1.8 Convergent Sequences

Proposition 4.1.49 (Convergent Sequence)

Let 𝑋 be a topological space, (𝑥𝑛) a sequence in 𝑋 and 𝑥∈𝑋. Let ℬ︀𝑥 be a local base for 𝑥. Then the following are equivalent

  1. For every 𝑉∈𝒰︀𝑥 there exists 𝑁 such that

    𝑛≥𝑁⟹𝑥𝑛∈𝑉

  2. For every 𝑉∈ℬ︀𝑥 there exists 𝑁 such that

    𝑛≥𝑁⟹𝑥𝑛∈𝑉

  3. The function

    ℕ∪{∞}→𝑋𝑛→𝑥𝑛∞→𝑥

    is continuous at ∞ with respect to the topology on ℕ∪{∞} given by open sets of the form {𝑁,𝑁+1,…,}∪{∞} and arbitrary subsets of ℕ.

In this case we say that 𝑥𝑛→𝑥.

Proposition 4.1.50 (Continuous ⟹ Sequentially Continuous)
Let 𝑓:𝑋→𝑌 be a continuous map and 𝑥𝑛→𝑥 a sequence in 𝑋. Then 𝑓(𝑥𝑛)→𝑓(𝑥).
Proof. If we regard the sequence as a continuous map ℕ∪∞ (4.1.49) then the result follows from (4.1.26). ∎
Proposition 4.1.51 (Limits are Unique in Kolmogorov Spaces)
Let 𝑋 be a Kolmogorov topological space. Then limits of sequences are unique.
Proposition 4.1.52 (Sequentially Continuous ⟹ Continuous)

Let 𝑋,𝑌 be topological spaces, 𝑓:𝑋→𝑌 a map and 𝑥∈𝑋 a point with a countable local base ℬ︀𝑥. Suppose that for all sequences 𝑥𝑛→𝑥 we have 𝑓(𝑥𝑛)→𝑓(𝑥). Then 𝑓 is continuous at 𝑥.

In particular if 𝑋 is first countable then 𝑓:𝑋→𝑌 is continuous iff it is sequentially continuous.

Proof. Consider a point 𝑥∈𝑋 and 𝑦≔𝑓(𝑥). By hypothesis ℬ︀𝑥={𝑈𝑛} is countable. By replacing with the base 𝑈𝑛′≔⋂𝑘=1𝑛𝑈𝑘 we may suppose that

𝑈1⊇𝑈2…⊇𝑈𝑛⊇…

is decreasing. Suppose 𝑓 is not continuous at 𝑥 then by (4.1.25).d) there exists 𝑉∈𝒰︀𝑓(𝑥) such that 𝑓(𝑈𝑛)∖𝑉≠∅ for all 𝑛. Therefore we may choose 𝑥𝑛∈𝑈𝑛 such that 𝑓(𝑥𝑛)∉𝑉. Consider 𝑈∈𝒰︀𝑥 then by definition there is some 𝑁 such that 𝑈𝑁⊆𝑈. Furthermore 𝑛≥𝑁⟹𝑈𝑛⊆𝑈𝑁⊆𝑈⟹𝑥𝑛∈𝑈. Therefore 𝑥𝑛→𝑥. By construction 𝑓(𝑥)∈𝑉 but 𝑓(𝑥𝑛)∉𝑉 for all 𝑛 and in particular 𝑓(𝑥𝑛)↛𝑓(𝑥). ∎
