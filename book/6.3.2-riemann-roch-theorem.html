6.3.2 Riemann-Roch Theorem

In what follows we generally assume that 𝑘̃=𝑘 (i.e. 𝐾 is geometrically integral).

Definition 6.3.1 (Divisors)

Let 𝐾/𝑘 be an algebraic function field. Denote by Div(𝐾) the free abelian group generated by the places of 𝐾/𝑘 (divisor group). The elements are written as a formal sum

𝐷=∑𝑃𝑛𝑃𝑃

with all but finitely many coefficients 𝑛𝑃=0. Define the degree homomorphism

Div(𝐾)⟶ℤ∑𝑃𝑛𝑃𝑃⟶∑𝑃𝑛𝑃⋅[𝑘(𝔪𝑃):𝑘]

We may also define 𝑣𝑃(𝐷)≔𝑛𝑃, and the support of 𝐷 by Supp(𝐷)≔{𝑃|𝑣𝑃(𝐷)≠0}, a finite set. Finally we may define a partial order

𝐷≤𝐷′⟺𝑣𝑃(𝐷)≤𝑣𝑃(𝐷′)∀𝑃
Definition 6.3.2 (Riemann-Roch Space)

Let 𝐾/𝑘 be an algebraic function field and 𝐷 a divisor. Define the Riemann-Roch Space associated to 𝐷 by

𝐿(𝐷)≔{𝑥∈𝐾⋆|(𝑥)≥−𝐷}∪{0}

and define

ℓ(𝐷)≔dim𝑘𝐿(𝐷)

(we shortly show it is a 𝑘-vector subspace of 𝐾)

Definition 6.3.3 (Principal Divisors)

Let 𝐾/𝑘 be an algebraic function field and 𝑥∈𝐾⋆. Then define the zero divisor (resp. pole divisor, principal divisor) of 𝑥 as follows

(𝑥)0≔∑𝑃:𝑣𝑃(𝑥)>0𝑣𝑃(𝑥)𝑃,(𝑥)∞≔∑𝑃:𝑣𝑃(𝑥)<0(−𝑣𝑃(𝑥))𝑃,(𝑥)≔(𝑥)0−(𝑥)∞=∑𝑃𝑣𝑃(𝑥)𝑃.

which is well-defined by (6.2.116).

Observe similarly 𝑥∈𝑘⋆⟺(𝑥)=0. Define the subgroup of principal divisors by

Princ(𝐾)≔{(𝑥)|𝑥∈𝐾}

and the quotient group Cl(𝐾)≔Div(𝐾)/Princ(𝐾) is called the divisor class group. We say two divisors 𝐷 and 𝐷′ are linearly equivalent if there exists 𝑥∈𝐾 such that 𝐷−𝐷′=(𝑥), equivalently if they become equal in the divisor class group. This is written 𝐷∼𝐷′.

We presently prove that these concepts are well-defined, namely every function 𝑥∈𝐾⋆ has only finitely many zeros. More precisely we can show

Proposition 6.3.4 (Principal Divisors have degree zero)

Let 𝐾/𝑘 be an algebraic function field and 𝑥∈𝐾∖𝑘̃ transcendental. Then

deg((𝑥)0)=deg((𝑥)∞)=[𝐾:𝑘(𝑥)]deg((𝑥))=0

In particular there is a well-defined homomorphism

deg:Cl(𝐾)⟶ℤ

Proof. Recall (6.2.114) 𝑘[𝑥] is a Dedekind domain, 𝑘[𝑥]― is a finite 𝑘[𝑥]-module and every place 𝒪︀𝑃 containing 𝑥 corresponds to prime ideal 𝔅 of 𝑘[𝑥]―. By (3.32.15) then 𝑣𝑃(𝑥) is equal to the ramification index 𝑒𝔅/(𝑥), and the inertial degree is 𝑓𝔅/(𝑥)=[𝑘(𝔪𝑃):𝑘] where (𝑥) is a prime ideal of 𝑘[𝑥]. From (3.35.61) we have the ramification formula

[𝐾:𝑘(𝑥)]=∑𝔅∣(𝑥)𝑒𝔅/(𝑥)𝑓𝔅/(𝑥)

Therefore deg((𝑥)0)=[𝐾:𝑘(𝑥)] and deg((𝑥)∞)=deg((𝑥−1)0)=[𝐾:𝑘(𝑥−1)]=[𝐾:𝑘(𝑥)]. ∎

Proposition 6.3.5

Let 𝐾/𝑘 be an algebraic function field and 𝐷 a divisor. Then

  1. 𝐿(𝐷) is a 𝑘-vector subspace of 𝐾
  2. If 𝐷∼𝐷′ then 𝐿(𝐷)=𝐿(𝐷′)
  3. 𝐿(0)=𝑘̃ so under our standing assumption ℓ(0)=1.
  4. If 𝐷<0 then 𝐿(𝐷)={0}

Proof.

  1. Given 𝑥,𝑦∈𝐿(𝐷) then by the strict triangle inequality for valuations 𝑣𝑃(𝑥+𝑦)≥min(𝑣𝑃(𝑥),𝑣𝑃(𝑦))≥𝑣𝑃(𝐷). Similarly for 𝜆∈𝑘 we have 𝑣𝑃(𝜆𝑥)=𝑣𝑃(𝜆)+𝑣𝑃(𝑥)=𝑣𝑃(𝑥) (6.2.114).

  2. By definition 𝐷′=𝐷+(𝑥) we may define a mapping

    𝐿(𝐷)⟶𝐿(𝐷′)𝑦⟶𝑦𝑥

    Then

    𝑣𝑃(𝑦𝑥)=𝑣𝑃(𝑥)+𝑣𝑃(𝑦)≥𝑣𝑃(𝑥)+𝑣𝑃(𝐷)=𝑣𝑃(𝐷′)

    so this is well-defined. As 𝑥−1 defines the inverse map it is a bijection.

  3. By (6.2.114).a) 𝑘̃⊂𝐿(0). Suppose 𝑥∈𝐾∖𝑘̃ then by definition 𝑥 is transcendental and so is 𝑥−1. By (6.2.116) 𝑥−1 has a zero 𝑃, i.e. 𝑣𝑃(𝑥−1)>0⟹𝑣𝑃(𝑥)<0. Therefore by definition 𝑥∉𝐿(0) as required.
  4. If 𝑥∈𝑘̃⋆ then (𝑥)=0 whence 𝑥∉𝐿(𝐷). If 𝑥∈𝐾∖𝑘̃ then as before 𝑥 has a pole therefore 𝑥∉𝐿(𝐷) as required.

 ∎

Lemma 6.3.6

Let 𝐾/𝑘 be an algebraic function field and 𝐷≤𝐷′ divisors. Then 𝐿(𝐷)⊂𝐿(𝐷′) and

dim𝑘𝐿(𝐷′)/𝐿(𝐷)≤deg(𝐷′)−deg(𝐷)

In particular ℓ(𝐷) is finite and we have

deg(𝐷)−ℓ(𝐷)≤deg(𝐷′)−ℓ(𝐷′)

Proof. Consider a divisor 𝐷 and suppose 𝐷′≔𝐷+𝑃 for some 𝑃 possibly in the support of 𝐷. Let 𝜋 be a uniformiser for 𝒪︀𝑃 and define 𝑡=𝜋𝑣𝑃(𝐷′). We then have a well-defined map

𝜓:𝐿(𝐷′)⟶𝑘(𝔪𝑃)𝑥⟶𝑥𝑡+𝔪𝑃

Observe that 𝜓(𝑥)=0⟺𝑥𝑡∈𝔪𝑃⟺𝑣𝑃(𝑥𝑡)>0⟺𝑣𝑃(𝑥)+𝑣𝑃(𝐷′)>0⟺𝑣𝑃(𝑥)≥−𝑣𝑃(𝐷)⟺𝑥∈𝐿(𝐷). Therefore ker(𝜓)=𝐿(𝐷) and so

dim𝑘(𝐿(𝐷′)/𝐿(𝐷))≤[𝑘(𝔪𝑃):𝑘]=deg(𝐷′)−deg(𝐷)

Now suppose 𝐷″=𝐷′+𝑄, then by the isomorphism theorem for vector spaces (…)

dim𝑘(𝐿(𝐷″)/𝐿(𝐷))=dim𝑘(𝐿(𝐷″)/𝐿(𝐷′))+dim𝑘(𝐿(𝐷′)/𝐿(𝐷))≤deg(𝐷″)−deg(𝐷′)+deg(𝐷′)−deg(𝐷)=deg(𝐷″)−deg(𝐷)

from which it follows the general case. ∎

Proposition 6.3.7 (Dimension Upper Bound)

Let 𝐾/𝑘 be an algebraic function field and 𝐷 a divisor. Then

ℓ(𝐷)≤deg(𝐷+)+dim𝑘𝑘̃

where 𝐷+ is the positive part of 𝐷.

Proof. 𝐿(𝐷)⊂𝐿(𝐷+) so it is enough to show the result for positive divisors. As 𝐿(0)=𝑘̃ (…) the result follows from (6.3.6). ∎

We say that the function deg(𝐷)−ℓ(𝐷) is increasing, we also show it is bounded above.

Lemma 6.3.8 (Dimension Lower Bound I)

Let 𝐾/𝑘 be an algebraic function field. There exists a positive divisor 𝐵 and integer 𝛾∈ℤ such that

deg(𝑚𝐵)−ℓ(𝑚𝐵)≤𝛾

for all positive integers 𝑚.

Proof. Let 𝑥∈𝐾 be transcendental and define 𝐵≔(𝑥)∞ with deg(𝐵)=𝑛. It’s positive by (…). Let 𝑢1,…,𝑢𝑛 be a 𝑘(𝑥)-basis for 𝐾 and a divisor 𝐶≥0 such that (𝑢𝑖)≥−𝐶. Then we claim that for all integers 𝑚 the elements {𝑥𝑖𝑢𝑗|0≤𝑖≤𝑚,1≤𝑗≤𝑛} are linearly independent over 𝑘. But this follows immediately because suppose

∑𝑖,𝑗𝜆𝑖𝑗𝑥𝑖𝑢𝑗=0

then

∑𝑗=1𝑛(∑𝑖=0𝑚𝜆𝑖𝑗𝑥𝑖)𝑢𝑗=0

and by linear independence we have 𝜆𝑖𝑗=0.

Further 𝑥𝑖∈𝐿(𝑖𝐵)⟹𝑥𝑖∈𝐿(𝑚𝐵) and 𝑢𝑗∈𝐿(𝐶)⟹𝑥𝑖𝑢𝑗∈𝐿(𝑚𝐵+𝐶). Therefore ℓ(𝑚𝐵+𝐶)≥𝑛(𝑚+1)=deg(𝑚𝐵)+𝑛 for all positive integers 𝑚.

On the other hand by (6.3.6) we have ℓ(𝑚𝐵+𝐶)≤ℓ(𝑚𝐵)+deg(𝐶). Therefore

deg(𝑚𝐵)−ℓ(𝑚𝐵)≤deg(𝐶)−𝑛

 ∎

We may extend this result to all divisors by way of the following lemma.

Lemma 6.3.9
Let 𝐾/𝑘 be an algebraic function field, 𝐵 a divisor satisfying the conclusion of (6.3.8) and 𝐷 an arbitrary divisor. Then there exists divisors 𝐷′,𝐷″ such that 𝐷≤𝐷′, 𝐷′∼𝐷″ and 𝐷″≤𝑚𝐵 for some 𝑚≥0.

Proof. Choose a positive divisor 𝐷′≥𝐷, then

deg(𝑚𝐵−𝐷′)−ℓ(𝑚𝐵−𝐷′)≤deg(𝑚𝐵)−ℓ(𝑚𝐵)≤𝛾

for all positive integers 𝑚. This means

ℓ(𝑚𝐵−𝐷′)≥𝑚deg(𝐵)−deg(𝐷′)−𝛾

which is positive for sufficiently large 𝑚. Therefore we may choose 0≠𝑧∈𝐿(𝑚𝐵−𝐷′). Let 𝐷″=𝐷′−(𝑧). By definition

(𝑧)≥𝐷′−𝑚𝐵⟹𝐷″≤𝑚𝐵

as required. ∎

Proposition 6.3.10 (Genus is well-defined)

Let 𝐾/𝑘 be an algebraic function field. Then there exists a positive integer 𝛾 such that

deg(𝐷)−ℓ(𝐷)≤𝛾

for all divisors 𝐷.

Proof. Then using the notation of (6.3.9) we have

deg(𝐷)−ℓ(𝐷)≤(6.3.6)deg(𝐷′)−ℓ(𝐷′)=(6.3.4)(6.3.5)deg(𝐷″)−ℓ(𝐷″)≤(6.3.6)deg(𝑚𝐵)−ℓ(𝑚𝐵)≤(6.3.8)𝛾

 ∎

Definition 6.3.11 (Genus of a Function Field)

Let 𝐾/𝑘 be an algebraic function field with 𝑘̃=𝑘. Define the genus by

𝑔≔max{deg(𝐷)−ℓ(𝐷)+1|𝐷∈Div(𝐾)}

Note as ℓ(0)=1 the genus 𝑔 is non-negative. For any divisor 𝐷∈Div(𝐾) define the index of specialty as follows

𝑖(𝐷)≔ℓ(𝐷)−deg(𝐷)+𝑔−1

which by definition is a non-negative integer. This satisfies a few key properties (6.3.6)

  1. 𝑖(0)=𝑔
  2. 𝑖(𝐷)≥0
  3. 𝐷∼𝐷′⟹𝑖(𝐷)=𝑖(𝐷′)
  4. 𝐷′≥𝐷⟹𝑖(𝐷′)≤𝑖(𝐷)

The Riemann-Roch theorem then takes the following form

ℓ(𝐷)−𝑖(𝐷)=deg(𝐷)−𝑔+1

We say that a divisor 𝐷 is non-special if 𝑖(𝐷)=0 (equivalently ℓ(𝐷)=deg(𝐷)−𝑔+1).

The full Riemann-Roch amounts to an explicit form of 𝑖(𝐷). In order to do this we identify it as the dimension of a certain vector space.

Definition 6.3.12 (Repartitions)

Let 𝐾/𝑘 be an algebraic function field. Define the 𝐾-algebra of repartitions of 𝐾 by

𝒜︀𝐾≔{(𝛼𝑃)𝑃∈ℙ𝐾|𝛼𝑃∈𝐾and𝛼𝑃∈𝒪︀𝑃for all but finitely many places𝑃}

For every divisor 𝐷∈Div(𝐾) define the 𝐾-subspace

𝒜︀𝐾(𝐷)≔{𝛼∈𝒜︀𝐾|𝑣𝑃(𝛼𝑃)≥−𝑣𝑃(𝐷)for all𝑃∈ℙ𝐾}

There is a canonical 𝐾-algebra homomorphism

𝐾↪︎𝒜︀𝐾

which is well-defined by (…). Then clearly we have 𝐾∩𝒜︀𝐾(𝐷)=𝐿(𝐷).

We prove a number of results

Lemma 6.3.13

Let 𝐾/𝑘 be an algebraic function field. For divisors 𝐷,𝐷′ such that 𝐷≤𝐷′ we have

  1. dim𝑘𝒜︀𝐾(𝐷′)/𝒜︀𝐾(𝐷)=deg(𝐷′)−deg(𝐷)
  2. dim𝑘((𝒜︀𝐾(𝐷′)+𝐾)/(𝒜︀𝐾(𝐷)+𝐾))=𝑖(𝐷)−𝑖(𝐷′)

Proof. We prove each in turn.

a) By the isomorphism theorem for 𝑘-modules it’s enough to demonstrate this for the case 𝐷′=𝐷+𝑃. Let 𝜋 be a uniformiser for 𝒪︀𝑃 and let 𝑟≔𝑣𝑃(𝐷)+1=𝑣𝑃(𝐷′). Then define the 𝑘-linear map

𝒜︀𝐾(𝐷′)⟶𝑘(𝔪𝑃)𝛼⟶(𝜋𝑟𝛼𝑃)(𝑃)

By definition we have 𝑣𝑃(𝛼𝑃)≥−𝑣𝑃(𝐷′)⟹𝑣𝑃(𝜋𝑟𝛼𝑃)≥0⟹𝜋𝑟𝛼𝑃∈𝒪︀𝑃 so that this map is well-defined. Further 𝜋𝑟𝛼𝑃∈𝔪𝑃⟺𝑣𝑃(𝜋𝑟𝛼𝑃)>0⟺𝑣𝑃(𝐷)+1+𝑣𝑃(𝛼𝑃)>0⟺𝑣𝑃(𝛼𝑃)≥−𝑣𝑃(𝐷) whence the kernel is precisely 𝒜︀𝐾(𝐷). It is clearly surjective because given 𝑓∈𝒪︀𝑃 we may consider the repartition given by

(1,…,1,𝜋−𝑟𝑓,1,…)

which clearly maps to 𝑓 under this map. Therefore by the isomorphism theorem we have

dim𝑘𝒜︀𝐾(𝐷′)/𝒜︀𝐾(𝐷)=[𝑘(𝔪𝑃):𝑘]=deg(𝐷′)−deg(𝐷)

b) We claim there is an exact sequence of 𝑘-modules

0⟶𝐿(𝐷′)/𝐿(𝐷)→𝜎1𝒜︀𝐾(𝐷′)/𝒜︀𝐾(𝐷)→𝜎2(𝒜︀𝐾(𝐷′)+𝐾)/(𝒜︀𝐾(𝐷)+𝐾)⟶0

from which the result follows immediately by comparing dimensions. The first map is given by

𝜎1([𝑓])≔(𝑣𝑃(𝑓))𝑃∈ℙ𝐾

which is obviously well-defined and injective. The map 𝜎2 is clearly well-defined and surjective. Further it is evident that 𝜎2∘𝜎1=0. Therefore it only remains to show that ker(𝜎2)⊂im(𝜎1). Suppose 𝛼∈𝒜︀𝐾(𝐷′) then 𝜎2(𝛼)=0⟹𝛼−(𝑓)∈𝒜︀𝐾(𝐷)⟹(𝑓)∈𝒜︀𝐾(𝐷′) for some 𝑓∈𝐾. Then by definition this means 𝑓∈𝐿(𝐷′) and 𝜎1(𝑓)=𝛼 as required. ∎

Proposition 6.3.14 (Riemann's Theorem)

Let 𝐾/𝑘 be an algebraic function field. Suppose 𝐷0 is non-special (there always exists such a divisor) then

  1. deg(𝐷)>ℓ(𝐷0)⟹𝐷 is non-special
  2. 𝒜︀𝐾=𝒜︀𝐾(𝐷0)+𝐾

Proof. We prove each in turn

  1. For an arbitrary divisor 𝐷 we have

    ℓ(𝐷−𝐷0)=𝑖(𝐷−𝐷0)+deg(𝐷−𝐷0)−𝑔+1≥0+deg(𝐷)−ℓ(𝐷0)

    So when deg(𝐷)>ℓ(𝐷0) we have ℓ(𝐷−𝐷0)>0. Choose 0≠𝑓∈𝐿(𝐷−𝐷0). Then we may define 𝐷′≔𝐷+(𝑓)≥𝐷0. Then by (…) 𝑖(𝐷)=𝑖(𝐷′)≤𝑖(𝐷0)=0 whence 𝑖(𝐷)=0.

  2. Given 𝛼∈𝒜︀𝐾 choose 𝐷≥𝐷0 such that 𝛼∈𝒜︀𝐾(𝐷). Then by (6.3.11) 𝑖(𝐷)≤𝑖(𝐷0)=0 and therefore 𝑖(𝐷)=0. By (6.3.13).b) 𝒜︀𝐾(𝐷)+𝐾=𝒜︀𝐾(𝐷0)+𝐾⟹𝛼∈𝒜︀𝐾(𝐷0)+𝐾 as required.

 ∎

Proposition 6.3.15 (Explicit form of 𝑖(𝐷))

Let 𝐾/𝑘 be an algebraic function field and 𝐷∈Div(𝐾). Then

𝑖(𝐷)=dim𝑘(𝒜︀𝐾/(𝒜︀𝐾(𝐷)+𝐾))
Proof. By (6.3.14) there exists some non-special divisor 𝐷′≥𝐷 such that 𝑖(𝐷′)=0 and 𝒜︀𝐾=𝒜︀𝐾(𝐷′)+𝐾. The formula then follows from (6.3.13).b). ∎
Definition 6.3.16 (Weil Divisors)

Let 𝐾/𝑘 be an algebraic function field and 𝐷∈Div(𝐾). Define

Ω𝐾(𝐷)≔{𝜔∈Hom𝑘(𝒜︀𝐾,𝑘)|𝒜︀𝐾(𝐷)+𝐾⊂ker(𝜔)}

As divisors form a directed system we may form the union of Weil Divisors

Ω𝐾≔⋃𝐷∈Div(𝐾)Ω𝐾(𝐷)⊂Hom𝑘(𝒜︀𝐾,𝑘)
Proposition 6.3.17

Let 𝐾/𝑘 be an algebraic function field. Then there is a canonical isomorphism

Hom𝑘(𝒜︀𝐾/(𝒜︀𝐾(𝐷)+𝐾),𝑘)⟶∼Ω𝐾(𝐷)

In particular 𝑖(𝐷)=dim𝑘Ω𝐾(𝐷). Taking 𝐷=0, since 𝑖(0)=𝑔 (6.3.11) we get

dim𝑘Ω𝐾(0)=𝑔

that is, the genus equals the dimension of the space of everywhere-regular Weil differentials.

Proof. This is just the isomorphism theorem for vector spaces. The statement on dimensions follows from (6.3.15) and (3.3.108). ∎
