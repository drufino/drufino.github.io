2.6.3 Product Categories and Bifunctors

Definition 2.6.18 (Product Category)

Given two categories 𝒞︀, 𝒟︀ we may construct the product category 𝒞︀×𝒟︀ as follows

  • The objects are given by pairs

    ob(𝒞︀×𝒟︀)≔ob(𝒞︀)×ob(𝒟︀)

  • The morphisms are given by pairs

    Mor((𝐶,𝐶′),(𝐷,𝐷′))≔Mor(𝐶,𝐶′)×Mor(𝐷,𝐷′)

    Concretely given 𝑓:𝐶→𝐶′ and 𝑔:𝐷→𝐷′ write (𝑓,𝑔):(𝐶,𝐷)→(𝐶′,𝐷′)

  • The law of composition is determined by

    (𝑓,𝑔)∘(ℎ,𝑘)≔(𝑓∘ℎ,𝑔∘𝑘)

It’s clear that the law of composition is associative and the identity morphism for (𝐶,𝐷) is (1𝐶,1𝐷). Furthermore we observe the following property

Proposition 2.6.20 (Reconstruct Bifunctor)

Consider a family of functors {𝐹𝐿(𝐶):𝒟︀→ℰ︀}𝐶∈ob(𝒞︀) and {𝐹𝑅(𝐷):𝒞︀→ℰ︀}𝐷∈ob(𝒟︀). Suppose the following conditions hold

  • 𝐹𝐿(𝐶)(𝐷)=𝐹𝑅(𝐷)(𝐶)
  • 𝐹𝐿(𝐶′)(𝑔)∘𝐹𝑅(𝐷)(𝑓)=𝐹𝑅(𝐷′)(𝑓)∘𝐹𝐿(𝐶)(𝑔)

𝐹(𝑓×𝑔)≔𝐹𝐿(𝐶′)(𝑔)∘𝐹𝑅(𝐷)(𝑓)
Proposition 2.6.21

Let 𝐹:𝒞︀×𝒟︀→ℰ︀ be a bifunctor. For 𝑓:𝐶→𝐶′ then there is a natural transformation

𝐹(𝑓,−):𝐹(𝐶,−)⇒𝐹(𝐶′,−)

given by 𝐹(𝑓,−)𝐷≔𝐹(𝑓,1𝐷). Similarly for 𝑔:𝐷→𝐷′ then there is a natural transformation

𝐹(−,𝑔):𝐹(−,𝐷)⇒𝐹(−,𝐷′)
Proof. The naturality condition is immediate from (*) and the commutative diagram in (2.6.19). ∎

Example 2.6.23 (Mor is a bifunctor)

The canonical example is the following, given any locally small category 𝒞︀, we have a bifunctor

Mor:𝒞︀op×𝒞︀→𝐒𝐞𝐭

given by the set of morphisms. The action on morphisms is given by

Mor(𝑓,𝑔)(𝜙)=𝑔∘𝜙∘𝑓

and we verify by associativity of 𝒞︀ that

Mor((𝑓,𝑔)∘(ℎ,𝑘))=Mor(𝑓,𝑔)∘Mor(ℎ,𝑘)

and it’s clear that the commutativity condition in (*) is satisfied.
