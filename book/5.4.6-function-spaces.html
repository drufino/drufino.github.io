5.4.6 Function Spaces

Definition 5.4.34 (Uniform Convergence)

Let 𝑆 be a metric space and 𝑌 a normed vector space. Suppose {𝑓𝑛:𝑆→𝑌} is a sequence of functions and 𝑓:𝑆→𝑌 is a function. We say that 𝑓𝑛→𝑓 converges uniformly if

∀𝜀>0∃𝑁s.t.𝑛≥𝑁⟹‖𝑓𝑛(𝑥)−𝑓(𝑥)‖<𝜀∀𝑥∈𝑆

Note this implies, but is strictly stronger than, pointwise convergence that is 𝑓𝑛(𝑥)→𝑓(𝑥) for all 𝑥∈𝑆, as 𝑁 may be chosen independently of 𝑥.

Proposition 5.4.35 (Uniform Limit of Continuous Functions is Continuous)
Let 𝑆 be a metric space and 𝑌 a normed vector space. Suppose that {𝑓𝑛:𝑆→𝑌} is a sequence of continuous functions converging uniformly to a limit 𝑓:𝑆→𝑌. Then 𝑓 is continuous.

Proof. Fix 𝜀>0. There exists 𝑁 such that

𝑛≥𝑁⟹‖𝑓𝑛(𝑥)−𝑓(𝑥)‖<𝜀∀𝑥∈𝑆

Fix 𝑥∈𝑆 then there exists 𝛿>0 such that

‖𝑦−𝑥‖<𝛿⟹‖𝑓𝑛(𝑦)−𝑓𝑛(𝑥)‖<𝜀

Therefore

‖𝑦−𝑥‖<𝛿⟹‖𝑓(𝑥)−𝑓(𝑦)‖≤‖𝑓(𝑥)−𝑓𝑛(𝑥)‖+‖𝑓𝑛(𝑥)−𝑓𝑛(𝑦)‖+‖𝑓(𝑦)−𝑓𝑛(𝑦)‖<3𝜀

Consequently 𝑓 is also continuous. ∎

Definition 5.4.36 (Bounded Function Space)

Let 𝑆 be a metric space and 𝑌 a normed vector space. Define the vector space of bounded functions by

ℬ︀(𝑆,𝑌)≔{𝑓:𝑆→𝑌|∃𝐶>0s.t.‖𝑓(𝑥)‖≤𝐶for all𝑥∈𝑆}

This is a normed vector space under the sup norm given by

‖𝑓‖∞≔sup𝑥∈𝑆‖𝑓(𝑥)‖

Evidently convergence in this norm is equivalent to uniform convergence.

Proposition 5.4.37 (Bounded Functions are Complete)
Suppose 𝑌 is a Banach Space, then ℬ︀(𝑆,𝑌) is also a Banach Space.

Proof. Let {𝑓𝑛} be a cauchy sequence of maps 𝑆→𝑌 with respect to the sup norm. Then for every 𝜀>0 there exists 𝑁 such that

𝑛,𝑚≥𝑁⟹‖𝑓𝑛−𝑓𝑚‖<𝜀

In particular for every 𝑥∈𝑆 we have {𝑓𝑛(𝑥)} is cauchy. As 𝑌 is complete it is a convergent sequence, for which we denote the limit by 𝑓(𝑥). For every 𝑥∈𝑆 we may also choose some 𝑚≥𝑁 such that

‖𝑓𝑚(𝑥)−𝑓(𝑥)‖<𝜀

then

‖𝑓(𝑥)−𝑓𝑛(𝑥)‖≤‖𝑓(𝑥)−𝑓𝑚(𝑥)‖+‖𝑓𝑚(𝑥)−𝑓𝑛(𝑥)‖<2𝜀

As this inequality is independent of 𝑚 we find that ‖𝑓−𝑓𝑛‖→0 as required. We may also show easily that it is bounded. ∎
