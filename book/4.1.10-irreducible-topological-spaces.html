Proposition 4.1.59 (Irreducible space)

Let 𝑋 be a topological space. Then the following are equivalent

  1. 𝑋=𝑍1∪𝑍2 closed implies either 𝑍1=𝑋 or 𝑍2=𝑋
  2. 𝑈,𝑉≠∅⟹𝑈∩𝑉≠∅ for open sets 𝑈,𝑉
  3. 𝑈≠∅⟹𝑈―=𝑋 i.e. every non-empty open set is dense

and we say 𝑋 is irreducible.

Proof. 𝑎)⟹𝑏) Suppose 𝑈,𝑉 are open sets such that 𝑈∩𝑉=∅. Then 𝑋=(𝑋∖𝑈)∪(𝑋∖𝑉). By hypothesis 𝑋=(𝑋∖𝑈) or 𝑋=(𝑋∖𝑉) whence either 𝑈 or 𝑉 is empty.

𝑏)⟹𝑎) Similar.

𝑐)⟺𝑏) Follows directly from (4.1.24) ∎

Proposition 4.1.60 (Irreducible Subset)

Let 𝑌⊂𝑋 be a subset of a topological space. Then the following conditions on 𝑌 are equivalent

  1. 𝑌 is irreducible in the subspace topology.
  2. 𝑌⊆𝑍1∪𝑍2⟹𝑌⊆𝑍1 or 𝑌⊆𝑍2 where 𝑍1,𝑍2 are closed subsets of 𝑋
  3. 𝑈∩𝑌≠∅,𝑉∩𝑌≠∅⟹(𝑈∩𝑉)∩𝑌≠∅ for 𝑈,𝑉 open

and we say 𝑌 is an irreducible subset.

Proof. 𝑎)⟹𝑏). Suppose that 𝑌 is irreducible in the subspace topology and 𝑌⊆𝑍1∪𝑍2. This implies 𝑌=(𝑍1∩𝑌)∪(𝑍2∩𝑌) is a decomposition of closed sets. So either 𝑍1∩𝑌=𝑌 or 𝑍2∩𝑌=𝑌⟹𝑌⊆𝑍1or𝑌⊆𝑍2 as required.

𝑏)⟹𝑎). Suppose that 𝑌=(𝑍1∩𝑌)∪(𝑍2∩𝑌). Then 𝑌⊆𝑍1∪𝑍2, and for example 𝑌⊆𝑍1, which implies 𝑍1∩𝑌=𝑌. ∎

Proposition 4.1.61

Let 𝑌⊂𝑋 be a closed subset then the following are equivalent

  1. 𝑌 is an irreducible subset
  2. 𝑌=𝑍1∪𝑍2⟹𝑌=𝑍1 or 𝑌=𝑍2 where 𝑍1,𝑍2 are closed subsets of 𝑋
  3. 𝑌⊆𝑍1∪𝑍2⟹𝑌⊆𝑍1 or 𝑌⊆𝑍2 where 𝑍1,𝑍2 are closed subsets of 𝑋

In other words in the lattice of closed subsets, the irreducible subsets are precisely the join-prime subsets.

Proof. 𝑎)⟹𝑏). Clearly 𝑍1,𝑍2 are also closed subsets of 𝑌, so the result follows by definition.

𝑏)⟺𝑐). This is (2.4.2).

𝑐)⟹𝑎). This was already proven in (4.1.60). ∎

Remark 4.1.62
Singletons {𝑥} are always irreducible.
Definition 4.1.63 (Irreducible Component)
We say that 𝑌 is an irreducible component if it is a maximal irreducible subset.
Proposition 4.1.64
Let 𝑓:𝑋→𝑌 be a continuous map and 𝑍⊂𝑋 irreducible. Then 𝑓(𝑍) is irreducible in 𝑌.
Proof. Suppose 𝑓(𝑍)⊂𝑌1∪𝑌2 for 𝑌1,𝑌2 closed. Then 𝑍⊂𝑓−1(𝑓(𝑍))⊂𝑓−1(𝑌1)∪𝑓−1(𝑌2). Then by (4.1.60).b) wlog 𝑍⊂𝑓−1(𝑌1) therefore 𝑓(𝑍)⊂𝑓(𝑓−1(𝑌1))⊂𝑌1. Therefore by the same criterion 𝑓(𝑍) is irreducible. ∎
Proposition 4.1.65
Let 𝑌⊂𝑋. Then 𝑌 is irreducible iff 𝑌― is.
Proof. This follows from (4.1.60).b) and (4.1.19) that 𝑌⊂𝑍⟺𝑌―⊂𝑍. ∎
Proposition 4.1.66 (Decomposition into Irreducible Components)

A topological space 𝑋 may be decomposed into irreducible components. More precisely

  1. Every irreducible component is closed
  2. Every irreducible closed subset is contained in an irreducible component
  3. 𝑋 is the union of irreducible components

Proof. We prove each in turn

  1. By (4.1.65) 𝑌― is irreducible and closed, so by maximality 𝑌=𝑌― is closed.
  2. We may show that the lattice of closed subsets is chain complete so we may use to show that the lattice of irreducible closed subsets is also chain complete (2.4.4). Therefore we may apply (2.1.56) to find an irreducible component.
  3. As {𝑥} is irreducible every element is contained in an irreducible component by 𝑏).

 ∎

Corollary 4.1.67
For 𝑥∈𝑋 the closure {𝑥}― is an irreducible closed subset.
Corollary 4.1.68
𝑋 is irreducible if and only if it has a single irreducible component.
Definition 4.1.69 (Generic Point)
Let 𝑍 be an irreducible closed subset of 𝑋, then we say 𝜂∈𝑋 is a generic point of 𝑍 if 𝑍={𝜂}―.
Proposition 4.1.70
Let 𝑋 be an irreducible space then every open subset 𝑈 is irreducible and connected.

Proof. By (4.1.59) 𝑈 is dense in 𝑋. Therefore by (4.1.65) 𝑈 is irreducible.

Suppose that 𝑈 is not connected then by (4.1.54) 𝑈=(𝑈∩𝑉)⊔(𝑈∩𝑊) with 𝑉,𝑊 open subsets of 𝑋 and 𝑈∩𝑉,𝑈∩𝑊 non-empty. Then as 𝑈 is irreducible we have 𝑈∩𝑉∩𝑊≠∅, a contradiction. ∎

Proposition 4.1.71
Let 𝑋=⋃𝑖=1𝑛𝑋𝑖 where 𝑋𝑖 are irreducible closed subsets, and mutually incomparable. Then the 𝑋𝑖 are the irreducible components of 𝑋.
Proof. For any irreducible closed subset 𝐸 we have by (4.1.61) 𝐸⊂𝑋𝑖 for some 𝑖. In particular every irreducible component is contained in some 𝑋𝑖. On the other hand every 𝑋𝑗 is contained in an irreducible component by (4.1.66). By incomparability we deduce that each 𝑋𝑖 is an irreducible component, and that the set is complete. ∎
Proposition 4.1.72
Let 𝑌⊂𝑋 such that 𝑌=⋃𝑖=1𝑛𝑌𝑖 where 𝑌𝑖 are the irreducible components of 𝑌. Then the set of irreducible components of 𝑌― is {𝑌𝑖―}𝑖=1…𝑛, and these are distinct.
Proof. By (4.1.65) 𝑌𝑖― are irreducible subsets which, by (4.1.23) cover 𝑌―. By (4.1.66) 𝑌𝑖 is closed in 𝑌 and so by (4.1.21) 𝑌𝑖―∩𝑌=𝑌𝑖. This shows that the 𝑌𝑖― are distinct and also incomparable. Then (4.1.71) shows that these must be the irreducible components. ∎
Proposition 4.1.73

Let 𝑈⊂𝑋 be an open subset. There is an order isomorphism

{𝑌⊂𝑈|𝑌irreducible, closed and non-empty}⟷{𝑍⊂𝑋|𝑍irreducible and closed and𝑍∩𝑈≠∅}𝑌→cl𝑋(𝑌)𝑍∩𝑈←𝑍

which restricts to irreducible components.

Proof. By (4.1.65) the first map is well-defined. Conversely 𝑍∩𝑈 is closed in 𝑈 and open in 𝑍, so irreducible by (4.1.70).

By (4.1.59) 𝑍∩𝑈 is dense in 𝑍, that is 𝑍=cl𝑍(𝑍∩𝑈)=(4.1.21)cl𝑋(𝑍∩𝑈)∩𝑍⟹𝑍⊂cl𝑋(𝑍∩𝑈), and so by minimality they are equal.

By (4.1.21) cl𝑋(𝑌)∩𝑈=cl𝑈(𝑌)=𝑌. Therefore the maps are mutually inverse and order preserving. ∎

Proposition 4.1.74

Suppose 𝑋 is a topological space with open cover ⋃𝑖∈𝐼𝑈𝑖 such that

  1. 𝑈𝑖 is irreducible for all 𝑖∈𝐼 and
  2. 𝑈𝑖∩𝑈𝑗 is non-empty for all 𝑖,𝑗∈𝐼.

Then 𝑋 is irreducible.

Proof. Suppose that 𝑋=𝑍1∪𝑍2. Pick some 𝑖∈𝐼 then by (4.1.60) wlog 𝑈𝑖∩𝑍1≠∅. Similarly for all 𝑗∈𝐽 by assumption 𝑈𝑖∩𝑈𝑗 is a non-empty open subset of 𝑈𝑗 and therefore dense (4.1.59). Further 𝑈𝑖∩𝑈𝑗 is contained in the closed subset 𝑍1∩𝑈𝑗. We conclude that 𝑈𝑗⊂𝑍1∩𝑈𝑗. Therefore 𝑋⊂𝑍1. By (4.1.60) we further conclude that 𝑋 is irreducible. ∎
