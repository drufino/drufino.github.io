3.19.1 Prime Fields

Recall that for 𝑝 prime the quotient ring ℤ/𝑝ℤ is a field (3.16.3), (3.17.22), (3.3.58).

Definition 3.19.1 (Finite field of order 𝑝)
Denote by 𝔽𝑝 the field ℤ/𝑝ℤ of order 𝑝.
Definition 3.19.2 (Rational Integers)
We denote by ℚ the field of rational numbers defined to be the quotient field of the integers, Frac(ℤ) (see (3.6.6)).
Definition 3.19.3 (Prime Field)

We say that a field 𝑘 is a prime field if it is isomorphic to one of 𝔽𝑝 or ℚ.

Note none of these fields are mutually isomorphic by considering cardinalities.

Proposition 3.19.4 (Prime Subfield Exists)
Let 𝑘 be a field. Then 𝑘 contains a prime subfield. It is the smallest subfield contained in 𝑘.

Proof. By (…) there is a unique ring homomorphism

As 𝑘 is not the zero-ring then 𝜙(1)=1≠0 and ker(𝜙) is a proper ideal. Then Im(𝜙) is an integral domain by (3.3.9). By (3.3.56) ℤ/ker(𝜙)≅Im(𝜙) and therefore by (3.3.58) ker(𝜙)≕𝔭 is a prime ideal.

By (3.16.3) the ideal 𝔭 is principal. Suppose 𝔭=(0). By (3.6.5) 𝜙 extends to a homomorphism 𝜙′:ℚ→𝑘 whose kernel is zero and therefore injective (…). In particular Im(𝜙′) is a prime subfield being isomorphic to ℚ.

Otherwise by (3.17.9) 𝔭=(𝑝) for 𝑝 a prime number and we have already observed that 𝔽𝑝=ℤ/𝑝ℤ≅Im(𝜙) is a prime subfield.

Let 𝑘′ be any subfield of 𝑘, then by induction we may show that Im(𝜙)⊂𝑘′. In the case ker(𝜙)={0} then we may also show Im(𝜙′)⊂𝑘′. Therefore 𝑘′ contains the prime subfield, whence it is the smallest subfield. ∎
