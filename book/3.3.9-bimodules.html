3.3.9 Bimodules

For applications it is often useful to have multiple rings acting on a single module (“bimodule”). For example an 𝐴-algebra 𝐵 naturally has an action from both 𝐴 and 𝐵. It may also allow us to generalise results which would otherwise only hold in the commutative case.

Definition 3.3.76 (Bimodule)

Let 𝐴,𝐵 be rings. We say an abelian group 𝑀 is a (𝐴,𝐵)-bimodule if it is both a left 𝐴-module and a right 𝐵-module for which the two actions commute

𝑎⋅(𝑚⋅𝑏)=(𝑎⋅𝑚)⋅𝑏∀𝑎∈𝐴,𝑏∈𝐵,𝑚∈𝑀

We may denote this by 𝑀𝐵𝐴 in order to emphasise the actions. If homomorphisms are defined in the obvious way then these constitute a category 𝐌𝐨𝐝𝐵𝐴. Recall that every abelian group is automatically both a left and right ℤ-module with the following action for 𝑁∈ℤ

Therefore we have the following generalisations

  • A right 𝐵-module is precisely a (ℤ,𝐵)-bimodule
  • A left 𝐴-module is precisely a (𝐴,ℤ)-bimodule

  • When 𝐴 is commutative an 𝐴-module has a well-defined (𝐴,𝐴)-bimodule structure by defining

    𝑎⋅𝑚⋅𝑎′≔𝑎′𝑎⋅𝑚=𝑎𝑎′⋅𝑚

    in other words 𝐴-𝐌𝐨𝐝 is (equivalent to) a full subcategory of 𝐌𝐨𝐝𝐴𝐴.

  • A ring 𝐴 has an obvious (𝐴,𝐴)-bimodule structure by associativity of the multiplication operation

We will understand by the notation 𝑁𝐵 that 𝑁 is a (ℤ,𝐵)-bimodule and by {}𝐴𝑀 that 𝑀 is a (𝐴,ℤ)-bimodule.

We generalize slightly the consideration of the third bullet point

Proposition 3.3.77 ((𝐴,𝐴)-Bimodule ≡ 𝐴-module)

Let 𝜑:𝐴→𝐵 be a homomorphism of commutative rings and 𝑍 a 𝐵-module. Then 𝑍 is naturally a (𝐵,𝐴)-module with the following action

𝑏⋅𝑚⋅𝑎≔𝜑(𝑎)𝑏⋅𝑚(⋆)∀𝑎∈𝐴,𝑏∈𝐵,𝑚∈𝑍

Suppose 𝑀 is another (𝐵,𝐴)-bimodule satisfying (⋆) then we may identify the (𝐵,𝐴)-bimodule homomorphisms and the left 𝐵-module homomorphisms.

Hom(𝑀,𝑍𝐴𝐵)⟶∼Hom(𝑀𝐵,𝑍𝐵)

In particular when 𝜑=1𝐴 and 𝐵=𝐴 we may identify (𝐴,𝐴)-bimodules and 𝐴-modules, as well as the corresponding homomorphisms. Explicitly for 𝑀,𝑍 𝐴-modules these have well-defined (𝐴,𝐴)-bimodule structures and there is a bijection

Hom(𝑀𝐴𝐴,𝑍𝐴𝐴)⟶∼Hom(𝑀,𝑍)

Proof. We may show that the right 𝐴-module structure on 𝑍 is well-defined because 𝐵 is commutative and using the associativity of the 𝐵-module action. Similarly the associativity of the 𝐵-module action ensures that the actions commute and so form a (𝐵,𝐴)-bimodule structure.

Suppose 𝜃:𝑀→𝑍 is a (left) 𝐵-module homomorphism. Then

𝜃(𝑚⋅𝑎)=𝜃(𝜑(𝑎)⋅𝑚)=𝜑(𝑎)𝜃(𝑚)=𝜃(𝑚)⋅𝑎

so it is (𝐵,𝐴)-bilinear as required. The converse is clear, so we see that the sets are equal as subsets of the set of abelian group homomorphisms.

The case 𝜑=1𝐴 is immediate. ∎

We generalize the notion of “Hom-Set”

Definition 3.3.78 (Hom Functors)

Let 𝐴,𝐵,𝐶 be arbitrary rings. Then we have an enriched hom functor for 𝐌𝐨𝐝𝐵𝐴

Hom:𝐌𝐨𝐝𝐵op𝐴×𝐌𝐨𝐝𝐵𝐴→𝐀𝐛𝐆𝐫𝐩

In order to generalise the Tensor-Hom adjunction we consider the following functors (“right-linear” and “left-linear” respectively)

RHom:𝐌𝐨𝐝𝐴op𝐵×𝐌𝐨𝐝𝐴𝐶→𝐌𝐨𝐝𝐵𝐶LHom:𝐌𝐨𝐝𝐵op𝐴×𝐌𝐨𝐝𝐶𝐴→𝐌𝐨𝐝𝐶𝐵

For example if 𝜓∈RHom(𝑀𝐶𝐵,𝑁𝐶𝐴) then the (𝐴,𝐵)-bimodule action is defined to be

(𝑎𝜓𝑏)(𝑚)≔𝑎𝜓(𝑏𝑚)

and similarly if 𝜑∈LHom(𝑀𝐵𝐴,𝑁𝐶𝐴) then

(𝑏𝜑𝑐)(𝑚)≔𝜑(𝑚𝑏)𝑐

which we may verify satisfies the axioms of a bimodule. In order to standardize notation we may write Hom𝐴 in place of RHom and LHom.

If 𝐵=𝐶=ℤ then we recover the simple case (3.3.25).

If 𝐴 is a commutative ring then we’ve observed that 𝐴-𝐌𝐨𝐝 is a full subcategory of 𝐌𝐨𝐝𝐴𝐴 and the functors RHom, LHom become equal to the simple case (3.3.25) when restricted to this subcategory.
