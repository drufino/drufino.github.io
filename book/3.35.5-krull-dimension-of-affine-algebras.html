3.35.5 Krull Dimension of Affine Algebras

The Krull Dimension of affine domains is particularly well-behaved. Specifically they are biequidimensional and therefore satisfy a co-dimension formula (3.35.35). Further there’s a geometric proof of the “Hauptidealsatz” (3.35.32). We first show that it is equal to transcendence degree in the integral case.

Proposition 3.35.28

Let 𝐴 be an affine algebra with normalising family 𝑥1,…,𝑥𝑛, then dim𝐴=𝑛.

In particular every affine domain 𝐴 satisfies dim𝐴=trdeg(Frac(𝐴)/𝑘), and the polynomial ring 𝑘[𝑋1,…,𝑋𝑛] has dimension 𝑛.

Proof. By definition 𝐴 is integral over 𝑘[𝑥1,…,𝑥𝑛], which is isomorphic to a polynomial ring so we may reduce to the case of polynomial ring by (3.24.22).

dim𝐴≥𝑛). This is clear by considering the chain of prime ideals

(𝑋1)⊊(𝑋1,𝑋2)⊊…⊊(𝑋1,…,𝑋𝑛)

dim𝐴≤𝑛). We may argue by the Strong Normalisation Lemma (3.35.12) and the subsequent remark that any chain of prime ideals must have length at most 𝑛, as any normalising family has order 𝑛.

Alternatively we may proceed by induction on 𝑛 to show dim𝑘[𝑋1,…,𝑋𝑛]=𝑛. Consider a maximal chain

𝔭0⊊𝔭1⊊…⊊𝔭𝑚

Clearly 𝔭0=0, and 𝔭1=(𝑓) principal by (3.22.5). By (3.35.9) there is an integral, injective map

𝑘[𝑌1,…,𝑌𝑛−1]↪︎𝑘[𝑋1,…,𝑋𝑛]/(𝑓)

whence dim(𝔭1)=dim(𝑘[𝑋1,…,𝑋𝑛]/(𝑓))=dim(𝑘[𝑌1,…,𝑌𝑛−1])=𝑛−1, and by definition 𝑚−1≤𝑛−1. As the maximal chain was arbitrarily chosen, we have dim𝑘[𝑋1,…,𝑋𝑛]≤𝑛. The reverse inequality was already shown so we are done.

The final statement follows from the existence of a normalising family (3.35.11) and (3.35.7) ∎

Corollary 3.35.29
Let 𝐴 be a finitely-generated 𝑘-algebra. Then dim𝐴[𝑇]=dim𝐴+1.

Proof. By (3.35.11) there is an injective integral ring homomorphism

𝜙:𝑘[𝑋1,…,𝑋𝑛]↪︎𝐴

where dim𝐴=𝑛. By (3.8.5) there is a ring homomorphism

𝑘[𝑋1,…,𝑋𝑛][𝑇]↪︎𝐴[𝑇]

which is injective. Further by assumption 𝐴=𝑘[𝑦1,…,𝑦𝑟] so 𝐴[𝑇]=𝑘[𝑦1,…,𝑦𝑟,𝑇] and by (3.24.4) the ring homomorphism is integral. This shows that 𝜙(𝑥1),…,𝜙(𝑥𝑛),𝑇 is a normalising family and the result follows from (3.35.28). ∎

The ideal (𝑋1,…,𝑋𝑟)◃𝑘[𝑋1,…,𝑋𝑛] has dimension 𝑛−𝑟.
Corollary 3.35.31
Let 𝐴 be an integral finitely-generated 𝑘-algebra and 0≠𝑓 then dim𝐴=dim𝐴𝑓

The following proof is due to Tate, and presented in the Red Book [Mum99] (I.7 Theorem 2).

Proposition 3.35.32 (Hypersurface has pure codimension 1)

Let 𝐴 be an affine domain of dimension 𝑛 and 0≠𝑓∈𝐴. Then

dim((𝑓))=𝑛−1ht((𝑓))=1

More precisely if 𝔭 is minimal over (𝑓) then it has dimension 𝑛−1 and height 1.

Proof. Consider the case 𝐴=𝑘[𝑋1,…,𝑋𝑛]. Suppose first that 𝑓 is prime, then 𝔭=(𝑓) and by (3.35.9) there is an integral injective map

𝑘[𝑌1,…,𝑌𝑛−1]↪︎𝐴/(𝑓)

Therefore

dim((𝑓))=dim(𝐴/(𝑓))=(3.24.22)dim(𝑘[𝑌1,…,𝑌𝑛−1])=(3.35.28)𝑛−1

When 𝑓 is not prime then, as 𝑘[𝑋1,…,𝑋𝑛] is a UFD, we have a prime factorisation

𝑓=∏𝑖=1𝑛𝑓𝑖𝑚𝑖

and the minimal prime decomposition is

(𝑓)=(𝑓1)∩…∩(𝑓𝑛)

In particular any prime minimal over (𝑓) has the form 𝔭=(𝑓𝑖) for some 𝑖, and we may reduce to the case of 𝑓 prime.

For an arbitrary 𝑘-algebra 𝐴 we have a decomposition into minimal primes of (𝑓)

(𝑓)=𝔭1∩…∩𝔭𝑛

and without loss of generality 𝔭=𝔭1. We may localize to the case of a single prime, for choose 𝑔∉𝔭 and 𝑔∈𝔭𝑖 for 𝑖=2…𝑛. Consider the localization 𝐴→𝐴𝑔, then we claim that

(𝑓/1)=𝔭𝐴𝑔

For by (3.6.19) there is a correspondence between primes of 𝐴𝑔 containing (𝑓/1) and primes of 𝐴 containing 𝑓 and not 𝑔, which are precisely the primes containing 𝔭 by (3.3.39) or (3.3.43). Therefore 𝔭𝐴𝑔 is the only minimal prime of 𝐴𝑔 containing (𝑓/1) and the claim follows from (3.3.45).

Note that dim𝐴=dim𝐴𝑔 as they have the same quotient field and therefore transcendence degree. Similarly dim(𝐴/𝔭)=dim((𝐴/𝔭)𝑔―)=dim(𝐴𝑔/𝔭𝑔)=dim(𝔭𝐴𝑔). So we may assume without loss of generality that 𝑛=1 and 𝔭=(𝑓).

By (3.35.11) there is an integral, injective map

𝜙:𝐵↪︎𝐴

where 𝐵=𝑘[𝑋1,…,𝑋𝑛], which induces an algebraic field extension

𝐾≔Frac(𝐵)↪︎Frac(𝐴)≕𝐿

We claim that there exists 𝑓0∈𝐵 such that

𝜙−1(𝑓)=(𝑓0)

Observe that in this case (𝑓0) is prime and therefore the unique minimal prime over (𝑓0). Therefore the result would follow from the first part and (3.24.23). Firstly for any 𝑔∈𝐴 we have (3.24.18)

Norm𝐿/𝐾(𝑔)∈𝐵∩𝜙−1((𝑔))

(because 𝐵 is a UFD and therefore integrally closed (3.24.12)). Define 𝑓0≔Norm𝐿/𝐾(𝑓) then we see that 𝑓0∈𝜙−1((𝑓))⟹(𝑓0)⊆𝜙−1((𝑓)). Conversely if 𝜙(𝑔)𝑛=ℎ𝑓 then 𝑔𝑛[𝐿:𝐾]=Norm(𝜙(𝑔)𝑛)=Norm(ℎ)𝑓0∈(𝑓0)⟹𝑔∈(𝑓0). Therefore the reverse inclusion holds.

Finally by the codimension 1 formula (3.22.8) we have ht((𝑓))=1. ∎

Corollary 3.35.34 (Height 1 formula)

Let 𝐴 be an affine domain. Then for a prime ideal 𝔭

ht(𝔭)=1⟹dim(𝔭)=dim(𝐴)−1

More generally if 𝐴 is an affine algebra and 𝔭1⊊𝔭2 is a saturated chain of prime ideals then

dim(𝔭2)=dim(𝔭1)−1

Proof. Choose 0≠𝑓∈𝔭 then it follows from the previous result (3.35.32). Alternatively by (3.35.11) there is an integral injective map

𝜙:𝑘[𝑋1,…,𝑋𝑛]↪︎𝐴

with 𝔮≔𝜙−1(𝔭) and 𝑛=dim𝐴. Then by Going Down we have ht(𝔮)=1. By (3.22.5) 𝔮 is principal and by (3.35.9) there is an integral injective map

𝑘[𝑌1,…,𝑌𝑛−1]↪︎𝑘[𝑋1,…,𝑋𝑛]/𝔮

therefore dim(𝔮)=𝑛−1. By (3.24.23) this equals dim(𝔭) and we are done.

For the second statement we may consider the affine domain 𝐴/𝔭1 and observe that ht(𝔭2/𝔭1)=1. Therefore

dim(𝔭2)=dim(𝔭2/𝔭1)=dim(𝐴/𝔭1)−1=dim(𝔭1)−1

 ∎

Corollary 3.35.35 (Biequidimensionality)

Let 𝐴 be an affine algebra. Then it is quasi-biequidimensional and satisfies the codimension formulae for 𝔭⊂𝔞

dim𝔞=dim𝔭+ht(𝔞/𝔭)ht(𝔞)=ht(𝔞/𝔭)+ht(𝔭)

If in addition 𝐴 is equidimensional (e.g. an integral domain) then it is biequidimensional and satisfies for all ideals 𝔞

dim𝐴=dim𝔞+ht(𝔞)

In particular for every prime ideal 𝔭 we have

dim𝐴=dim𝐴𝔭+dim𝐴/𝔭

and for every maximal ideal

dim𝐴=dim𝐴𝔪
Proof. By (3.35.34) 𝐴 satisfies the criteria in (3.22.10).e) and so is quasi-biequidimensional. The codimension formulas follow from (3.22.12). ∎

We may also consider the following result

Proposition 3.35.36 (Generalized Hauptidealsatz for Affine Algebras)

Let 𝐴 be an affine algebra and 𝔭 a prime ideal. Then

  1. If ht(𝔭)=𝑛 then it is minimal over some ideal 𝔞≔(𝑥1,…,𝑥𝑛). Furthermore 𝔞 may be chosen such that ht(𝔞)=𝑛 and every minimal prime is of height 𝑛

  2. We have the following characterization of height of a prime ideal

    ht(𝔭)=min{𝑛|𝔭minimal over(𝑥1,…,𝑥𝑛)}

In particular if 𝔭 is minimal over (𝑥1,…,𝑥𝑛) then ht(𝔭)≤𝑛.

Furthermore the same result holds for localization of 𝐴 at any prime ideal.

Proof. This is largely a restatement of results in the Hauptidealsatz section. By (3.35.32) we have 𝐴/𝔭 is hauptidealsatz for every prime ideal 𝔭. Furthermore by (3.35.35) 𝐴 is quasi-biequidimensional and so catenary (3.22.10). Then by (3.27.4) this means 𝐴 is a generalized hauptidealsatz ring (and so is every localization 𝐴𝔭).

Then a) and b) follows from (3.27.12). ∎

Corollary 3.35.37

Let 𝐴 be an affine algebra and 𝔭 a prime ideal. Denote the unique maximal ideal of 𝐴𝔭 by 𝔪≔𝔭𝐴𝔭. Then

dim𝐴𝔭=min{𝑛|(𝑥1,…,𝑥𝑛)=𝔪}≤dim𝑘(𝔪)𝔪/𝔪2

with equality iff 𝐴𝔭 is a regular local ring.

Proof. By (3.35.36) 𝐴𝔭 is a Noetherian local ring satisfying the generalized hauptidealsatz. Therefore the result follows by (3.28.2). ∎
3.35.5.1 * Biequidimensionality by Strong Normalisation *

We may prove more directly the biequidimensionality property by using the strong form of the Normalisation Lemma (3.35.12) and Going Down (3.24.19). First we prove a technical result

Lemma 3.35.38 (Saturated pairs)

Let 𝜙:𝐵→𝐴 be an integral map and 𝔭0⊊𝔭1 prime ideals lying over 𝔮0⊊𝔮1. Then

  1. 𝔮0⊊𝔮1 saturated ⟹𝔭0⊊𝔭1 saturated.
  2. 𝐵/𝔮0 integrally closed and 𝔭0⊊𝔭1 saturated ⟹𝔮0⊊𝔮1 saturated

We may relax the condition in b) to the existence of another integral map 𝜓:𝐶→𝐵 such that 𝐶/𝜓−1(𝔮0) is integrally closed.

Proof. The first follows by incomparability (3.24.17). The second follows by applying Going Down (3.24.19) to the integral map 𝐵/𝔮0↪︎𝐴/𝔭0. More precisely if 𝔮0⊊𝔮⊊𝔮1 then (0)⊊𝔮/𝔮0⊊𝔮1/𝔮0 whence there exists 𝔭 such that (0)⊊𝔭/𝔭0⊊𝔭1/𝔭0. This means 𝔭0⊊𝔭⊊𝔭1, a contradiction.

The final statement can be demonstrated by applying b) to 𝐶→𝐴 and then a) to 𝐵→𝐴. ∎

Proposition 3.35.39
Let 𝐴 be an affine domain, then every maximal chain has order 𝑛=dim𝐴, i.e. 𝐴 is irreducible and biequidimensional.

Proof of (3.35.39). Consider a maximal chain 𝔭0⊊𝔭1⊊…⊊𝔭𝑚. Clearly 𝔭𝑚 is maximal by (3.3.36), and as 𝐴 is integral 𝔭0=0. Apply (3.35.12) to find an integral, injective map

𝜙:𝑘[𝑋1,…,𝑋𝑛]↪︎𝐴

such that

𝔮𝑖≔𝜙−1(𝔭𝑖)=(𝑋1,…,𝑋ℎ(𝑖))0≤𝑖≤𝑚

Note 𝑛=dim𝐴 by (3.35.28). Clearly ℎ(0)=0 and by (3.24.16) 𝔮𝑚 is maximal so ℎ(𝑚)=𝑛. Observe that

𝑘[𝑋1,…,𝑋𝑛]/𝔮𝑖→∼𝑘[𝑋ℎ(𝑖)+1,…,𝑋𝑛]

is integrally closed for all 𝑖 (3.24.12). Therefore we may apply (3.35.38) to 𝜙 and each pair 𝔭𝑖⊊𝔭𝑖+1, to see that each chain 𝔮𝑖⊊𝔮𝑖+1 is saturated. This can only happen if ℎ(𝑗)=𝑗 and therefore 𝑚=𝑛. ∎
