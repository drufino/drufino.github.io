6.2.18 Normal Varieties

Definition 6.2.99

Let 𝐴 be an integral domain. We say that the ring 𝐴 is normal if it is integrally closed in its field of fractions.

Recall that 𝐴 factorial ⟹ 𝐴 is normal (3.24.12).

Proposition 6.2.100
The polynomial ring 𝐴[𝑋1,…,𝑋𝑛] is normal for every unique factorisation domain 𝐴.
Proof. See (3.24.12) and (3.17.46). ∎
Proposition 6.2.101 (Normal Variety)

Let 𝑋 be an integral variety. Then the following are equivalent

  1. 𝒪︀𝑋(𝑈) is normal for every open subset 𝑈⊂𝑋
  2. 𝒪︀𝑋,𝑊 is normal for every irreducible subset 𝑊⊂𝑋
  3. 𝒪︀𝑋,𝑥 is normal for every point 𝑥∈𝑋

In this case we say that 𝑋 is a normal variety. If 𝑋 is affine then this is equivalent to 𝒪︀𝑋(𝑋) being normal.

Proof. 𝑎)⟹𝑏) Let 𝑈⊂𝑋 be any affine open subset such that 𝑈∩𝑊≠∅, then by (…) 𝒪︀𝑋,𝑊→∼𝒪︀𝑈,𝑊∩𝑈→∼𝒪︀𝑋(𝑈)𝔐𝑈,𝑊∩𝑈, and the result follows from (3.24.10).

𝑏)⟹𝑐) is straightforward because 𝒪︀𝑋,𝑥=𝒪︀𝑋,{𝑥}.

𝑐)⟹𝑎) We first show this for the case that 𝑈 is affine. By (…) 𝒪︀𝑋,𝑥→∼𝒪︀𝑈,𝑥→∼𝒪︀𝑋(𝑈)𝔐𝑈,𝑥 is normal for every 𝑥∈𝑈. Therefore by (6.2.21).b) and (3.24.10) 𝒪︀𝑋(𝑈) is normal.

Now let 𝑈 be an arbitrary open set and 𝑉⊂𝑈 affine. Then by (6.2.15) we have inclusions

𝒪︀𝑋(𝑉)↪︎𝒪︀𝑋(𝑈)↪︎Frac(𝒪︀𝑋(𝑈))↪︎𝑘(𝑋)

and 𝑘(𝑋) is a fraction field for 𝒪︀𝑋(𝑉). If 𝛼∈Frac(𝒪︀𝑋(𝑈)) is integral over 𝒪︀𝑋(𝑈), then it is a-fortiori integral over 𝒪︀𝑋(𝑉). By the affine case then 𝛼∈𝒪︀𝑋(𝑉) and we conclude that 𝒪︀𝑋(𝑈) is normal as required.

For the case 𝑋 is affine we may use (3.24.10). ∎

Corollary 6.2.102
The standard affine space 𝔸𝑘𝑛(𝑘―) is normal.
Proof. The coordinate ring is a UFD (3.17.46) and therefore normal (3.24.12). ∎
Proposition 6.2.103
Let 𝑋 be an integral variety with open cover 𝑋=⋃𝑖=1𝑛𝑈𝑖. Then 𝑋 is normal iff 𝑈𝑖 are normal.
Proof. Straightforward, since normality is a local condition. ∎
