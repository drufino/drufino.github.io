6.2.13 Rational Maps

Definition 6.2.70 (Rational Maps)

Let 𝑋,𝑌 be varieties. A rational map 𝜙:𝑋⟶𝑌 is an equivalence class of regular morphisms

𝜙:𝑈→𝑌

where 𝑈 is a dense open subset of 𝑋. We say (𝑈,𝜙)∼(𝑈′,𝜙′) if there exists a dense open subset 𝑊⊂𝑈∩𝑈′ such that 𝜙|𝑊=𝜙′|𝑊.

We say the domain of definition of a rational map 𝜙 is the set of points 𝑥∈𝑋 for which there exists a neighbourhood 𝑈 of 𝑥 and a representative (𝑈,𝜙). This is a dense open subset denoted by dom(𝜙).

Proposition 6.2.71
Let 𝜙:𝑋⟶𝑌 be a rational map with 𝑌 separated. Then there exists a representative regular morphism 𝜙:dom(𝜙)→𝑌.
Proof. It is enough to show that for any two representatives (𝑈,𝜙) and (𝑈′,𝜙′) we have 𝜙|𝑈∩𝑈′=𝜙′|𝑈∩𝑈′. By (6.2.63) the equaliser is closed in 𝑈∩𝑈′ and by definition contains a dense open subset 𝑊. Therefore it equals 𝑈∩𝑈′ as required. ∎
