2.6.8 Representable Functors

Many algebraic constructions (e.g. tensor product) may be formalised in terms of “representable” functors (2.6.60). The usual definition involves the set-valued functor Mor(𝑋,−) as defined in (2.6.11).

As a generalisation follows we consider an enriched hom functor (2.6.59) Hom(−,−) taking values in the concrete category (𝒮︀,𝑈) where 𝑈 reflects isomorphisms. This clearly generalises the usual case.

Definition 2.6.60 (Representable Functor)

Let 𝐹:𝒞︀→𝒮︀ a covariant functor. We say 𝐹 is representable if there is a pair (𝑋,Φ) where 𝑋∈𝒞︀ and Φ is a natural isomorphism

We say 𝐹 is represented by the pair (𝑋,Φ). Note this implies 𝑈∘𝐹 is represented by 𝑈Φ.

It is often useful to have another conception of representable functor in terms of universal properties as this is more intuitive for applications.

Definition 2.6.61 (Universal Element)

Let 𝐹:𝒞︀→𝒮︀ be a covariant functor, then we say that a pair (𝑋,𝑖), where 𝑋∈𝒞︀,𝑖∈(𝑈∘𝐹)(𝑋), is a universal element for 𝐹 if for all 𝑌∈𝒞︀ there are bijections

Mor(𝑋,𝑌)⟶(𝑈∘𝐹)(𝑌)𝑓→(𝑈∘𝐹)(𝑓)(𝑖)

In this case we may also say 𝐹 is represented by the universal element (𝑋,𝑖).

We show the equivalence of the two notions by some abstract nonsense

Proposition 2.6.62 (Representable Set-Valued Functor = Universal Element)

Let 𝐹:𝒞︀→𝐒𝐞𝐭 be a covariant functor then there is a natural correspondence between representations and universal elements

{representations of 𝐹}⟷{universal elements of 𝐹}(𝑋,Φ)⟶(𝑋,Φ(1𝑋))(𝑋,𝑓→𝐹(𝑓)(𝑖))⟵(𝑋,𝑖)

The following may in particular be used to show that representations are unique up to isomorphism.

In practice we typically have a family of representations, and we want to show the construction is functorial

Definition 2.6.65 (Representable Bifunctor)

Let 𝐹:𝒟︀op×𝒞︀→𝒮︀ be a bifunctor. We say that it is representable by (𝐺,Φ) where 𝐺:𝒟︀→𝒞︀ is a functor, if Φ is a natural isomorphism of bifunctors

Φ:Hom(𝐺(−),−)⟹∼𝐹(−,−)

The following shows we only need to construct the representation pointwise.

Proposition 2.6.68 (Functorial Yoneda Lemma)

Let 𝐺,𝐺′:𝒟︀→𝒞︀ be functors. Then there is a bijection

  • id𝐺̂=id
  • 𝑔∘𝑓̂=𝑓̂∘𝑔̂
  • 𝑓 is a natural isomorphism iff Hom(𝑓,−) is a natural isomorphism

Proof. In order to demonstrate that Hom(𝑓𝐷,1𝐶) is a natural transformation of bifunctors it’s enough by (2.6.21) to show the following diagram commutes for ℎ:𝐷′→𝐷

However 𝑓𝐷∘𝐺(ℎ)=𝐺′(ℎ)∘𝑓𝐷′ by definition of natural transformation, from which it follows immediately. The map is a pointwise bijection (for every 𝐷∈𝒟︀) with the inverse given by the usual Yoneda Lemma.

The first two properties are straightforward. From these it follows that if 𝑓 is a natural isomorphism then so is Hom(𝑓,−). Conversely if Hom(𝑓,−) is a natural isomorphism then it has a two-sided inverse which is of the form Hom(𝑔,−). It’s then straightforward to argue that 𝑔 is a two-sided inverse of 𝑓. ∎
