3.35.2 Normalisation

The following normalisation results can be seen as a refinement of results on transcendence bases (see the Transcendental Field Extensions subsection).

Definition 3.35.5 (Algebraically Independent)

Let 𝐴 be a 𝑘-algebra and 𝑥1,…,𝑥𝑛 elements of 𝐴. Then we say they are algebraically independent if one of the following equivalent conditions holds

  • The unique 𝑘-algebra homomorphism 𝜙:𝑘[𝑋1,…,𝑋𝑛]→𝐴 such that 𝜙(𝑋𝑖)=𝑥𝑖 (evaluation homomorphism) is injective

  • There are no non-zero polynomials 𝑓(𝑋1,…,𝑋𝑛) such that 𝑓(𝑥1,…,𝑥𝑛)=0.

Note in particular it induces an isomorphism 𝑘[𝑋1,…,𝑋𝑛]→∼𝑘[𝑥1,…,𝑥𝑛]⊂𝐴.

Definition 3.35.6 (Normalising Family)

Let 𝐴 be a finitely-generated 𝑘-algebra. A normalising family is a set {𝑥1,…,𝑥𝑛} of elements of 𝐴 such that

  • 𝑥1,…,𝑥𝑛 are algebraically independent over 𝑘
  • 𝐴 is a finite 𝑘[𝑥1,…,𝑥𝑛]-module (equivalently integral over 𝑘[𝑥1,…,𝑥𝑛]).

NB this is completely equivalent to specifying an integral, injective map

𝑘[𝑋1,…,𝑋𝑛]↪︎𝐴

This may be seen as a refined transcendence base. More precisely we have the following

Proposition 3.35.7 (Relationship to Transcendence Base)

Let 𝐴 be an integral finitely-generated 𝑘-algebra with 𝐾≔Frac(𝐴). Let 𝑆⊂𝐴 be a subset. Then

  • If 𝐴 is integral over 𝑘[𝑆] then 𝐾/𝑘(𝑆) is algebraic
  • If 𝑆 is a normalising family for 𝐴 then 𝑆 is a transcendence basis for 𝐾/𝑘

In particular normalising families have order trdeg(𝐾/𝑘).

Proof. By (3.19.59) the set {𝑥∈𝐾|𝑥algebraic over𝑘(𝑆)} forms a subfield containing 𝐴, and therefore equals 𝐾.

The final statement follows from (3.19.155). ∎

The following is useful as it removes the necessity of showing algebraic independence in certain cases.

Corollary 3.35.8

Let 𝐴 be an integral finitely-generated 𝑘-algebra with 𝐾≔Frac(𝐴). Let 𝑆⊂𝐴 be a subset such that

  • 𝐴 is integral over 𝑘[𝑆]
  • #︎𝑆≤trdeg𝑘(𝐾)

then 𝑆 is a normalising family.

Proof. This follows from the previous result and (3.19.155). ∎

There are a few forms of the Normalisation Lemma which we prove, of progressively stronger form.

Lemma 3.35.9 (Hypersurface Normalisation Lemma)

Let 𝐴=𝑘[𝑋1,…,𝑋𝑛] be a polynomial ring over an infinite field 𝑘 and 0≠𝐹∈𝐴. Then there exists 𝜆1,…,𝜆𝑛−1∈𝑘 such that

  • 𝑥𝑖≔𝑋𝑖−𝜆𝑖𝐹1≤𝑖≤𝑛−1
  • 𝑥1,…,𝑥𝑛−1,𝐹 is a normalising family for 𝐴
  • 𝐹𝐴∩𝑘[𝑥1,…,𝑥𝑛−1,𝐹]=𝐹𝑘[𝑥1,…,𝑥𝑛−1,𝐹]

This may be viewed as a commutative diagram

where the horizontal arrows are injective, integral (and finite) and 𝜙−1((𝐹))=(𝑌𝑛).

Remark 3.35.10

Reversing the arrows we get the geometric picture, where horizontal arrows are finite and surjective

in other words after a linear change of variables we may express 𝑉(𝐹) as a finite covering of a standard hyperplane.

Proposition 3.35.11 (Nagata Normalisation Lemma)

Let 𝐴=𝑘[𝑥1,…,𝑥𝑛] be a finitely-generated 𝑘-algebra such that 𝑘 is infinite. Then there exists a normalising family 𝑦1,…,𝑦𝑑∈𝐴 such that each 𝑦𝑖 is a 𝑘-linear combination of 𝑥1,…,𝑥𝑛. Further if 𝔞1◃𝐴 is a proper ideal, then these may be chosen such that

𝔞1∩𝑘[𝑦1,…,𝑦𝑑]=(𝑦1,…,𝑦ℎ)𝑘[𝑦1,…,𝑦𝑑]

for some 0≤ℎ≤𝑑, where ℎ=0 denotes the zero ideal.

Proposition 3.35.12 (Bourbaki Normalisation Lemma)

Let 𝐴=𝑘[𝑥1,…,𝑥𝑛] be a finitely-generated 𝑘-algebra such that 𝑘 is infinite. Then there exists a normalising family 𝑦1,…,𝑦𝑑∈𝐴 such that each 𝑦𝑖 is a 𝑘-linear combination of 𝑥1,…,𝑥𝑛.

Furthermore for any finite chain of proper ideals in 𝐴

𝔞1⊆𝔞2⊆…⊆𝔞𝑝⊊𝐴

the family may be chosen such that

𝔞𝑗∩𝑘[𝑦1,…,𝑦𝑑]=(𝑦1,…,𝑦ℎ(𝑗))𝑘[𝑦1,…,𝑦𝑑]1≤𝑗≤𝑝.

for some non-decreasing sequence of integers ℎ(𝑗), where ℎ(𝑗)=0 denotes the zero ideal.

Remark 3.35.13 (Geometric Interpretation)

Note the normalisation here is equivalent to a commutative diagram

where the horizontal arrows are integral and injective, and the top arrow is given by

𝜙:𝑌𝑖→∑𝑗𝜆𝑖𝑗𝑥𝑗

such that 𝜙−1(𝔞𝑖)=(𝑌1,…,𝑌ℎ(𝑖))

As before this expresses 𝐴 as a finite covering of 𝔸𝑑 under which each subvariety is also a finite covering of a standard linear subspace.

We first prove the weaker form of the Normalisation Lemma

Proof of (3.35.9). First decompose 𝐹 into homogenous polynomials (monomials of the same degree)

𝐹=𝐹0+𝐹1+…+𝐹𝑚

and observe that the monomial 𝑋𝑛𝑚 appears only in 𝐹𝑚. Define

𝐹′≔𝐹(𝑋1+𝜆1𝑋𝑛,…,𝑋𝑛−1+𝜆𝑛−1𝑋𝑛,𝑋𝑛)

Furthermore in terms of homogenous polynomials

𝐹𝑚′=𝐹𝑚(𝑋1+𝜆1𝑇,…,𝑋𝑛−1+𝜆𝑛−1𝑋𝑛,𝑋𝑛)

and the coefficient of 𝑋𝑛𝑚 in 𝐹′ is simply 𝐹𝑚′(0,…,0,1)=𝐹𝑚(𝜆1,…,𝜆𝑛−1,1)∈𝑘. There are only finitely many values of 𝜆 such that 𝐹𝑚 is zero, whence there exists a 𝜆 such that 𝑋𝑛𝑚 has non-zero coefficient in 𝐹′, whence 𝐹′ is monic. By the previous Lemma we have 𝐹′(𝑥1,…,𝑥𝑛−1,𝑋𝑛)=0, with the leading coefficient in 𝑋𝑛 constant.

Let 𝐵≔𝑘[𝑥1,…𝑥𝑛−1,𝐹]. Then clearly 𝐵[𝑋𝑛]=𝐴 and 𝑋𝑛 is integral over 𝐵. Therefore by (3.24.4) 𝐴 is a finite 𝐵-module. As trdeg(𝐴/𝑘)=𝑛 by (3.35.8) {𝑥1,…,𝑥𝑛−1,𝐹} is a normalising family, and 𝐵 is isomorphic to a polynomial ring.

As 𝐵 is a polynomial ring it is integrally closed (3.24.12). Then the final statement is a consequence of the following lemma (3.35.14) (essentially to prove that 𝑉(𝐹)→𝔸𝑛−1 is surjective). ∎

Lemma 3.35.14

Let 𝐴 be integrally closed, 𝜙:𝐴↪︎𝐵 injective and integral and 𝑎∈𝐴. Then

(𝑎)ec=𝜙−1(𝜙(𝑎)𝐵)=(𝑎)

Proof. Let 𝐾=Frac(𝐴) and 𝐿=Frac(𝐵), then 𝜙 extends to an injection 𝜙:𝐾↪︎𝐿.

Generically we have (𝑎)⊆(𝑎)ec. Suppose 𝑎′∈(𝑎)ec, then 𝜙(𝑎′)=𝜙(𝑎)𝑏. Therefore 𝜙(𝑎′𝑎−1)=𝑏 is integral over 𝐴, whence so is 𝑎′𝑎−1. As 𝐴 is integrally closed we have 𝑎′∈(𝑎) as required. ∎

Before proving the stronger version of Normalisation Lemma, we need some preliminary technical results

Lemma 3.35.15
Let 𝐴 be a 𝑘-algebra and 𝔞◃𝐴 an ideal. Then 𝔞 is proper ⟺ 𝔞∩𝑘={0}.
Lemma 3.35.16

Let 𝐴=𝑘[𝑋1,…,𝑋𝑛] be a polynomial ring and 𝔞◃𝐴 a proper ideal. Then TFAE

  1. 𝔞=(𝑋1,…,𝑋ℎ)
    1. 𝑋1,…,𝑋ℎ∈𝔞
    2. 𝔞∩𝑘[𝑋ℎ+1,…,𝑋𝑛]={0}

In this case 𝔞∩𝑘[𝑋1,…,𝑋ℎ]=(𝑋1,…,𝑋ℎ)𝑘[𝑋1,…,𝑋ℎ]

Proof. We claim that there is a direct sum of 𝑘-vector spaces

𝑘[𝑋1,…,𝑋𝑛]=(𝑋1,…,𝑋ℎ)⊕𝑘[𝑋ℎ+1,…,𝑋𝑛]

from which the result largely follows. Let 𝑆 be the set of monomials in which at least one of 𝑋1,…,𝑋ℎ appears, and let 𝑇 be the set for which none appears (but including 1). Then clearly 𝐴=⟨𝑆⟩⊕⟨𝑇⟩ and 𝑘[𝑋ℎ+1,…,𝑋𝑛]=⟨𝑇⟩. We argue that (𝑋1,…,𝑋ℎ)=⟨𝑆⟩. First 𝑆 is stable under multiplication by 𝑋1,…,𝑋𝑛, and so ⟨𝑆⟩ is an ideal. One inclusion is obvious, furthermore it’s clear that 𝑆⊆(𝑋1,…,𝑋𝑛) from which the claim follows. ∎

We may now proceed to the proof of the stronger versions of the Normalisation Lemma.

Reduction to polynomial ring case for (3.35.11), (3.35.12). We show that for both forms it is possible to reduce to the case of a polynomial ring. For let 𝐴 be a finitely-generated 𝑘-algebra then we may write 𝐴≔𝑘[𝑋1,…,𝑋𝑛]/𝔞 for some ideal 𝔞. Then the polynomial ring case for 𝑝=1 shows the existence of an integral, injective map

𝜙:𝑘[𝑌1,…,𝑌𝑚]↪︎𝐴

If 𝔞𝑖 is a chain of ideals in 𝐴, then 𝔞𝑖′≔𝜙−1(𝔞𝑖) is a chain of ideals in 𝑘[𝑌1,…,𝑌𝑚]. The general case for a polynomial ring shows the existence of an integral map

𝜓:𝑘[𝑍1,…,𝑍𝑚]↪︎𝑘[𝑌1,…,𝑌𝑚]

such that

𝜓−1(𝔞𝑖′)=(𝑍1,…,𝑍ℎ(𝑖))

The composition 𝜙∘𝜓 gives the required normalisation of 𝐴. Geometrically express 𝐴 as a finite covering of affine space, by considering it as a subvariety of larger affine space. ∎

Proof of (3.35.11) in the polynomial ring case. Let 𝐴=𝑘[𝑋1,…,𝑋𝑛] and proceed by induction on 𝑛.

Note that as 𝔞1 is proper, we must have 𝔞1∩𝑘={0}, and we may obviously also assume that 𝔞1≠(0) (as otherwise we may take ℎ=0).

Choose 0≠𝑥1∈𝔞1. Then by (3.35.9) there exists 𝑡2,…,𝑡𝑛∈𝐴 such that 𝑥1,𝑡2,…,𝑡𝑛 is a normalising family and (𝑥1)∩𝐵=𝑥1𝐵 where 𝐵≔𝑘[𝑥1,𝑡2,…,𝑡𝑛]. In the case that 𝔞1 is principal we are done, since the choice of 𝑥1 was arbitrary, and in this case ℎ(1)=1. In particular this covers the base case 𝑛=1 because 𝐴 is a PID (3.16.4).

Otherwise 𝐵′≔𝑘[𝑡2,…,𝑡𝑛] is a polynomial ring and 𝔞1′≔𝔞1∩𝐵′ is proper by (3.35.15). By induction on 𝑛 there is a normalising family 𝑥2,…,𝑥𝑛 for 𝐵′ such that 𝔞1′∩𝐶′=(𝑥2,…,𝑥ℎ)𝐶′ where 𝐶′≔𝑘[𝑥2,…,𝑥𝑛] and 𝐵′ is integral over 𝐶′.

Define 𝐶≔𝑘[𝑥1,…,𝑥𝑛]=𝐶′[𝑥1] then 𝑥1,𝑡2,…,𝑡𝑛 are integral over 𝐶, so 𝐵 is integral over 𝐶 (3.24.4), and 𝐴 is integral over 𝐶 (3.24.5), so by (3.35.8) 𝑥1,…,𝑥𝑛 is a normalising family for 𝐴.

We claim that 𝔞1″≔𝔞1∩𝐶=(𝑥1,…,𝑥ℎ)𝐶. Clearly 𝑥1,…,𝑥ℎ∈𝔞1″, and 𝔞1″∩𝑘[𝑥ℎ+1,…,𝑥𝑛]=𝔞1′∩𝑘[𝑥ℎ+1,…,𝑥𝑛]={0} by (3.35.16) applied to the ring 𝐶′. Then (3.35.16) applied to the ring 𝐶 demonstrates the claim. ∎

Proof of (3.35.12) in the polynomial ring case. We can then show the case 𝑝>1 by induction, for by the induction hypothesis there exists a normalising family 𝑡1,…,𝑡𝑛 for 𝐴 such that

𝔞𝑗∩𝐵=(𝑡1,…,𝑡ℎ(𝑗))𝐵1≤𝑗≤𝑝−1𝐵≔𝑘[𝑡1,…,𝑡𝑛]

Let 𝑟=ℎ(𝑝−1), then by the case 𝑝=1 applied to the ring 𝐵′≔𝑘[𝑡𝑟+1,…,𝑡𝑛] and ideal 𝔞𝑝∩𝐵′ there exists a normalising family 𝑥𝑟+1,…,𝑥𝑛 for 𝐵′ such that for some 𝑠≤𝑛 (possibly equal to 𝑟 to denote the zero ideal),

𝔞𝑝∩𝐶′=(𝑥𝑟+1,…,𝑥𝑠)𝐶′𝐶′≔𝑘[𝑥𝑟+1,…,𝑥𝑛]

We claim that 𝑡1,…,𝑡𝑟,𝑥𝑟+1,…,𝑥𝑛 is a suitable normalising family for 𝐴, with ℎ(𝑝)=𝑠.

For define 𝐶≔𝑘[𝑡1,…,𝑡𝑟,𝑥𝑟+1,…,𝑥𝑛]=𝐶′[𝑡1,…,𝑡𝑟]. Recall 𝐵′ is integral over 𝐶′, and 𝑡1,…,𝑡𝑟 are obviously integral over 𝐶 so 𝐵=𝐵′[𝑡1,…,𝑡𝑟] is integral over 𝐶 by (3.24.7). Then 𝐴 is integral over 𝐶 by (3.24.5), and this is a normalising family by (3.35.8), and in particular algebraically independent.

For 𝑗≤𝑝−1 and ℎ≔ℎ(𝑗)≤𝑟, apply (3.35.16) to the ideal 𝔞𝑗∩𝐵 to see 𝔞𝑗∩𝑘[𝑡ℎ+1,…,𝑡𝑛]={0} and therefore 𝔞𝑗∩𝑘[𝑡ℎ+1,…,𝑡𝑟,𝑥𝑟+1,…,𝑥𝑛]={0}. As 𝑡1,…,𝑡ℎ∈𝔞𝑗 we see by (3.35.16) that 𝔞𝑗∩𝐶=(𝑡1,…,𝑡ℎ)𝐶 as required.

Similarly by (3.35.16) 𝔞𝑝∩𝑘[𝑥𝑠+1,…,𝑥𝑛]={0} and clearly 𝑡1,…,𝑡𝑟,𝑥𝑟+1,…,𝑥𝑠∈𝔞𝑝. Then by (3.35.16) again 𝔞𝑝∩𝐶=(𝑡1,…,𝑡𝑟,𝑥𝑟+1,…,𝑥𝑠)𝐶 as required. ∎

Remark 3.35.17
In Bourbaki’s proof the reduction to the polynomial ring case increases 𝑝 to 𝑝+1, so in particular the 𝑝=1 case requires the more complex reduction argument at the end of the proof. Here we use a simplified argument from [Sha00].
