2.1.6 Galois Connections

Definition 2.1.49 (Galois Connection)

Let (𝑋,≤𝑋) and (𝑌,≤𝑌) be posets. A pair of functions (𝑓⋆,𝑓⋆)

𝑋⇌𝑓⋆𝑓⋆𝑌

is called an antitone Galois connection if it satisfies the adjoint property

  • 𝑥≤𝑋𝑓⋆(𝑦)⟺𝑦≤𝑌𝑓⋆(𝑥)∀𝑥∈𝑋,𝑦∈𝑌

We say it is a monotone Galois connection if instead

  • 𝑥≤𝑋𝑓⋆(𝑦)⟺𝑓⋆(𝑥)≤𝑌𝑦∀𝑥∈𝑋,𝑦∈𝑌

We will assume that if not otherwise specified the connection is antitone.

Proposition 2.1.50 (Equivalent Condition for Galois Connection)

Let (𝑋,≤𝑋) and (𝑌,≤𝑌) be posets. Consider a pair of functions

𝑋⇌𝑓⋆𝑓⋆𝑌

Then this constitutes an antitone Galois Connection if and only if

  • 𝑓⋆ and 𝑓⋆ are both antitone
  • 𝑥≤𝑋𝑓⋆(𝑓⋆(𝑥)) and 𝑦≤𝑌𝑓⋆(𝑓⋆(𝑦)) for all 𝑥∈𝑋,𝑦∈𝑌 (i.e. 𝑓⋆∘𝑓⋆ and 𝑓⋆∘𝑓⋆ are extensive)

Similarly it constitutes a monotone Galois Connection if and only if

  • 𝑓⋆ and 𝑓⋆ are both monotone
  • 𝑥≤𝑋𝑓⋆(𝑓⋆(𝑥)) and 𝑓⋆(𝑓⋆(𝑦))≤𝑌𝑦 for all 𝑥∈𝑋,𝑦∈𝑌

Proof. We consider only the antitone case, as the monotone follows from duality (flip ≤𝑌).

Suppose that (𝑓⋆,𝑓⋆) satisfies the adjoint property

𝑓⋆(𝑥)=𝑓⋆(𝑥)⟹𝑓⋆(𝑥)≤𝑌𝑓⋆(𝑥)⟹𝑥≤𝑋𝑓⋆(𝑓⋆(𝑥))𝑓⋆(𝑦)=𝑓⋆(𝑦)⟹𝑓⋆(𝑦)≤𝑌𝑓⋆(𝑦)⟹𝑦≤𝑌𝑓⋆(𝑓⋆(𝑦))

whence the extensive property follows. Furthermore

𝑥≤𝑋𝑥′⟹𝑥≤𝑋𝑓⋆(𝑓⋆(𝑥′))⟹𝑓⋆(𝑥′)≤𝑌𝑓⋆(𝑥)𝑦≤𝑌𝑦′⟹𝑦≤𝑌𝑓⋆(𝑓⋆(𝑦′))⟹𝑓⋆(𝑦)≤𝑋𝑓⋆(𝑦′)

which shows that the functions 𝑓⋆ and 𝑓⋆ are antitone.

Conversely suppose they satisfy the given conditions. Then by the antitone and extensive properties in turn

𝑥≤𝑋𝑓⋆(𝑦)⟹𝑓⋆(𝑓⋆(𝑦))≤𝑌𝑓⋆(𝑥)⟹𝑦≤𝑌𝑓⋆(𝑥)

and

𝑦≤𝑌𝑓⋆(𝑥)⟹𝑓⋆(𝑓⋆(𝑥))≤𝑋𝑓⋆(𝑦)⟹𝑥≤𝑋𝑓⋆(𝑦).

which is the adjoint property as required. ∎

Definition 2.1.51 (Closed sets)

Let (𝑓⋆,𝑓⋆) be a Galois connection. Then define the closed sets to be

𝑋⋆≔𝑓⋆(𝑌)𝑌⋆≔𝑓⋆(𝑋)
Proposition 2.1.52 (Isomorphism on closed sets)

Consider an antitone (resp. monotone) Galois connection 𝑋⇌𝑓⋆𝑓⋆𝑌. Then it restricts to a dual isomorphism (resp. order isomorphism) on closed sets

𝑋⋆⇌𝑓⋆𝑓⋆𝑌⋆

Furthermore the following properties hold

  • 𝑓⋆∘𝑓⋆∘𝑓⋆=𝑓⋆
  • 𝑓⋆∘𝑓⋆∘𝑓⋆=𝑓⋆
  • In the antitone case 𝑓⋆∘𝑓⋆ and 𝑓⋆∘𝑓⋆ are closure operators.
  • In the monotone case 𝑓⋆∘𝑓⋆ is a closure operator and 𝑓⋆∘𝑓⋆ is a kernel operator.
  • 𝑋⋆=Fix(𝑓⋆∘𝑓⋆)=Im(𝑓⋆∘𝑓⋆)
  • 𝑌⋆=Fix(𝑓⋆∘𝑓⋆)=Im(𝑓⋆∘𝑓⋆)

Proof. We detail the antitone case as the monotone case follows by duality. We first prove so-called triangular identities, for by the extensive property (2.1.50)

𝑥≤𝑓⋆(𝑓⋆(𝑥))⟹𝑓⋆(𝑓⋆(𝑓⋆(𝑥)))≤𝑓⋆(𝑥)

and by the other extensive property

𝑓⋆(𝑥)≤𝑓⋆(𝑓⋆(𝑓⋆(𝑥)))

whence they are equal. The other case is similar.

It’s immediate that 𝑓⋆∘𝑓⋆ and 𝑓⋆∘𝑓⋆ are idempotent, and they are extensive by (2.1.50). And the composition of two antitone functions is monotone so 𝑓⋆∘𝑓⋆ and 𝑓⋆∘𝑓⋆ are closure operators.

Observe

Im(𝑓⋆∘𝑓⋆)⊆Im(𝑓⋆)⊆Fix(𝑓⋆∘𝑓⋆)

where the first inclusion is trivial and the second inclusion follows from the second triangular identity. However both sides are equal by (2.1.15) and the expression for 𝑋⋆ follows. The expression for 𝑌⋆ follows similarly.

This shows that the maps are mutual inverses as required. ∎

In certain circumstances we may consider a smaller subset of 𝑋, by applying a suitable closure operator which is compatible with the Galois correspondence:

Proposition 2.1.53 (Subordinated Closure Operator)

Let 𝑋⇌𝑓⋆𝑓⋆𝑌 be a Galois connection and 𝑐:𝑋→𝑋 be a closure operator with image 𝑋𝑐. Then

𝑐(𝑥)≤(𝑓⋆∘𝑓⋆)(𝑥)∀𝑥∈𝑋⟺Im(𝑓⋆)⊆Fix(𝑐)⟺𝑋⋆⊆𝑋𝑐

In this case

𝑓⋆(𝑐(𝑥))=𝑓⋆(𝑥)

Proof. Suppose 𝑐(𝑥)≤(𝑓⋆∘𝑓⋆)(𝑥). Substitute 𝑥=𝑓⋆(𝑦) then, because 𝑐 is extensive,

𝑓⋆(𝑦)≤𝑐(𝑓⋆(𝑦))≤(𝑓⋆∘𝑓⋆∘𝑓⋆)(𝑦)=𝑓⋆(𝑦).

Therefore 𝑐(𝑓⋆(𝑦))=𝑓⋆(𝑦) and Im(𝑓⋆)⊆Fix(𝑐) as required. Conversely suppose this holds, then by the monotone property of 𝑐 and extensive property of 𝑓⋆∘𝑓⋆

𝑥≤(𝑓⋆∘𝑓⋆)(𝑥)⟹𝑐(𝑥)≤𝑐((𝑓⋆∘𝑓⋆)(𝑥))=(𝑓⋆∘𝑓⋆)(𝑥).

as required. Finally by the extensive property of 𝑐

𝑥≤𝑐(𝑥)≤(𝑓⋆∘𝑓⋆)(𝑥)

and by the antitone/monotone property of 𝑓⋆ and triangular identity

𝑓⋆(𝑥)≤𝑓⋆(𝑐(𝑥))≤𝑓⋆(𝑥)

whence 𝑓⋆(𝑐(𝑥))=𝑓(𝑥) as required. ∎

The meaning of the “adjoint” criterion can be explained by the following rather generic situation

Example 2.1.54 (Canonical example of an antitone Galois connection)

Suppose there is a predicate

𝜓:𝑋×𝑌→{0,1}

Define a connection

𝒫︀(𝑋)⇌𝑓⋆𝑓⋆𝒫︀(𝑌)

by

𝑓⋆(𝑆)={𝑦∈𝑌|𝜓(𝑥,𝑦)=1∀𝑥∈𝑆}𝑓⋆(𝑇)={𝑥∈𝑋|𝜓(𝑥,𝑦)=1∀𝑦∈𝑇}

Then

𝑆⊆𝑓⋆(𝑇)⟺𝜓(𝑠,𝑡)=1∀𝑠∈𝑆𝑡∈𝑇⟺𝑇⊆𝑓⋆(𝑆)
Proposition 2.1.55 (Joins under Galois Correspondence)

Let (𝑋,≤𝑋) and (𝑌,≤𝑌) be complete lattices with an antitone Galois connection 𝑋⇌𝑓⋆𝑓⋆𝑌. Then for 𝑆⊆𝑋

𝑓⋆(⋁𝑆)=⋀𝑓⋆(𝑆)

Similarly for 𝑇⊆𝑌 we have

𝑓⋆(⋁𝑇)=⋀𝑓⋆(𝑇)

Proof. Let 𝑎=⋁𝑆 and 𝑏=⋀𝑓⋆(𝑆). Then 𝑠≤𝑎⟹𝑓⋆(𝑎)≤𝑓⋆(𝑠) for all 𝑠∈𝑆, which implies 𝑓⋆(𝑎)≤𝑏. Similarly 𝑏≤𝑓⋆(𝑠)⟹𝑠≤𝑓⋆(𝑏) by the adjoint criterion. Therefore 𝑎≤𝑓⋆(𝑏) by definition of join, which implies 𝑏≤𝑓⋆(𝑎) by the adjoint criterion again. Whence 𝑓⋆(𝑎)=𝑏 as required.

The second statement follows from duality. ∎
