2.6.18 Pullback and Kernel Pairs

Definition 2.6.144 (Pullback)

Let 𝑓:𝑋→𝑍, 𝑔:𝑌→𝑍 be morphisms in 𝒞︀. We say (𝑊,𝑝1,𝑝2), with 𝑝1:𝑊→𝑋 and 𝑝2:𝑊→𝑌, is a pullback of (𝑓,𝑔) if 𝑓∘𝑝1=𝑔∘𝑝2, and for every (𝑊′,𝑝1′,𝑝2′) with 𝑓∘𝑝1′=𝑔∘𝑝2′ there is a unique morphism 𝜙:𝑊′→𝑊 such that 𝑝1∘𝜙=𝑝1′ and 𝑝2∘𝜙=𝑝2′

This is equivalent to (𝑊,{𝑝1,𝑝2}) being a limit for the evident diagram 𝐹:𝚲→𝒞︀ determined by (𝑓,𝑔), where 𝚲≔{𝑎→𝑐←𝑏} denotes the category with three objects and, besides identities, two further morphisms 𝑎→𝑐, 𝑏→𝑐. We write 𝑊≕𝑋×𝑍𝑌.

Proposition 2.6.145 (Pullback from Products and Equalisers)
Let 𝑓:𝑋→𝑍, 𝑔:𝑌→𝑍 be morphisms such that 𝑋×𝑌 exists with projections 𝜋𝑋,𝜋𝑌, and such that (𝑊,𝑒)≔eq(𝑓∘𝜋𝑋,𝑔∘𝜋𝑌:𝑋×𝑌→𝑍) exists. Then (𝑊,𝜋𝑋∘𝑒,𝜋𝑌∘𝑒) is a pullback of (𝑓,𝑔).

Proof. Drafted by Claude Sonnet 5, reviewed by David Rufino.

Write 𝑝1≔𝜋𝑋∘𝑒, 𝑝2≔𝜋𝑌∘𝑒. By definition of the equaliser, 𝑓∘𝜋𝑋∘𝑒=𝑔∘𝜋𝑌∘𝑒, that is 𝑓∘𝑝1=𝑔∘𝑝2.

Suppose (𝑊′,𝑝1′,𝑝2′) satisfies 𝑓∘𝑝1′=𝑔∘𝑝2′. By the universal property of the product there is a unique ℎ:𝑊′→𝑋×𝑌 with 𝜋𝑋∘ℎ=𝑝1′, 𝜋𝑌∘ℎ=𝑝2′. Then 𝑓∘𝜋𝑋∘ℎ=𝑓∘𝑝1′=𝑔∘𝑝2′=𝑔∘𝜋𝑌∘ℎ, so ℎ equalises (𝑓∘𝜋𝑋,𝑔∘𝜋𝑌), whence by the universal property of the equaliser there is a unique 𝜙:𝑊′→𝑊 with 𝑒∘𝜙=ℎ. Then 𝑝1∘𝜙=𝜋𝑋∘𝑒∘𝜙=𝜋𝑋∘ℎ=𝑝1′, and similarly 𝑝2∘𝜙=𝑝2′.

If 𝜙′ also satisfies 𝑝1∘𝜙′=𝑝1′, 𝑝2∘𝜙′=𝑝2′, then 𝜋𝑋∘(𝑒∘𝜙′)=𝑝1′=𝜋𝑋∘ℎ and 𝜋𝑌∘(𝑒∘𝜙′)=𝑝2′=𝜋𝑌∘ℎ, so by uniqueness in the universal property of the product, 𝑒∘𝜙′=ℎ; as 𝑒 is monic (2.6.135), 𝜙′=𝜙. ∎

Definition 2.6.146 (Kernel Pair)

Let 𝑓:𝑋→𝑌 be a morphism. A kernel pair of 𝑓 is a pullback (𝑍,𝑝1,𝑝2) of (𝑓,𝑓), that is 𝑝1,𝑝2:𝑍→𝑋 with 𝑓∘𝑝1=𝑓∘𝑝2, universal among such pairs

In 𝐒𝐞𝐭, the kernel pair of 𝑓 is precisely the relation {(𝑥,𝑥′)∈𝑋×𝑋|𝑓(𝑥)=𝑓(𝑥′)}⊆𝑋×𝑋, together with its two projections: it records exactly which pairs of elements 𝑓 identifies. So 𝑓 is injective if and only if this relation is trivial, i.e. reduces to the diagonal {(𝑥,𝑥)|𝑥∈𝑋} – this is the picture behind (2.6.147) below, which characterises monomorphisms in general as those morphisms whose kernel pair is trivial.

Proposition 2.6.147 (Monomorphisms via Kernel Pairs)
Let 𝜇:𝑋→𝑌 be a morphism with a kernel pair (𝑍,𝑝1,𝑝2). Then 𝜇 is monic if and only if 𝑝1=𝑝2.

Proof. Drafted by Claude Sonnet 5, reviewed by David Rufino.

(⇐) Suppose 𝑝1=𝑝2. If 𝑎,𝑏:𝑊→𝑋 satisfy 𝜇∘𝑎=𝜇∘𝑏, then by the universal property of the kernel pair there is a unique 𝜓:𝑊→𝑍 with 𝑝1∘𝜓=𝑎 and 𝑝2∘𝜓=𝑏; since 𝑝1=𝑝2 this gives 𝑎=𝑝1∘𝜓=𝑝2∘𝜓=𝑏. So 𝜇 is monic.

(⇒) Suppose 𝜇 is monic. Since 𝜇∘𝑝1=𝜇∘𝑝2 by definition of the kernel pair (2.6.146), monicity of 𝜇 gives 𝑝1=𝑝2. ∎

Corollary 2.6.148 (Monomorphisms have Trivial Kernel Pair)
Let 𝜇:𝑋→𝑌 be a morphism. Then 𝜇 is monic if and only if (𝑋,1𝑋,1𝑋) is a kernel pair of 𝜇.

Proof. Drafted by Claude Sonnet 5, reviewed by David Rufino.

(⇐) This is the case 𝑍≔𝑋, 𝑝1=𝑝2≔1𝑋 of (2.6.147) (⇐).

(⇒) Suppose 𝜇 is monic. Given 𝑎,𝑏:𝑊→𝑋 with 𝜇∘𝑎=𝜇∘𝑏, monicity gives 𝑎=𝑏; then 𝜙≔𝑎 satisfies 1𝑋∘𝜙=𝑎 and 1𝑋∘𝜙=𝑏 (as 𝑎=𝑏), and is the unique such morphism, since any 𝜙′ with 1𝑋∘𝜙′=𝑎 must equal 𝑎. So (𝑋,1𝑋,1𝑋) satisfies the universal property of a kernel pair of 𝜇 (2.6.146). ∎

Proposition 2.6.149 (Regular Epimorphism is the Coequaliser of its Kernel Pair)
Let 𝑒:𝑋→𝑌 be a regular epimorphism with kernel pair (𝑍,𝑝1,𝑝2). Then 𝑒 is the coequaliser of (𝑝1,𝑝2).

Proof. Drafted by Claude Sonnet 5, reviewed by David Rufino.

Since 𝑒 is regular, by (2.6.158) there are morphisms 𝛾,𝛿:𝑊⇉𝑋 such that 𝑒 is the coequaliser of (𝛾,𝛿). Since 𝑒∘𝛾=𝑒∘𝛿, the universal property of the kernel pair (𝑍,𝑝1,𝑝2) gives a unique morphism ℎ:𝑊→𝑍 with 𝑝1∘ℎ=𝛾 and 𝑝2∘ℎ=𝛿.

By definition of the kernel pair, 𝑒∘𝑝1=𝑒∘𝑝2. Suppose 𝑘:𝑋→𝑉 satisfies 𝑘∘𝑝1=𝑘∘𝑝2. Then

𝑘∘𝛾=𝑘∘𝑝1∘ℎ=𝑘∘𝑝2∘ℎ=𝑘∘𝛿

so, as 𝑒 is the coequaliser of (𝛾,𝛿), there is a unique morphism 𝑙:𝑌→𝑉 such that 𝑙∘𝑒=𝑘. Since 𝑒 is (in particular) an epimorphism (2.6.158), this 𝑙 is the unique morphism satisfying 𝑙∘𝑒=𝑘 among all morphisms 𝑌→𝑉, in particular the unique one factoring 𝑘 through 𝑒. So 𝑒 satisfies the universal property of the coequaliser of (𝑝1,𝑝2). ∎
