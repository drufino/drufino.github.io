4.1.6 Symmetric Spaces

Definition 4.1.40
Let 𝑋 be a topological space. We say that 𝑥 is quasi-closed if 𝑦∈{𝑥}―⟹𝑥∈{𝑦}―.
Proposition 4.1.41 (Symmetric Topology)

Let 𝑋 be a topological space. Then the following are equivalent

  1. For all 𝑥∈𝑋 we have

    {𝑥}―={𝑦∈𝑋|𝑦topologically indistinguishable from𝑥},
  2. For all 𝑥,𝑦∈𝑋𝑥∈{𝑦}―⟹𝑦∈{𝑥}― (i.e. every point is quasi-closed),
  3. Every open neighbourhood 𝑈 of 𝑥 contains {𝑥}―,
  4. 𝑋 is partitioned by sets of the form {𝑥}―, indexed by 𝑋0.

In this case we say that 𝑋 is symmetric or 𝑅0. Further 𝑥∈{𝑦}― if and only if 𝑥 and 𝑦 become equal in 𝑋0.

Proposition 4.1.42

Let 𝑋 be a symmetric topological space and 𝑥,𝑦∈𝑋. Then the following are equivalent

  1. 𝑥 and 𝑦 are topologically indistinguishable,
  2. 𝑥∈{𝑦}―,
  3. 𝑦∈{𝑥}―,
  4. {𝑥}―={𝑦}―,
  5. 𝑥 and 𝑦 become equal in the Kolmogorov Quotient 𝑋0
Proposition 4.1.43 (Open Subspace is Symmetric)

Let 𝑋 be a symmetric space and 𝑈⊂𝑋 an open subset. Then 𝑈 is symmetric and for every 𝑥∈𝑈 we have

cl𝑋({𝑥})=cl𝑈({𝑥})
Proof. By (4.1.21) we have cl𝑈({𝑥})=cl𝑋({𝑥})∩𝑈, so these are equal by (4.1.41).c). We conclude that the sets {𝑥}― partition 𝑈 so that 𝑈 is also symmetric. ∎
Proposition 4.1.44 (Symmetric Open Cover ⟹ Symmetric)
Let 𝑋 be a topological space with open cover ⋃𝑖=1𝑛𝑈𝑖 such that 𝑈𝑖 are symmetric in the subspace topology. Then 𝑋 is symmetric.
Proof. Suppose 𝑥∈cl𝑋({𝑦}) then by (4.1.43) we have 𝑥∈cl𝑈𝑖({𝑦}), whence 𝑦∈cl𝑈𝑖({𝑥}) by (4.1.41) and 𝑦∈cl𝑋({𝑥}). Therefore by (4.1.41) we have 𝑋 is symmetric. ∎
Proposition 4.1.45

Let 𝑋 be a topological space. Then the following are equivalent

  1. 𝑋 is both Symmetric and Kolmogorov
  2. Every point 𝑥∈𝑋 is closed.
Corollary 4.1.46
Let 𝑋 be a symmetric topological space. Then every point of 𝑋0 is closed.
