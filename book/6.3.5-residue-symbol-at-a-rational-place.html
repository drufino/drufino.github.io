6.3.5 Residue Symbol at a Rational Place

The residue symbol Res𝑃(𝑧d𝑡) built in this subsection is defined via the Laurent coefficients of 𝑧𝜕𝜋(𝑡) relative to a choice of uniformiser 𝜋, and shown independent of that choice (6.3.31) – exactly the invariance a genuine differential-form residue ought to have. This invariance is not straightforward in characteristic 𝑝: it reduces (6.3.31) to computing (𝜓𝑛𝜓′)−1 for the change-of-uniformiser series 𝜓, an identity which the naive derivative-of-a-power argument only settles when 𝑛+1 is invertible – automatic in characteristic 0, but not when 𝑝∣(𝑛+1). We settle it uniformly in every characteristic by proving the identity once, universally, over ℤ[𝑎1,𝑎1−1,𝑎2,𝑎3,…] (indeterminate coefficients, torsion-free over ℤ) and specialising (6.3.26) – the same universal-ℤ-polynomial philosophy used, e.g., in [Sti09] (Chap. 4, Prop. 4.2.9); another route entirely avoiding a characteristic-𝑝 detour is via finite-potent operators [Tat68] (§§1–2).

We restrict throughout this subsection to rational places 𝑃, that is 𝑘(𝔪𝑃)=𝑘 – geometrically, points of the curve valued in the constant field 𝑘 itself, rather than in some finite extension of it. This is exactly what allows us to embed 𝐾 in 𝑘((𝑋)).

Proposition 6.3.23 (Laurent Coefficients at a Rational Place)

Let 𝑃 be a rational place of 𝐾/𝑘, 𝜋 a uniformiser of 𝒪︀𝑃. There is a unique 𝑘-linear map

𝐿𝜋:𝐾→𝑘((𝑋))

with 𝐿𝜋(𝜋𝑛)=𝑋𝑛 and 𝑣𝑋(𝐿𝜋(𝑧))=𝑣𝑃(𝑧) for every 𝑧∈𝐾⋆ (𝐿𝜋 preserves valuation; 𝑣𝑋 being the valuation of the DVR 𝑘[[𝑋]] (3.10.7)).

Writing 𝐿𝜋(𝑧)=∑𝑗𝑎𝑗𝑋𝑗 for 𝑧∈𝐾⋆: 𝑎𝑗=0 for 𝑗<𝑣𝑃(𝑧) and 𝑎𝑣𝑃(𝑧)≠0, and for every 𝑛≥𝑣𝑃(𝑧),

𝑧−∑𝑗=𝑣𝑃(𝑧)𝑛𝑎𝑗𝜋𝑗∈𝔪𝑃𝑛+1

(the Laurent coefficients of 𝑧 at 𝑃 relative to 𝜋). Moreover 𝐿𝜋 is injective, 𝐿𝜋(𝒪︀𝑃)⊆𝑘[[𝑋]] (equivalently, 𝑧∈𝒪︀𝑃⟺𝐿𝜋(𝑧)∈𝑘[[𝑋]], for every 𝑧∈𝐾), and 𝐿𝜋 is a 𝑘-algebra homomorphism.

Proof. Drafted by Claude Sonnet 5, reviewed by David Rufino.

As 𝑃 is rational, 𝑘→𝒪︀𝑃→𝒪︀𝑃/𝔪𝑃=𝑘(𝔪𝑃) is an isomorphism, that is 𝑘⊆𝒪︀𝑃 is a coefficient field for the DVR 𝒪︀𝑃. By (3.34.22) (with 𝐴≔𝒪︀𝑃, 𝔪≔𝔪𝑃), there is an injective 𝑘-algebra homomorphism 𝐿𝜋:𝐾→𝑘((𝑋)), extending the injective 𝐿𝜋:𝒪︀𝑃→𝑘[[𝑋]] of that proposition, with 𝐿𝜋(𝜋)=𝑋.

It is valuation-preserving: for 𝑧=𝑎/𝑠∈𝐾⋆ (𝑎,𝑠∈𝒪︀𝑃), 𝐿𝜋(𝑧)=𝐿𝜋(𝑎)𝐿𝜋(𝑠)−1 (that proposition’s extension formula), so 𝑣𝑋(𝐿𝜋(𝑧))=𝑣𝑋(𝐿𝜋(𝑎))−𝑣𝑋(𝐿𝜋(𝑠))=𝑣𝑃(𝑎)−𝑣𝑃(𝑠)=𝑣𝑃(𝑧) (multiplicativity of 𝑣𝑋 (3.10.6), and the valuation formula on 𝒪︀𝑃, that proposition again). In particular 𝑧∈𝒪︀𝑃⟺𝑣𝑃(𝑧)≥0⟺𝑣𝑋(𝐿𝜋(𝑧))≥0⟺𝐿𝜋(𝑧)∈𝑘[[𝑋]], for 𝑧∈𝐾.

𝐿𝜋(𝜋𝑛)=𝑋𝑛 for every 𝑛∈ℤ. For 𝑛≥0 this is 𝐿𝜋(𝜋)𝑛=𝑋𝑛 (𝐿𝜋 multiplicative); for 𝑛<0, as 𝐿𝜋 is a field homomorphism and 𝑋=𝐿𝜋(𝜋)≠0, 𝐿𝜋(𝜋−1)=𝐿𝜋(𝜋)−1=𝑋−1, so 𝐿𝜋(𝜋𝑛)=𝐿𝜋(𝜋−1)−𝑛=𝑋𝑛.

Laurent coefficients. Let 𝑧∈𝐾⋆, 𝑛0≔𝑣𝑃(𝑧), and 𝑢≔𝑧𝜋−𝑛0∈𝒪︀𝑃, a unit (as 𝑣𝑃(𝑢)=0, (3.31.2)). By (3.34.22), for every 𝑚≥0 there is a unique (𝑐0(𝑢),…,𝑐𝑚(𝑢))∈𝑘𝑚+1 with 𝑢−∑𝑖=0𝑚𝑐𝑖(𝑢)𝜋𝑖∈𝔪𝑃𝑚+1, compatible as 𝑚 varies, and 𝑐0(𝑢)≠0 (else 𝑢∈𝔪𝑃, contradicting 𝑢 a unit); setting 𝑎𝑗≔𝑐𝑗−𝑛0(𝑢) for 𝑗≥𝑛0 gives 𝑎𝑛0=𝑐0(𝑢)≠0, and, multiplying the congruence at 𝑚≔𝑛−𝑛0 by 𝜋𝑛0,

𝑧−∑𝑗=𝑛0𝑛𝑎𝑗𝜋𝑗=𝜋𝑛0(𝑢−∑𝑖=0𝑛−𝑛0𝑐𝑖(𝑢)𝜋𝑖)∈𝜋𝑛0𝔪𝑃𝑛−𝑛0+1=𝔪𝑃𝑛+1

for every 𝑛≥𝑛0 (using 𝔪𝑃=(𝜋)). As 𝐿𝜋(𝑧)=𝐿𝜋(𝜋𝑛0)𝐿𝜋(𝑢)=𝑋𝑛0∑𝑖𝑐𝑖(𝑢)𝑋𝑖=∑𝑗≥𝑛0𝑎𝑗𝑋𝑗, this is exactly the stated Laurent coefficient sequence for 𝑧; uniqueness of (𝑎𝑗)𝑗 follows from that of (𝑐𝑖(𝑢))𝑖 (any sequence satisfying the congruence gives, dividing by 𝜋𝑛0, a coefficient sequence for 𝑢, forced equal to (𝑐𝑖(𝑢))𝑖 by (3.34.22)‘s uniqueness).

Uniqueness among such maps. Suppose 𝜙:𝐾→𝑘((𝑋)) is 𝑘-linear with 𝜙(𝜋𝑛)=𝑋𝑛 for every 𝑛∈ℤ and 𝑣𝑋(𝜙(𝑧))=𝑣𝑃(𝑧) for every 𝑧∈𝐾⋆. Fix 𝑧∈𝐾⋆ with Laurent coefficients (𝑎𝑗)𝑗≥𝑣𝑃(𝑧) as above; for every 𝑛≥𝑣𝑃(𝑧), writing 𝑧=∑𝑗=𝑣𝑃(𝑧)𝑛𝑎𝑗𝜋𝑗+𝑟𝑛 with 𝑟𝑛∈𝔪𝑃𝑛+1 (i.e. 𝑟𝑛=0 or 𝑣𝑃(𝑟𝑛)≥𝑛+1), 𝑘-linearity and 𝜙(𝜋𝑗)=𝑋𝑗 give

Definition 6.3.24 (Residue Symbol of a Laurent Series)

Let 𝐴 be a commutative ring, 𝜋 an indeterminate, and 𝑧,𝑡∈𝐴((𝜋)). Write 𝜕𝜋 for the formal derivative, 𝜕𝜋(∑𝑛𝑎𝑛𝜋𝑛)≔∑𝑛𝑛𝑎𝑛𝜋𝑛−1, and define

Res𝜋(𝑧d𝑡)≔(𝑧𝜕𝜋(𝑡))−1∈𝐴,

the coefficient of 𝜋−1 in 𝑧𝜕𝜋(𝑡).

Definition 6.3.25 (Residue Symbol at a Rational Place)

Let 𝑃 be a rational place of 𝐾/𝑘, 𝜋 a uniformiser, and 𝑡,𝑧∈𝐾. Let 𝐿𝜋:𝐾↪︎𝑘((𝜋)) be the completion embedding, and define

Res𝑃(𝑧d𝑡)≔Res𝜋(𝐿𝜋(𝑧)d𝐿𝜋(𝑡))∈𝑘,

where Res𝜋 is the residue of (6.3.24) with 𝐴≔𝑘, and d𝐿𝜋(𝑡)≔𝜕𝜋(𝐿𝜋(𝑡))d𝜋.

This is the special case 𝐴=𝑘 of (6.3.24), applied to 𝐿𝜋(𝑧),𝐿𝜋(𝑡)∈𝑘((𝜋)). Explicitly, since 𝐿𝜋 commutes with the uniformiser derivation, 𝐿𝜋(𝜕𝜋(𝑡))=𝜕𝜋(𝐿𝜋(𝑡)), one has

which is the formula used previously. We show that it is independent of the choice of uniformiser 𝜋 below (6.3.31).

Lemma 6.3.26 (Residue of 𝜓𝑛𝜓′)

Let 𝜓∈𝑋𝑘[[𝑋]] with 𝜓′(0)≠0 (equivalently, 𝜓 has order exactly 1). For every 𝑛∈ℤ,

(𝜓𝑛𝜓′)−1=𝛿𝑛,−1
Remark 6.3.27
The simplest proof using the identity (𝑛+1)𝜓𝑛𝜓′=(𝜓𝑛+1)′ fails when char(𝑘)=𝑝∣𝑛+1, so some additional work is required.

Proof. Drafted by Claude Sonnet 5, reviewed by David Rufino.

𝑛≥0. 𝜓∈𝑋𝑘[[𝑋]] has no negative powers, so neither do 𝜓𝑛 or 𝜓′; their product has no 𝑋−1 term, giving 0, matching 𝛿𝑛,−1=0.

𝑛=−1. Write 𝜓(𝑋)=𝑋𝑣(𝑋) with 𝑣∈𝑘[[𝑋]]⋆ a unit (𝑣(0)=𝜓′(0)≠0). Then 𝜓′=𝑣+𝑋𝑣′, so

𝜓−1𝜓′=𝑣+𝑋𝑣′𝑋𝑣=1𝑋+𝑣′𝑣

The second term is a genuine power series (product of 𝑣′ and the unit 𝑣−1, both in 𝑘[[𝑋]]), so has zero −1-coefficient; the first contributes (𝑋−1)−1=1. Total: 1, matching 𝛿−1,−1.

𝑛≤−2: a universal identity. Fix 𝑛≤−2; we prove the identity over a universal coefficient ring first, then specialise (see the remark above for why). Let 𝑅≔ℤ[𝑎1,𝑎2,𝑎3,…][𝑎1−1], the polynomial ring over ℤ in countably many indeterminates 𝑎1,𝑎2,…, localised to invert 𝑎1 – an integral domain of characteristic 0 (a localisation of the domain ℤ[𝑎1,𝑎2,…], itself a domain as a polynomial ring over the domain ℤ, (3.8.3), applied to finitely many variables at a time since any product only ever involves finitely many of them). Let Ψ≔∑𝑖≥1𝑎𝑖𝑋𝑖∈𝑋𝑅[[𝑋]], so Ψ=𝑎1𝑋⋅𝑈 for 𝑈≔1+∑𝑖≥2(𝑎𝑖/𝑎1)𝑋𝑖−1∈1+𝑋𝑅[[𝑋]]; as 𝑈 has constant coefficient 1∈𝑅⋆, (3.10.2) gives 𝑈∈𝑅[[𝑋]]⋆, and hence Ψ∈𝑅((𝑋))⋆ too (𝑎1∈𝑅⋆, and 𝑋 a unit of 𝑅((𝑋)) directly by construction of the formal Laurent series ring, (3.10.4)). So Ψ𝑛Ψ′∈𝑅((𝑋)) is well-defined, with a well-defined coefficient of 𝑋−1, which we still write (Ψ𝑛Ψ′)−1∈𝑅.

As before (the power rule and vanishing of a derivative’s 𝑋−1-coefficient being valid over any commutative ring, not just 𝑘), (𝑛+1)Ψ𝑛Ψ′=(Ψ𝑛+1)′ ((3.10.10), Ψ being a unit) has vanishing 𝑋−1-coefficient:

Specialising. Write 𝜓=∑𝑖≥1𝑐𝑖𝑋𝑖, so 𝑐1=𝜓′(0)≠0 by hypothesis. As 𝑐1∈𝑘⋆, the assignment 𝑎1↦𝑐1, 𝑎𝑖↦𝑐𝑖 (𝑖≥2) extends, by the universal property of localisation (3.6.5) (applied to ℤ[𝑎1,𝑎2,…]→𝑘, 𝑎1↦𝑐1 a unit), to a ring homomorphism 𝜙:𝑅→𝑘. Applying 𝜙 coefficientwise gives a ring homomorphism 𝜙̂:𝑅((𝑋))→𝑘((𝑋)) (directly from the convolution formula for products, (3.10.4)) with 𝜙̂(Ψ)=𝜓, compatible with the formal derivative (𝜙̂(𝑓)′=𝜙̂(𝑓′), immediate from the coefficientwise formulas 𝑓′=∑𝑚𝑚𝑐𝑚𝑋𝑚−1 and 𝜙(𝑚𝑐𝑚)=𝑚𝜙(𝑐𝑚)) and with the coefficient of 𝑋−1 (literally the same index, carried along by 𝜙). So

(𝜓𝑛𝜓′)−1=𝜙̂(Ψ𝑛Ψ′)−1=𝜙((Ψ𝑛Ψ′)−1)=𝜙(0)=0

using (Ψ𝑛Ψ′)−1=0 in 𝑅 (just shown), matching 𝛿𝑛,−1=0 for 𝑛≤−2. ∎

Corollary 6.3.31 (Well-Definedness of the Residue Symbol at a Rational Place)
