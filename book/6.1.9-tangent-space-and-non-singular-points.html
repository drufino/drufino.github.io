6.1.9 Tangent Space and Non-Singular Points

We propose two definitions for the tangent space and show that under mild technical conditions they are naturally isomorphic. The latter definition may be identified geometrically.

Definition 6.1.65

Let 𝑋 be an affine variety and let 𝑊⊂𝑋 be an irreducible subset. We define the cotangent space at 𝑊 to be the 𝑘(𝔪𝑋,𝑊)-vector space

𝑇𝑊⋆𝑋≔𝔪𝑋,𝑊/𝔪𝑋,𝑊2

and the tangent space to be the 𝑘(𝔪𝑋,𝑊)-vector space

𝑇𝑊𝑋≔Der𝑘(𝒪︀𝑋,𝑊,𝑘(𝔪𝑋,𝑊))

For points this has a concrete interpretation

Proposition 6.1.67 (Dimension Formula of Tangent Space)

Let 𝑋=𝑉(𝔞)⊂𝔸𝑘𝑛 be an integral affine variety, 𝑊⊂𝑋 an irreducible subset. Suppose that 𝔞=⟨𝐹1,…,𝐹𝑚⟩ and let 𝜉≔(𝑋―1,…,𝑋―𝑛)∈𝑘(𝔪𝑋,𝑊)𝑛 be the image of the coordinate functions. Then we have the dimension formula

dim𝑇𝑊𝑋=dimDer(𝑘[𝑋],𝑘(𝔪𝑋,𝑊))=𝑛−rk(𝜕𝐹𝑖𝜕𝑋𝑗(𝜉))

Suppose that 𝑍⊂𝑊 is an irreducible subset then we have the inequality

dim𝑇𝑍𝑋≥dim𝑇𝑊𝑋

and

dim𝑇𝑊𝑋≥dim𝑇𝑋𝑋=Der(𝑘(𝑋))

which equals dim𝑋 when 𝑘(𝑋) is separably generated (e.g. 𝑘 is perfect).

Proof. We have isomorphisms

dim𝑇𝑊𝑋≔Der𝑘(𝒪︀𝑋,𝑊,𝑘(𝔪𝑋,𝑊))→(6.1.57)Der𝑘(𝒪︀𝑋(𝑋)𝔐𝑋,𝑊,𝑘(𝔪𝑋,𝑊))→(3.26.10)Der𝑘(𝒪︀𝑋(𝑋),𝑘(𝔪𝑋,𝑊))→(6.1.26)Der𝑘(𝑘[𝑋],𝑘(𝔪𝑋,𝑊))

and the rank formula follows from (3.35.41) and (3.3.134).

Let 𝜁 be the corresponding generic point of 𝑍, then because 𝔐𝑋,𝑊⊂𝔐𝑋,𝑍 we may construct a 𝑘-algebra homomorphism 𝑘[𝜉]→𝑘[𝜁]. So by the determinant criteria of rank (3.5.40) we deduce that

Observe that 𝑘(𝔪𝑋,𝑋)≕𝑘(𝑋) and 𝑇𝑋𝑋→∼Der𝑘(𝑘[𝑋],𝑘(𝑋))→∼Der𝑘(𝑘(𝑋)). When 𝑘(𝑋) is separably generated this equals dim𝑋 by (3.35.44). ∎

Definition 6.1.71 (Non-Singular Points)

Let 𝑋 be an affine variety and 𝑊⊂𝑋 be an irreducible subset. We say that 𝑋 is non-singular at 𝑊 if

  1. 𝑊 is contained in only one irreducible component 𝑋𝛼, and
  2. dim𝑇𝑊𝑋=dimDer𝑘(𝑘(𝑋𝛼))

Note that by (6.1.61) a) ⟺ 𝒪︀𝑋,𝑊 is an integral domain.

Recall by (6.1.67) that dim𝑇𝑊𝑋=dim𝑇𝑊𝑋𝛼≥dimDer𝑘(𝑘(𝑋𝛼)) and in the case of 𝑘 perfect this equals dim𝑋𝛼.

Definition 6.1.74 (Regular Point)

Let 𝑋 be an affine variety and 𝑊⊂𝑋 an irreducible subset. Then we say that 𝑊 is regular if

  1. 𝑊 is contained in precisely one irreducible component 𝑋𝛼, and
  2. 𝒪︀𝑋,𝑊 is a regular local ring (i.e. dim𝑇𝑊⋆𝑋=dim𝒪︀𝑋,𝑊)

We say 𝑋 is regular if it is regular at all irreducible subsets.

Fortunately these concepts are typically equivalent.
