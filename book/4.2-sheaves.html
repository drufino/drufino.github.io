4.2 Sheaves

Sheaves are typically used to organise sets of functions which are defined by a local differentiability condition (e.g. holomorphic, smooth, etc.). However in some cases it may be convenient to take an abstract category-theoretic approach as the proofs become more conceptual and naturally lead to further generalisations where the topological space is replaced by an arbitrary category. However for the sheafification construction we take (the standard) concrete approach as this is simpler and nicely coincides with the notion of “étale space”.

In general we assume that the value category 𝒞︀ admits arbitrary products and equalisers (and therefore also limits, and in particular terminal objects), as well as filtered colimits / direct limits. In some cases we may also require it to be algebraic.

We now show that 𝑝 is a coequaliser of (𝛼,𝛽) in the category of presheaves. Evidently 𝑝∘𝛼=𝑝∘𝛽, since this holds pointwise by hypothesis. Suppose that 𝑝′:𝒢︀→ℋ︀ is a morphism of presheaves with 𝑝′∘𝛼=𝑝′∘𝛽, then 𝑝𝑈′∘𝛼𝑈=𝑝𝑈′∘𝛽𝑈 whence there is a unique factorisation ℎ𝑈 such that ℎ𝑈∘𝑝𝑈=𝑝𝑈′.

The large square and left-hand square commute, which we may use to show that

Finally suppose ℎ′ is another morphism of presheaves with ℎ′∘𝑝=𝑝′. Then for every 𝑈, ℎ𝑈′∘𝑝𝑈=𝑝𝑈′=ℎ𝑈∘𝑝𝑈, so by the uniqueness of the pointwise factorisation ℎ𝑈′=ℎ𝑈, that is ℎ′=ℎ.

4.2.10 Image Sheaf

Unlike kernels and equalisers (4.2.24), the image is not itself a limit — it is initial in the category of factorisations through a monomorphism (2.6.167) — so the argument via (4.2.3) and (2.6.99) used there does not apply, and the pointwise image presheaf need not satisfy the sheaf condition even when ℱ︀,𝒢︀ are sheaves; instead an image sheaf is obtained by sheafifying the image presheaf (4.2.40). We do however first record the following easier pointwise construction of an image presheaf, whenever 𝒞︀ has regular epi-mono factorisations.

So applying (4.2.33) to the pair of morphisms of presheaves (𝑝1,𝑝2):ℱ︀×𝒢︀ℱ︀⇉ℱ︀, this pointwise family of coequalisers assembles into a presheaf coeq(𝑝1,𝑝2) with restriction maps uniquely determined by making the induced map 𝑞≔{𝑒𝑈} a morphism of presheaves, and (𝑞,coeq(𝑝1,𝑝2)) is a coequaliser of (𝑝1,𝑝2) in 𝐏𝐒𝐡(𝑋;𝒞︀).

Restriction maps. Fix 𝑉⊆𝑈. Since 𝛼 is a morphism of presheaves, 𝜌𝑈𝑉𝒢︀∘𝛼𝑈=𝛼𝑉∘𝜌𝑈𝑉ℱ︀, so applying (2.6.177) to the regular epi-mono factorisations 𝛼𝑈=𝑚𝑈∘𝑒𝑈, 𝛼𝑉=𝑚𝑉∘𝑒𝑉 with 𝑎≔𝜌𝑈𝑉ℱ︀, 𝑏≔𝜌𝑈𝑉𝒢︀ gives a unique morphism 𝜌𝑈𝑉Im:Im(𝛼𝑈)→Im(𝛼𝑉) making the following diagram commute

namely

𝜌𝑈𝑉Im∘𝑒𝑈=𝑒𝑉∘𝜌𝑈𝑉ℱ︀and𝑚𝑉∘𝜌𝑈𝑉Im=𝜌𝑈𝑉𝒢︀∘𝑚𝑈

The first identity is precisely the condition for 𝑒𝛼 to be a morphism of presheaves, and the second for 𝑚𝛼 to be one.

Im(𝛼) is a presheaf. By the functoriality clause of (2.6.177) (taking 𝑎=1ℱ︀(𝑈), 𝑏=1𝒢︀(𝑈)), 𝜌𝑈𝑈Im=1Im(𝛼𝑈); and for 𝑊⊆𝑉⊆𝑈, taking 𝑎≔𝜌𝑈𝑉ℱ︀, 𝑏≔𝜌𝑈𝑉𝒢︀, 𝑐≔𝜌𝑉𝑊ℱ︀, 𝑑≔𝜌𝑉𝑊𝒢︀, the composition law gives 𝜌𝑉𝑊Im∘𝜌𝑈𝑉Im=𝜌𝑈𝑊Im (using 𝜌𝑉𝑊ℱ︀∘𝜌𝑈𝑉ℱ︀=𝜌𝑈𝑊ℱ︀, and likewise for 𝒢︀). So Im(𝛼), with these restriction maps, is a presheaf, and 𝑒𝛼, 𝑚𝛼 are morphisms of presheaves. Uniqueness of restriction maps making 𝑒𝛼, 𝑚𝛼 into morphisms of presheaves is immediate from the uniqueness clause of (2.6.177), since these two identities are exactly what is required of 𝑒𝛼,𝑚𝛼.

Finally (𝑚𝛼∘𝑒𝛼)𝑈=𝑚𝑈∘𝑒𝑈=𝛼𝑈 for every 𝑈, so 𝑚𝛼∘𝑒𝛼=𝛼.

Regularity. By (4.2.38) (using that 𝒞︀ has products and equalisers, and each 𝑒𝑈 is regular), 𝑒𝛼 is a regular epimorphism of 𝐏𝐒𝐡(𝑋;𝒞︀). Since 𝑚𝑈 is monic in 𝒞︀ for every 𝑈, 𝑚𝛼 is monic in 𝐏𝐒𝐡(𝑋;𝒞︀) (4.2.28). So 𝛼=𝑚𝛼∘𝑒𝛼 is a regular epi-mono factorisation of 𝛼 in 𝐏𝐒𝐡(𝑋;𝒞︀) (2.6.173), and hence also an image of 𝛼 there (2.6.174).

For the first statement, if 𝒞︀ has regular epi-mono factorisations for every morphism then every 𝛼𝑈:ℱ︀(𝑈)→𝒢︀(𝑈) has one, giving such a family for every morphism 𝛼 of presheaves, so the above applies to every such 𝛼 and 𝐏𝐒𝐡(𝑋;𝒞︀) has regular epi-mono factorisations for every morphism.

The algebraic case. If 𝒞︀ is algebraic, taking Im(𝛼𝑈)≔𝑈(𝛼𝑈)(𝑈(ℱ︀(𝑈))) as in (2.6.196), the restriction maps constructed above may be identified with those inherited from 𝒢︀: since 𝛼 is a morphism of presheaves, for 𝜎∈ℱ︀(𝑈), 𝜌𝑈𝑉𝒢︀(𝛼𝑈(𝜎))=𝛼𝑉(𝜌𝑈𝑉ℱ︀(𝜎))∈Im(𝛼𝑉), so 𝜌𝑈𝑉𝒢︀(Im(𝛼𝑈))⊆Im(𝛼𝑉); by the uniqueness clause of (2.6.177), this restriction of 𝜌𝑈𝑉𝒢︀ is exactly 𝜌𝑈𝑉Im. So in this case Im(𝛼) is simply the subpresheaf of 𝒢︀ of sections pointwise in the image of 𝛼 (4.2.30), with 𝑒𝛼 the (pointwise surjective) corestriction of 𝛼 and 𝑚𝛼 the inclusion — without needing (2.6.177) to construct the restriction maps in the first place. ∎

Proposition 4.2.40 (Image Sheaf)

Let 𝒞︀ be algebraic (2.6.184), hence have regular epi-mono factorisations for every morphism (2.6.196). Then 𝐒𝐡(𝑋;𝒞︀) has regular epi-mono factorisations for every morphism.

More precisely, let 𝛼:ℱ︀→𝒢︀ be a morphism of sheaves, and define

Im(𝛼)(𝑈)≔{𝜏∈𝑈(𝒢︀(𝑈))|𝑈=⋃𝑖∈𝐼𝑈𝑖for some open cover, with𝜏|𝑈𝑖∈𝑈(𝛼𝑈𝑖)(𝑈(ℱ︀(𝑈𝑖)))for every𝑖}

the subsheaf of 𝒢︀ of sections locally in the (pointwise) image of 𝛼 (4.2.32). Then

𝑒:ℱ︀⟶Im(𝛼),𝑒𝑈(𝜎)≔𝛼𝑈(𝜎)and𝑚:Im(𝛼)↪︎𝒢︀the inclusion

are morphisms of sheaves with 𝑚∘𝑒=𝛼, and this is a regular epi-mono factorisation of 𝛼 in 𝐒𝐡(𝑋;𝒞︀); hence (Im(𝛼),𝑒,𝑚) is an image of 𝛼 there (2.6.174).

Proof. Drafted by Claude Sonnet 5, reviewed by David Rufino.

Setup. Let (Impsh(𝛼),𝑒𝛼,𝑚𝛼) be the image of 𝛼 in 𝐏𝐒𝐡(𝑋;𝒞︀), so 𝛼=𝑚𝛼∘𝑒𝛼 is a regular epi-mono factorisation there (4.2.39), with Impsh(𝛼)(𝑈)=Im(𝛼𝑈)⊆𝒢︀(𝑈) and (𝑚𝛼)𝑈 the inclusion. In particular 𝑚𝛼 is a monomorphism of presheaves (4.2.28), and “locally in the image of 𝑚𝛼” is the same condition as “locally in the image of 𝛼”, so applying (4.2.32) to 𝑚𝛼: Im(𝛼) (as defined above) is a subsheaf of 𝒢︀, and the corestriction 𝜄′:Impsh(𝛼)→Im(𝛼) of 𝑚𝛼 is a sheafification of Impsh(𝛼). Since 𝜄′ is a corestriction of the injective map (𝑚𝛼)𝑈 for every 𝑈, 𝜄′ is itself monic (4.2.28).

Factorisation. For 𝜎∈ℱ︀(𝑈), 𝜄𝑈′((𝑒𝛼)𝑈(𝜎))=(𝑚𝛼)𝑈((𝑒𝛼)𝑈(𝜎))=𝛼𝑈(𝜎), so 𝑒=𝜄′∘𝑒𝛼; as ℱ︀ and Im(𝛼) are sheaves and 𝜄′,𝑒𝛼 are morphisms of presheaves, 𝑒 is a morphism of sheaves. Writing 𝑗:Im(𝛼)↪︎𝒢︀ for the inclusion, 𝑗∘𝜄′=𝑚𝛼, so

𝑚∘𝑒=𝑗∘𝜄′∘𝑒𝛼=𝑚𝛼∘𝑒𝛼=𝛼

Monicity. 𝑚 is the inclusion of the subsheaf Im(𝛼) into 𝒢︀, hence monic by the remark following (4.2.30).

Regularity. By (4.2.27), 𝑒𝛼 has a kernel pair (ℱ︀×Impsh(𝛼)ℱ︀,𝑝1,𝑝2) in 𝐏𝐒𝐡(𝑋;𝒞︀), with 𝑈-components {(𝜎,𝜏)∈ℱ︀(𝑈)2|(𝑒𝛼)𝑈(𝜎)=(𝑒𝛼)𝑈(𝜏)}; as each (𝑒𝛼)𝑈=𝑒𝑈 is regular (4.2.38) exhibits 𝑒𝛼 as its coequaliser there.

As 𝜄′ is monic, (𝑒𝛼)𝑈(𝜎)=(𝑒𝛼)𝑈(𝜏) if and only if 𝜄𝑈′((𝑒𝛼)𝑈(𝜎))=𝜄𝑈′((𝑒𝛼)𝑈(𝜏)), that is 𝑒𝑈(𝜎)=𝑒𝑈(𝜏), so the 𝑈-components of the kernel pairs of 𝑒𝛼 and of 𝑒 agree, with the same projections 𝑝1,𝑝2 into ℱ︀; by the uniqueness clause of (4.2.27), the two kernel-pair presheaves coincide. Since ℱ︀ and Im(𝛼) are both sheaves, applying (4.2.27) to 𝑒 directly, in its sheaf case, shows this common kernel pair is itself a sheaf, with 𝑝1,𝑝2 morphisms of sheaves.

It remains to show 𝑒 is a coequaliser of (𝑝1,𝑝2) in 𝐒𝐡(𝑋;𝒞︀). Let 𝑞′:ℱ︀→𝒦︀ be a morphism of sheaves with 𝑞′∘𝑝1=𝑞′∘𝑝2. As 𝑒𝛼 is a coequaliser of (𝑝1,𝑝2) in 𝐏𝐒𝐡(𝑋;𝒞︀), there is a unique morphism of presheaves 𝑞̃:Impsh(𝛼)→𝒦︀ with 𝑞̃∘𝑒𝛼=𝑞′. 𝑞̄∘𝑒=𝑞̄∘𝜄′∘𝑒𝛼=𝑞̃∘𝑒𝛼=𝑞′

For uniqueness, if 𝑞̄′:Im(𝛼)→𝒦︀ also satisfies 𝑞̄′∘𝑒=𝑞′, then (𝑞̄′∘𝜄′)∘𝑒𝛼=𝑞̄′∘𝑒=𝑞′=𝑞̃∘𝑒𝛼, and as 𝑒𝛼 is epic (2.6.135), 𝑞̄′∘𝜄′=𝑞̃; by uniqueness in the bijection (−)♯, 𝑞̄′=𝑞̄.

Corollary 4.2.42 (Stalk Criterion for Surjectivity)
Let 𝒞︀ be algebraic (2.6.184), 𝛼:ℱ︀→𝒢︀ a morphism of sheaves, and 𝛼=𝑚∘𝑒 a regular epi-mono factorisation of 𝛼 in 𝐒𝐡(𝑋;𝒞︀) (4.2.40). Then 𝑚 is an isomorphism if and only if 𝛼𝑥 is surjective for every 𝑥∈𝑋.

Proof. Drafted by Claude Sonnet 5, reviewed by David Rufino.

The canonical factorisation. Write 𝛼=𝑚0∘𝑒0 for the canonical factorisation of (4.2.40), where 𝑒0=𝜂∘𝑒𝛼, with (Impsh(𝛼),𝑒𝛼,𝑚𝛼) the image of 𝛼 in 𝐏𝐒𝐡(𝑋;𝒞︀), built from pointwise regular epi-mono factorisations {𝛼𝑈=𝑚𝑈∘𝑒𝑈}𝑈⊂𝑋 (4.2.39), and 𝜂:Impsh(𝛼)→Im(𝛼)≕(Impsh(𝛼))+ the corestriction of 𝑚𝛼, a sheafification of Impsh(𝛼) (4.2.32), i.e. the unit of the sheafification as defined in (4.2.13).

Fix 𝑥∈𝑋. As 𝒞︀ is algebraic and 𝑒𝑈 is a regular epimorphism of 𝒞︀ for every 𝑈, (2.6.195) shows 𝑒𝑈 is surjective for every 𝑈. Given 𝜉∈Impsh(𝛼)𝑥, by (4.2.6) 𝜉=𝜄𝑥 for some 𝜄∈Impsh(𝛼)(𝑈)=Im(𝛼𝑈), 𝑈∋𝑥; as 𝑒𝑈 is surjective, 𝜄=𝑒𝑈(𝜎) for some 𝜎∈ℱ︀(𝑈), so 𝜉=𝜄𝑥=(𝑒𝑈(𝜎))𝑥=(𝑒𝛼)𝑥(𝜎𝑥). So (𝑒𝛼)𝑥 is surjective.

By (4.2.18), 𝜂𝑥 is an isomorphism, in particular surjective. Hence (𝑒0)𝑥=𝜂𝑥∘(𝑒𝛼)𝑥, being a composite of surjections, is surjective.

Since 𝛼𝑥=(𝑚0)𝑥∘(𝑒0)𝑥 (4.2.7) with (𝑒0)𝑥 surjective, 𝛼𝑥 is surjective if and only if (𝑚0)𝑥 is surjective: if (𝑚0)𝑥 is surjective this is immediate, as a composite of surjections; conversely if 𝛼𝑥 is surjective then Im((𝑚0)𝑥)⊇Im(𝛼𝑥)=𝒢︀𝑥. So by (4.2.29), 𝑚0 is an isomorphism if and only if 𝛼𝑥 is surjective for every 𝑥∈𝑋.

General regular epi-mono factorisations. Let 𝛼=𝑚∘𝑒 be any regular epi-mono factorisation of 𝛼 in 𝐒𝐡(𝑋;𝒞︀). By (2.6.178), applied to this and the canonical factorisation 𝛼=𝑚0∘𝑒0, there is an isomorphism 𝜙 with 𝑚0∘𝜙=𝑚. So 𝑚 is an isomorphism if and only if 𝑚0 is (composing with the isomorphism 𝜙 on the right preserves being an isomorphism, in either direction), which by the above holds if and only if 𝛼𝑥 is surjective for every 𝑥∈𝑋. ∎

4.2.11 Exactness

Definition 4.2.43 (Exact Sequence of Sheaves)
Let 𝒞︀ be pointed and algebraic; then 𝐒𝐡(𝑋;𝒞︀) is evidently pointed (zero morphisms computed pointwise, the constant zero presheaf being a zero object), with a regular epi-mono factorisation (4.2.40) for every morphism, so (2.6.200) applies with 𝐒𝐡(𝑋;𝒞︀) in place of 𝒞︀: a pair of composable morphisms of sheaves ℱ︀⟶𝛼𝒢︀⟶𝛽ℋ︀ is exact at 𝒢︀ if Im(𝛼)=ker(𝛽), that is (Im(𝛼),𝑚) is (literally) a kernel of 𝛽, for any (equivalently, by the same argument, every) choice of image factorisation 𝛼=𝑚∘𝑒 — in particular we may as well take the canonical construction of (4.2.40).
Proposition 4.2.44 (Concrete Criterion for Exactness of Sheaves)

Let 𝒞︀ be pointed and algebraic and ℱ︀⟶𝛼𝒢︀⟶𝛽ℋ︀ a pair of composable morphisms of sheaves. Regard Im(𝛼) (4.2.40) and ker(𝛽) (4.2.25) as the canonical constructions, both literal subsheaves of 𝒢︀ since 𝒞︀ is algebraic. Then the following are equivalent

  1. the pair is exact at 𝒢︀ (4.2.43) (for any choice of image factorisation of 𝛼)
  2. Im(𝛼)(𝑈)=ker(𝛽)(𝑈), as subsets of 𝒢︀(𝑈), for every open 𝑈⊂𝑋
  3. 𝛽∘𝛼=0, and every section of ker(𝛽)(𝑈) is locally in the image of 𝛼, for every open 𝑈⊂𝑋

Proof. Drafted by Claude Sonnet 5, reviewed by David Rufino.

Write 𝛼=𝑚∘𝑒 for the canonical image factorisation (4.2.40) and 𝑖:ker(𝛽)↪︎𝒢︀ for the canonical kernel (4.2.25).

𝑎)⟺𝑏) Reduction to the canonical representatives. By (4.2.43) (applied with 𝐒𝐡(𝑋;𝒞︀) in place of 𝒞︀), exactness at 𝒢︀ does not depend on the choice of image factorisation of 𝛼, so it suffices to treat the canonical representative 𝑚: the pair is exact at 𝒢︀ if and only if (Im(𝛼),𝑚) is a kernel of 𝛽. Since (ker(𝛽),𝑖) is also a kernel of 𝛽, by (2.6.80) this holds if and only if there is an isomorphism 𝜃:Im(𝛼)→ker(𝛽) with 𝑖∘𝜃=𝑚.

(⟸) If Im(𝛼)(𝑈)=ker(𝛽)(𝑈) for every 𝑈, then 𝑚=𝑖 as morphisms of sheaves, both being the inclusion of this common subsheaf into 𝒢︀, with the (unique, (2.6.193)) induced structure making it a morphism. So 𝜃≔1Im(𝛼) satisfies 𝑖∘𝜃=𝑖=𝑚.

(⟹) Suppose there is an isomorphism 𝜃:Im(𝛼)→ker(𝛽) with 𝑖∘𝜃=𝑚. Fix 𝑈⊂𝑋 and 𝜎∈Im(𝛼)(𝑈); since 𝑚𝑈 is the inclusion of Im(𝛼)(𝑈) into 𝒢︀(𝑈), 𝑖𝑈(𝜃𝑈(𝜎))=𝑚𝑈(𝜎)=𝜎, and 𝑖𝑈 is also an inclusion, so 𝜃𝑈(𝜎)=𝜎; hence 𝜎∈ker(𝛽)(𝑈), giving Im(𝛼)(𝑈)⊆ker(𝛽)(𝑈). As 𝜃 is an isomorphism, 𝜃𝑈 is a bijection onto ker(𝛽)(𝑈), so every element of ker(𝛽)(𝑈) equals 𝜃𝑈(𝜎)=𝜎 for some 𝜎∈Im(𝛼)(𝑈), giving ker(𝛽)(𝑈)⊆Im(𝛼)(𝑈). So Im(𝛼)(𝑈)=ker(𝛽)(𝑈) for every 𝑈.

𝑏)⟺𝑐) Condition 𝑏) is, for every 𝑈, the conjunction of Im(𝛼)(𝑈)⊆ker(𝛽)(𝑈) and ker(𝛽)(𝑈)⊆Im(𝛼)(𝑈); the latter is precisely “every section of ker(𝛽)(𝑈) is locally in the image of 𝛼” (4.2.40), so it remains to show Im(𝛼)(𝑈)⊆ker(𝛽)(𝑈) for every 𝑈 is equivalent to 𝛽∘𝛼=0.

(⟸) Suppose 𝛽∘𝛼=0. For 𝜏∈Im(𝛼)(𝑈), take a witnessing cover 𝑈=⋃𝑗𝑈𝑗 with 𝜏|𝑈𝑗=𝛼𝑈𝑗(𝜎𝑗) for some 𝜎𝑗∈ℱ︀(𝑈𝑗); then 𝛽𝑈(𝜏)|𝑈𝑗=𝛽𝑈𝑗(𝜏|𝑈𝑗)=𝛽𝑈𝑗(𝛼𝑈𝑗(𝜎𝑗))=(𝛽∘𝛼)𝑈𝑗(𝜎𝑗)=0 for every 𝑗, so by separation of ℋ︀ (4.2.4), 𝛽𝑈(𝜏)=0, i.e. 𝜏∈ker(𝛽)(𝑈).

(⟹) Suppose Im(𝛼)(𝑈)⊆ker(𝛽)(𝑈) for every 𝑈. For any 𝑈 and 𝜎∈ℱ︀(𝑈), 𝛼𝑈(𝜎)∈Im(𝛼)(𝑈) (witnessed by the trivial cover {𝑈}), so 𝛼𝑈(𝜎)∈ker(𝛽)(𝑈), that is 𝛽𝑈(𝛼𝑈(𝜎))=0. As 𝑈,𝜎 were arbitrary, 𝛽∘𝛼=0. ∎

Lemma 4.2.45 (Subsheaves are Determined by Stalks)
