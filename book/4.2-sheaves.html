4.2 Sheaves

Sheaves are typically used to organise sets of functions which are defined by a local differentiability condition (e.g. holomorphic, smooth, etc.). However in some cases it may be convenient to take an abstract category-theoretic approach as the proofs become more conceptual and naturally lead to further generalisations where the topological space is replaced by an arbitrary category. However for the sheafification construction we take (the standard) concrete approach as this is simpler and nicely coincides with the notion of “étale space”.

In general we assume that the value category 𝒞︀ admits arbitrary products and equalisers (and therefore also limits, and in particular terminal objects), as well as filtered colimits / direct limits. In some cases we may also require it to be algebraic.

Definition 4.2.1 (Abstract Presheaf)

Let 𝑋 be a topological space, ℬ︀ a base for the topology and 𝒞︀ a category. A ℬ︀-presheaf on 𝑋 is a functor

ℱ︀:(ℬ︀,⊇)⟶𝒞︀

from the poset category of basic open subsets to 𝒞︀ ordered by reverse inclusion for which

ℱ︀(∅)=⋆is a terminal object in𝒞︀

This means for every pair of open subsets 𝑈,𝑉∈ℬ︀ such that 𝑉⊆𝑈 there exist morphisms 𝜌𝑈𝑉:ℱ︀(𝑈)→ℱ︀(𝑉) which satisfy the following properties

  1. 𝜌𝑈𝑈=1ℱ︀(𝑈)
  2. 𝜌𝑉𝑊∘𝜌𝑈𝑉=𝜌𝑈𝑊 for any basic open subset 𝑊⊂ℬ︀

A morphism of presheaves is precisely a natural transformation. That is 𝛼:ℱ︀→𝒢︀ is a family of morphisms 𝛼𝑈:ℱ︀(𝑈)→𝒢︀(𝑈) for which

𝜌𝑈𝑉∘𝛼𝑈=𝛼𝑉∘𝜌𝑈𝑉

This determines a category 𝐏𝐒𝐡(ℬ︀;𝒞︀). We may omit the value category 𝒞︀ if there are no additional hypotheses.

Definition 4.2.2 (Abstract Sheaf)

Let 𝑋 be a topological space, ℬ︀ a base for the topology closed under finite intersections and ℱ︀ a ℬ︀-presheaf.

For every family of non-empty basic open subsets {𝑈𝑖}𝑖∈𝐼 with 𝑈=⋃𝑖∈𝐼𝑈𝑖, consider the sequence of morphisms

where 𝑒=(𝜌𝑈𝑈𝑖)𝑖∈𝐼, 𝑑0=(𝜌𝑈𝑖(𝑈𝑖∩𝑈𝑗)∘𝜋𝑖)(𝑖,𝑗)∈𝐼×𝐼 and 𝑑1=(𝜌𝑈𝑗(𝑈𝑖∩𝑈𝑗)∘𝜋𝑗)(𝑖,𝑗)∈𝐼×𝐼.

We say that ℱ︀ is a ℬ︀-sheaf if 𝑒 is an equaliser for (𝑑0,𝑑1) for all such families of open subsets.

This determines a full subcategory 𝐒𝐡(ℬ︀;𝒞︀) of 𝐏𝐒𝐡(ℬ︀;𝒞︀).

When ℬ︀ is just the collection of all open subsets of 𝑋 then we say ℱ︀ is a sheaf, and we denote the category by 𝐒𝐡(𝑋;𝒞︀).

It may be convenient to write the sheaf condition as the existence of a limit.

Lemma 4.2.3 (Sheaf Condition as a Limit)

Let ℱ︀ be a presheaf on 𝑋 and 𝑈=⋃𝑖∈𝐼𝑈𝑖 a cover; write 𝑈𝑖𝑗≔𝑈𝑖∩𝑈𝑗 for (𝑖,𝑗)∈𝐼×𝐼, and let 𝑑0,𝑑1:∏𝑖∈𝐼ℱ︀(𝑈𝑖)→∏(𝑖,𝑗)∈𝐼×𝐼ℱ︀(𝑈𝑖𝑗) be as in (4.2.2).

Let 𝐼(1) be the small category with objects 𝐼⊔(𝐼×𝐼) and, for each (𝑖,𝑗)∈𝐼×𝐼, morphisms 𝑢𝑖𝑗:𝑖→(𝑖,𝑗) and 𝑣𝑖𝑗:𝑗→(𝑖,𝑗) (together with identities, and no further morphisms), and let 𝐷ℱ︀:𝐼(1)→𝒞︀ be the diagram

𝐷ℱ︀(𝑖)≔ℱ︀(𝑈𝑖),𝐷ℱ︀((𝑖,𝑗))≔ℱ︀(𝑈𝑖𝑗),𝐷ℱ︀(𝑢𝑖𝑗)≔𝜌𝑈𝑖𝑈𝑖𝑗,𝐷ℱ︀(𝑣𝑖𝑗)≔𝜌𝑈𝑗𝑈𝑖𝑗

Then, as 𝒞︀ has arbitrary products, the contravariant functors 𝒞︀op→𝐒𝐞𝐭

𝑋↦{𝜉:𝑋→∏𝑖∈𝐼ℱ︀(𝑈𝑖)|𝑑0∘𝜉=𝑑1∘𝜉},𝑋↦{cones over𝐷ℱ︀with apex𝑋}

(both via precomposition) are naturally isomorphic, via 𝜉↦(𝜋𝑖∘𝜉)𝑖∈𝐼, extended to (𝑖,𝑗)∈𝐼×𝐼 by 𝐷ℱ︀(𝑢𝑖𝑗)∘𝜋𝑖∘𝜉.

Consequently 𝐷ℱ︀ has (ℱ︀(𝑈),{𝜌𝑈𝑈𝑖}𝑖∈𝐼,{𝜌𝑈𝑈𝑖𝑗}(𝑖,𝑗)∈𝐼×𝐼) as a limit over 𝐼(1) if and only if ℱ︀ satisfies the sheaf condition for this cover (4.2.2).

Proof. Given 𝜉 with 𝑑0∘𝜉=𝑑1∘𝜉, set 𝜉𝑖≔𝜋𝑖∘𝜉 and 𝜉(𝑖,𝑗)≔𝐷ℱ︀(𝑢𝑖𝑗)∘𝜉𝑖; then also 𝜉(𝑖,𝑗)=𝐷ℱ︀(𝑣𝑖𝑗)∘𝜉𝑗, since both sides equal 𝜋(𝑖,𝑗)∘𝑑0∘𝜉=𝜋(𝑖,𝑗)∘𝑑1∘𝜉 by definition of 𝑑0,𝑑1. So (𝑋,{𝜉𝑘}𝑘∈𝐼(1)) is a cone over 𝐷ℱ︀, the only nontrivial conditions being exactly this equality at 𝑢𝑖𝑗,𝑣𝑖𝑗.

Conversely, given a cone (𝑋,{𝜉𝑘}𝑘∈𝐼(1)), bundling (𝜉𝑖)𝑖∈𝐼 via the universal property of the product (2.6.81) gives 𝜉≔(𝜉𝑖)𝑖:𝑋→∏𝑖∈𝐼ℱ︀(𝑈𝑖) with 𝜋𝑖∘𝜉=𝜉𝑖; then for all (𝑖,𝑗), 𝜋(𝑖,𝑗)∘𝑑0∘𝜉=𝐷ℱ︀(𝑢𝑖𝑗)∘𝜉𝑖=𝜉(𝑖,𝑗)=𝐷ℱ︀(𝑣𝑖𝑗)∘𝜉𝑗=𝜋(𝑖,𝑗)∘𝑑1∘𝜉, using the cone conditions at 𝑢𝑖𝑗,𝑣𝑖𝑗, so 𝑑0∘𝜉=𝑑1∘𝜉.

These two constructions are mutually inverse, directly from 𝜋𝑖∘𝜉=𝜉𝑖 and the universal property of the product, and are evidently compatible with precomposition, i.e. natural in 𝑋: for 𝜙:𝑋′→𝑋, both sides simply precompose 𝜉, resp. every 𝜉𝑘, by 𝜙. So 𝐷ℱ︀ has a limit over 𝐼(1) iff (𝑑0,𝑑1) has an equaliser, and the two agree. ∎

Proposition 4.2.4 (Sheaf Conditions for Concrete Categories)

Let 𝑋 be a topological space, ℬ︀ a base for the topology closed under finite intersections and 𝒞︀ is an algebraic category (2.6.184). Then a ℬ︀-presheaf ℱ︀ is a sheaf if and only if it satisfies the following conditions, for every open cover 𝑈=⋃𝑖∈𝐼𝑈𝑖 with 𝑈𝑖 non-empty

  1. 𝜎,𝜏∈ℱ︀(𝑈) such that 𝜌𝑈𝑈𝑖(𝜎)=𝜌𝑈𝑈𝑖(𝜏) for all 𝑖∈𝐼 implies 𝜎=𝜏
  2. 𝜎𝑖∈ℱ︀(𝑈𝑖) such that 𝜌𝑈𝑖(𝑈𝑖∩𝑈𝑗)(𝜎𝑖)=𝜌𝑈𝑗(𝑈𝑖∩𝑈𝑗)(𝜎𝑗) for all (𝑖,𝑗)∈𝐼×𝐼 implies there exists 𝜎∈ℱ︀(𝑈) such that 𝜌𝑈𝑈𝑖(𝜎)=𝜎𝑖.

Proof. Suppose that ℱ︀ is a sheaf in the abstract sense. By assumption the forgetful functor 𝒞︀→𝐒𝐞𝐭 preserves limits and so by (2.6.197) 𝑒 is injective and

Im(𝑒)={(𝜎𝑖)𝑖∈𝐼|𝜎𝑖|𝑈𝑖∩𝑈𝑗=𝜎𝑗|𝑈𝑖∩𝑈𝑗}

Conversely this condition ensures the sequence is an equaliser in 𝐒𝐞𝐭, and so by hypothesis is so in 𝒞︀. ∎
