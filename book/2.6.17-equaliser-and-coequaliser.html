2.6.17 Equaliser and Coequaliser

Definition 2.6.134 (Equaliser)

Consider a commutative diagram in a category 𝒞︀

such that 𝛼∘𝑒=𝛽∘𝑒. We say that (𝑋,𝑒) is an equaliser for (𝛼,𝛽) if for all 𝑒′:𝑋′→𝑌 such that 𝛼∘𝑒′=𝛽∘𝑒′ there is a unique factorisation

This is equivalent to (𝑋,𝑒) being a limit for the obvious functor 𝐹:𝟐→𝒞︀ where 𝟐 denotes the category ·⇉·.

Proposition 2.6.135
Every equaliser is a monomorphism, and every coequaliser is an epimorphism.

Proof. Suppose that (𝑋,𝑒) is an equaliser for 𝛼,𝛽:𝑌⇉𝑍, and 𝑒∘𝑓=𝑒∘𝑔=ℎ. Then evidentally 𝛼∘ℎ=𝛽∘ℎ so there is a unique morphism 𝑘 such that 𝑒∘𝑘=ℎ, whence 𝑘=𝑓=𝑔.

The second statement follows by duality. ∎

Proposition 2.6.136
Suppose (𝑋,𝑒)=eq(𝛼,𝛽). Then 𝛼=𝛽 if and only if 𝑒 is an isomorphism.

Proof. By hypothesis 𝛼∘𝑒=𝛽∘𝑒 so if 𝑒 is an isomorphism 𝛼=𝛼∘𝑒∘𝑒−1=𝛽∘𝑒∘𝑒−1=𝛽 as required.

Conversely if 𝛼=𝛽, then 𝛼∘1𝑌=𝛽∘1𝑌 so there is a morphism 𝑖:𝑌→𝑋 such that 𝑒∘𝑖=1𝑌. Therefore 𝑒 is split-epic and monic (2.6.135) and therefore an isomorphism (2.6.36). ∎

Proposition 2.6.137
Let (𝑋,𝑒) be an equaliser for 𝛼,𝛽:𝑌⇉𝑍. Suppose 𝜙:𝑋′→∼𝑋 be an isomorphism. Then (𝑋′,𝑒∘𝜙) is also an equaliser for (𝛼,𝛽).

Proof. Drafted by Claude Sonnet 5, reviewed by David Rufino.

By definition of Equaliser, (𝑋,𝑒) is a limit for the diagram 𝐹:𝟐→𝒞︀ determined by (𝛼,𝛽), and since 𝛼∘𝑒=𝛽∘𝑒, (𝑋′,𝑒∘𝜙) is a cone for 𝐹. As 𝑒∘𝜙=𝑒∘𝜙, the isomorphism 𝜙:𝑋′→𝑋 is (tautologically) an isomorphism of cones (𝑋′,𝑒∘𝜙)→(𝑋,𝑒), so by (2.6.79) (𝑋′,𝑒∘𝜙) is also a limit for 𝐹, that is, an equaliser for (𝛼,𝛽). ∎

Proposition 2.6.138
Let (𝑋,𝑒) be a coequaliser for 𝛼,𝛽:𝑌⇉𝑍. Suppose 𝜙:𝑋→∼𝑋′ is an isomorphism. Then (𝑋′,𝜙∘𝑒) is also a coequaliser for (𝛼,𝛽).

Proof. Drafted by Claude Sonnet 5, reviewed by David Rufino.

By definition of Coequaliser, (𝑋,𝑒) is a colimit for the diagram 𝐹:𝟐→𝒞︀ determined by (𝛼,𝛽), and since 𝑒∘𝛼=𝑒∘𝛽, (𝑋′,𝜙∘𝑒) is a co-cone for 𝐹. As 𝜙∘𝑒=𝜙∘𝑒, the isomorphism 𝜙:𝑋→𝑋′ is (tautologically) an isomorphism of co-cones (𝑋,𝑒)→(𝑋′,𝜙∘𝑒), so by (2.6.90) (𝑋′,𝜙∘𝑒) is also a colimit for 𝐹, that is, a coequaliser for (𝛼,𝛽). ∎

Definition 2.6.140 (Coequaliser)

Consider a commutative diagram in a category 𝒞︀

such that 𝜋∘𝛼=𝜋∘𝛽. We say that 𝜋 is a co-equaliser for (𝛼,𝛽) if for all 𝜋′:𝑌→𝑍′ such that 𝜋′∘𝛼=𝜋′∘𝛽 there is a unique factorisation

This is equivalent to (𝑍,𝜋) being a colimit for the obvious functor 𝐹:𝟐→𝒞︀ where 𝟐 denotes the category ·⇉·.

The following diagram is a coequaliser

Proposition 2.6.143 (Coequalisers in a Reflective Subcategory)

Let 𝒞︀⇌𝐺𝐹𝒟︀ be adjoint functors with 𝐺 fully faithful and unit 𝜂; by (2.6.73) we may regard 𝒟︀ as a full subcategory of 𝒞︀ via 𝐺. Let 𝛼,𝛽:𝑋⇉𝑌 be morphisms of 𝒟︀ and suppose 𝜋:𝑌→𝑍 is a coequaliser of (𝛼,𝛽) in 𝒞︀. Then 𝜂𝑍∘𝜋:𝑌→𝐹(𝑍) is a coequaliser of (𝛼,𝛽) in 𝒟︀.

In particular if every pair of morphisms of 𝒟︀ has a coequaliser in 𝒞︀ then 𝒟︀ has all coequalisers.

Proof. Drafted by Claude Sonnet 5, reviewed by David Rufino.

Write 𝑞≔𝜂𝑍∘𝜋. Since 𝜋∘𝛼=𝜋∘𝛽 we have 𝑞∘𝛼=𝜂𝑍∘𝜋∘𝛼=𝜂𝑍∘𝜋∘𝛽=𝑞∘𝛽.

Suppose 𝑞′:𝑌→𝐻 is a morphism of 𝒟︀ with 𝑞′∘𝛼=𝑞′∘𝛽. Regarding 𝑞′ as a morphism of 𝒞︀ (as 𝒟︀ is a subcategory), the universal property of the coequaliser 𝜋 gives a unique morphism 𝑞̃:𝑍→𝐻 of 𝒞︀ such that 𝑞̃∘𝜋=𝑞′.

Since 𝐹 is left adjoint to 𝐺 with unit 𝜂 (2.6.71), there is a bijection Mor(𝐹(𝑍),𝐻)→∼Mor(𝑍,𝐻), 𝜙↦𝜙∘𝜂𝑍, so there is a unique morphism 𝑞―:𝐹(𝑍)→𝐻 of 𝒟︀ such that 𝑞―∘𝜂𝑍=𝑞̃. Then

𝑞―∘𝑞=𝑞―∘𝜂𝑍∘𝜋=𝑞̃∘𝜋=𝑞′

so 𝑞― is a factorisation of 𝑞′ through 𝑞.

Suppose 𝑞―′ is another morphism of 𝒟︀ with 𝑞―′∘𝑞=𝑞′, that is (𝑞―′∘𝜂𝑍)∘𝜋=𝑞′=𝑞̃∘𝜋. As 𝜋 is a coequaliser it is an epimorphism (2.6.135), whence 𝑞―′∘𝜂𝑍=𝑞̃, and by the uniqueness of the factorisation above 𝑞―′=𝑞―.

Therefore 𝑞=𝜂𝑍∘𝜋 satisfies the universal property of the coequaliser of (𝛼,𝛽) in 𝒟︀.

For the final statement, if every pair of morphisms of 𝒟︀ has a coequaliser in 𝒞︀ then the above applies to every such pair, so 𝒟︀ has all coequalisers. ∎
