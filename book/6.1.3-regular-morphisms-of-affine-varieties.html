6.1.3 Regular Morphisms of Affine Varieties

Definition 6.1.29 (Regular Morphism of Affine Varieties)

Let 𝑋⊂𝔸𝑘𝑛, 𝑌⊂𝔸𝑘𝑚 be affine varieties and 𝜙:𝑋(𝑘―)→𝑌(𝑘―) a function. Then we say that 𝜙 is a regular morphism (at 𝑥) if 𝜋𝑗∘𝜙 is regular (at 𝑥) in the sense of (6.1.25) for 𝑗=1,…,𝑚.

A regular morphism is an isomorphism if it has a two-sided inverse which is also regular.

Note a regular function 𝑋→𝑘― is completely equivalent to a regular morphism 𝑋→𝔸𝑘1.

Proposition 6.1.30 (Regular Morphisms of Affine Varieties)

Let 𝑋=𝑉(𝔞) and 𝑌=𝑉(𝔟) be affine varieties. Then there is a bijection

AlgHom𝑘(𝒪︀𝑌(𝑌),𝒪︀𝑋(𝑋))⟷{𝜙:𝑋→𝑌|𝜙regular}𝜙♯→(𝜙♯(𝑌―1)(−),…,𝜙♯(𝑌―𝑚)(−))𝐺―→𝐺(𝜋1∘𝜙,…,𝜋𝑚∘𝜙𝑚)←𝜙
Proof. Let 𝜙♯ be a 𝑘-algebra homomorphism, then 𝜙♯(𝑌―𝑖)∈𝒪︀𝑌(𝑌) is a regular function. Therefore by definition the first map is well-defined. Conversely 𝜋𝑗∘𝜙 is regular so corresponds to an element of 𝜙𝑗∈𝒪︀𝑋(𝑋) and the second map is well-defined. As 𝜙 is uniquely determined by 𝜙♯(𝑌𝑗) for 𝑗=1,…,𝑚 we see that the maps are mutually inverse. ∎
Lemma 6.1.31

Let 𝜙:𝑋→𝑌 be a regular morphism of affine varieties and 𝑊⊂𝑋 an irreducible subset. Then

(𝜙♯)−1(𝔐𝑋,𝑊)=𝔐𝑌,𝜙(𝑊)=𝔐𝑌,𝜙(𝑊)―

Proposition 6.1.32

Let 𝜙:𝑋→𝑌 be a regular morphism of affine varieties and 𝑊⊂𝑋, 𝑍⊂𝑌 irreducible. Then

𝑍⊂𝜙(𝑊)―⟺(𝜙♯)−1(𝔐𝑋,𝑊)⊂𝔐𝑌,𝑍

𝑍―=𝜙(𝑊)―⟺(𝜙♯)−1(𝔐𝑋,𝑊)=𝔐𝑌,𝑍

Proposition 6.1.33

Let 𝜙:𝑋→𝑌 be a regular morphism of affine varieties and 𝑥∈𝑋, 𝑦∈𝑌 points. Then the following are equivalent

  1. 𝑦∈{𝜙(𝑥)}―
  2. 𝜙(𝑥)∈{𝑦}―
  3. {𝜙(𝑥)}―={𝑦}―
  4. (𝜙♯)−1(𝔐𝑋,𝑥)=𝔐𝑌,𝑦

When 𝑘 is algebraically closed then this is equivalent to 𝜙(𝑥)=𝑦.

Proof. We have a)-c) are equivalent because 𝑌 is symmetric (6.1.9), (4.1.42), and then c) ⟺ d) follows from (6.1.32). ∎

Proposition 6.1.36 (Affine Varieties form a Category)

The collection of affine varieties together with regular morphisms form a category 𝐀𝐟𝐟𝐕𝐚𝐫𝑘. Furthermore there is an equivalence of categories

𝐀𝐟𝐟𝐕𝐚𝐫𝑘⟶∼𝐑𝐞𝐝𝐮𝐜𝐞𝐝𝐅𝐠𝐀𝐥𝐠𝑘op
Proof. By using the correspondence with 𝑘-algebra homomorphisms (6.1.30) we may show that the law of composition for regular morphisms also satisfy the axioms of a category. We have therefore shown that the assignment 𝑋→𝑘[𝑋] is full and faithful functor. To show it is essentially surjective consider a reduced finitely-generated 𝑘-algebra 𝐴. Then by definition 𝐴≅𝑘[𝑋1,…,𝑋𝑛]/𝔞 for some ideal 𝔞. As 𝐴 is reduced we see 𝔞 is radical and therefore 𝐴 is the coordinate ring of the affine variety 𝑋=𝑉(𝔞) and by definition 𝑘[𝑋]≅𝐴. ∎
Lemma 6.1.37

Let 𝜙:𝑋→𝑌 be a regular morphism of affine varieties. Suppose that 𝑉⊂𝑌 is open and 𝑓:𝑉→𝑘― is regular. Then 𝑓∘𝜙:𝜙−1(𝑉)→𝑘― is regular.

In other words there is a well-defined functor

𝐀𝐟𝐟𝐕𝐚𝐫𝑘→𝐋𝐨𝐜𝐚𝐥𝐒𝐩𝐚𝐜𝐞𝐅𝐮𝐧𝐜𝐭𝐢𝐨𝐧𝐬𝑘

which we show is full and faithful in (6.1.39).

When considering regular maps over an open subset of an affine variety, there are two plausible definitions which we show are equivalent.

Corollary 6.1.41

Let 𝑋 be an affine variety, 𝑈⊂𝑋 an open subset and 𝑓∈𝒪︀𝑋(𝑈). Then the set

𝐷(𝑓)≔{𝑥∈𝑈|𝑓(𝑥)≠0}

Corollary 6.1.42 (Affine Mapping Property)

Let 𝑋,𝑌 be affine varieties. Then there are bijections

Mor(𝑋,𝑌)⟶∼Mor((𝑋,𝒪︀𝑋),(𝑌,𝒪︀𝑌))⟶∼AlgHom𝑘(𝒪︀𝑌(𝑌),𝒪︀𝑋(𝑋))

where the second map is given by 𝜙𝑌♯. Furthermore these are natural in either 𝑋 or 𝑌, and preserve isomorphisms.

Proof. The first map is well-defined by (6.1.39) and evidently injective. The composite map is bijective by (6.1.30), which shows the first map is surjective and hence bijective. We conclude trivially the second map is also a bijection. ∎
Proposition 6.1.43 (Equaliser is Closed)

Let 𝜙,𝜓:𝑋→𝑌 be regular morphisms of affine varieties. Then the equaliser

{𝑥∈𝑋(𝑘―)|𝜙(𝑥)=𝜓(𝑥)}

is closed.

Proof. By applying the projections we may reduce to the case 𝑌=𝔸𝑘1. Then 𝜙−𝜓 is regular and the equaliser is the inverse image of {0} which is evidently closed in 𝔸𝑘1. As regular maps are continuous then we are done. ∎
