3.32.2 Extensions of Power Series Rings

The main goal of this subsection is to show that, for 𝑘 algebraically closed, the valuation of 𝑘((𝑡)) has a unique extension to any finite separable extension 𝐸 of 𝑘((𝑡)), and that 𝐸 is itself a power series field 𝑘((𝑢)): see (3.32.33) and (3.32.34). The main tool is the Weierstrass division lemma for formal power series, which we record first.

Remark 3.32.28
[Che51] (Ch. II, §6) treats the case of (3.32.33) where the ramification index 𝑒 is prime to char(𝑘), obtaining the sharper conclusion that 𝑡 itself is an 𝑒-th power in 𝑘((𝑢)) (a literal Puiseux expansion 𝑘((𝑡1/𝑒))), via Hensel’s lemma applied to 𝑊𝑒−𝑣 for the unit 𝑣≔𝑡/𝑢𝑒. That argument is unavailable when char(𝑘)=𝑝∣𝑒: the derivative 𝑒𝑊𝑒−1 vanishes identically, so 𝑊𝑒−𝑣 has no simple root to lift, and 𝑡 need not be an 𝑒-th power at all (an Artin-Schreier extension gives a counterexample). (3.32.33) instead only produces 𝑡=𝑢𝑒𝑐 for some unit 𝑐, obtained by solving 𝜏𝐺(𝜏)=𝑢𝑒 by direct coefficient-stabilisation rather than root extraction; this sidesteps the characteristic obstruction entirely, at the cost of the sharper radical form, which is in any case not needed below.
Lemma 3.32.29 (Division with Remainder by a Distinguished Power Series)
Let 𝑅 be a commutative ring, 𝑚≥1 an integer, 𝑤(𝑋)∈𝑅[[𝑋]]⋆, and 𝜏(𝑋)≔𝑋𝑚𝑤(𝑋). Then every 𝑝∈𝑅[[𝑋]] is uniquely 𝑝=𝑞𝜏+𝑟 with 𝑞∈𝑅[[𝑋]] and 𝑟∈𝑅[𝑋] of degree <𝑚. In particular 𝜏 is a non-zero-divisor in 𝑅[[𝑋]].

Proof. Drafted by Claude Sonnet, reviewed by David Rufino.

For existence, split 𝑝=𝑋𝑚𝑝′+𝑟 with 𝑟 the truncation below degree 𝑚 and 𝑝′ the shift, and put 𝑞≔𝑝′𝑤−1 (as 𝑤∈𝑅[[𝑋]]⋆ (3.32.13)). For uniqueness, if 𝑞𝜏+𝑟=0 then 𝑟=−𝑞𝜏∈𝑋𝑚𝑅[[𝑋]] has degree <𝑚, so 𝑟=0; then 𝑞𝑋𝑚𝑤=0 gives 𝑞=0, as 𝑤 is a unit and 𝑋𝑚 is a non-zero-divisor. In particular if 𝑞𝜏=0 then 𝑞=0, that is 𝜏 is a non-zero-divisor. ∎

Lemma 3.32.33 (Finite Separable Extensions of 𝑘((𝑡)) are Power Series Fields)
Let 𝑘 be algebraically closed, 𝐹≔𝑘((𝑡)), and 𝐸/𝐹 a finite separable extension of degree 𝑛. Then there are 𝑢∈𝐸, an element 𝜏∈𝑢𝑘[[𝑢]] with 𝜏=𝑢𝑛𝑐 for some 𝑐∈𝑘[[𝑢]]⋆, and a field isomorphism Ψ:𝐸→∼𝑘((𝑢)) with Ψ|𝐹 the substitution 𝑡↦𝜏 and Ψ(𝑢)=𝑢. In particular 1,𝑢,…,𝑢𝑛−1 is an 𝐹-basis of 𝐸.

Proof. Drafted by Claude Sonnet, reviewed by David Rufino.

Step 1: a DVR. Let 𝒪︀𝐹≔𝑘[[𝑡]], a DVR with fraction field 𝐹 (3.32.16) (so a Dedekind domain (3.31.3)), and let 𝐶 be the integral closure of 𝒪︀𝐹 in 𝐸. By (3.23.26) 𝐶 is a finite 𝒪︀𝐹-module, and by (3.31.11) it is Dedekind. By lying-over (3.23.17) there is a prime 𝔭⊂𝐶 with 𝔭∩𝒪︀𝐹=(𝑡). Put 𝐵≔𝐶𝔭, a DVR with fraction field 𝐸, and let 𝑒≔𝑣𝐵(𝑡)≥1.

Step 2: residue field and valuation. 𝐶/𝔭 is a domain, finite over 𝒪︀𝐹/(𝑡)=𝑘, so 𝑘↪︎𝐶/𝔭 is injective and integral, hence 𝐶/𝔭 is a field (3.23.14), finite over 𝑘. As 𝐵=𝐶𝔭, (3.5.43) gives 𝐵/𝔪𝐵≅Frac(𝐶/𝔭)=𝐶/𝔭 (𝐶/𝔭 already a field); as 𝑘 is algebraically closed, 𝑘→𝐵/𝔪𝐵 is an isomorphism. Every 𝑎∈𝒪︀𝐹× is a unit of 𝐵, so 𝑣𝐵(𝑥)=𝑒𝑣𝐹(𝑥) for 𝑥∈𝐹×. For the inductive step, write the current remainder as 𝑡𝑁𝑦 with 𝑦∈𝐵 (inductive hypothesis); since 𝐵/𝑢𝐵=𝑘, repeated division by 𝑢 gives 𝑦=∑𝑖<𝑒𝑑𝑖𝑢𝑖+𝑢𝑒𝑦′ with 𝑑𝑖∈𝑘, 𝑦′∈𝐵 (there is no need to iterate past degree 𝑒−1: stop as soon as 𝑒 terms are extracted). Using 𝑢𝑒=𝑡𝑤−1,

with 𝑤−1𝑦′∈𝐵; so taking 𝑐𝑖,𝑁≔𝑑𝑖 advances the induction, with new remainder 𝑡𝑁+1(𝑤−1𝑦′)∈𝑡𝑁+1𝐵. Put 𝑓𝑖≔∑𝑗𝑐𝑖𝑗𝑡𝑗∈𝑘[[𝑡]]. Then 𝑧≔𝑏−∑𝑖𝑓𝑖𝑢𝑖 satisfies 𝑧∈𝑡𝑁𝐵 for every 𝑁, so 𝑣𝐵(𝑧)≥𝑒𝑁 for all 𝑁 and 𝑧=0.

𝜎(𝑃)(𝑢)=𝑢𝑒−𝜏∑𝑖<𝑒𝑔𝑖(𝜏)𝑢𝑖=𝑢𝑒−𝜏𝐺(𝜏)=0.

So it factors through 𝐸≅𝐹[𝑌]/(𝑃), giving a field homomorphism Ψ:𝐸→𝑘((𝑢)) with Ψ|𝐹=𝜎 and Ψ(𝑢)=𝑢. It is injective, as 𝐸 is a field.

Step 8: surjectivity. Since 𝜏=𝑢𝑒𝑐 with 𝑐∈𝑘[[𝑢]]⋆, the Weierstrass division lemma (3.32.30) (with 𝑅≔𝑘, 𝑋≔𝑢, 𝑚≔𝑒, 𝑤≔𝑐, so the series of the lemma is 𝜏) gives 𝑘[[𝑢]]=⨁𝑖<𝑒𝜎(𝑘[[𝑡]])𝑢𝑖. Multiplying by powers of 𝜏 gives 𝑘((𝑢))=∑𝑖<𝑒𝜎(𝐹)𝑢𝑖=𝜎(𝐹)[𝑢]. But imΨ=Ψ(𝐹[𝑢])=𝜎(𝐹)[𝑢], so Ψ is surjective.

Hence Ψ:𝐸→∼𝑘((𝑢)) is an isomorphism of fields over 𝐹, with Ψ(𝑢)=𝑢 and Ψ(𝑡)=𝜏=𝑢𝑛𝑐. ∎

Corollary 3.32.34 (Uniqueness of the Extended Valuation)
In the setting of (3.32.33), let 𝐶 be the integral closure of 𝑘[[𝑡]] in 𝐸. Then 𝐶 has exactly one prime 𝔭 lying over (𝑡), the DVR 𝐶𝔭 is the unique valuation ring of 𝐸 containing 𝑘[[𝑡]] whose maximal ideal contracts to (𝑡), and its ramification index is [𝐸:𝐹].

Proof. Drafted by Claude Sonnet, reviewed by David Rufino.

Let 𝔮 be any prime of 𝐶 over (𝑡), which exists by lying-over. Steps 2–4 of the proof of (3.32.33) apply verbatim to the DVR 𝐵≔𝐶𝔮 (which has residue field 𝑘), and give 𝑒𝔮=𝑣𝐵(𝑡)=[𝐸:𝐹]=𝑛.

Now apply the Ramification Formula (3.31.20) to the AKLB situation 𝑘[[𝑡]]⊂𝐶 over 𝐸/𝐹 (both Dedekind by (3.31.11), and 𝐸/𝐹 separable), at the prime (𝑡):

∑𝔮∣(𝑡)𝑒𝔮𝑓𝔮=[𝐶/𝑡𝐶:𝑘]=𝑛.

Each 𝑓𝔮=[𝐶/𝔮:𝑘] is finite, and 𝐶/𝔮 is a field algebraic over 𝑘, hence 𝑓𝔮=1. So ∑𝔮𝑒𝔮=𝑛. By the previous paragraph each term equals 𝑛, so there is exactly one prime 𝔭 over (𝑡) and 𝑒𝔭=𝑛.

For uniqueness of the valuation ring, let 𝑉⊆𝐸 be a valuation ring with 𝑘[[𝑡]]⊆𝑉 and 𝔪𝑉∩𝑘[[𝑡]]=(𝑡). By (3.24.14), 𝐶⊆𝑉. Then 𝔪𝑉∩𝐶 is a prime of 𝐶 over (𝑡), hence equals 𝔭. Every element of 𝐶∖𝔭 lies outside 𝔪𝑉, so is a unit of 𝑉; hence 𝐶𝔭⊆𝑉. Since 𝐶𝔭 is a valuation ring of 𝐸 and valuation rings are maximal for domination among proper subrings of 𝐸 (3.24.2), 𝑉=𝐶𝔭. ∎
