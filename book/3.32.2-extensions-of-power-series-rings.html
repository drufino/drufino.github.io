3.32.2 Extensions of Power Series Rings

The main goal of this subsection is to show that the valuation of 𝑘((𝑡)) has a unique extension to any finite separable extension 𝐸 of 𝑘((𝑡)), whose ramification index and residue degree satisfy 𝑒𝑓=[𝐸:𝐹] (3.32.33) and (3.32.35), and that for 𝑘 algebraically closed 𝐸 is itself a power series field 𝑘((𝑢)) (3.32.34). The main tool is the Weierstrass division lemma for formal power series, which we record first.

Remark 3.32.28
[Che51] (Ch. II, §6) treats the case of (3.32.34) where the ramification index 𝑒 is prime to char(𝑘), obtaining the sharper conclusion that 𝑡 itself is an 𝑒-th power in 𝑘((𝑢)) (a literal Puiseux expansion 𝑘((𝑡1/𝑒))), via Hensel’s lemma applied to 𝑊𝑒−𝑣 for the unit 𝑣≔𝑡/𝑢𝑒. That argument is unavailable when char(𝑘)=𝑝∣𝑒: the derivative 𝑒𝑊𝑒−1 vanishes identically, so 𝑊𝑒−𝑣 has no simple root to lift, and 𝑡 need not be an 𝑒-th power at all (an Artin-Schreier extension gives a counterexample). (3.32.34) instead only produces 𝑡=𝑢𝑒𝑐 for some unit 𝑐, obtained by solving 𝜏𝐺(𝜏)=𝑢𝑒 by direct coefficient-stabilisation rather than root extraction; this sidesteps the characteristic obstruction entirely, at the cost of the sharper radical form, which is in any case not needed below. The statement of (3.32.34) over an algebraically closed field, in every characteristic, is [Lan82] (Ch. I, §4, Prop. 4.3), whose proof, like ours, expands the elements of 𝐸 in powers of a uniformiser 𝑢 with coefficients in 𝑘[[𝑡]] and compares valuations.
Lemma 3.32.29 (Division with Remainder by a Distinguished Power Series)

Let 𝐴 be a commutative ring, 𝑚≥1 an integer and 𝜏=𝑋𝑚𝑤(𝑋) for 𝑤(𝑋)∈𝐴[[𝑋]]⋆.

Then every 𝑝∈𝐴[[𝑋]] may be written uniquely as 𝑝=𝑞𝜏+𝑟 with 𝑞∈𝐴[[𝑋]] and 𝑟∈𝐴[𝑋] of degree <𝑚.

Proof. Drafted by Claude Sonnet, reviewed by David Rufino.

For existence, split 𝑝=𝑋𝑚𝑝′+𝑟 with 𝑟 the truncation below degree 𝑚 and 𝑝′ the shift, and put 𝑞≔𝑝′𝑤−1 (as 𝑤∈𝐴[[𝑋]]⋆ (3.32.13)). For uniqueness, if 𝑞𝜏+𝑟=0 then 𝑟=−𝑞𝜏∈𝑋𝑚𝐴[[𝑋]] has degree <𝑚, so 𝑟=0; then 𝑞𝑋𝑚𝑤=0 gives 𝑞=0, as 𝑤 is a unit and 𝑋𝑚 is a non-zero-divisor. ∎

Corollary 3.32.35 (Uniqueness of the Extended Valuation)
Let 𝑘 be a field, 𝐹≔𝑘((𝑡)), 𝐸/𝐹 a finite separable extension of degree 𝑛 and 𝐶 the integral closure of 𝑘[[𝑡]] in 𝐸. Then 𝐶 has exactly one prime 𝔭 lying over (𝑡), and its ramification index 𝑒 and residue degree 𝑓 satisfy 𝑒𝑓=𝑛. Moreover the DVR 𝐶𝔭 is the unique valuation ring of 𝐸 containing 𝑘[[𝑡]] whose maximal ideal contracts to (𝑡). If 𝑘 is algebraically closed then 𝑓=1 and 𝑒=𝑛.

Proof. Drafted by Claude Sonnet, reviewed by David Rufino.

Let 𝔮 be any prime of 𝐶 over (𝑡), which exists by lying-over. By (3.32.33) its ramification index and residue degree satisfy 𝑒𝔮𝑓𝔮=𝑛.

Now apply the Ramification Formula (3.31.20) to the AKLB situation 𝑘[[𝑡]]⊂𝐶 over 𝐸/𝐹 (both Dedekind by (3.31.11), and 𝐸/𝐹 separable), at the prime (𝑡):

∑𝔮∣(𝑡)𝑒𝔮𝑓𝔮=[𝐶/𝑡𝐶:𝑘]=𝑛.

Each term equals 𝑛 by the first paragraph, so there is exactly one prime 𝔭 over (𝑡) and 𝑒𝔭𝑓𝔭=𝑛. If 𝑘 is algebraically closed then 𝐶/𝔭, a finite extension of 𝑘, equals 𝑘, so 𝑓𝔭=1 and 𝑒𝔭=𝑛.

For uniqueness of the valuation ring, let 𝑉⊆𝐸 be a valuation ring with 𝑘[[𝑡]]⊆𝑉 and 𝔪𝑉∩𝑘[[𝑡]]=(𝑡). By (3.24.14), 𝐶⊆𝑉. Then 𝔪𝑉∩𝐶 is a prime of 𝐶 over (𝑡), hence equals 𝔭. Every element of 𝐶∖𝔭 lies outside 𝔪𝑉, so is a unit of 𝑉; hence 𝐶𝔭⊆𝑉. Since 𝐶𝔭 is a valuation ring of 𝐸 and valuation rings are maximal for domination among proper subrings of 𝐸 (3.24.2), 𝑉=𝐶𝔭. ∎
