3.4.4 Tensor Product Commutes with Direct Sum

Proposition 3.4.21 (Tensor Product Commutes with Sum)

Let (𝑀𝑖)𝑖∈𝐼 be a family of (𝐴,𝐵)-bimodules and (𝑁𝑗)𝑗∈𝐽 a family of (𝐵,𝐶)-bimodules. Then there is an isomorphism of (𝐴,𝐶)-bimodules

(⨁𝑖∈𝐼𝑀𝑖)⊗𝐵(⨁𝑗∈𝐽𝑁𝑗)≅⨁(𝑖,𝑗)∈𝐼×𝐽(𝑀𝑖⊗𝐵𝑁𝑗)
Corollary 3.4.22

Suppose 𝑀 is an (𝐴,𝐵)-bimodule and 𝑁 is a (𝐵,𝐶)-bimodule and 𝑀′⊆𝑀 and 𝑁′⊆𝑁 are direct factors. Then the canonical map is injective

𝑀′⊗𝐵𝑁′↪︎𝑀⊗𝐵𝑁
Corollary 3.4.23

Let 𝑁 be free left 𝐴-module with basis (𝑛𝑖)𝑖∈𝐼 and 𝑀 a (𝐵,𝐴)-bimodule. Then there is an isomorphism of left 𝐵-modules

𝑀⊗𝐴𝑁≅⨁𝑖∈𝐼𝑀𝑚⊗∑𝑖𝑎𝑖𝑛𝑖→(𝑚⋅𝑎𝑖)𝑖∈𝐼∑𝑖(𝑚𝑖⊗𝑛𝑖)←(𝑚𝑖)𝑖∈𝐼

When 𝐴 is commutative then this is a (𝐵,𝐴)-bimodule isomorphism. Further when 𝑀 is an 𝐴-module then this is an isomorphism of 𝐴-modules.

Proof. By (3.4.21) and (3.4.18)

𝑀⊗𝐴𝑁≅𝑀⊗𝐴(⨁𝑖∈𝐼𝐴)≅⨁𝑖∈𝐼(𝑀⊗𝐴𝐴)≅⨁𝑖∈𝐼𝑀𝑚⊗∑𝑖𝑎𝑖𝑛𝑖→𝑚⊗(𝑎𝑖)𝑖∈𝐼→(𝑚⊗𝑎𝑖)𝑖∈𝐼→(𝑚⋅𝑎𝑖)𝑖∈𝐼

 ∎

Corollary 3.4.24
If 𝑀,𝑁 are free 𝐴-modules with bases {𝑚𝑖}𝑖∈𝐼 and {𝑛𝑗}𝑗∈𝐽 then 𝑀⊗𝐴𝑁 is a free 𝐴-module with basis {𝑚𝑖⊗𝑛𝑗}(𝑖,𝑗)∈𝐼×𝐽.
Corollary 3.4.25 (Free modules are flat)

Let 𝑁 be a free left 𝐴-module and 𝑖:𝑀′→𝑀 an injective map of (𝐵,𝐴)-bimodules. Then the corresponding map

𝑖⊗1𝑁:𝑀′⊗𝐴𝑁→𝑀⊗𝐴𝑁

is injective.

Corollary 3.4.26 (Extension of Scalars (Free Module))

Let 𝑀 be free left 𝐴-module with basis {𝑚𝑖}𝑖∈𝐼 and 𝜑:𝐴→𝐵 a ring homomorphism. Then there is a canonical isomorphism of left 𝐵-modules

𝑀(𝐵)≅⨁𝑖∈𝐼𝐵𝑏⊗∑𝑖𝑎𝑖𝑚𝑖→(𝑏𝜑(𝑎𝑖))𝑖∈𝐼

In particular {1⊗𝑚𝑖}𝑖∈𝐼 is a basis for 𝑀(𝐵). Further when 𝑍 is a left 𝐵-module then there is an isomorpihsm of abelian groups

Hom𝐴(𝑀,𝑍)≅Hom𝐵(𝑀(𝐵),𝑍)≅∏𝑖∈𝐼𝑍
𝜃→(𝜃(𝑚𝑖))𝑖∈𝐼

which is 𝐵-linear when 𝐵 is commutative.

When 𝑀 is a finite free 𝐴-module of rank 𝑛 (with basis {𝑣𝑖}) then 𝑀(𝐵) is a finite free 𝐵-module of rank 𝑛 (with basis {1⊗𝑣𝑖}). When 𝐵 is commutative, 𝑀(𝐵)∨ is also a finite free 𝐵-module of rank 𝑛.

Proof. The first isomorphism follows directly from (3.4.23). Then by (3.3.82) there is an isomorphism

Hom(𝑀(𝐵),𝑍)≅Hom(⨁𝑖∈𝐼𝐵,𝑍)≅∏𝑖∈𝐼Hom(𝐵,𝑍)≅∏𝑖∈𝐼𝑍

 ∎

Proposition 3.4.27 (Extension of Scalars (Hom-Set))

Let 𝜑:𝐴→𝐵 be a homomorphism of commutative rings, 𝑀 a finite-free 𝐴-module and 𝑁 a finite-free 𝐵-module. Then

Hom𝐴(𝑀,𝑁)≅Hom𝐵(𝑀(𝐵),𝑁)

is a finite-free 𝐵-module. More precisely suppose 𝑀 has basis {𝑣1,…,𝑣𝑛} and 𝑁 has basis {𝑤1,…,𝑤𝑚} then Hom𝐴(𝑀,𝑁) has basis

{𝑤𝑗𝑣𝑖∨}𝑖,𝑗

Proof. By (3.4.26) there are isomorphisms of 𝐵-modules

Hom𝐴(𝑀,𝑁)≅Hom𝐵(𝑀(𝐵),𝑁)≅⨁𝑖=1𝑛𝑁≅⨁𝑖=1𝑛⨁𝑗=1𝑚𝐵𝜃→1⊗𝜃→(𝜃(𝑣𝑖))𝑖→(𝑤𝑗∨(𝜃(𝑣𝑖)))𝑖,𝑗

Under this isomorphism the standard basis of the right hand side corresponds to the set {𝑤𝑗𝑣𝑖∨}𝑖,𝑗 whence these constitute a basis. ∎
