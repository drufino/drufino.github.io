3.14 Chain Conditions

Definition 3.14.1 (Noetherian / Artinian / Finite Modules)

We say an 𝐴-module 𝑀 is Noetherian if it satisfies the ascending chain condition, namely any ascending chain of submodules

𝑀0⊆𝑀1⊆⋯⊆𝑀

eventually stabilizes, i.e 𝑀𝑛=𝑀𝑛+1 for all 𝑛≥𝑁.

Similarly we say an 𝐴-module 𝑀 is Artinian if it satisfies the descending chain condition, namely any descending chain of submodules

𝑀⊇𝑀0⊇𝑀1⊇⋯

eventually stabilizes.

Definition 3.14.2 (Noetherian / Artinian Ring)
We say a ring 𝐴 is Noetherian (resp. Artinian) if it is Noetherian (resp. Artinian) as an 𝐴-module.

The following is useful

Proposition 3.14.3 (Noetherian criterion)

Let 𝑀 be an 𝐴-module. The following are equivalent

  1. 𝑀 is Noetherian
  2. Every submodule 𝑁⊆𝑀 is finitely-generated
  3. Every set of submodules has a maximal element
Proposition 3.14.4
Let 𝑀 be an 𝐴-module and 𝐴 a Noetherian ring. Then 𝑀 is Noetherian if and only if it is finitely generated as an 𝐴-module.
Proposition 3.14.5 (Restriction of Scalars preserves finiteness)
Let 𝜙:𝐴→𝐵 be a finite 𝐴-algebra and 𝑀 a (3.3.89) 𝐵-module. Then [𝑀]𝜙 is a (3.3.89) 𝐴-module.
Proof. We suppose that 𝑀 is generated by 𝑚1,…,𝑚𝑛, and 𝐵 is generated by 𝑏1,…,𝑏𝑚. Then we claim that the elements 𝑏𝑖𝑚𝑗 generate [𝑀]𝜙. ∎
Proposition 3.14.6
Let 𝐴 be a Noetherian ring and 𝔞◃𝐴 an ideal. Then 𝐴/𝔞 is Noetherian.
Proof. Consider an increasing sequence of ideals 𝔞𝑖◃𝐴/𝔞. Then by (3.3.56) this corresponds to an increasing sequence of ideals 𝔞𝑖′◃𝐴 containing 𝔞. As 𝐴 is Noetherian, this sequence eventually stabilizes. Again by (3.3.56) the original sequence stabilizes. ∎
Proposition 3.14.7 (Localisation preserves Noetherian)
Let 𝐴 be a Noetherian ring and 𝑆⊂𝐴 a multiplicative set. Then 𝑆−1𝐴 is Noetherian.
Proof. Let 𝔟0⊆𝔟1⊆⋯ be an ascending chain of ideals of 𝑆−1𝐴. Contracting gives an ascending chain of ideals 𝔟0𝑐⊆𝔟1𝑐⊆⋯ of 𝐴, which stabilizes as 𝐴 is Noetherian. As every ideal of 𝑆−1𝐴 is extended (3.5.18), 𝔟𝑖=𝔟𝑖𝑐𝑒 for each 𝑖, so the original chain stabilizes too. ∎
Proposition 3.14.8

Let 𝐴 be a Noetherian ring then every finitely-generated 𝐴-algebra is Noetherian.

In particular 𝐴[𝑋1,…,𝑋𝑛] is Noetherian.

Proof. By (3.14.6) it’s enough to show that 𝐴[𝑋1,…,𝑋𝑛] is Noetherian. By induction and (3.10.4) it’s enough to consider the case 𝑛=1. Let 𝔞◃𝐴[𝑋] then by (3.14.3) it’s enough to show that 𝔞 is finitely-generated.

Define

𝔞̃𝑖≔{ℓ(𝑓)|𝑓∈𝔞s.t.deg(𝑓)=𝑖}

Then clearly 𝔞̃𝑖◃𝐴 is an ideal. This is an increasing sequence of ideals, and so it stabilizes for 𝑖≥𝑑 for some 𝑑>0. Furthermore each ideal is finitely generated

𝔞̃𝑖≔(𝑐𝑖1,…,𝑐𝑖𝑛(𝑖))

where 𝑐𝑖𝑗=ℓ(𝑓𝑖𝑗) for polynomials 𝑓𝑖𝑗∈𝔞 of degree 𝑖. We claim that

𝔞=(𝑓𝑖𝑗)𝑖≤𝑑;𝑗≤𝑛(𝑖)

Denote the right hand side by 𝔟, then clearly 𝔟⊆𝔞. We show by induction on 𝑚=deg(𝑓) that 𝑓∈𝔞⟹𝑓∈𝔟. Let 𝑚′≔min(𝑚,𝑑). Then by assumption ℓ(𝑓)∈𝔞̃𝑚=𝔞̃𝑚′ so there exists 𝜆𝑗∈𝐴 such that

ℓ(𝑓)=∑𝑗=1𝑛(𝑚′)𝑐𝑚′𝑗𝜆𝑗

Consider the decomposition

𝑓=(𝑓−∑𝑗=1𝑛(𝑚′)𝜆𝑗𝑓𝑚′𝑗𝑋𝑚−𝑚′)+∑𝑗=1𝑛(𝑚′)𝜆𝑗𝑓𝑚′𝑗𝑋𝑚−𝑚′

The first term has strictly smaller degree than 𝑓, so by the inductive hypothesis lies in 𝔟. Therefore 𝑓∈𝔟 as required. ∎
