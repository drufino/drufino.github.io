6.2.5 Open Subvarieties

Proposition 6.2.36 (Open Subset is Abstract Variety)

Let 𝑋 be a variety (resp. separated variety) and 𝑈⊂𝑋 an open subset. Then (𝑈,𝒪︀𝑋|𝑈) is a variety (resp. separated variety), which we call an open subvariety. We may write 𝒪︀𝑈≔𝒪︀𝑋|𝑈.

The inclusion 𝑖:𝑈↪︎𝑋 is an open immersion and an open immersion 𝜙:𝑋→𝑌 induces an isomorphism between 𝑋 and the open subvariety 𝜙(𝑋).

Proof. By definition 𝑋=⋃𝑖=1𝑛𝑈𝑖 where 𝑈𝑖 is affine. By (6.2.13) we have

𝑈∩𝑈𝑖=⋃𝛼∈𝒜︀𝑖𝐷(𝑓𝛼)

where 𝑓𝛼∈𝒪︀𝑋(𝑈𝑖). By (6.2.24).c) (𝐷(𝑓𝛼),𝒪︀𝑋|𝐷(𝑓𝛼)) is affine. Therefore we have an open cover of affine sets. By (6.2.10) 𝑈 is quasi-compact and therefore there exists a finite affine cover. This shows that 𝑈 is a variety.

Clearly the inclusion map 𝑈→𝑋 is injective and regular and so we see that 𝑈 inherits separatedness. ∎

Proposition 6.2.38 (Morphisms into Open Subvarieties)

Let 𝑋,𝑌 be varieties and 𝑈⊂𝑌 an open subvariety. Then a regular map 𝜙:𝑋→𝑈 is regular when regarded as a map 𝜙:𝑋→𝑌.

Conversely a regular map 𝜙:𝑋→𝑌 such that 𝜙(𝑋)⊂𝑈 may be regarded as a regular map 𝜙:𝑋→𝑈.

Proposition 6.2.39 (Morphisms from Open Subvarieties)
Let 𝜙:𝑋→𝑌 be a regular map of varieties and 𝑈⊂𝑋 an open subvariety. Then 𝜙|𝑈:𝑈→𝑌 is a regular map.
Proposition 6.2.40 (Patching Varieties)

Suppose 𝑋=⋃𝑖=1𝑛𝑈𝑖 is a finite union of sets, (𝑈𝑖,𝒪︀𝑈𝑖) is a variety, and suppose that for all 𝑖,𝑗=1…𝑛

  • 𝑈𝑖∩𝑈𝑗 is open in 𝑈𝑗, and
  • the identity map 1:(𝑈𝑖∩𝑈𝑗,𝒪︀𝑈𝑖|𝑈𝑖∩𝑈𝑗)→(𝑈𝑗∩𝑈𝑖,𝒪︀𝑈𝑗|𝑈𝑖∩𝑈𝑗) is a regular morphism of spaces of functions.

Then there exists a unique structure of a variety on 𝑋 such that

  • 𝑈𝑖 is open in 𝑋, and
  • 𝒪︀𝑈𝑖=𝒪︀𝑋|𝑈𝑖.

Suppose 𝑋=⋃𝑖=1𝑛𝑈𝑖 is a finite union of sets and there exists bijections 𝜃𝑖:𝑈𝑖→∼𝑊𝑖, with 𝑊𝑖 varieties such that for all 𝑖,𝑗=1…𝑛

  • 𝜃𝑖𝑗=𝜃𝑗∘𝜃𝑖−1:𝜃𝑖(𝑈𝑖∩𝑈𝑗)→𝜃𝑗(𝑈𝑖∩𝑈𝑗) is regular.

  • 𝜃𝑖 is a regular isomorphism.

Proof. The topology exists by (4.3.28) and we may simply define

𝒪︀𝑋(𝑈)≔{𝑓:𝑈→𝑘―|𝑓∘𝜃𝑖−1|𝜃𝑖(𝑈𝑖∩𝑈)regular for all𝑖=1…𝑛}

which gives 𝑋 the structure of a space of functions. As each 𝑈𝑖 is a finite union of affine varieties, so is 𝑋. ∎

Corollary 6.2.43 (Glueing Affine Varieties with a Common Field of Fractions)

Let 𝐾 be a field containing 𝑘, 𝐵,𝐵′⊆𝐾 finitely generated 𝑘-subalgebras and 𝑎∈𝐵, 𝑎′∈𝐵′ with 𝐵𝑎=𝐵𝑎′′ as subrings of 𝐾. Then Frac(𝐵)=Frac(𝐵𝑎)=Frac(𝐵′), and there is an integral variety 𝑋 with an open cover by affine varieties 𝑈,𝑈′ and isomorphisms of 𝑘-algebras

𝜄:𝒪︀𝑋(𝑈)→∼𝐵and𝜄′:𝒪︀𝑋(𝑈′)→∼𝐵′

such that

  1. 𝑈∩𝑈′=𝑈𝑎=𝑈𝑎′′≠∅, where 𝑈𝑎≔{𝑥∈𝑈|𝜄−1(𝑎)(𝑥)≠0} and 𝑈𝑎′′≔{𝑥∈𝑈′|𝜄′−1(𝑎′)(𝑥)≠0} are the principal open subsets defined by 𝜄−1(𝑎) and 𝜄′−1(𝑎′);
  2. the induced isomorphisms 𝜄𝑎:𝒪︀𝑋(𝑈𝑎)→∼𝐵𝑎 and 𝜄𝑎′′:𝒪︀𝑋(𝑈𝑎′′)→∼𝐵𝑎′′ coincide on 𝒪︀𝑋(𝑈∩𝑈′) as maps to 𝐵𝑎=𝐵𝑎′′, that is 𝜄𝑎=𝜄𝑎′′;
  3. under the canonical identifications 𝑘(𝑈)=𝑘(𝑋)=𝑘(𝑈′), the isomorphisms 𝑘(𝑈)→∼Frac(𝐵) and 𝑘(𝑈′)→∼Frac(𝐵′) induced by 𝜄 and 𝜄′ coincide, giving one isomorphism 𝑘(𝑋)→∼Frac(𝐵)=Frac(𝐵′), and dim𝑋=trdeg(Frac(𝐵)/𝑘).

Proof. Drafted by Claude Sonnet, reviewed by David Rufino.

As 𝐵𝑎=𝐵𝑎′′ is a subring of the field 𝐾, 𝑎≠0 and 𝑎′≠0, so the three rings have the same field of fractions. Apply (6.2.42) with 𝜌 the identity of 𝐵𝑎=𝐵𝑎′′. The varieties 𝑈,𝑈′ are integral as 𝒪︀𝑋(𝑈)≅𝐵 and 𝒪︀𝑋(𝑈′)≅𝐵′ are domains (6.2.7), and 𝑈∩𝑈′=𝑈𝑎 is non-empty as 𝑎≠0 in the domain 𝐵. So 𝑋=𝑈∪𝑈′ is irreducible, hence integral. As 𝑈 is a non-empty open subset, 𝑘(𝑋)≅𝑘(𝑈) (4.3.18), and 𝜄 induces 𝑘(𝑈)→∼Frac(𝐵), so dim𝑋=trdeg(𝑘(𝑋)/𝑘)=trdeg(Frac(𝐵)/𝑘) (6.1.58). The same holds for 𝑈′ and 𝜄′, and the two isomorphisms 𝑘(𝑋)→∼Frac(𝐵) agree because 𝜄𝑎=𝜄𝑎′′ on 𝒪︀𝑋(𝑈∩𝑈′). ∎
