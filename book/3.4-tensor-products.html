3.4 Tensor Products

3.4.1 Commutative Tensor Product

We consider the “Tensor Product” of modules over a single commutative ring as an important special case, and as a guide to the general bimodule case.

In order to streamline the (typically tedious) verification of standard properties we make heavy use of the notion of representable bifunctor (2.6.65) however all the results may be proved in an essentially identical “low-tech” way.

First we define the so-called “Internal Hom Functor”

Definition 3.4.1 (Internal Hom)

Let 𝐴 be a commutative ring and 𝑀,𝑁 be 𝐴-modules. The set of 𝐴-linear homomorphisms 𝑀→𝑁 is itself an 𝐴-module. Therefore we have an enriched hom functor

Hom:𝐴-𝐌𝐨𝐝op×𝐴-𝐌𝐨𝐝→𝐴-𝐌𝐨𝐝

where

(𝑎⋅𝜓)(𝑚)≔𝑎⋅𝜓(𝑚)=𝜓(𝑎⋅𝑚)

and in particular we have a bijection

Mor(𝑀,𝑁)⟶∼Nat(Hom(𝑁,−),Hom(𝑀,−))
Definition 3.4.2 (Bilinear Map)

Let 𝑀,𝑁,𝑍 be 𝐴-modules. Then a map

𝜓:𝑀×𝑁→𝑍

is bilinear if it satisfies

  • additive 𝜓(𝑚+𝑚′,𝑛)=𝜓(𝑚,𝑛)+𝜓(𝑚′,𝑛) and 𝜓(𝑚,𝑛+𝑛′)=𝜓(𝑚,𝑛)+𝜓(𝑚,𝑛′)
  • 𝐴-linear 𝜓(𝑎𝑚,𝑛)=𝜓(𝑚,𝑎𝑛)=𝑎𝜓(𝑚,𝑛) for all 𝑎∈𝐴

This yields a bifunctor which we denote by

Bilin(−,−;−):(𝐴-𝐌𝐨𝐝×𝐴-𝐌𝐨𝐝)op×𝐴-𝐌𝐨𝐝→𝐴-𝐌𝐨𝐝
Remark 3.4.3 (𝐴-linearity)

The 𝐴-module structure on the image of Hom and Bilin is only well-defined because 𝐴 is commutative. For given 𝜓:𝑀→𝑁 an 𝐴-linear map then for (𝑎⋅𝜓) to be 𝐴-linear we may verify this requires

𝑎′𝑎𝜓(𝑚)=(𝑎𝜓)(𝑎′𝑚)=𝑎𝑎′𝜓(𝑚)

which in general does not hold unless 𝐴 is commutative.

Proposition 3.4.4 (Existence of Tensor Product)

The bifunctor Bilin(−,−;−) is representable by a functor

⊗𝐴:𝐴-𝐌𝐨𝐝×𝐴-𝐌𝐨𝐝→𝐴-𝐌𝐨𝐝

for which there exists an isomorphism natural in 𝑀,𝑁,𝑍

Φ:Hom(𝑀⊗𝑁,𝑍)≅Bilin(𝑀,𝑁;𝑍)

For every pair 𝑀,𝑁 there is a distinguished bilinear map

𝑖:𝑀×𝑁→𝑀⊗𝑁

through which every bilinear map 𝑀×𝑁→𝑍 factors uniquely. We denote the elementary tensor by

𝑚⊗𝑛≔𝑖(𝑚,𝑛)

Further for morphisms 𝑓:𝑀→𝑀′ and 𝑔:𝑁→𝑁′ there is a unique morphism

(𝑓⊗𝑔):𝑀⊗𝑁→𝑀′⊗𝑁′

such that

(𝑓⊗𝑔)(𝑚⊗𝑛)=𝑓(𝑚)⊗𝑔(𝑛)

Proof. In light of (2.6.66) it is sufficient to show that Bilin(𝑀,𝑁;−) is representable for fixed 𝑀,𝑁 and the rest of the properties follow, observing that 𝑖=Φ(1𝑀⊗𝑁).

For let 𝐹 be the free abelian group on 𝑀×𝑁 and let 𝑆⊂𝐹 be the subgroup generated by elements of the form

  • (𝑚+𝑚′,𝑛)−(𝑚,𝑛)−(𝑚′,𝑛)
  • (𝑚,𝑛′+𝑛)−(𝑚,𝑛′)−(𝑚,𝑛)
  • (𝑎𝑚,𝑛)−𝑎(𝑚,𝑛)
  • (𝑚,𝑛)−(𝑚,𝑎𝑛)

Observe there is a canonical map 𝑀×𝑁→𝐹. Suppose that 𝑍 is an abelian group and 𝜓:𝑀×𝑁→𝑍 is a bilinear map. Then we may extend by linearity to a group homomorphism 𝜓:𝐹→𝑍. By construction 𝑆⊆ker(𝜓) whence there is a unique group homomorphism 𝜓̂:𝐹/𝑆→𝑍 such that 𝜓̂∘𝑖=𝜓 where 𝑖:𝑀×𝑁→𝐹/𝑆 is the canonical inclusion. This shows that (𝐹/𝑆,𝑖) is a universal element for Bilin(−,−;−) as we required and we denote this by 𝑀⊗𝑁. The representation exists as a functor of sets by (2.6.62), and Φ is clearly an isomorphism of 𝐴-modules because

Φ(𝑓+𝑔)=(𝑓+𝑔)∘𝑖=𝑓∘𝑖+𝑔∘𝑖=Φ(𝑓)+Φ(𝑔)

and

Φ(𝜆𝑓)=(𝜆𝑓)∘𝑖=𝜆(𝑓∘𝑖)=𝜆Φ(𝑓)

 ∎

Lemma 3.4.5 (Currying Lemma)

Let 𝑀,𝑁,𝑍 be 𝐴-modules then there are natural isomorphisms

Bilin(𝑀,𝑁,𝑍)≅Hom(𝑀,Hom(𝑁,𝑍))≅Hom(𝑁,Hom(𝑀,𝑍))≅Bilin(𝑁,𝑀,𝑍)𝜓𝑚→(𝑛→𝜓(𝑚,𝑛))𝑛→(𝑚→𝜓(𝑚,𝑛))(𝑛,𝑚)→𝜓(𝑚,𝑛)

This gives the following important result (which may be seen as an adjoint relationship)

Proposition 3.4.6 (Tensor-Hom Adjunction)

Let 𝑀,𝑁,𝑍 be 𝐴-modules then we have the following natural isomorphism of functors

Hom(𝑀⊗𝑁,𝑍)≅Hom(𝑀,Hom(𝑁,𝑍))𝜃→(𝑚→(𝑛→𝜃(𝑚⊗𝑛)))

We may also use the Currying Lemma (3.4.5) to demonstrate symmetry of the tensor product

Proposition 3.4.7 (Symmetry of Tensor Product)

There is a natural isomorphism

𝑀⊗𝑁≅𝑁⊗𝑀𝑚⊗𝑛→𝑛⊗𝑚

Proof. We observe that there is a natural isomorphism of functors which the tensor products represent

Bilin(𝑀,𝑁;𝑍)≅Bilin(𝑁,𝑀;𝑍)

so the result follows from (2.6.68). ∎

Proposition 3.4.8 (Associativity of Tensor Product)

There is a natural isomorphism of 𝐴-modules

(𝑀⊗𝑁)⊗𝑃≅𝑀⊗(𝑁⊗𝑃)(𝑚⊗𝑛)⊗𝑝→𝑚⊗(𝑛⊗𝑝)

Proof. We may make repeated use of the Tensor-Hom adjunction to exhibit natural isomorphisms

Hom((𝑀⊗𝑁)⊗𝑃,𝑍)≅Hom(𝑀⊗𝑁,Hom(𝑃,𝑍))≅Hom(𝑀,Hom(𝑁,Hom(𝑃,𝑍)))≅Hom(𝑀,Hom(𝑁⊗𝑃,𝑍))≅Hom(𝑀⊗(𝑁⊗𝑃),𝑍)

The required isomorphism is then a consequence of (2.6.68). For explicit form set 𝑍=𝑀⊗(𝑁⊗𝑃) and consider the identity map 1𝑍. We see tracing back under these natural isomorphisms this corresponds to the given map. ∎

3.4.2 Bimodule Tensor Product

To develop the tensor product in general it is convenient to work with bimodules. We make heavy use of the notion if bimodule hom-sets (3.3.78).

Definition 3.4.9 (Bilinear maps)

Consider the bimodules 𝑀𝐵𝐴, 𝑁𝐶𝐵. We say that a map

𝜓:𝑀𝐵𝐴×𝑁𝐶𝐵→𝑍𝐶𝐴

is (𝐴,𝐶)-bilinear if it satisfies all of the following conditions

  • additive 𝜓(𝑚+𝑚′,𝑛+𝑛′)=𝜓(𝑚,𝑛)+𝜓(𝑚′,𝑛)+𝜓(𝑚,𝑛′)+𝜓(𝑛,𝑛′)
  • balanced 𝜓(𝑚𝑏,𝑛)=𝜓(𝑚,𝑏𝑛)
  • 𝐴-linear 𝜓(𝑎𝑚,𝑛)=𝑎𝜓(𝑚,𝑛)
  • 𝐶-linear 𝜓(𝑚,𝑛𝑐)=𝜓(𝑚,𝑛)𝑐

Extending the notation from earlier we denote the set of additive, balanced and (𝐴,𝐶)-bilinear maps by Bilin(𝑀,𝑁;𝑍). This determines a functor

Bilin:(𝐌𝐨𝐝𝐵𝐴×𝐌𝐨𝐝𝐶𝐵)op×𝐌𝐨𝐝𝐶𝐴→𝐀𝐛𝐆𝐫𝐩

where addition is defined pointwise. We may also consider the functor of only additive and balanced maps

Balan:(𝐌𝐨𝐝𝐵×𝐌𝐨𝐝𝐵)op×𝐀𝐛𝐆𝐫𝐩→𝐀𝐛𝐆𝐫𝐩

and the functor

RBilin:(𝐌𝐨𝐝𝐶𝐵×𝐌𝐨𝐝𝐷𝐶)op×𝐌𝐨𝐝𝐷𝐴→𝐌𝐨𝐝𝐵𝐴

where we drop the 𝐵-linear requirement but also introduce an extra (𝐴,𝐵)-module structure on the maps, as follows

(𝑎𝜓𝑏)(𝑚,𝑛)≔𝑎𝜓(𝑏𝑚,𝑛)
Proposition 3.4.10 (Existence of Bimodule Tensor Product)

Let 𝐴,𝐵,𝐶 be arbitrary rings

  • The bifunctor Balan(𝑀,𝑁;−) is represented by the balanced tensor product

    ⊗𝐵:𝐌𝐨𝐝𝐵×𝐌𝐨𝐝𝐵→𝐀𝐛𝐆𝐫𝐩

    with natural isomorphism for 𝑍 an Abelian group

    Φ:Hom(𝑀𝐵⊗𝐵𝑁𝐵,𝑍)≅Balan(𝑀𝐵,𝑁𝐵;𝑍)𝜃→𝜃∘𝑖

    where 𝑖 is a universal balanced map

    𝑖:𝑀×𝑁→𝑀𝐵⊗𝐵𝑁𝐵

    The tensor product is generated by elementary tensors of the form 𝑚⊗𝑛≔𝑖(𝑚,𝑛).

  • The bifunctor Bilin(𝑀,𝑁;−) is represented by the balanced tensor product 𝑀⊗𝐵𝑁 with an additional (𝐴,𝐶)-bimodule structure given by

    𝑎⋅(𝑚⊗𝑛)⋅𝑐≔(𝑎⋅𝑚)⊗(𝑛⋅𝑐)

    This determines a bifunctor

    ⊗𝐵:𝐌𝐨𝐝𝐵𝐴×𝐌𝐨𝐝𝐶𝐵→𝐌𝐨𝐝𝐶𝐴

    and a natural isomorphism of Abelian groups

    Hom(𝑀𝐵𝐴⊗𝐵𝑁𝐶𝐵,𝑍)≅Bilin(𝑀𝐵𝐴,𝑁𝐶𝐵;𝑍𝐶𝐴)𝜃→𝜃∘𝑖

In either case, if 𝑓:𝑀→𝑀′ and 𝑔:𝑁→𝑁′ are morphisms then there is a unique morphism

(𝑓⊗𝑔):(𝑀⊗𝑁)→(𝑀′⊗𝑁′)

such that

(𝑓⊗𝑔)(𝑚⊗𝑛)=𝑓(𝑚)⊗𝑔(𝑛)

Furthermore for maps 𝑓′:𝑀′→𝑀″ and 𝑔′:𝑁′→𝑁″ the following property is satisfised

(𝑓′⊗𝑔′)∘(𝑓⊗𝑔)=(𝑓′∘𝑓)⊗(𝑔′∘𝑔)

Proof. In light of (2.6.65) we only need to show that the functors are representable for fixed 𝑀,𝑁, as the functoriality will follow immediately by abstract nonsense.

We first show that Balan(−,−;−) is representable. For let 𝐹 be the free abelian group on 𝑀×𝑁 and let 𝑆⊂𝐹 be the subgroup generated by elements of the form

  • (𝑚+𝑚′,𝑛)−(𝑚,𝑛)−(𝑚′,𝑛)
  • (𝑚,𝑛′+𝑛)−(𝑚,𝑛′)−(𝑚,𝑛)
  • (𝑚𝑏,𝑛)−(𝑚,𝑏𝑛)

Observe there is a canonical map 𝑀×𝑁→𝐹. Suppose that 𝑍 is an abelian group and 𝜓:𝑀×𝑁→𝑍 is a balanced additive map. Then we may extend by linearity to a group homomorphism 𝜓:𝐹→𝑍. By construction 𝑆⊆ker(𝜓) whence there is a unique group homomorphism 𝜓̂:𝐹/𝑆→𝑍 such that 𝜓̂∘𝑖=𝜓 where 𝑖:𝑀×𝑁→𝐹/𝑆 is the canonical inclusion. This shows that (𝐹/𝑆,𝑖) is a universal element for Balan(−,−;−) and we denote this by 𝑀𝐵⊗𝐵𝑁𝐵. Then (2.6.62) exhibits Φ as a natural isomorphism of sets, which may readily observed to be additive.

In order to define the (𝐴,𝐶)-bimodule structure in the second case let 𝜓𝑎:𝑀→𝑀 denote multiplication by 𝑎 and 𝜓𝑐:𝑁→𝑁 multiplication by 𝑐. We note these are both 𝐵-module homomorphisms by definition so we may define the action by functoriality of the balanced tensor product case

𝑎⋅𝑣≔(𝜓𝑎⊗1𝑁)(𝑣)∀𝑣∈𝑀⊗𝑁𝑣⋅𝑐≔(1𝑀⊗𝜓𝑐)(𝑣)∀𝑣∈𝑀⊗𝑁

These are 𝐵-linear by construction and we may demonstrate (𝐴,𝐶)-bimodule actions are associative and commute by using the functoriality of the tensor product

(𝜓𝑎′⊗1𝑁)∘(𝜓𝑎⊗1𝑁)=(𝜓𝑎′∘𝜓𝑎)⊗1𝑁=(𝜓𝑎′𝑎)⊗1𝑁(1𝑀⊗𝜓𝑐)∘(1𝑀⊗𝜓𝑐′)=1𝑀⊗(𝜓𝑐∘𝜓𝑐′)=1𝑀⊗𝜓𝑐𝑐′(𝜓𝑎⊗1𝑁)∘(1𝑀⊗𝜓𝑐)=𝜓𝑎⊗𝜓𝑐=(1𝑀⊗𝜓𝑐)⊗(𝜓𝑎⊗1𝑁)

Further by construction the canonical map 𝑖 is (𝐴,𝐶)-bilinear. Consider any (𝐴,𝐶)-bilinear map 𝜓:𝑀×𝑁→𝑍 then by the previous part there is a unique group homomorphism 𝜓̂:𝑀⊗𝐵𝑁→𝑍 such that

𝜓̂(𝑚⊗𝑛)≔𝜓(𝑚,𝑛)

which we see by construction (and linearity) is (𝐴,𝐶)-bilinear. It is clearly the unique such map so that we may conclude Bilin(−,−;−) has universal element (𝑀𝐵𝐴⊗𝐵𝑁𝐶𝐵,𝑖). The (2.6.62) exhibits Φ as a natural isomorphism of sets which may be readily exhibited to be additive. ∎

Lemma 3.4.11 (Currying Lemma)

Let 𝑀,𝑁,𝑃,𝑌,𝑍 be bimodules. Then there is an isomorphism of abelian groups and (𝐴,𝐵)-bimodules respectively

Bilin(𝑀𝐵𝐴,𝑁𝐶𝐵;𝑌𝐶𝐴)≅Hom(𝑀𝐵𝐴,RHom(𝑁𝐶𝐵,𝑌𝐶𝐴))≅Hom(𝑁𝐶𝐵,LHom(𝑀𝐵𝐴,𝑌𝐶𝐴))RBilin(𝑁𝐶𝐵,𝑃𝐷𝐶;𝑍𝐷𝐴)≅RHom(𝑁𝐶𝐵,RHom(𝑃𝐷𝐶,𝑍𝐷𝐴))

contravariant in 𝑀,𝑁,𝑃 and covariant in 𝑌 and 𝑍.

Proof. The maps on abelian groups are clear, namely given a bi-additive map 𝜓 we have a well-defined homomorphism of abelian groups

𝑚→(𝑛→𝜓(𝑚,𝑛))

The verification that the maps are well-defined and bijective is tedious but mechanical. ∎

Proposition 3.4.13 (Associativity of Tensor Product)

Let 𝑀𝐵𝐴, 𝑁𝐶𝐵, 𝑃𝐷𝐶 be bimodules then there is a natural isomorphism of (𝐴,𝐷)-bimodules

3.4.3 Extensions of Scalars

We observe that for a ring homomorphism 𝜑:𝐴→𝐵 we have on 𝐵 a natural (𝐴,𝐵)-bimodule and (𝐵,𝐴)-bimodule structure. Using the tensor product then we may use this to extend the coefficients from 𝐴 to 𝐵. We first prove some elementary lemmas

Lemma 3.4.14 (Unit Hom)

Let 𝜑:𝐵→𝐶 be a ring homomorphism and 𝑍 a (𝐴,𝐶)-bimodule. Then there is a natural isomorphism of (𝐴,𝐵)-bimodules

RHom(𝐶𝐶𝐵,𝑍𝐶𝐴)≅𝑍𝐵𝐴𝜃→𝜃(1)𝑧(−)←𝑧

and similarly let 𝜑:𝐴→𝐵 be a ring homomorphism and 𝑌 a (𝐵,𝐶)-bimodule then there is a natural isomorphism of (𝐴,𝐶)-bimodules

LHom(𝐵𝐴𝐵,𝑌𝐶𝐵)≅𝑌𝐶𝐴𝜃→𝜃(1)

Proof. Observe

(𝑎𝜃𝑏)(1)=𝑎𝜃(𝜑(𝑏))=𝑎𝜃(1)𝜑(𝑏)
