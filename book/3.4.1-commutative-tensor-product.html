3.4.1 Commutative Tensor Product

We consider the “Tensor Product” of modules over a single commutative ring as an important special case, and as a guide to the general bimodule case.

In order to streamline the (typically tedious) verification of standard properties we make heavy use of the notion of representable bifunctor (2.6.65) however all the results may be proved in an essentially identical “low-tech” way.

First we define the so-called “Internal Hom Functor”

Definition 3.4.1 (Internal Hom)

Let 𝐴 be a commutative ring and 𝑀,𝑁 be 𝐴-modules. The set of 𝐴-linear homomorphisms 𝑀→𝑁 is itself an 𝐴-module. Therefore we have an enriched hom functor

Hom:𝐴-𝐌𝐨𝐝op×𝐴-𝐌𝐨𝐝→𝐴-𝐌𝐨𝐝

where

(𝑎⋅𝜓)(𝑚)≔𝑎⋅𝜓(𝑚)=𝜓(𝑎⋅𝑚)

and in particular we have a bijection

Mor(𝑀,𝑁)⟶∼Nat(Hom(𝑁,−),Hom(𝑀,−))
Definition 3.4.2 (Bilinear Map)

Let 𝑀,𝑁,𝑍 be 𝐴-modules. Then a map

𝜓:𝑀×𝑁→𝑍

is bilinear if it satisfies

  • additive 𝜓(𝑚+𝑚′,𝑛)=𝜓(𝑚,𝑛)+𝜓(𝑚′,𝑛) and 𝜓(𝑚,𝑛+𝑛′)=𝜓(𝑚,𝑛)+𝜓(𝑚,𝑛′)
  • 𝐴-linear 𝜓(𝑎𝑚,𝑛)=𝜓(𝑚,𝑎𝑛)=𝑎𝜓(𝑚,𝑛) for all 𝑎∈𝐴

This yields a bifunctor which we denote by

Bilin(−,−;−):(𝐴-𝐌𝐨𝐝×𝐴-𝐌𝐨𝐝)op×𝐴-𝐌𝐨𝐝→𝐴-𝐌𝐨𝐝
Remark 3.4.3 (𝐴-linearity)

The 𝐴-module structure on the image of Hom and Bilin is only well-defined because 𝐴 is commutative. For given 𝜓:𝑀→𝑁 an 𝐴-linear map then for (𝑎⋅𝜓) to be 𝐴-linear we may verify this requires

𝑎′𝑎𝜓(𝑚)=(𝑎𝜓)(𝑎′𝑚)=𝑎𝑎′𝜓(𝑚)

which in general does not hold unless 𝐴 is commutative.

Proposition 3.4.4 (Existence of Tensor Product)

The bifunctor Bilin(−,−;−) is representable by a functor

⊗𝐴:𝐴-𝐌𝐨𝐝×𝐴-𝐌𝐨𝐝→𝐴-𝐌𝐨𝐝

for which there exists an isomorphism natural in 𝑀,𝑁,𝑍

Φ:Hom(𝑀⊗𝑁,𝑍)≅Bilin(𝑀,𝑁;𝑍)

For every pair 𝑀,𝑁 there is a distinguished bilinear map

𝑖:𝑀×𝑁→𝑀⊗𝑁

through which every bilinear map 𝑀×𝑁→𝑍 factors uniquely. We denote the elementary tensor by

𝑚⊗𝑛≔𝑖(𝑚,𝑛)

Further for morphisms 𝑓:𝑀→𝑀′ and 𝑔:𝑁→𝑁′ there is a unique morphism

(𝑓⊗𝑔):𝑀⊗𝑁→𝑀′⊗𝑁′

such that

(𝑓⊗𝑔)(𝑚⊗𝑛)=𝑓(𝑚)⊗𝑔(𝑛)

Proof. In light of (2.6.66) it is sufficient to show that Bilin(𝑀,𝑁;−) is representable for fixed 𝑀,𝑁 and the rest of the properties follow, observing that 𝑖=Φ(1𝑀⊗𝑁).

For let 𝐹 be the free abelian group on 𝑀×𝑁 and let 𝑆⊂𝐹 be the subgroup generated by elements of the form

  • (𝑚+𝑚′,𝑛)−(𝑚,𝑛)−(𝑚′,𝑛)
  • (𝑚,𝑛′+𝑛)−(𝑚,𝑛′)−(𝑚,𝑛)
  • (𝑎𝑚,𝑛)−𝑎(𝑚,𝑛)
  • (𝑚,𝑛)−(𝑚,𝑎𝑛)

Observe there is a canonical map 𝑀×𝑁→𝐹. Suppose that 𝑍 is an abelian group and 𝜓:𝑀×𝑁→𝑍 is a bilinear map. Then we may extend by linearity to a group homomorphism 𝜓:𝐹→𝑍. By construction 𝑆⊆ker(𝜓) whence there is a unique group homomorphism 𝜓̂:𝐹/𝑆→𝑍 such that 𝜓̂∘𝑖=𝜓 where 𝑖:𝑀×𝑁→𝐹/𝑆 is the canonical inclusion. This shows that (𝐹/𝑆,𝑖) is a universal element for Bilin(−,−;−) as we required and we denote this by 𝑀⊗𝑁. The representation exists as a functor of sets by (2.6.62), and Φ is clearly an isomorphism of 𝐴-modules because

Φ(𝑓+𝑔)=(𝑓+𝑔)∘𝑖=𝑓∘𝑖+𝑔∘𝑖=Φ(𝑓)+Φ(𝑔)

and

Φ(𝜆𝑓)=(𝜆𝑓)∘𝑖=𝜆(𝑓∘𝑖)=𝜆Φ(𝑓)

 ∎

Lemma 3.4.5 (Currying Lemma)

Let 𝑀,𝑁,𝑍 be 𝐴-modules then there are natural isomorphisms

Bilin(𝑀,𝑁,𝑍)≅Hom(𝑀,Hom(𝑁,𝑍))≅Hom(𝑁,Hom(𝑀,𝑍))≅Bilin(𝑁,𝑀,𝑍)𝜓𝑚→(𝑛→𝜓(𝑚,𝑛))𝑛→(𝑚→𝜓(𝑚,𝑛))(𝑛,𝑚)→𝜓(𝑚,𝑛)

This gives the following important result (which may be seen as an adjoint relationship)

Proposition 3.4.6 (Tensor-Hom Adjunction)

Let 𝑀,𝑁,𝑍 be 𝐴-modules then we have the following natural isomorphism of functors

Hom(𝑀⊗𝑁,𝑍)≅Hom(𝑀,Hom(𝑁,𝑍))𝜃→(𝑚→(𝑛→𝜃(𝑚⊗𝑛)))

We may also use the Currying Lemma (3.4.5) to demonstrate symmetry of the tensor product

Proposition 3.4.7 (Symmetry of Tensor Product)

There is a natural isomorphism

𝑀⊗𝑁≅𝑁⊗𝑀𝑚⊗𝑛→𝑛⊗𝑚

Proof. We observe that there is a natural isomorphism of functors which the tensor products represent

Bilin(𝑀,𝑁;𝑍)≅Bilin(𝑁,𝑀;𝑍)

so the result follows from (2.6.68). ∎

Proposition 3.4.8 (Associativity of Tensor Product)

There is a natural isomorphism of 𝐴-modules

(𝑀⊗𝑁)⊗𝑃≅𝑀⊗(𝑁⊗𝑃)(𝑚⊗𝑛)⊗𝑝→𝑚⊗(𝑛⊗𝑝)

Proof. We may make repeated use of the Tensor-Hom adjunction to exhibit natural isomorphisms

Hom((𝑀⊗𝑁)⊗𝑃,𝑍)≅Hom(𝑀⊗𝑁,Hom(𝑃,𝑍))≅Hom(𝑀,Hom(𝑁,Hom(𝑃,𝑍)))≅Hom(𝑀,Hom(𝑁⊗𝑃,𝑍))≅Hom(𝑀⊗(𝑁⊗𝑃),𝑍)

The required isomorphism is then a consequence of (2.6.68). For explicit form set 𝑍=𝑀⊗(𝑁⊗𝑃) and consider the identity map 1𝑍. We see tracing back under these natural isomorphisms this corresponds to the given map. ∎
