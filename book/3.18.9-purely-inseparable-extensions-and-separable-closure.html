3.18.9 Purely Inseparable Extensions and Separable Closure

In what follows we let 𝑝 be the characteristic exponent of 𝑘. In other words when char(𝑘)=0 then 𝑝=1 and the statements are trivial.

Definition 3.18.84 (Purely Inseparable Element)

An element 𝑥∈𝐾/𝑘 is purely inseparable (or 𝑝-radical) if there exists an integer 𝑛≥0 such that 𝑥𝑝𝑛∈𝑘. The height of 𝑥 is the least such integer.

We say an extension 𝐾/𝑘 is purely inseparable if every 𝑥∈𝐾/𝑘 is purely inseparable.

Further 𝐾/𝑘 is purely inseparable of height ≤𝑛 if additionally every 𝑥∈𝐾 has height at most 𝑛.

Lemma 3.18.85
Let 𝑎∈𝑘. For every integer 𝑒≥0 the polynomial 𝑓(𝑋)≔𝑋𝑝𝑒−𝑎 has at most one root in any extension field 𝐾/𝑘.
Proof. Suppose that 𝑏 is a root, then by (3.18.6) we have 𝑓(𝑋)=(𝑋−𝑏)𝑝𝑒. Then evidently 𝑏 is the unique root. ∎
Lemma 3.18.86
Let 𝑎∈𝑘∖𝑘𝑝. Then for every integer 𝑒≥0 the polynomial 𝑋𝑝𝑒−𝑎 is irreducible in 𝑘[𝑋].

Proof. Let 𝐾/𝑘 be a splitting field for 𝑓(𝑋)=𝑋𝑝𝑒−𝑎, 𝑏∈𝐾 a root and 𝑔(𝑋) be the minimal polynomial of 𝑏. By (3.18.6) 𝑓(𝑋)=(𝑋−𝑏)𝑝𝑒 in 𝐾[𝑋]. Suppose 𝜋 is a monic irreducible factor of 𝑓 in 𝑘[𝑋] then by unique factorisation in 𝐾[𝑋] we have 𝜋(𝑋)=(𝑋−𝑏)𝑟 for some 𝑟. In particular 𝜋 has 𝑏 as a root and therefore is divisible by 𝑔. By irreducibility we conclude every irreducible factor is equal to 𝑔 and the irreducible factorization of 𝑓 is 𝑔𝑠. Furthermore 𝑝𝑒=𝑟𝑠. Consequently both 𝑟 and 𝑠 are 𝑝th powers, and we have

𝜋=(𝑋−𝑏)𝑝𝑑𝑓=𝜋𝑝𝑒−𝑑

for some 0≤𝑑≤𝑒. In particular 𝑏𝑝𝑑∈𝑘 and 𝑎=𝑏𝑝𝑒−𝑑. By assumption 𝑎 is not a 𝑝-th power and so we must have 𝑑=𝑒, and 𝑓=𝜋 is irreducible. ∎

Proposition 3.18.87

Let 𝑥∈𝐾/𝑘 be purely inseparable of height 𝑒. Then the minimal polynomial of 𝑥 is 𝑋𝑝𝑒−𝑥𝑝𝑒. Furthermore

[𝑘(𝑥):𝑘]=𝑝𝑒[𝑘(𝑥):𝑘]𝑠=1

More precisely for every field 𝐿/𝑘 there is at most one morphism 𝑘(𝑥)/𝑘→𝐿/𝑘.

Proof. By definition 𝑥𝑝𝑒∈𝑘 is not a 𝑝-th power. Therefore the given polynomial is irreducible by (3.18.86) and therefore is the minimal polynomial by uniqueness. The first relation follows from (3.18.42) and the second from (3.18.77). ∎
Corollary 3.18.88

Let 𝑥∈𝐾/𝑘. Then the following are equivalent

  1. 𝑥 is purely inseparable
  2. 𝑚𝑥(𝑋)=(𝑋−𝑥)𝑟 for some 𝑟

In this case we must have 𝑟=𝑝𝑛 where 𝑛 is the height of 𝑥.

Corollary 3.18.89
Suppose 𝑥∈𝐾/𝑘 is both separable and purely inseparable. Then 𝑥∈𝑘.
Proof. The minimal polynomial is 𝑋𝑝𝑒−𝑥𝑝𝑒=(𝑋−𝑥)𝑝𝑒. Therefore by (3.18.11) 𝑒=0 which means precisely 𝑥∈𝑘. ∎

We first establish the following two results, which will be used below.

Lemma 3.18.90 (Inseparable Polynomials)
Let 𝑓∈𝑘[𝑋] be a non-constant polynomial and 𝑝 the characteristic exponent of 𝑘. Then 𝑓′=0⟺𝑝>1 and 𝑓∈𝑘[𝑋𝑝].
Proposition 3.18.91 (Separable Irreducible Polynomials)

Let 𝑓∈𝑘[𝑋] be an irreducible polynomial and 𝑝 the characteristic exponent of 𝑘. Then the following are equivalent

  1. 𝑓 is separable (i.e. 𝑓 and 𝑓′ are co-maximal)
  2. 𝑓′≠0
  3. 𝑝=1 or 𝑓∉𝑘[𝑋𝑝]

Proof. Note by assumption 𝑓 is not a unit and therefore not constant.

𝑎)⟹𝑏) Suppose 𝑓′=0 then (𝑓,𝑓′)=(𝑓)≠𝑘[𝑋] and therefore 𝑓 is not separable.

𝑏)⟹𝑎) Suppose 𝑓 is not separable. By (…) (𝑓) is maximal and so we must have 𝑓′∈(𝑓). As deg(𝑓′)<deg(𝑓) this implies 𝑓′=0.

𝑏)⟺𝑐) This is just the contrapositive of (3.18.90). ∎

Proposition 3.18.92 (Relative Separable Closure)

Let 𝐾/𝑘 be a field extension. The subextension 𝐾𝑠/𝑘⊂𝐾/𝑘 of separable elements form a separable algebraic subfield. Furthermore if 𝐾/𝑘 is algebraic then 𝐾/𝐾𝑠 is purely inseparable and the restriction map

Mor𝑘(𝐾,𝐿)→Mor𝑘(𝐾𝑠,𝐿)

is bijective for every normal extension 𝐿/𝐾. In particular when 𝐾𝑠/𝑘 is finite then we have the relation

[𝐾𝑠:𝑘]=[𝐾𝑠:𝑘]𝑠=[𝐾:𝑘]𝑠

Proof. Suppose 𝛼,𝛽∈𝐾 are separable algebraic then the field 𝑘(𝛼,𝛽) is separable algebraic by (3.18.80). As this contains 𝛼±𝛽 and 𝛼𝛽 then 𝐾𝑠 is a subfield which is by definition separable. Then by (3.18.78) we have [𝐾𝑠:𝑘]=[𝐾𝑠:𝑘]𝑠 when this is finite.

For 𝑥∈𝐾 let 𝑓(𝑋) be the minimal polynomial over 𝑘. There exists some integer 𝑚≥0 such that 𝑓(𝑋)∈𝑘[𝑋𝑝𝑚] but not in 𝑘[𝑋𝑝𝑚+1]. In other words 𝑓(𝑋)=𝑔(𝑋𝑝𝑚) and 𝑔(𝑋)∉𝑘[𝑋𝑝]. As 𝑓 is irreducible so is 𝑔, and is therefore the minimal polynomial of 𝑥𝑝𝑚. By (3.18.91) 𝑔 is separable and therefore 𝑥𝑝𝑚∈𝐾𝑠. This shows that 𝐾/𝐾𝑠 is purely inseparable.

Let 𝐿/𝐾/𝑘 be tower such that 𝐿/𝑘 is normal. Consider the mapping

Mor𝑘(𝐾,𝐿)→Mor𝑘(𝐾𝑠,𝐿)

obtained by restriction. By (3.18.70) it is surjective. Consider 𝜓:𝐾𝑠→𝐿 and 𝜓̂:𝐾→𝐿 an extension. For every 𝑥∈𝐾 we have 𝜓̂|𝐾𝑠(𝑥)=𝑖𝐾𝑠(𝑥)𝐿 by (3.18.87). This shows that 𝜓̂ is unique and the mapping is bijective. ∎

Definition 3.18.93 (Degree of Inseparability)

Let 𝐾/𝑘 be a finite extension. Then in light of (3.18.92) we may define the degree of inseparability to be the integer

[𝐾:𝑘]𝑖≔[𝐾:𝑘][𝐾:𝑘]𝑠

By (3.18.16) and (3.18.78) it is multiplicative in the sense that for a tower of finite extensions

[𝐿:𝑘]𝑖=[𝐿:𝐾]𝑖[𝐾:𝑘]𝑖

Further by (3.18.78) 𝐾/𝑘 is separable if and only if [𝐾:𝑘]𝑖=1.

We may refine this further by showing that [𝐾:𝑘]𝑖 is always a power of the characteristic exponent.

Proposition 3.18.94

Let 𝑘(𝛼)/𝑘 be a simple algebraic extension with characteristic exponent 𝑝. There exists a unique integer 𝑟≥0 such that every root of 𝑚𝛼,𝑘(𝑋) has multiplicity 𝑝𝑟 (in any splitting field).

Further

[𝑘(𝛼):𝑘]𝑖=𝑝𝑟

and 𝑚𝛼,𝑘(𝑋) has [𝑘(𝛼):𝑘]𝑠 distinct roots.

Proof. Let 𝐿/𝑘(𝛼)/𝑘 be a normal overfield, and let 𝛼1,…,𝛼𝑠 be the distinct roots of 𝑚𝛼,𝑘(𝑋) with 𝛼1=𝛼. Then we have a factorisation in 𝐿[𝑋] given by

𝑚𝛼,𝑘(𝑋)=∏𝑖=1𝑠(𝑋−𝛼𝑖)𝑚𝑖

for some integers 𝑚𝑖>0.

For every 1≤𝑖≤𝑛 there exists an automorphism 𝜎𝑖∈Aut(𝐿/𝑘) such that 𝜎𝑖(𝛼)=𝛼𝑖 (3.18.50). By definition 𝜎𝑖 fixes 𝑚𝛼,𝑘(𝑋) so by unique factorisation we deduce that 𝑚𝑖=𝑚1, so all the roots have the same multiplicity 𝑚.

If 𝑚=1 then by (3.16.39) 𝑚𝛼,𝑘(𝑋) is separable. Otherwise by (3.18.91) there is a polynomial 𝑔(𝑋) such that 𝑓(𝑋)=𝑔(𝑋𝑝) which has 𝛼𝑝 as a root. We may continue inductively to find an integer 𝑟≥0 such that 𝑓(𝑋)=𝑔(𝑋𝑝𝑟) and 𝑔(𝑋) is separable. Then 𝛼𝑝𝑟 is separable. By definition 𝛼 is purely inseparable over 𝑘(𝛼𝑝𝑟) so by (3.18.87)

[𝑘(𝛼):𝑘(𝛼𝑝𝑟)]𝑖=𝑝𝑟

By (3.18.78) we have

[𝑘(𝛼𝑝𝑟):𝑘]𝑖=1

and by multiplicativity [𝑘(𝛼):𝑘]𝑖=𝑝𝑟. By (3.18.77) the number of distinct roots of 𝑚𝛼,𝑘(𝑋) is [𝑘(𝛼):𝑘]𝑠. Comparing degrees we have deg(𝑚𝛼,𝑘(𝑋))=[𝑘(𝛼):𝑘]=[𝑘(𝛼):𝑘]𝑠⋅𝑚 whence 𝑚=[𝑘(𝛼):𝑘]𝑖=𝑝𝑟 as required. ∎

Proposition 3.18.95

Let 𝐾/𝑘 be a finite field extension with characteristic exponent 𝑝. Then [𝐾:𝑘]𝑖=𝑝𝑟 for some integer 𝑟≥0.

𝐾/𝑘 is separable if and only if 𝑝=1 or 𝑟=0.

Proof. We may decompose 𝐾/𝑘 into a tower of simple extensions, and the result follows from multiplicativity and (3.18.94). ∎
