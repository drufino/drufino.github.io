3.36.4 Geometrically Integral Algebras

Clearly a geometrically (resp. algebraically) integral algebra is integral, we are interested in providing sufficient conditions for the reverse implication.

Proposition 3.36.42
Let 𝐴 be a geometrically integral 𝑘-algebra and 𝐵 an integral 𝑘-algebra. Then 𝐴⊗𝑘𝐵 is an integral domain.
Proof. Let 𝐿=Frac(𝐵) then 𝐴⊗𝑘𝐵 is a subring of 𝐴⊗𝑘𝐿 and we are done. ∎
Proposition 3.36.43 (Criteria for Geometrically Integral in terms of Quotient Field)

Let 𝐴 be an integral 𝑘-algebra and 𝐾≔Frac(𝐴). Then 𝐴⊗𝑘𝐿 is integral if and only if 𝐾⊗𝑘𝐿 is integral.

In particular 𝐴 is geometrically (resp. algebraically) integral if and only if 𝐾 is geometrically (resp. algebraically) integral.

Proof. This follows exactly the same lines as (3.36.40). ∎
Proposition 3.36.44 (Criteria to be Geometrically Integral)

Let 𝐴 be a 𝑘-algebra. Then the following are equivalent

  1. 𝐴 is geometrically integral
  2. 𝐴 is geometrically reduced and irreducible
  3. 𝐴⊗𝑘𝑘― is an integral domain
  4. 𝐴⊗𝑘𝑘′ is an integral domain for every finite extension 𝑘′/𝑘

Note we may restrict d) to only finite separable, and finite purely inseparable extensions of height at most 1.

Proof. This is largely a collection of existing results. 𝑎)⟺𝑏) is by definition and (3.3.67). Then 𝑏)⟺𝑐)⟺𝑑) is from the reduced (3.36.26) and irreducible (3.36.36) cases, together with the trivial observation that reduced + irreducible ⟺ integral (3.3.67). ∎
Proposition 3.36.45 (Regular Extension)

Let 𝐾/𝑘 be a finitely generated field extension. Then the following are equivalent

  1. 𝐾/𝑘 is geometrically integral
  2. 𝐾/𝑘 is separably generated and relatively separably closed
  3. 𝐾/𝑘 is separably generated and relatively algebraically closed
  4. 𝐾⊗𝑘𝑘′ is an integral domain for every finite extension 𝑘′/𝑘
  5. 𝐾⊗𝑘𝑘′ is a field for every algebraic extension 𝑘′/𝑘
  6. 𝐾⊗𝑘𝑘― is an integral domain
  7. 𝐾⊗𝑘𝑘― is a field

We say such an extension is regular.

Proof. 𝑎)⟹𝑏) is (3.36.29) and (3.36.38). 𝑐)⟹𝑏) is immediate. For the converse suppose 𝛼∈𝐾/𝑘 is algebraic then 𝑘(𝛼)/𝑘 is geometrically reduced (3.36.29) and therefore separable algebraic (3.36.18). By hypothesis 𝛼∈𝑘 as required, giving 𝑏)⟺𝑐). For 𝑏)⟹𝑎): 𝐾/𝑘 being separably generated, it is geometrically reduced (3.36.24), and being relatively separably closed, it is geometrically irreducible (3.36.38) together these give 𝑎) (3.3.67). This closes the loop 𝑎)⟺𝑏)⟺𝑐). 𝑎)⟺𝑑)⟺𝑓) was already proven in (3.36.44), and 𝑓)⟺𝑔) was proven in (3.35.57). 𝑒)⟹𝑑),𝑔) is immediate.

For 𝑓)⟹𝑒) fix an algebraic 𝑘′/𝑘 and an embedding 𝑘′↪︎𝑘― (exists by (3.19.61)); the canonical map 𝐾⊗𝑘𝑘′→𝐾⊗𝑘𝑘― is injective (3.36.2) (𝑘′ being a 𝑘-subalgebra of 𝑘―), so 𝐾⊗𝑘𝑘′ is a subring of the domain 𝐾⊗𝑘𝑘―, hence itself a domain; as 𝑘′/𝑘 is algebraic, this domain is moreover a field (3.35.57). ∎

Corollary 3.36.46

Let 𝐴 be a finitely-generated integral 𝑘-algebra and 𝐾 the quotient field. Then the following are equivalent

  1. 𝐴 is geometrically integral
  2. 𝐾/𝑘 is geometrically integral
  3. 𝐾/𝑘 is separably generated and relatively separably closed
  4. 𝐾⊗𝑘𝑘′ is an integral domain for every finite extension 𝑘′/𝑘
  5. 𝐾⊗𝑘𝑘― is an integral domain
  6. 𝐾⊗𝑘𝑘― is a field
Proof. 𝑎)⟺𝑏) is (3.36.43) and the rest is (3.36.45). ∎

The mechanism underlying (6.3.44) below (base-changing a place of an algebraic function field along an algebraic extension of the constant field) is really a fact about discrete valuation rings, unrelated to the transcendence degree 1 setting of function fields; we isolate it here.

Proposition 3.36.47 (Unramified Base Extension of a Discrete Valuation Ring)

Let 𝑘 be a perfect field and 𝒪︀ a discrete valuation ring containing 𝑘, with fraction field 𝐹 and maximal ideal 𝔪, such that 𝐹/𝑘 is geometrically integral and 𝑘(𝔪)≔𝒪︀/𝔪 is finite over 𝑘.

Let 𝑘′/𝑘 be algebraic, write 𝒪︀𝑘′≔𝒪︀⊗𝑘𝑘′ and 𝔟≔𝔪𝒪︀𝑘′. Then

  1. 𝒪︀𝑘′ has exactly 𝑟 maximal ideals 𝔭1,…,𝔭𝑟, the preimages of those of 𝒪︀𝑘′/𝔟
  2. the localisation (𝒪︀𝑘′)𝔭𝑗 is a place of 𝐹𝑘′ unramified over 𝒪︀: a uniformiser of 𝒪︀ generates the maximal ideal 𝔭𝑗(𝒪︀𝑘′)𝔭𝑗, and
  3. every valuation ring 𝑅 of 𝐹𝑘′ with 𝑘′⊂𝑅, 𝒪︀⊂𝑅, and 𝑅∩𝐹=𝒪︀ is of the form 𝑅=(𝒪︀𝑘′)𝔭𝑗 for some 𝑗, and
  4. when 𝑘′/𝑘 is finite then (𝒪︀𝑘′)𝔭𝑗 is a discrete valuation ring, and
  5. (𝒪︀𝑘′)𝔭𝑗∩𝐹=𝒪︀ and 𝔭𝑗(𝒪︀𝑘′)𝔭𝑗∩𝐹=𝔪, inside 𝐹𝑘′: that is (𝒪︀𝑘′)𝔭𝑗 dominates 𝒪︀ and restricts to it. Together with c), the valuation rings 𝑅 of 𝐹𝑘′ with 𝑘′⊂𝑅, 𝒪︀⊂𝑅 and 𝑅∩𝐹=𝒪︀ are therefore exactly the (𝒪︀𝑘′)𝔭𝑗.

Proof. Drafted by Claude Sonnet, reviewed by David Rufino.

Let 𝜋 be a uniformiser of 𝒪︀, so 𝔪=(𝜋) (3.31.2). Since 𝐹/𝑘 is geometrically integral and 𝑘′/𝑘 algebraic, 𝐹𝑘′ is a field (3.36.45), and 𝒪︀𝑘′ is a subring (…) and in particular an integral domain. Further 𝒪︀𝑘′ integral over 𝒪︀ (3.24.20).

𝒪︀𝑘′/𝔟=𝒪︀𝑘′/𝔪𝒪︀𝑘′≅(𝒪︀/𝔪)⊗𝑘𝑘′=𝑘(𝔪)⊗𝑘𝑘′ as 𝑘-algebras (3.4.42): a finite-dimensional (as 𝑘(𝔪)/𝑘 is finite, by hypothesis) reduced (𝑘(𝔪)/𝑘 and 𝑘′/𝑘 both separable, 𝑘 being perfect, (3.19.103), (3.36.28)) 𝑘-algebra, hence a finite product of 𝑟 fields (3.36.12), with every prime maximal (3.36.13).

a) Finitely many maximal ideals. 𝒪︀𝑘′/𝔟, being a finite product of 𝑟 fields (above), has finitely many maximal ideals 𝔭1′,…,𝔭𝑟′, all its primes maximal (3.36.13). Every maximal ideal of 𝒪︀𝑘′ contracts to a maximal ideal of 𝒪︀ (3.24.16), that is to 𝔪 (𝒪︀ being local), hence contains 𝔟; so, by the ideal correspondence for 𝒪︀𝑘′↠𝒪︀𝑘′/𝔟 (3.3.56), the maximal ideals of 𝒪︀𝑘′ are exactly the preimages 𝔭1,…,𝔭𝑟 of 𝔭1′,…,𝔭𝑟′ – and, every prime of the quotient being maximal, these are also every prime of 𝒪︀𝑘′ containing 𝔟.

As 𝔟 is radical (the quotient being reduced), the Prime Nullstellensatz (3.3.46) gives 𝔟=𝔭1∩⋯∩𝔭𝑟; as the 𝔭𝑗 are pairwise comaximal (distinct maximal ideals), this equals 𝔭1⋯𝔭𝑟 (3.3.62).

b) Unramifiedness. Write 𝒪︀𝑗≔(𝒪︀𝑘′)𝔭𝑗. By (3.6.18).a) and multiplicativity of localisation on ideals (3.30.9),

𝜋𝒪︀𝑗=𝔟𝒪︀𝑗=𝔟𝔭𝑗=(𝔭1)𝔭𝑗⋯(𝔭𝑟)𝔭𝑗=𝔭𝑗𝒪︀𝑗

(the first equality as 𝔟=𝜋𝒪︀𝑘′; the factors 𝑖≠𝑗 becoming the unit ideal, as 𝔭𝑖⊄𝔭𝑗). So 𝜋 generates 𝔭𝑗𝒪︀𝑗, once 𝒪︀𝑗 is known to be a valuation ring of 𝐹𝑘′ properly between 𝑘′ and 𝐹𝑘′ – established next.

d) The finite case: 𝒪︀𝑗 is a DVR. Suppose 𝑘′/𝑘 is finite. Then 𝒪︀𝑘′ is a finite, free 𝒪︀-module (3.4.26), hence a Noetherian 𝒪︀-module (3.15.4) (𝒪︀ Noetherian, being a PID (3.16.2)), so Noetherian as a ring (3.15.3) (every ideal being in particular an 𝒪︀-submodule). So 𝒪︀𝑗 is Noetherian (3.15.7), local with maximal ideal 𝔭𝑗𝒪︀𝑗 (3.6.42), and a domain (above). It has Krull dimension 1: as 𝒪︀↪︎𝒪︀𝑘′ is injective and integral (module-finite), dim𝒪︀𝑘′=dim𝒪︀=1 (3.24.22) (𝒪︀ a DVR, hence of dimension 1 (3.31.2)); as 𝒪︀𝑘′ is a domain, (0)⊊𝔭𝑗 gives ht(𝔭𝑗)≥1, while ht(𝔭𝑗)≤dim𝒪︀𝑘′=1, so dim𝒪︀𝑗=ht(𝔭𝑗)=1 (3.22.4).b). With 𝔭𝑗𝒪︀𝑗=(𝜋) (above), 𝒪︀𝑗 is a DVR (3.31.2).

In general, 𝒪︀𝑗 is a valuation ring. Write 𝑘′=⋃𝑖𝑘𝑖′ as the directed union of its finite subextensions (3.19.47), so 𝒪︀𝑘′=⋃𝑖𝒪︀𝑘𝑖′ for 𝒪︀𝑘𝑖′≔𝒪︀⊗𝑘𝑘𝑖′ (a directed union of subrings, via (3.36.2)). For each 𝑖, 𝔭𝑖,𝑗≔𝔭𝑗∩𝒪︀𝑘𝑖′ contains 𝔪𝒪︀𝑘𝑖′ (as 𝔭𝑗⊇𝔟), so corresponds, under the ideal correspondence for the finite-dimensional ring 𝒪︀𝑘𝑖′/𝔪𝒪︀𝑘𝑖′=𝑘(𝔪)⊗𝑘𝑘𝑖′ (as 𝑘-algebras, (3.4.42)) (reduced, as 𝑘(𝔪)/𝑘 and 𝑘𝑖′/𝑘 are separable, 𝑘 perfect (3.19.103), (3.36.28), so a finite product of fields (3.36.12), as in the statement, applied to 𝑘𝑖′ in place of 𝑘′), to a prime of that ring, automatically maximal (3.36.13) so 𝔭𝑖,𝑗 is a maximal ideal of 𝒪︀𝑘𝑖′. By the finite case just established (taking 𝑘𝑖′ for 𝑘′), (𝒪︀𝑘𝑖′)𝔭𝑖,𝑗 is a DVR of 𝐹𝑘𝑖′, with 𝒪︀⊊(𝒪︀𝑘𝑖′)𝔭𝑖,𝑗 and 𝜋 generating 𝔭𝑖,𝑗(𝒪︀𝑘𝑖′)𝔭𝑖,𝑗.

Claim: 𝒪︀𝑗=⋃𝑖(𝒪︀𝑘𝑖′)𝔭𝑖,𝑗. For 𝑘𝑖′⊆𝑘𝑙′, 𝔭𝑙,𝑗∩𝒪︀𝑘𝑖′=𝔭𝑗∩𝒪︀𝑘𝑖′=𝔭𝑖,𝑗, so (3.6.9) gives (𝒪︀𝑘𝑖′)𝔭𝑖,𝑗⊆(𝒪︀𝑘𝑙′)𝔭𝑙,𝑗: a directed system. As 𝒪︀𝑘′=⋃𝑖𝒪︀𝑘𝑖′ is a directed union of subrings with 𝔭𝑖,𝑗=𝔭𝑗∩𝒪︀𝑘𝑖′, (3.6.10) gives 𝒪︀𝑗=⋃𝑖(𝒪︀𝑘𝑖′)𝔭𝑖,𝑗.

Claim: 𝑘′⊊𝒪︀𝑗⊊𝐹𝑘′. As 𝜋 generates the maximal ideal of every (𝒪︀𝑘𝑖′)𝔭𝑖,𝑗, (3.31.6) applies directly (with 𝐹𝑘′=⋃𝑖𝐹𝑘𝑖′ the corresponding directed union of fields): 𝒪︀𝑗=⋃𝑖(𝒪︀𝑘𝑖′)𝔭𝑖,𝑗 is a valuation ring of 𝐹𝑘′ with maximal ideal (𝜋)𝒪︀𝑗, and 𝒪︀𝑗⊊𝐹𝑘′. Also 𝑘′⊊𝒪︀𝑗: 𝑘′=⋃𝑖𝑘𝑖′⊆⋃𝑖(𝒪︀𝑘𝑖′)𝔭𝑖,𝑗=𝒪︀𝑗 is immediate, while 𝜋∈𝒪︀𝑗∖𝑘′, as 𝜋∉𝑘𝑖′ for every 𝑖 (else 𝜋∈𝔭𝑖,𝑗∩𝑘𝑖′ would be a unit of 𝒪︀𝑘𝑖′ (nonzero elements of the subfield 𝑘𝑖′⊂𝒪︀𝑘𝑖′ are units), contradicting 𝜋∉((𝒪︀𝑘𝑖′)𝔭𝑖,𝑗)⋆), so 𝜋∉𝑘′.

This closes b): 𝒪︀𝑗 is a valuation ring of 𝐹𝑘′ with 𝑘′⊊𝒪︀𝑗⊊𝐹𝑘′ – that is, a place of 𝐹𝑘′ – and 𝜋 generates both 𝔪 and 𝔭𝑗𝒪︀𝑗 (above): the same uniformiser generating the maximal ideal on both sides of 𝒪︀⊂𝒪︀𝑗 is exactly what it means for 𝔪 to be unramified in 𝒪︀𝑘′ – matching the factorisation 𝔟=𝔭1⋯𝔭𝑟 above. When 𝑘′/𝑘 is finite, 𝒪︀𝑗 is moreover a discrete valuation ring (d)), recovering the usual ramification index 𝑒(𝒪︀𝑗/𝒪︀)=𝑣𝒪︀𝑗(𝜋)=1.

c) Every valuation ring 𝑅 with 𝑅∩𝐹=𝒪︀ is some 𝒪︀𝑗. Let 𝑅 be a valuation ring of 𝐹𝑘′ with 𝑘′⊂𝑅, 𝒪︀⊂𝑅, and 𝑅∩𝐹=𝒪︀. As 𝒪︀𝑘′ is generated as a ring by (the images of) 𝒪︀ and 𝑘′ (spanned by simple tensors 𝑎⊗𝑏=𝑎⋅𝑏), both contained in 𝑅, 𝒪︀𝑘′⊂𝑅. So 𝔮≔𝔪𝑅∩𝒪︀𝑘′ is prime (a contraction of the prime 𝔪𝑅 along 𝒪︀𝑘′↪︎𝑅); it contains 𝔟, as 𝜋∈𝔪𝑅 (else 𝜋−1∈𝑅, so 𝜋−1∈𝑅∩𝐹=𝒪︀, contradicting 𝜋 a non-unit of 𝒪︀), giving 𝔟=𝜋𝒪︀𝑘′⊂𝔪𝑅∩𝒪︀𝑘′=𝔮. So 𝔮 corresponds to a prime, automatically maximal (3.36.13), of the finite-dimensional ring 𝒪︀𝑘′/𝔟≅∏𝑗𝐸𝑗; so 𝔮=𝔭𝑗 for some 𝑗.

Every 𝑠∈𝒪︀𝑘′∖𝔭𝑗 then satisfies 𝑠∉𝔮=𝔪𝑅∩𝒪︀𝑘′, that is 𝑠∉𝔪𝑅, so 𝑠 is a unit of 𝑅; by the universal property of localisation, 𝒪︀𝑗 then embeds in 𝑅. As 𝔪𝒪︀𝑗=𝜋𝒪︀𝑗 and 𝜋∈𝔪𝑅, 𝔪𝒪︀𝑗⊆𝔪𝑅∩𝒪︀𝑗; conversely 𝔪𝑅∩𝒪︀𝑗 is a proper ideal of the local ring 𝒪︀𝑗, hence ⊆𝔪𝒪︀𝑗: so 𝔪𝒪︀𝑗=𝔪𝑅∩𝒪︀𝑗, that is 𝑅 dominates 𝒪︀𝑗 (3.25.4). As 𝒪︀𝑗 is a valuation ring, it is maximal under domination (3.25.13), so 𝑅=𝒪︀𝑗.

e) 𝒪︀𝑗 restricts to 𝒪︀. As 𝒪︀𝑘′⊂𝐹𝑘′ is a domain, 𝒪︀𝑗 is the subring of 𝐹𝑘′ of fractions 𝑎/𝑠 with 𝑎∈𝒪︀𝑘′, 𝑠∈𝒪︀𝑘′∖𝔭𝑗, so 𝒪︀⊂𝒪︀𝑘′⊂𝒪︀𝑗. Moreover 𝔪⊆𝔪𝒪︀𝑘′=𝔟⊆𝔭𝑗 (a)), so 𝔪⊆𝔭𝑗𝒪︀𝑗=𝔪𝒪︀𝑗 (3.6.42): and 𝔭𝑗𝒪︀𝑗 is a proper ideal. Since 𝒪︀ is a valuation ring of 𝐹, (3.25.6) (with 𝐵=𝒪︀𝑗, 𝔫=𝔭𝑗𝒪︀𝑗) gives 𝒪︀𝑗∩𝐹=𝒪︀ and 𝔭𝑗𝒪︀𝑗∩𝐹=𝔪. Combined with c) this shows that the valuation rings 𝑅 of 𝐹𝑘′ with 𝑘′⊂𝑅, 𝒪︀⊂𝑅 and 𝑅∩𝐹=𝒪︀ are precisely 𝒪︀1,…,𝒪︀𝑟. ∎

Remark 3.36.48 (Relation to the Stacks Project)

For 𝑘′/𝑘 finite, this result also follows by combining two general facts from [Stacks]: unramified ring maps are stable under base change ([Stacks] (Tag 00US), applied to the finite separable, hence unramified, extension 𝑘→𝑘(𝔪), base-changed along 𝑘→𝑘′), and a finite-dimensional algebra over a field splits, after any base change, into a product of local factors dual to its maximal ideals (the Stacks Project analogue of (3.36.15), (3.36.13) above).

For 𝑘′/𝑘 merely algebraic, the Stacks Project’s treatment of unramified morphisms is developed for morphisms of finite type, so does not directly cover this case; the passage to infinite 𝑘′ via the directed union of finite subextensions, as in In general, 𝒪︀𝑗 is a valuation ring above, has no immediate analogue there and is instead the kind of limit argument found in the Stacks Project’s chapter on limits of rings (e.g. [Stacks] (Tag 07RN ff.), on writing a ring as a filtered colimit and transporting properties of the terms to the colimit).
