2.6.15 Direct Limit / Directed Colimit

Here we consider only a certain type of colimit, which is referred to variously as direct limit [AM69], limite inductive [Gro64] or filtered colimit [Lan98]. We also restrict to the case where the indexing category is “thin”, which is more properly refered to as a directed colimit.

Definition 2.6.123 (Directed Preorder)

We say a category 𝐼 is a directed preorder if it is small, thin and filtered, that is for any 𝑖,𝑗∈𝐼

  • there exists at most one morphism 𝑖→𝑗, and
  • there exists 𝑘∈𝐼 and morphisms 𝑖→𝑘 and 𝑗→𝑘

If there exists a morphism 𝑖→𝑗 then we write 𝑖≺𝑗.

Example 2.6.124
A typical example is any family of subsets of a set 𝑋 ordered by reversion inclusion which are closed under finite intersection.
Definition 2.6.125 (Direct limit / Directed colimit)

Let 𝐼 be a directed preorder and 𝐹:𝐼→𝒞︀ a diagram.

A cocone over 𝐹 is a pair (𝐿,{𝜇:𝐹(𝑖)→𝐿}𝑖∈𝐼) such that

𝑖≺𝑗⟹𝜇𝑗∘𝐹(𝑖→𝑗)=𝜇𝑖

The cocones over 𝐹 constitute a category (𝐹↓Δ) where morphisms 𝛼:(𝐿,{𝜇𝑖})→(𝐿′,{𝜇𝑖′}) are simply morphisms 𝛼:𝐿→𝐿′ such that 𝛼∘𝜇𝑖=𝜇𝑖′ for all 𝑖∈𝐼.

A cone (𝐿,{𝜇𝑖}𝑖∈𝐼) over 𝐹 is a direct limit for 𝐹 if it is an initial object (2.6.48) in the cocone category (𝐹↓Δ).

Explicitly for every cocone (𝐿′,{𝜇𝑖′}𝑖∈𝐼) there is a unique morphism 𝛼:𝐿→𝐿′ such that 𝛼∘𝜇𝑖=𝜇𝑖′ for all 𝑖∈𝐼.

We denote this by lim⟶𝑖∈𝐼𝐹(𝑖).

Definition 2.6.126 (Cofinal Subpreorder)

Let 𝐼 be a directed preorder. Then a full subcategory 𝐼′⊂𝐼 is said to be cofinal if

∀𝑖∈𝐼∃𝑖′∈𝐼′s.t.𝑖≤𝑖′

In this case 𝐼′ is also directed.

Proposition 2.6.127 (Cofinal Direct Limit)

Let 𝐼 be a directed preorder and 𝐼′ a full subcategory which is also directed. Suppose 𝐹:𝐼→𝒞︀ is a diagram then there is a unique morphism of cocones

lim⟶𝑖∈𝐼′𝐹(𝑖)⟶lim⟶𝑖∈𝐼𝐹(𝑖)

provided both these exist.

If 𝐼′ is cofinal in 𝐼 then this is an isomorphism.

Proposition 2.6.128 (Direct Limits of Sets)

Let 𝐼 be a directed preorder and 𝐹:𝐼→𝐒𝐞𝐭 a diagram. Consider

𝐿≔{(𝑖,𝑥)|𝑖∈𝐼,𝑥∈𝐹(𝑖)}/∼

where (𝑖,𝑥)∼(𝑗,𝑦) if for some 𝑘∈𝐼 we have 𝑖≺𝑘,𝑗≺𝑘 and 𝐹(𝑖→𝑘)(𝑥)=𝐹(𝑗→𝑘)(𝑦), and let 𝜇𝑖:𝐹(𝑖)→𝐿, 𝜇𝑖(𝑥)≔[(𝑖,𝑥)].

Then ∼ is an equivalence relation, and (𝐿,{𝜇𝑖}𝑖∈𝐼) is a direct limit for 𝐹 (2.6.125), that is 𝐿=lim⟶𝑖∈𝐼𝐹(𝑖). Moreover the 𝜇𝑖 are jointly surjective, that is every element of 𝐿 is of the form 𝜇𝑖(𝑥) for some 𝑖∈𝐼, 𝑥∈𝐹(𝑖).

Proof. Drafted by Claude Sonnet 5, reviewed by David Rufino.

∼ is an equivalence relation. Reflexivity and symmetry are immediate, using 𝑖≺𝑖 (via the identity morphism). For transitivity, suppose (𝑖,𝑥)∼(𝑗,𝑦) via 𝑘 (so 𝑖,𝑗≺𝑘, 𝐹(𝑖→𝑘)(𝑥)=𝐹(𝑗→𝑘)(𝑦)) and (𝑗,𝑦)∼(𝑙,𝑧) via 𝑚 (so 𝑗,𝑙≺𝑚, 𝐹(𝑗→𝑚)(𝑦)=𝐹(𝑙→𝑚)(𝑧)). As 𝐼 is directed there is 𝑛 with 𝑘,𝑚≺𝑛, and applying 𝐹(𝑘→𝑛), resp. 𝐹(𝑚→𝑛), to the two witnessing equations and using functoriality of 𝐹,

𝐹(𝑖→𝑛)(𝑥)=𝐹(𝑘→𝑛)(𝐹(𝑖→𝑘)(𝑥))=𝐹(𝑘→𝑛)(𝐹(𝑗→𝑘)(𝑦))=𝐹(𝑗→𝑛)(𝑦)𝐹(𝑗→𝑛)(𝑦)=𝐹(𝑚→𝑛)(𝐹(𝑗→𝑚)(𝑦))=𝐹(𝑚→𝑛)(𝐹(𝑙→𝑚)(𝑧))=𝐹(𝑙→𝑛)(𝑧)

so 𝐹(𝑖→𝑛)(𝑥)=𝐹(𝑙→𝑛)(𝑧), that is (𝑖,𝑥)∼(𝑙,𝑧) via 𝑛.

Joint surjectivity. Immediate, as every element of 𝐿 is by construction the class [(𝑖,𝑥)]=𝜇𝑖(𝑥) of some pair.

(𝐿,{𝜇𝑖}) is a cocone. For 𝑖≺𝑗, taking 𝑘≔𝑗 in the equivalence relation (using 𝐹(𝑗→𝑗)=1𝐹(𝑗)) gives (𝑖,𝑥)∼(𝑗,𝐹(𝑖→𝑗)(𝑥)), that is 𝜇𝑗(𝐹(𝑖→𝑗)(𝑥))=𝜇𝑖(𝑥), as required by (2.6.125).

(𝐿,{𝜇𝑖}) is initial. Let (𝐿′,{𝜇𝑖′}) be any cocone over 𝐹. Define 𝛼:𝐿→𝐿′ by 𝛼([(𝑖,𝑥)])≔𝜇𝑖′(𝑥); this is well-defined, for if (𝑖,𝑥)∼(𝑗,𝑦) via 𝑘 then, using the cocone condition for 𝜇′,

𝜇𝑖′(𝑥)=𝜇𝑘′(𝐹(𝑖→𝑘)(𝑥))=𝜇𝑘′(𝐹(𝑗→𝑘)(𝑦))=𝜇𝑗′(𝑦)

and by construction 𝛼∘𝜇𝑖=𝜇𝑖′ for all 𝑖. Any morphism of cocones 𝛽:𝐿→𝐿′ satisfies 𝛽(𝜇𝑖(𝑥))=𝜇𝑖′(𝑥) for all 𝑖,𝑥, so 𝛽=𝛼 by joint surjectivity of the 𝜇𝑖; hence 𝛼 is the unique such morphism, and (𝐿,{𝜇𝑖}) is initial in (𝐹↓Δ), that is a direct limit for 𝐹. ∎
