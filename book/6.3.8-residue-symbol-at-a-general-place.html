6.3.8 Residue Symbol at a General Place

At a non-rational place there is no element of 𝑘 to reduce against: (6.3.23)‘s recursion needs, at each step, a canonical way to lift a class of 𝑘(𝔪𝑃) back into 𝒪︀𝑃, and 𝑘(𝔪𝑃)≠𝑘 means 𝑘⊂𝒪︀𝑃 no longer supplies one. Rather than search for a section of 𝒪︀𝑃↠𝑘(𝔪𝑃) directly, we base change to 𝑘― instead: 𝑃 splits completely into 𝑑≔[𝑘(𝔪𝑃):𝑘] rational places of 𝐾𝑘―/𝑘―, each of which already has the Laurent coefficient theory of (6.3.23) available. The 𝑑 resulting elements of 𝑘― are exactly the Galois conjugates of a single element of 𝑘(𝔪𝑃), which we take as the residue at 𝑃.

Definition 6.3.49 (Conjugate Places)
Let 𝑃 be a place of 𝐾/𝑘, 𝑑≔[𝑘(𝔪𝑃):𝑘], and 𝜄1,…,𝜄𝑑:𝑘(𝔪𝑃)↪︎𝑘― the embeddings, with 𝑃𝑗′ the corresponding place of 𝐾𝑘―/𝑘― over 𝑃, 𝜄𝑗=𝜄𝑃𝑗′ (6.3.48). As each 𝑃𝑗′/𝑃 is unramified, every uniformiser 𝜋 at 𝑃 remains a uniformiser at 𝑃𝑗′ (6.3.44), so the residue symbol Res𝑃𝑗′(𝑤d𝑠) at the rational place 𝑃𝑗′ of 𝐾𝑘―/𝑘― (6.3.25) is defined for 𝑤,𝑠∈𝐾𝑘―.

Let 𝑃 be a place of 𝐾/𝑘, 𝜋 a uniformiser, and 𝑃1′,…,𝑃𝑑′ the conjugate rational places of 𝐾𝑘―/𝑘― over 𝑃 (6.3.49), at each of which 𝜋⊗1 remains a uniformiser.

Definition 6.3.53 (Residue Symbol at a General Place)

Let 𝑃 be a place of 𝐾/𝑘, and 𝑡,𝑧∈𝐾. Define

Res𝑃(𝑧d𝑡)≔∑𝑗=1𝑑Res𝑃𝑗′((𝑧⊗1)d(𝑡⊗1))∈𝑘

(residues at the conjugate rational places 𝑃1′,…,𝑃𝑑′, (6.3.49)) – landing in 𝑘 by (6.3.52).

Once the module of differentials Ω𝐾/𝑘 is available, it is a 1-dimensional 𝐾-vector space dual to Der𝑘(𝐾,𝐾) (both being trdeg𝑘(𝐾)=1-dimensional, (3.35.44)), and the pairing of 𝑧d𝑡∈Ω𝐾/𝑘 with 𝜕𝜋 is exactly 𝑧𝜕𝜋(𝑡); Res𝑃(𝑧d𝑡) above is then the residue, in this sense, of the differential 𝑧d𝑡.

Proposition 6.3.54 (Elementary Properties of the Residue Symbol at a General Place)

Let 𝑃 be a place of 𝐾/𝑘, 𝑡∈𝐾. Then

  1. 𝑧↦Res𝑃(𝑧d𝑡) is 𝑘-linear 𝐾→𝑘;
  2. if 𝑃 is rational (so 𝑑=1, 𝑃1′ canonically identified with 𝑃), Res𝑃(𝑧d𝑡) agrees with (6.3.25), for every 𝑧∈𝐾.

Proof. Drafted by Claude Sonnet 5, reviewed by David Rufino.

𝑘-linearity. Fix a uniformiser 𝜋 at 𝑃. For 𝑧,𝑧′∈𝐾 and 𝑎,𝑏∈𝑘, (𝑎𝑧+𝑏𝑧′)𝜕𝜋(𝑡)=𝑎(𝑧𝜕𝜋(𝑡))+𝑏(𝑧′𝜕𝜋(𝑡)) (𝐾-bilinearity of multiplication, 𝑘-linearity of 𝜕𝜋), so by (6.3.50) and 𝑘-linearity of 𝐿𝜋(−)−1 ((6.3.23) and (6.3.24)),

Res𝑃𝑗′(((𝑎𝑧+𝑏𝑧′)⊗1)d(𝑡⊗1))=𝑎Res𝑃𝑗′((𝑧⊗1)d(𝑡⊗1))+𝑏Res𝑃𝑗′((𝑧′⊗1)d(𝑡⊗1))

for every 𝑗; summing over 𝑗 gives Res𝑃((𝑎𝑧+𝑏𝑧′)d𝑡)=𝑎Res𝑃(𝑧d𝑡)+𝑏Res𝑃(𝑧′d𝑡).

Rational case. If 𝑃 is rational, 𝑑=1, and 𝑃1′ is identified with 𝑃 under 𝐾⊂𝐾𝑘― (with any uniformiser 𝜋 at 𝑃 remaining one at 𝑃1′); the sum defining Res𝑃(𝑧d𝑡) then has the single term Res𝑃1′((𝑧⊗1)d(𝑡⊗1))=Res𝑃(𝑧d𝑡) of (6.3.25) applied verbatim (as 𝑧⊗1,𝑡⊗1 agree with 𝑧,𝑡 under 𝐾⊂𝐾𝑘―, and 𝑃1′ with 𝑃). ∎

Lemma 6.3.55 (Regular Differentials have Zero Residue)
Let 𝑃 be a place of 𝐾/𝑘, 𝜋 a uniformiser at 𝑃, and 𝑡,𝑧∈𝐾 with 𝑧𝜕𝜋(𝑡)∈𝒪︀𝑃. Then Res𝑃(𝑧d𝑡)=0. In particular this holds if 𝑃 is rational and 𝑧,𝑡∈𝒪︀𝑃.

Proof. Drafted by Claude Sonnet 5, reviewed by David Rufino.

Write 𝑤≔𝑧𝜕𝜋(𝑡)∈𝒪︀𝑃. By (6.3.50), for every 𝑗,

Res𝑃𝑗′((𝑧⊗1)d(𝑡⊗1))=𝐿𝜋(𝑤⊗1)−1.

As 𝑃𝑗′ lies over 𝑃, 𝑤⊗1∈𝒪︀𝑃⊗1⊆𝒪︀𝑃𝑗′, so 𝐿𝜋(𝑤⊗1) has no negative powers (6.3.23) and its 𝑋−1-coefficient is 0. Summing over 𝑗 gives Res𝑃(𝑧d𝑡)=0. If 𝑃 is rational and 𝑡∈𝒪︀𝑃 then 𝜕𝜋(𝑡)∈𝒪︀𝑃 (6.3.30), so 𝑧𝜕𝜋(𝑡)∈𝒪︀𝑃 when also 𝑧∈𝒪︀𝑃. ∎

Fix a uniformiser 𝜋 at 𝑃. By (6.3.20) (applied to 𝐷≔𝜕𝜋), 𝜕𝜋=𝜕𝜋(𝑥)⋅𝜕𝑥, so 𝑧𝜕𝜋(𝑡)=(𝑧⋅𝜕𝑥(𝑡))𝜕𝜋(𝑥). By (6.3.50), for every 𝑗,

Res𝑃𝑗′((𝑧⊗1)d(𝑡⊗1))=𝐿𝜋((𝑧𝜕𝜋(𝑡))⊗1)−1=𝐿𝜋(((𝑧⋅𝜕𝑥(𝑡))𝜕𝜋(𝑥))⊗1)−1=Res𝑃𝑗′(((𝑧⋅𝜕𝑥(𝑡))⊗1)d(𝑥⊗1))

Summing over 𝑗 (6.3.53) gives Res𝑃(𝑧d𝑡)=Res𝑃((𝑧⋅𝜕𝑥(𝑡))d𝑥). ∎
